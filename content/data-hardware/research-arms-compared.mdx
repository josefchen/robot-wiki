Arms used in robot-learning research split at the control interface. The SO-101 and the Koch arm (version 1.1) cost a few hundred dollars in parts and run natively in LeRobot over a hobby-servo bus, while the Franka Research 3, UR5e and Kinova Gen3 expose 500 Hz to 1 kHz Ethernet interfaces with ROS 2 drivers and list no price on their datasheets.

Between those tiers, the AgileX PiPER, Trossen WidowX AI and UFACTORY xArm 7 list at US\$1,999 to US\$10,194 and reach LeRobot through plugins. All figures are the makers' own, read on 14 November 2026; LeRobot support is checked against release 0.7.0 and `main` at commit `fe1c545`. [Hardware Taxonomy](/data-hardware/hardware-taxonomy/) carries the SO-101 and WidowX AI prices.

## What the spec sheets leave out

Payload means different things. Trossen recommends no more than 50% arm extension when the ViperX 300 S carries its 750 g working payload <Cite id="trossen-vx300s-specs" />. Kinova's Gen3 ratings exclude the gripper <Cite id="kinova-gen3-specs-2026" />; OpenArm's 4.1 kg includes the end effector and is a one-minute hold at full extension <Cite id="openarm-docs-2026" />.

Repeatability is comparable only for Franka and UR, which cite ISO 9283; Franka's figure holds inside a 0.4 m cube about half a metre in front of the base <Cite id="franka-fr3-datasheet-2026" /> <Cite id="ur5e-datasheet-2026" />. Trossen pairs its 1 mm repeatability with 5 to 8 mm accuracy <Cite id="trossen-vx300s-specs" /> <Cite id="trossen-wx250s-specs" />. PiPER and xArm 7 name no test standard.

Trossen's documentation now calls the WidowX AI its previous-generation arm and lists WidowX Core, Pro and Heavy at 2, 4 and 6 kg payload, as preliminary specifications <Cite id="trossen-arms-docs-2026" />.

LeRobot's paper lists ALOHA among its first three setups <Cite id="lerobot-paper-2026" />, yet neither 0.7.0 nor `main` ships an ALOHA or ViperX class <Cite id="lerobot-robots-source-2026" />; Trossen's own plugin covers WidowX and ALOHA-style arms <Cite id="lerobot-third-party-robots-docs-2026" />.

## Choosing

- **A first LeRobot policy:** SO-101. Koch's parts cost about twice as much for the same native support: \$477 against \$229.88 for a leader and follower pair <Cite id="koch-v1-1-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="so-arm100-repo-2026" />.
- **A 6-DoF arm with a 1.5 kg payload:** PiPER or WidowX AI. The PiPER costs less than half as much <Cite id="agilex-piper-2026" /> <Cite id="trossen-widowx-ai-2026" />; the WidowX AI is sold as a leader too <Cite id="trossen-widowx-ai-2026" /> and has Trossen's own LeRobot plugin <Cite id="lerobot-third-party-robots-docs-2026" />.
- **Two 7-DoF arms in LeRobot without a plugin:** OpenArm, the only arm here beyond the SO-101 and Koch with a native LeRobot class <Cite id="lerobot-robots-source-2026" />. Its CAD and bill of materials are open (CERN-OHL-S-2.0) <Cite id="openarm-repo-2026" />.
- **Contact-rich tasks or torque control:** Franka Research 3 or Kinova Gen3. The FR3 has link-side torque sensors in all seven joints <Cite id="franka-fr3-datasheet-2026" />, and the Gen3 offers low-level torque control at 1 kHz <Cite id="kinova-gen3-specs-2026" />. The UR5e measures force at its tool flange instead <Cite id="ur5e-datasheet-2026" />.
