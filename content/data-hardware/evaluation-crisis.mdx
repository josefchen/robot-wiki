The evaluation crisis is the gap between the success rates robot papers report and what those rates can establish. A <Term id="success-rate">success rate</Term> folds trial count, task length, scene setup and scorer into one number, and papers often omit the number of runs, initial conditions or success criteria <Cite id="kress-gazit-evaluation-2024" />.

## Long tasks multiply small risks

<span id="what-the-success-rate-hides-the-horizon" />

In an illustrative model, every decision succeeds with the same probability $p$ given that all earlier ones did, so a task of $n$ decisions completes with probability $p^n$ <Cite id="maker-2025" />. At $p = 0.95$, 30 decisions give 21.5% and 100 give 0.6%.

Behavior cloning compounds the same way: a policy with error rate $\varepsilon$ on the expert's states can make up to $T^2\varepsilon$ mistakes over $T$ steps of its own <Cite id="dagger-2011" />.

Benchmarks now measure long horizons directly. CALVIN scores 1,000 chains of five tasks <Cite id="calvin-2021" />, and the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge ranks 100 full-length household tasks with partial credit <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2026" />. Its second-placed team reports 41.2% full-task success on the private test set <Cite id="behavior-2026-second-place" />.

## Small samples cannot rank policies

Real trials are slow, so results rest on few. TRI tested its Large Behavior Models, multitask <Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion Policy</Term> models <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, in 1,800 real rollouts and over 47,000 simulated ones. Each policy got 50 real attempts per task and condition, run blind in randomized order <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

TRI plots the whole spread of success probabilities each policy's trials support. It warns that many robotics papers risk measuring statistical noise, with too few trials to detect a real difference <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Snyder and colleagues add that a test finding no significant difference does not show two policies are equivalent <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />.

<TrialTally className="my-6" />

Deep reinforcement learning met the same problem <Cite id="henderson-rl-matters-2018" /> and answered with interval estimates and performance profiles instead of point estimates <Cite id="agarwal-statistical-precipice-2021" />.

Smarter tests need fewer trials. Snyder and colleagues' sequential test STEP stops once enough evidence accumulates, using up to 32% fewer trials than two earlier sequential tests <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />. A 2026 follow-up extends it to partial credit and continuous scores, with up to 70% less evaluation effort than fixed-size batches <Cite id="beyond-binary-success-2026" />.

Also: training seeds and statistical power <Cite id="random-seeds-power-2018" />, experiment design in RL <Cite id="empirical-design-rl-2023" />.

## Clean benchmarks overstate

Fixed benchmarks reward policies fitted to their exact scenes. On LIBERO's 130 simulated tasks <Cite id="libero-2023" />, models that score 95% fall below 30% under LIBERO-Plus's modest camera or initial-state shifts <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />. Shuffling or removing the instruction barely moves them, a sign the models largely ignore language <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />.

<Image id="libero-benchmark-overview-2023" />

Other stress tests agree:

| Benchmark | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| LIBERO-PRO | Models above 90% on standard LIBERO fall to 0.0% in its generalized setting <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" /> |
| THE COLOSSEUM | 30 to 50% degradation per perturbation factor, at least 75% when they combine <Cite id="colosseum-2024" /> |
| RoboDojo (42 simulated, 18 real tasks) | Best of 30 policies: 21.98 standard, 1.57 randomized, a 92.9% relative drop <Cite id="robodojo-2026" /> |
| MolmoSpaces-CommonSense (October 2026) | Zero-shot generalists lose over half their success as distractors increase, and act on the wrong object 30% of the time in clutter <Cite id="molmospaces-commonsense-2026" /> |
| RoboCasa365 | The best multi-task baseline, GR00T N1.5, averages 20.0% <Cite id="robocasa365-2026" /> |

Newer benchmarks build the variation in. RoboTwin 2.0 randomizes clutter, lighting, background, table height and language <Cite id="robotwin-2-2025" />, and RoboLab's 120 tasks target the training-evaluation overlap that trivializes success rates on existing benchmarks <Cite id="robolab-2026" />. For real robots, Marc Pollefeys, Hermann Blum and colleagues propose "stochastic challenges": reproducible real scenes with randomly sampled tasks, so labs are compared on draws from one distribution <Cite id="stochastic-challenges-2026" />.

Also: FurnitureBench, repeatable real-world assembly <Cite id="furniturebench-2023" />.

## Simulation stands in only when it tracks reality

SIMPLER tests whether simulation predicts real performance. It matches real backgrounds and textures, and fits the simulated controller to the real robot by <Term id="system-identification">system identification</Term> <Cite id="simpler-2024" />. Over about 1,500 episodes each on real and simulated Google Robot and WidowX setups <Cite id="simpler-project-page" />, agreement varied by task, and one WidowX comparison ranked policies wrongly <Cite id="simpler-2024" />.

SimFoundry's simulated evaluations reached a mean 0.911 correlation with real results over 7 tasks and 5 policy architectures <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />. Google DeepMind validated a Veo [world model](/world-models/evaluation) evaluator against 1,600+ real evaluations of eight Gemini Robotics checkpoints <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. SureSim corrects simulator bias with a few paired real and simulated trials, saving over 20 to 25% of hardware evaluation effort <Cite id="suresim-2025" />.

World models drift over long episodes. WorldOracle (October 2026) replays the actions of 100 two-minute AgiBot G2 episodes through seven world models and scores each predicted video against reality, out of 100 <Cite id="worldoracle-2026" />:

| World model | First 30 seconds | Full episode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DreamDojo | 50.56 | 27.08 <Cite id="worldoracle-2026" /> |
| Cosmos 3 Nano | 53.99 | 26.60 <Cite id="worldoracle-2026" /> |
| GE-Sim 2.0 (AgiBot; four authors shared with the benchmark) | 73.86 | 63.33 <Cite id="ge-sim-2-2026" /> |

Also: GraspNet-1Billion <Cite id="graspnet-1billion-2020" />, SoftGym <Cite id="softgym-2020" />, safe-control-gym <Cite id="safe-control-gym-2021" />, WorldEval <Cite id="worldeval-2025" />, Ctrl-World <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />.

## Blind pairwise arenas rank policies better

<Image id="roboarena-evaluation-2025" />

RoboArena spreads evaluation across seven universities on DROID's Franka Panda platform <Cite id="droid-2024" /> <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />. Evaluators run anonymous policy pairs double-blind and back-to-back from matched initial conditions. Its 612 pairwise comparisons of seven policies matched a 4,284-rollout reference ranking better than a conventional 17-task evaluation <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />.

RoboChallenge lets users drive its hosted robots remotely, with inference on their side, and cannot verify that a remote model matches its submitted name <Cite id="robochallenge-2025" />.

Automation cuts the labor: OpenVLA's evaluation needed over 2,500 rollouts and 100 hours of human labor, while AutoEval's automatic resets and success detection run around the clock <Cite id="autoeval-2025" />. RACE (November 2026) ranks checkpoints without rollouts, and its real-robot rankings matched deployment success <Cite id="race-checkpoint-selection-2026" />. ArmnetBench released all 3,718 operator-labeled episodes from its seven-policy comparison, 73% of them failures <Cite id="rollout-then-recycle-2026" />.

Also: Chatbot Arena <Cite id="chatbot-arena-2024" />, XPolicyLab <Cite id="xpolicylab-2026" />.

## Five questions for a result table

- How many rollouts produced the number, and is its uncertainty reported <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />?
- How long is the task?
- What survives perturbation <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />?
- Who scored the rollouts, with what agreement against the tested population <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />?
- What correlation backs a simulation number <Cite id="simpler-2024" />?

Even the scorer can be wrong. In the object-search benchmark HM3D-OVON a stop counts only when the annotated name matches the goal word: a couch labeled "sofa" fails a request for a couch. StopWatch's audit dropped 23% of unseen-category validation episodes for bad labels; on the rest, the demonstrating expert scores 72% officially and 96% corrected <Cite id="stopwatch-2026" />.

Pass-or-fail scores also hide how a task went. Eval-Actions argues this with over 13,000 real-robot episodes across 150+ tasks <Cite id="eval-actions-2026" />, and fine-grained task progress separates policies with fewer trials than binary success <Cite id="beyond-binary-success-2026" />. The [reliability gap](/frontier/reliability-gap) carries this arithmetic into deployment.
