<Term id="teleoperation">Teleoperation</Term> rigs are the hardware a person moves to drive a robot, or a stand-in for one, while a demonstration is recorded. Rigs close to the robot give data that transfers directly, but 2026's largest corpora, over 100,000 hours each, came from wearable and handheld devices <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" /> <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />.

## Leader arms record in the robot's own joints

In ALOHA, an operator moves leader arms that copy the robot's, so demonstrations land directly in the robot's joint positions. ACT, the [action-chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking) policy built for it, learned six two-handed tasks from about 10 minutes of demonstrations each and reached 84 to 96% success on four. The setup cost \$18k, or \$20k with cameras and add-ons <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="puppeteer" />

Mobile ALOHA added a wheeled base, and co-training with static ALOHA data raised success by up to 90% at 50 demonstrations per task <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />. ALOHA 2's designs are open source <Cite id="aloha-2-2024" />, and Trossen sells the two-armed Stationary AI for \$23,995.95 <Cite id="trossen-ai-2026" />.

Each collector needs a workstation. ALOHA Unleashed's 35 operators collected over 26,000 episodes on 10 robots in 8 months <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />; RT-1's 13 robots gathered about 130,000 in 17 months <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

## GELLO copies the arm in printed parts

GELLO shrinks the leader to a scaled replica of 3D-printed links and off-the-shelf motors, whose joint readings map one-to-one onto robot commands. Parts cost under \$300, and each arm needs its own design. In a 12-person study, GELLO beat VR controllers and a 3D spacemouse on reliability and speed <Cite id="gello-2023" />.

<KinematicTwin className="my-6" />

Exoskeletons work too. A policy trained only on AirExo-2 data performed about as well as one trained on teleoperation <Cite id="airexo-2-2025" />. HOMIE's \$500 humanoid cockpit finishes tasks in half the time of earlier systems <Cite id="homie-2025" />.

Leader arms can also take corrections. JoyLo+ motorizes GELLO-style leaders to follow the running policy until the operator grabs a grip, for about \$2,515 in parts. On four household tasks, 100 corrections raised average success 81 points, 30 more than 100 extra demonstrations did <Cite id="i3l-2026" />.

## UMI takes the robot out of collection

The operator holds a 3D-printed two-finger gripper with a wrist-mounted GoPro, and <Term id="slam">SLAM</Term>, which maps the scene while tracking the camera, recovers the gripper's path. The rig costs \$371, \$73 for the gripper and \$298 for the camera kit <Cite id="umi-2024" />. Arranging cups, it records 111 demonstrations an hour against 35 by spacemouse <Cite id="umi-gripper-site-2024" />.

Actions are recorded relative to the camera and timed to the robot's delays, so UMI policies run unretrained on any arm with a compatible gripper and camera. The data carry no force <Cite id="umi-2024" />.

| Force add-on | Result |
| --- | --- |
| ForceMimic | Zucchini peeled in 5 minutes, against over 13 with <Term id="force-feedback">force-feedback</Term> teleoperation <Cite id="forcemimic-2024" /> |
| FOMI | Object forces reach the hand without force sensors; about 29% more efficient than the best handheld baseline <Cite id="fomi-2026" /> |
| FACTR 2 | Force feedback on commodity arms from estimated joint torques <Cite id="factr-2-2026" /> |

## VR headsets scale across sites

In VR teleoperation the robot copies the operator's hand or controller poses through a <Term id="retargeting">retargeting</Term> layer. In [DROID](/data-hardware/datasets), 50 operators at 13 institutions used Quest 2 headsets to collect 76,000 trajectories, 350 hours in all. <Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion policies</Term> trained on it beat the next-best dataset by 22% in familiar conditions <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

| System | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| RoboTurk | A phone interface, statistically similar to VR controllers on completion time <Cite id="roboturk-2018" /> |
| OPEN TEACH | Runs on a \$500 Meta Quest 3 <Cite id="open-teach-2024" /> |
| Open-TeleVision | Stereo view of the robot's surroundings, mirrored arm and hand motion on humanoids <Cite id="open-television-2024" /> |
| Bunny-VisionPro | Low-cost haptics; keeps the arm clear of collisions and singular poses <Cite id="bunny-visionpro-2024" /> |

Apple Vision Pro launched at \$3,499 <Cite id="apple-visionpro-price-2024" />, and the October 2025 M5 model keeps that price <Cite id="apple-vision-pro-m5-2025" />.

Camera systems track the bare hand instead. Human and robot arms share no kinematics, so every action passes through calibration and <Term id="inverse-kinematics">inverse kinematics</Term>, which turns a hand position into joint angles. [HumanPlus](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc) shadows a person from one RGB camera and reaches 60 to 100% success with up to 40 demonstrations <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />.

Also: DexPilot <Cite id="dexpilot-2019" />, TeachNet <Cite id="teachnet-2018" />, AnyTeleop <Cite id="anyteleop-2023" />, Holo-Dex <Cite id="holo-dex-2022" />.

## Gloves bring the fingers, and force makes them precise

| Glove or wearable | Result |
| --- | --- |
| DexCap | SLAM and electromagnetic finger tracking with 3D scene capture <Cite id="dexcap-2024" /> |
| DexUMI | Hand exoskeleton with haptic feedback; 86% average success <Cite id="dexumi-2025" /> |
| SEED-UMI | Collects 3.0 times faster than exoskeleton teleoperation <Cite id="seed-umi-2026" /> |
| Sunday Skill Capture Glove | Matches the robot hand; 90% glove-to-robot conversion, company-reported <Cite id="sunday-act1-2025" /> |
| DITTO-X | Joint force and fingertip contact fed back; beats a commercial glove on quality and throughput <Cite id="ditto-x-2026" /> |
| DexPort | The operator carries the robot's own hand <Cite id="dexport-2026" /> |

FOMO-Dex puts a force controller between glove and robot hand: operators logged 30.7 minutes of successful screwdriving per hour, against 12.4 with stiff control. Its cloned policies averaged 6.3 screw turns per episode, against 0.6 <Cite id="fomo-dex-2026" />. PanTact adds force patches on the demonstrator's forearms and knees, and a Unitree G1 then embraced a bulky object in 17 of 20 trials <Cite id="pantact-2026" />.

Boston Dynamics argues that wearables capture only proxy signals for a dexterous hand and that simulated reinforcement learning is essential <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />. EgoMimic found an extra hour of hand data worth more than an extra hour of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />. Wearable corpora keep growing: 829 hours in Apple's EgoDex <Cite id="egodex-2025" />, over 270,000 for Generalist's GEN-0 <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />.

## Pick the gap you can afford

Leader arms and GELLO leave the smallest gap between collection and deployment; UMI, gloves and headsets trade some of that match for scale across scenes, as DROID did <Cite id="droid-2024" />. [The Data Bottleneck](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck) covers what the extra hours buy.
