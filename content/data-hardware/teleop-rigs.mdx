<Term id="teleoperation">Teleoperation</Term> rigs are the hardware a human interacts with to control a robot (or a stand-in for one) during data collection. While using hardware similar to the target robot results in data that transfers directly, the largest datasets of 2026 (each with more than 100,000 hours) instead came from wearable and handheld devices <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" /> <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />.

## Leader arms record in the robot's own joints

In ALOHA, an operator controls the robot by moving leader arms that mimic the configuration of the robot arms. Because of this, teleoperated demonstrations are given directly in terms of the robot's joint positions. ACT, the [action-chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking) policy built for it, learned six two-handed tasks from around 10 minutes of demonstration data each and achieved 84 to 96% success on four of them. The setup costs \$18k (\$20k including cameras and add-ons) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="puppeteer" />

Mobile ALOHA mounts ALOHA on a wheeled base, and co-training on data from Mobile ALOHA and static ALOHA improved its success rate by as much as 90% with 50 demonstrations per task <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />. The hardware designs for ALOHA 2 have been released as open source <Cite id="aloha-2-2024" />. The two-armed Stationary AI can be purchased from Trossen for \$23,995.95 <Cite id="trossen-ai-2026" />.

Each [collector](/data-hardware/data-market/) needs a workstation. ALOHA Unleashed's team of 35 operators used 10 robots to collect over 26,000 episodes over 8 months <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />, and the RT-1 team used 13 robots to collect about 130,000 episodes over 17 months <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

## GELLO copies the arm in printed parts

GELLO's leader is a scaled replica of the follower built from 3D-printed links and off-the-shelf motors, with a one-to-one correspondence between its joint readings and robot commands. All parts can be procured for less than \$300, but the leader must be custom-designed for each arm. In a study with 12 participants, GELLO outperformed a 3D spacemouse and VR controllers in reliability and speed <Cite id="gello-2023" />.

<KinematicTwin className="my-6" />

Exoskeletons work too. A policy learned with only AirExo-2 data performed roughly on par with one learned using teleoperation <Cite id="airexo-2-2025" />. HOMIE's \$500 humanoid cockpit completes tasks in half the time of prior systems <Cite id="homie-2025" />.

Leader arms can also take corrections. With JoyLo+, GELLO-style leaders are motorized and follow the running policy until an operator grabs a grip. Parts for two R1 Pro leaders cost around \$2,515. On four household tasks, each policy trained on 100 corrective trajectories. Success rose by an average of 81 percentage points from baseline, 30 points more than with 100 extra demonstrations per task <Cite id="i3l-2026" />.

## UMI takes the robot out of collection

The operator holds a 3D-printed two-finger gripper with a wrist-mounted GoPro, and <Term id="slam">SLAM</Term> is used to simultaneously map the environment and localize the camera in that map, resulting in an estimate of the gripper trajectory. The rig costs \$371, \$73 for the gripper and \$298 for the GoPro kit <Cite id="umi-2024" />. When arranging cups, the authors collected 111 demonstrations per hour with UMI, compared to 35 per hour with a spacemouse <Cite id="umi-gripper-site-2024" />.

Since actions are represented relative to the camera and timed to align with the robot's delays, policies trained with UMI data can be deployed with no retraining on any arm with a compatible gripper and camera setup. A limitation of UMI is that it cannot record force data <Cite id="umi-2024" />.

| Force add-on | Result |
| --- | --- |
| ForceMimic | Zucchini peeled in 5 minutes, against over 13 with <Term id="force-feedback">force-feedback</Term> teleoperation <Cite id="forcemimic-2024" /> |
| FOMI | Object forces reach the hand without force sensors; about 29% more efficient than the best handheld baseline <Cite id="fomi-2026" /> |
| FACTR 2 | Force feedback on commodity arms from estimated joint torques <Cite id="factr-2-2026" /> |

## VR headsets scale across sites

In VR teleoperation, a <Term id="retargeting">retargeting</Term> layer is used to command a robot using poses from a VR controller or the operator's tracked hands. The [DROID](/data-hardware/datasets) dataset was collected using VR teleoperation with Quest 2 headsets by 50 operators at 13 institutions, totaling 350 hours and 76,000 trajectories. <Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion policies</Term> co-trained on DROID outperformed the next-best method by 22 percentage points in success rate in familiar conditions <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

| System | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| RoboTurk | A phone interface, statistically similar to VR controllers on completion time <Cite id="roboturk-2018" /> |
| OPEN TEACH | Runs on a \$500 Meta Quest 3 <Cite id="open-teach-2024" /> |
| Open-TeleVision | Stereo view of the robot's surroundings, mirrored arm and hand motion on humanoids <Cite id="open-television-2024" /> |
| Bunny-VisionPro | Low-cost haptics; keeps the arm clear of collisions and singular poses <Cite id="bunny-visionpro-2024" /> |

Apple Vision Pro launched at \$3,499 <Cite id="apple-visionpro-price-2024" />, and the M5 model launched in October 2025 with no change in price <Cite id="apple-vision-pro-m5-2025" />.

Camera-based systems track the user's bare hand instead. Since human and robot arms do not share kinematic structures, every action must pass through calibration and <Term id="inverse-kinematics">inverse kinematics</Term> to map hand poses to joint angles. [HumanPlus](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc) imitates human actions using one RGB camera and achieves 60 to 100% success rates with up to 40 demonstrations <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />.

Also: DexPilot <Cite id="dexpilot-2019" />, TeachNet <Cite id="teachnet-2018" />, AnyTeleop <Cite id="anyteleop-2023" />, Holo-Dex <Cite id="holo-dex-2022" />.

## Gloves bring the fingers, and force makes them precise

| Glove or wearable | Result |
| --- | --- |
| DexCap | SLAM and electromagnetic finger tracking with 3D scene capture <Cite id="dexcap-2024" /> |
| DexUMI | Hand exoskeleton with haptic feedback; 86% average success <Cite id="dexumi-2025" /> |
| SEED-UMI | Collects 3.0 times faster than exoskeleton teleoperation <Cite id="seed-umi-2026" /> |
| Sunday Skill Capture Glove | Matches the robot hand; 90% glove-to-robot conversion, company-reported <Cite id="sunday-act1-2025" /> |
| DITTO-X | Joint force and fingertip contact fed back; beats a commercial glove on quality and throughput <Cite id="ditto-x-2026" /> |
| DexPort | The operator carries the robot's own hand <Cite id="dexport-2026" /> |

FOMO-Dex integrates a force controller into the mapping between a glove and a robot hand: operators logged 30.7 minutes of successful screwdriving per hour, against 12.4 with stiff control. Its cloned policies achieve an average of 6.3 screw turns per episode (compared to 0.6 turns) <Cite id="fomo-dex-2026" />. PanTact equips a demonstrator with patches that sense force applied to their forearms and knees, and a Unitree G1 then embraced a bulky object in 17 of 20 trials <Cite id="pantact-2026" />.

Boston Dynamics argues that data from wearables is only a proxy for dexterity in a robot hand and that reinforcement learning in simulation is essential <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />. EgoMimic reports that collecting an additional hour of hand data is more effective than collecting an additional hour of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />. The size of datasets collected using wearable technology has also increased, from 829 hours in Apple's EgoDex <Cite id="egodex-2025" /> to over 270,000 hours for Generalist's GEN-0 <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />.

## Pick the gap you can afford

Leader arms and GELLO prioritize minimizing the gap between data collection and deployment, whereas UMI, headsets and gloves accept a bigger gap in order to collect more diverse data at scale, as DROID did <Cite id="droid-2024" />. [The Data Bottleneck](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck) discusses the advantages brought by more data collection hours.
