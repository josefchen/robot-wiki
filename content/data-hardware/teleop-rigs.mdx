## Bimanual workstations: fidelity first

In ALOHA, an operator drives a leader arm that twins the follower, so demonstrations arrive in the robot's own joint space. ACT, the [action-chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking) policy built for that rig, learned six bimanual tasks, each from about 10 minutes of demonstrations. It reached 84 to 96% final success on four of them, 64% on taping and 20% on threading a velcro tie. The paper put the bimanual setup at \$18k, or \$20k with add-ons such as cameras <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

Mobile ALOHA added a base; with 50 demonstrations per task and co-training on static ALOHA data, success rose by up to 90% on tasks like sauteing shrimp and opening a two-door wall cabinet <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />. ALOHA 2's hardware designs are open source <Cite id="aloha-2-2024" />. The line now sells as Trossen AI at \$23,995.95 for the bimanual Stationary AI, \$33,695.95 for Mobile AI and \$4,545.95 for a single WidowX AI arm <Cite id="trossen-ai-2026" />.

Each extra collector needs another workstation. In ALOHA Unleashed, a pool of 35 operators without researcher oversight collected over 26,000 episodes for 5 tasks on 10 robots in 2 buildings over 8 months <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />. RT-1's fleet of 13 robots gathered about 130k episodes over 17 months <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

## GELLO: the kinematic twin

GELLO is a scaled replica of the target arm built from 3D-printed links and off-the-shelf motors, so joint readings map one-to-one onto robot commands. Parts cost under \$300, and the published designs cover Franka, UR5 and xArm. In the paper's user study, 12 participants ran five tasks on a bimanual pair of UR5 arms, and GELLO was more reliable and faster than VR controllers or a 3D spacemouse. The \$300 buys a controller only, and every new arm needs its own design <Cite id="gello-2023" />.

A policy trained only on adapted AirExo-2 data performed comparably to one trained on teleoperated data <Cite id="airexo-2-2025" />. HOMIE's \$500 humanoid cockpit pairs an exoskeleton arm and motion-sensing gloves with a pedal-driven locomotion policy, finishing tasks in half the time of earlier systems <Cite id="homie-2025" />.

## UMI: collection without a robot

The UMI operator holds a 3D-printed parallel-jaw gripper with a wrist-mounted GoPro and a 155-degree fisheye lens, and SLAM recovers the gripper pose from the video. The paper bills the printed gripper at \$73 and the GoPro and accessories at \$298, for a \$371 collection rig <Cite id="umi-2024" />. On cup arrangement the project site reports 111 demonstrations per hour, against 35 for spacemouse teleoperation and 231 by bare hand <Cite id="umi-gripper-site-2024" />.

Latency matching at inference time and a camera-relative action representation let UMI policies deploy zero-shot on any arm with a compatible gripper and camera, shown on UR5 and Franka. The data lack a force channel <Cite id="umi-2024" />. ForceMimic adds one: its operator peeled a zucchini in 5 minutes, against over 13 with force-feedback teleoperation <Cite id="forcemimic-2024" />.

Four collectors gathered enough UMI data in one afternoon for about 90% success in new environments <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />. A policy post-trained only on HiFi-UMI data matched in-domain teleoperation across three backbones <Cite id="hifi-umi-2026" />.

## VR teleoperation: scale through headsets

In VR teleoperation the robot mirrors the operator's hand or controller poses through a <Term id="retargeting">retargeting</Term> layer. In [DROID](/data-hardware/datasets), 50 operators at 13 institutions used Meta Quest 2 headsets on Franka Panda rigs to collect 76,000 trajectories totaling 350 hours over 12 months. <Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion policies</Term> trained on that pool beat the next-best dataset by 22% in-distribution <Cite id="droid-2024" />. RoboTurk's phone interface was statistically similar to VR controllers on task completion time <Cite id="roboturk-2018" />.

OPEN TEACH runs on a \$500 Meta Quest 3 at up to 90 Hz <Cite id="open-teach-2024" />. Open-TeleVision renders the robot's surroundings in stereo and mirrors the operator's arm and hand motion on two humanoids <Cite id="open-television-2024" />. Bunny-VisionPro adds low-cost haptic feedback and collision and singularity avoidance in retargeting <Cite id="bunny-visionpro-2024" />. The Apple Vision Pro it runs on launched at \$3,499 <Cite id="apple-visionpro-price-2024" />, and the October 2025 M5 model keeps that price <Cite id="apple-vision-pro-m5-2025" />.

Human and robot arms share no kinematics, so every action passes through <Term id="inverse-kinematics">IK</Term> and calibration. [HumanPlus](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc) shadows a person from one RGB camera and reaches 60 to 100% success with up to 40 demonstrations <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />.

## Gloves and wearables

DexUMI's hand exoskeleton gives the operator direct haptic feedback and adapts human motion to feasible robot-hand motion, for an 86% average success rate <Cite id="dexumi-2025" />. SEED-UMI collects data 3.0x faster than exoskeleton-based teleoperation <Cite id="seed-umi-2026" />. Sunday's Skill Capture Glove shares its robot hand's geometry and sensor layout, and Sunday reports converting glove data to robot data with a 90% success rate <Cite id="sunday-act1-2025" />. DITTO-X, from Stanford and Columbia researchers (September 2026), renders joint-level force and fingertip contacts from the robot hand and improved demonstration quality and throughput over a commercial hand-tracking glove <Cite id="ditto-x-2026" />.

Boston Dynamics argues that wearables capture only proxy signals for controlling a dexterous hand, and that reinforcement learning in simulation is an essential component <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />. EgoMimic found an extra hour of hand data worth more than an extra hour of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />. Apple's EgoDex recorded 829 hours with Vision Pro hand tracking <Cite id="egodex-2025" />, and Generalist AI reports over 270,000 hours for GEN-0 <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />.

## Choosing by axis

Leader arms and GELLO leave the smallest gap between collection and deployment, while UMI, gloves and headsets scale across scenes, as DROID did across 13 institutions <Cite id="droid-2024" />. [The Data Bottleneck](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck) covers what the extra hours buy.
