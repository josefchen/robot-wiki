A robot-learning system is a pipeline that turns recorded physical events into a trained policy, and the policy's decisions back into motion. The trained checkpoint is one component among capture, data formats, training, evaluation, serving and recovery. The open-source LeRobot library spans that stack, from motor-control middleware to dataset storage, streaming and asynchronous inference <Cite id="lerobot-paper-2026" />.

<Image id="openvla-overview-2024" />

Machine learning systems often incur massive ongoing maintenance costs from data dependencies, configuration issues and hidden feedback loops <Cite id="hidden-technical-debt-2015" />. Treating the stack as seven contracts, each a handoff between stages with a saved artifact and a test, lets a failure be traced to one boundary.

## Contract 1: hardware and calibration

Record firmware, control mode and calibrated transforms for every camera, encoder, gripper and sensor: no later data split can correct a camera move nobody recorded. Time offsets between sensors are calibration parameters too, estimable together with the spatial ones <Cite id="kalibr-2013" />, and [calibration](/classical/calibration) covers the residuals.

<Image id="droid-camera-calibration-2024" />

## Contract 2: episode capture

An episode, one recorded attempt at a task, holds raw observations, robot state, commanded action, measured response, outcome, interventions and timestamps. ROS 2 Iron made MCAP its default recording format after testing found SQLite3 storage often not performant enough <Cite id="ros2-iron-mcap-2023" />, and RLDS is a standard, lossless format for sharing episodes <Cite id="rlds-2021" />. Keep resets and failures; RoboMIND releases 5,000 real-world failure demonstrations with annotated causes <Cite id="robomind-2024" />. DROID standardized [collection hardware](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs) and protocols across institutions <Cite id="droid-2024" />, while <Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> normalized over a million trajectories from 22 embodiments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

## Contract 3: a versioned training schema

Every preprocessing step, from frame selection to normalization and splits, needs a version. LeRobot provides datasets, policies and tools around one workflow <Cite id="lerobot-docs-2026" />. Its dataset format 3.0 packs many episodes into each file, since one file per episode hit file-system limits at millions of episodes <Cite id="lerobot-dataset-v3-2025" />, and its November 2026 release streams training data from the Hugging Face Hub, which then held almost 300 TB of LeRobot-format data <Cite id="lerobot-v0-7-2026" />.

Joint targets, changes in gripper position and torques are different [action labels](/manipulation/action-spaces) even in arrays of equal width. Tokenizers that bin each dimension separately perform poorly on high-frequency dexterous data, and FAST+ is a universal tokenizer trained on 1M real trajectories <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />.

Split by the factor a claim generalizes across, since random splits leak near-identical scenes into testing. Across 15,000+ real rollouts, environment and object diversity mattered far more than demonstration count <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />.

## Contract 4: reproducible training

A training run saves its configuration, code revision, dataset hash, seeds and normalization statistics with its weights. Identical seeds do not guarantee reproducibility across framework releases or between CPU and GPU, as PyTorch's documentation warns <Cite id="pytorch-reproducibility-2026" />. Safetensors files store each tensor's type and shape in a header before the data <Cite id="safetensors-format" />. RACE (November 2026) picks checkpoints without robot trials, scoring calibrated action deviations on held-out successful trajectories <Cite id="race-checkpoint-selection-2026" />.

In robomimic's study of offline human demonstrations, recurrent behavior cloning was strong while offline reinforcement learning struggled on the same data <Cite id="robomimic-2021" />. The OpenVLA-OFT recipe raised OpenVLA <Cite id="openvla-2024" /> from 76.5% to 97.1% average LIBERO success and its action throughput 26-fold <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Physical Intelligence reports that RECAP, which learns from deployment experience, more than doubles throughput on some of its hardest tasks <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

## Contract 5: simulation and offline evaluation

Simulation helps only if its scores rank policies as hardware does. Isaac Lab runs large batches of environments <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />, and version 3.0 (October 2026) one task interface across several physics and rendering backends <Cite id="isaac-lab-3-0-2026" />. SIMPLER measures that ranking agreement on matched tasks <Cite id="simpler-2024" />. PolaRiS rebuilds real scenes from short video scans and reports much stronger correlation with real performance than existing simulated benchmarks <Cite id="polaris-2025" />, and SimFoundry reports a mean correlation of 0.911 across 7 tasks <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />. Co-training on simulated and real data raised real-world task performance by 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />.

## Contract 6: real-world evaluation

Papers often report success rates with little or no information on run counts, initial conditions or success criteria <Cite id="kress-gazit-evaluation-2024" />, and LIBERO-Plus showed that modest perturbations expose gaps high benchmark scores hide <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />; the [evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis) covers the statistics. RoboArena ranks policies in double-blind pairwise trials at several universities <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />, AutoEval runs evaluations around the clock <Cite id="autoeval-2025" />, and RoboDojo pairs 42 simulated and 18 real-world tasks <Cite id="robodojo-2026" />. A sequential test that can stop early cut evaluation effort by up to 70% against batch methods <Cite id="beyond-binary-success-2026" />.

## Contract 7: serving and robot integration

On the robot, the policy sits in a chain: an observation assembler, a model server, a command gate that checks outputs, a controller bridge and a supervisor, with safety and high-rate feedback running below the learned policy. ROS 2, the common robot middleware, was redesigned from the ground up because ROS 1 lacked production-grade features <Cite id="ros2-science-robotics-2022" />; the [ROS 2 guide](/classical/ros2-for-ml-engineers) defines the interfaces. For the controller bridge, ros2_control provides real-time-capable control <Cite id="ros2-control-docs-2026" />. Intrinsic released its ROS-compatible Intrinsic Core under Apache 2.0 in September 2026 <Cite id="intrinsic-core-2026" />, the month Qualcomm agreed to acquire PickNik, steward of the MoveIt motion planner <Cite id="qualcomm-picknik-2026" />.

Real-time chunking generates the next action chunk while the current one executes, avoiding pauses between chunks <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />, and SmolVLA decouples action prediction from execution <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />; [real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution) has the latency budgets.

The RAI Institute's Exploy (October 2026) exports a PyTorch policy's observation code, network and action processing into one ONNX file for a C++ runtime, checking numerical equivalence before hardware runs <Cite id="exploy-2026" />. ONNX, an open model format, versions the graph and its operator sets <Cite id="onnx-ir-spec" />, and ONNX Runtime partitions that graph across available execution providers <Cite id="onnx-runtime-architecture" />. A TensorRT engine is by default compatible only with the TensorRT version that built it <Cite id="tensorrt-engine-compatibility-2026" />.

## The data loop after deployment

In imitation learning, future observations depend on the policy's own actions, breaking the independence assumption of standard statistical learning <Cite id="dagger-2011" />. Failures should return to the dataset tagged with their cause. Sirius re-weights samples by approximated human trust and reports 27% higher real-hardware success than prior methods <Cite id="sirius-2022" />, CUPID shows under 33% of curated data can yield state-of-the-art diffusion policies on RoboMimic <Cite id="cupid-2025" />, and LoopWAM (October 2026) keeps failed rollouts and weights supervision by quality: three deploy-and-train rounds raised its RoboChallenge success from 23.67% to 43.33% <Cite id="loopwam-2026" />. Mulligan (Stanford, October 2026) starts each supervised round at observed failures and untried object placements; with value-based action selection it raised final success on three real tasks by 10 to 34 percentage points over 2,550 held-out, blinded episodes <Cite id="mulligan-2026" />.

## A minimal stack for a small team

Start with versioned episode files that are never edited in place, one versioned conversion command, a checkpoint registry, a deterministic offline test batch <Cite id="lerobot-dataset-v3-2025" /> <Cite id="hidden-technical-debt-2015" />, an evaluation sheet linked to episode IDs <Cite id="kress-gazit-evaluation-2024" />, and a policy process kept apart from the gate that approves its commands <Cite id="lerobot-paper-2026" /> <Cite id="safety-filter-2023" />.
