Robot learning datasets consist of observations (images from cameras, other sensor data) and actions taken by the robot. Open datasets trade breadth for consistency: <Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> consists of more than a million trajectories collected using 22 robot embodiments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />, while DROID contains 76,000 trajectories all using one robotic arm setup <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

<Image id="open-x-embodiment-overview-2023" />

## Count datasets in hours, not episodes

<DatasetTable className="my-6 wide-measure" />

A trajectory can last eight seconds or eight minutes: on average about 17 seconds in DROID and 11 seconds in AgiBot World <Cite id="droid-2024" /> <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. Some datasets report their size in bytes (NVIDIA's open Physical AI dataset is 15 terabytes for more than 320,000 trajectories <Cite id="nvidia-physical-ai-dataset-2025" />) or in hours (more than 1,200 hours of teleoperated data in 10,000 demonstrations for the 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2025" />).

Also: synthetic InternData-A1, 7,433 hours <Cite id="interndata-a1-2025" />.

## Open X-Embodiment pools many labs in one format

OXE (2023) is a collection of 60 datasets released by 34 labs, standardized in the RLDS episode format <Cite id="rlds-2021" />, containing more than a million trajectories and encompassing 527 skills across 22 embodiments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

Training on such a pool was particularly helpful on datasets that, on their own, are small: RT-1-X outperformed each one's original method by an average of 50%, and RT-2-X saw a roughly threefold improvement in success rate over RT-2 on tasks only demonstrated on other embodiments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

Later <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> pools are larger:

| Pool | Size | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ARIO | About 3 million episodes | <Cite id="ario-2024" /> |
| CrossFormer | 900,000 trajectories from 20 embodiments, quadcopters among them | <Cite id="crossformer-2024" /> |
| OXE-AugE | Over 4.4 million trajectories, adding nine robot types | <Cite id="oxe-auge-2025" /> |

Federation costs consistency: datasets in OXE use different action spaces, control frequencies, camera setups and licenses, and four robot types make up more than 85% of OXE's real data <Cite id="oxe-auge-2025" />. Moritz Reuss's ICLR 2026 review of VLA papers describes OXE data as mostly low-quality <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />. The weighting scheme in Re-Mix outperforms sampling in proportion to dataset size by 38% and RT-X's hand-picked weighting by 32% <Cite id="remix-2024" />.

Also: RoboNet <Cite id="robonet-2019" />; [VLA models](/manipulation/vla-models) trained on OXE, Octo <Cite id="octo-2024" /> and OpenVLA <Cite id="openvla-2024" />.

## DROID trades robot variety for scene variety

Fifty operators in 13 institutions gathered its 76,000 trajectories in 564 scenes, all on one rig: a Franka Panda arm equipped with three cameras and [teleoperated](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs) using a Meta Quest 2 headset <Cite id="droid-2024" />. <Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion policies</Term> trained on DROID outperformed the second-best method by 22 percentage points in-domain and by 17 percentage points out-of-domain <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

DROID received updates to include language annotations on 75,000 episodes and improved camera calibrations on 36,000 <Cite id="droid-project-page" />. DROID is released under the CC BY 4.0 license <Cite id="droid-2024" />, which allows commercial use as well as adaptation given attribution <Cite id="cc-by-4-0-deed" />.

Single-platform collections can be bigger:

| Dataset | Size | Source |

| Language-Table | Nearly 600,000 language-labeled trajectories | <Cite id="language-table-2022" /> |
| Galaxea Open-World | 500 hours across 50 scenes, one embodiment | <Cite id="galaxea-open-world-2025" /> |

## BridgeData V2 is built on a cheap arm

BridgeData V2, collected using the WidowX 250 six-degree-of-freedom arm, contains 60,096 trajectories <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-project-page" />. Of these, 50,365 are VR-teleoperated demonstrations and 9,731 are scripted pick-and-place runs collected in 24 environments <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />. The 2021 version of BridgeData showed that joint training with 50 demonstrations of a new task in a new environment doubled success on average <Cite id="bridge-data-2021" />.

<Image id="bridgedata-v2-sink-tasks-2023" />

BridgeData V2 is available under the CC BY 4.0 license <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-project-page" /> and is included, for the most part, in OXE <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. The paper also presents goal-conditioned <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> baselines <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />.

## AgiBot World is one company's fleet data

The Beta release contained 1,001,552 trajectories from AgiBot's G1 robot, totaling 2,976 hours. AgiBot reports 217 tasks and claims pretraining on AgiBot World Beta leads to a 30% performance uplift on average over OXE pretraining, a vendor figure with no independent replication <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. Despite having over a million trajectories, AgiBot World Beta contains less interaction than Ego4D, which contains 3,670 hours of passive video <Cite id="ego4d-2022" />.

AgiBot World currently contains 1,003,672 trajectories available under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license <Cite id="agibot-world-repo" />, meaning that it cannot be used commercially <Cite id="cc-by-nc-sa-4-0-deed" />. AgiBot World 2026, collected using AgiBot's newer G2 robot, is also under the same license and contains 17.5 TB, although AgiBot does not specify how many episodes or hours this corresponds to <Cite id="agibot-world-2026" />. AgiBot also [sells data](/data-hardware/data-market/) through its unit Mifeng; in April 2026, Yicai reported Mifeng's Yao Maoqing putting China's real-robot data at roughly RMB 500 to 1,000 an hour <Cite id="yicai-mifeng-2026" />.

## RoboMIND records four robots under one protocol

RoboMIND reports 107,000 successful trajectories (305.5 hours of real and simulated data) collected while performing 479 tasks on four robots. It also releases 5,000 failed real-world trajectories with annotations describing failure causes <Cite id="robomind-2024" />. RoboMIND 2.0 (December 2025) expands on RoboMIND with over 310,000 dual-arm trajectories collected from six robot embodiments, 12,000 of which include tactile sensing <Cite id="robomind-2-2025" />.

Although RoboMIND's [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/x-humanoid-robomind/RoboMIND) lists an Apache-2.0 license, downloading the data is subject to access conditions <Cite id="robomind-dataset-card" />.

## A usable dataset is legal, consistent and versioned

A dataset must be legal for its use, consistent in actions, units, frames and rates, and downloadable as versioned episodes. In LeRobot's case, episodes were initially released one per file but ran into file-system limitations, and in 2025 the format changed to files with multiple episodes <Cite id="lerobot-dataset-v3-2025" />.

Mixing needs one schema for observations and actions, with adapters to convert incoming datasets to that schema. It is important to keep track of which dataset an episode is from throughout training to understand if performance gains are due to real transfer or one dataset dominating <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" /> <Cite id="octo-2024" /> <Cite id="remix-2024" />. The [robot-learning stack](/data-hardware/robot-learning-stack) covers the tooling.
