A robot learning dataset records robot episodes: what the cameras and sensors saw, paired with the commands the robot carried out. Open datasets trade breadth for consistency. <Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> pools over a million trajectories from 22 kinds of robot <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />, while all of DROID's 76,000 come from one arm setup <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

## How the datasets compare

<DatasetTable className="my-6 wide-measure" />

Episode counts mislead, because a trajectory can last eight seconds or eight minutes. DROID's average about 17 seconds and AgiBot World's about 11 <Cite id="droid-2024" /> <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. Some report size in bytes: NVIDIA's open Physical AI dataset holds 15 terabytes and more than 320,000 trajectories <Cite id="nvidia-physical-ai-dataset-2025" />. Newer releases give hours: the 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge supplies 10,000 teleoperated demonstrations totaling over 1,200 hours <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2025" />, and the synthetic InternData-A1 over 630,000 trajectories and 7,433 hours <Cite id="interndata-a1-2025" />.

## Open X-Embodiment: many labs, one format

Open X-Embodiment (OXE, 2023) converted 60 existing datasets from 34 labs into RLDS, a shared lossless format for robot episodes <Cite id="rlds-2021" />, giving over a million trajectories across 22 robot types and 527 skills <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. RoboNet had pooled 15 million video frames from seven robots in 2019 <Cite id="robonet-2019" />.

The pool helped small datasets most. RT-1-X beat each small dataset's original method by 50% on average but fell behind RT-1 trained on a large dataset's own data, which the authors attribute to underfitting, too little model capacity for the mix. The larger RT-2-X roughly tripled success over RT-2 on skills seen only in another robot's data <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. Octo trained on 800,000 of its trajectories <Cite id="octo-2024" /> and OpenVLA on 970,000 <Cite id="openvla-2024" /> ([VLA models](/manipulation/vla-models)). Later <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> pools are larger: ARIO reports about 3 million episodes <Cite id="ario-2024" />, CrossFormer trained one policy on 900,000 trajectories from 20 embodiments, quadcopters among them <Cite id="crossformer-2024" />, and OXE-AugE adds nine robot types for over 4.4 million trajectories <Cite id="oxe-auge-2025" />.

Federation costs consistency: the sources differ in action spaces, control rates, cameras and licenses, and the top four robot types supply over 85% of OXE's real data <Cite id="oxe-auge-2025" />. Moritz Reuss's review of VLA papers at ICLR 2026 calls OXE mostly low-quality data and notes that <Term id="imitation-learning">imitation learning</Term> still lacks good ways to measure data quality <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />. Re-Mix's learned weighting of the sources beat an even mix by 38% on average <Cite id="remix-2024" />.

## DROID: one robot setup, many places

DROID trades robot variety for scene variety: fifty operators at 13 institutions spent 12 months collecting 76,000 trajectories in 564 scenes, all on one rig: a Franka Panda arm with two external cameras and a wrist camera, [teleoperated](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs) through a Meta Quest 2 headset <Cite id="droid-2024" />. One rig gives one action space and camera geometry, but ties the data to that robot. <Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion policies</Term> trained on DROID beat those trained on the next-best dataset by 22% in conditions like the training data and 17% outside them <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

Language labels for 75,000 episodes followed in December 2024, and better camera calibrations for 36,000 in April 2025 <Cite id="droid-project-page" />. DROID's CC BY 4.0 license <Cite id="droid-2024" /> allows commercial use and adaptation with attribution, though other rights may still limit a particular use <Cite id="cc-by-4-0-deed" />.

Single-platform collections can be bigger: RT-1's 13 robots gathered about 130,000 episodes over 17 months <Cite id="rt1-2022" />, Language-Table released nearly 600,000 language-labeled trajectories <Cite id="language-table-2022" />, and the Galaxea Open-World Dataset holds 500 hours across 50 scenes from one embodiment <Cite id="galaxea-open-world-2025" />.

## BridgeData V2: the academic workhorse

BridgeData V2 is built around a cheap arm, the WidowX 250 with six degrees of freedom. Its 60,096 trajectories, averaging 38 steps <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-project-page" />, are 50,365 demonstrations teleoperated with a VR controller at five commands a second and 9,731 scripted pick-and-place runs, in 24 environments <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />. With its 2021 predecessor, 7,200 demonstrations of 71 tasks in 10 environments, training jointly with 50 demonstrations of a new task in a new setting doubled success on average <Cite id="bridge-data-2021" />.

<Image id="bridgedata-v2-sink-tasks-2023" />

The project page states CC BY 4.0 for all data <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-project-page" />, most of it also ships inside OXE <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />, and the paper includes baselines such as goal-conditioned <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term>, ACT and RT-1 <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />.

## AgiBot World: one company's fleet

AgiBot World is a robot maker's fleet data. Its Beta release holds 1,001,552 trajectories totaling 2,976 hours, all from the AgiBot G1 robot. AgiBot reports 217 tasks in five deployment scenarios and a 30% average gain over OXE pretraining for policies pretrained on its data, a vendor figure with no independent replication <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. At about 11 seconds a trajectory, the million-trajectory headline covers less interaction than Ego4D's 3,670 hours of passive video <Cite id="ego4d-2022" />. The repository now lists 1,003,672 trajectories under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 <Cite id="agibot-world-repo" />, whose NonCommercial term bars commercial use <Cite id="cc-by-nc-sa-4-0-deed" />.

AgiBot World 2026 was recorded on the newer G2 in real commercial and home spaces, as long restocking episodes with a digital-twin simulation counterpart. By November 2026 its card listed 17.5 TB under the same license, still with no episode or hour totals <Cite id="agibot-world-2026" />.

## RoboMIND: multi-embodiment under one protocol

RoboMIND records several robots under one protocol: its paper reports 107,000 successful trajectories, 305.5 hours of real and simulated demonstrations, across 479 tasks on four robots: a Franka Emika Panda, a UR5e, an AgileX dual-arm robot and a humanoid with two dexterous hands. It also releases 5,000 real-world failures with annotated causes and a digital twin of its tasks in Isaac Sim <Cite id="robomind-2024" />. RoboMIND 2.0 (December 2025) reports over 310,000 dual-arm trajectories from six embodiments, including 12,000 with touch sensing <Cite id="robomind-2-2025" />. The [public dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/x-humanoid-robomind/RoboMIND) carries an Apache-2.0 tag but requires accepting access conditions before download, so check the terms before commercial use <Cite id="robomind-dataset-card" />.

## Releases since 2025

Community data now trains real models: SmolVLA trained on fewer than 30,000 episodes, all from community-contributed datasets <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />, and continual pretraining on the community-built AXIS, over 50,000 trajectories across 207 tasks, raised π0.5's overall success rate by 5.8% <Cite id="axis-2026" />. The largest open releases of 2026, though, record people.

## Data beyond trajectories

Some datasets record more senses: RH20T pairs vision, force, audio and actions in over 110,000 contact-rich sequences <Cite id="rh20t-2023" />, and Humanoid Everyday adds depth, LiDAR and touch to 10,300 humanoid trajectories across 260 tasks, and offers cloud evaluation <Cite id="humanoid-everyday-2025" />.

Others supply labels that trajectories lack. TransCG gives true depth and masks for transparent objects in clutter <Cite id="transcg-2022" />, and DREDS simulates a depth camera's noise to train depth repair <Cite id="dreds-2022" />. ContactDB records where hands touch objects with thermal imaging <Cite id="contactdb-2019" />, ContactPose pairs contact with hand and object pose <Cite id="contactpose-2020" />, DexYCB benchmarks grasps for human-to-robot handover <Cite id="dexycb-2021" />, HOI4D labels hands, objects and actions in egocentric depth video <Cite id="hoi4d-2022" />, and OakInk links what objects afford to recorded interactions <Cite id="oakink-2022" />. Object-model sets supply shapes: categorized CAD models in ShapeNet <Cite id="shapenet-2015" />, part hierarchies in PartNet <Cite id="partnet-2018" />, parameterized curves and surfaces in ABC <Cite id="abc-models-2018" />, and real objects with scans and manipulation protocols in YCB <Cite id="ycb-models-2015" />.

## Choosing and mixing datasets

A useful dataset is legal for the intended use, consistent in actions, units, frames and rates, complete for what it claims, and downloadable as versioned episodes. Datasheets for Datasets asks every dataset to document its motivation, composition, collection and recommended uses <Cite id="datasheets-for-datasets-2018" />. Scale strains formats too: LeRobot's moved to multi-episode files in 2025, after one file per episode hit file-system limits at millions of episodes <Cite id="lerobot-dataset-v3-2025" />.

Mixing datasets needs one schema for observations and actions, an adapter per source, and source labels kept through training, so real transfer can be told from one corpus dominating <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" /> <Cite id="octo-2024" /> <Cite id="remix-2024" />. On AgiBot World, task variety was worth more than demonstrations per task, and high-quality data from one robot made multi-robot pretraining optional <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />. The [robot-learning stack](/data-hardware/robot-learning-stack) covers the tooling, and the [robot-learning roadmap](/manipulation/robot-learning-roadmap) the wider program.
