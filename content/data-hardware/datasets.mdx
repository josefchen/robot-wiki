Five open datasets anchor robot learning in 2026, and each one is a different bet on how to collect at scale. <Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> federated existing data across 34 labs <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. DROID sent one standardized rig to 13 institutions and collected for a year <Cite id="droid-2024" />. BridgeData V2 ran a multi-site academic effort on a low-cost arm <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />. AgiBot World pointed a corporate fleet at the problem <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. RoboMIND put four different robots through one collection protocol <Cite id="robomind-2024" />. The bet matters as much as the count, because it decides what the data can teach and who is allowed to use it. ## Open X-Embodiment: breadth by federation

OXE pooled 60 existing datasets from 34 labs into one standardized RLDS format: over a million trajectories spanning 22 robot embodiments, 527 skills, and 160,266 tasks <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. It is the broadest <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> resource available, and the RT-X experiments showed what breadth buys, with a limit attached. On the small-scale dataset domains, RT-1-X trained on the pool beat the original method for each domain by 50% on average <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. On the large-scale domains it went the other way: RT-1-X underperformed the RT-1 baseline trained on that domain's own data, which the authors attribute to underfitting. The higher-capacity RT-2-X is where cross-embodiment transfer paid off, roughly tripling performance on out-of-distribution skills over the same model trained without the pool <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

The cost of federation is heterogeneity. The constituent datasets vary wildly in action spaces, control frequencies, and camera setups, and the licensing is mixed per constituent, which complicates commercial use. By 2026 a common critique holds that much of the pooled data is low quality, with no good method for quantifying data quality in <Term id="imitation-learning">imitation learning</Term> <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />. Most generalist policies train on it regardless, because nothing else is as broad (see [VLA models](/manipulation/vla-models)).

## DROID: depth on one platform

DROID is the largest open dataset collected on a single robot platform: 76,000 trajectories totaling 350 hours, across 564 scenes and 86 tasks, gathered by 50 operators at 13 institutions over 12 months <Cite id="droid-2024" />. Every trajectory came from the same rig, a Franka Panda with two ZED 2 cameras and a ZED Mini wrist camera, teleoperated through a Meta Quest 2. One platform means one action space and one camera geometry, which makes the data far easier to consume than a federation, at the price of embodiment lock-in.

The payoff showed up in controlled comparisons: <Term id="diffusion-policy">diffusion policies</Term> trained on DROID beat policies trained on the next-best dataset by 22% in-distribution and 17% out-of-distribution <Cite id="droid-2024" />. The project also maintains the data after release: improved camera calibrations for 36,000 episodes shipped in April 2025, and language annotations for 75,000 episodes in December 2024 <Cite id="droid-2024" />. The paper releases the full dataset under CC BY 4.0 <Cite id="droid-2024" />. Its deed allows sharing and adaptation, including commercial purposes, subject to attribution, a license link, change notices and the other license terms. The deed also warns that other rights may limit a particular use <Cite id="cc-by-4-0-deed" />.

## BridgeData V2: the academic workhorse

BridgeData V2 is the mid-scale counterpoint: 60,096 trajectories on a WidowX 250, a 6-DoF arm cheap enough for any lab to buy <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />. Of those, 50,365 are teleoperated demonstrations collected with a VR controller at 5 Hz; the remaining 9,731 are scripted pick-and-place rollouts. The official project page reports an average trajectory length of 38 timesteps and a control frequency of 5 Hz <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />. The data spans 24 environments, mostly toy kitchens, and 13 skills from pick-and-place to cloth folding <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />.

Two things keep BridgeData V2 on every shortlist. The official project page states that all data is provided under CC BY 4.0 <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />. And the paper shipped trained baselines, from goal-conditioned <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> to ACT and an RT-1 fine-tune, which made the dataset a default benchmark pool for academic generalists <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />. Most of it also ships inside OXE as one of the 60 constituents, so policies trained on the pool have already seen it <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

## AgiBot World: the corporate fleet

AgiBot World is what a robot manufacturer can collect when the collection fleet is the product line: the Beta release holds 1,001,552 trajectories totaling 2,976 hours, all from the AgiBot G1 platform with a multi-camera rig <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. The trajectory count on the release repository has since grown to 1,003,672. The paper reports 217 tasks in five deployment scenarios, and claims that policies pretrained on AgiBot World improve 30% on average over policies pretrained on OXE, in-domain and out-of-distribution <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. That figure is vendor-reported; no independent replication exists.

The catch is the license. Everything ships under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so commercial use is out. The hour count, unlike OXE's, is published, and it is the number worth carrying: 2,976 hours across a million trajectories is about 11 seconds each, so the million-trajectory headline covers less interaction than Ego4D's passive video. AgiBot World 2026, the newest release, is 13.6 TB collected entirely in real-world scenes, but publishes no episode, hour, or task counts at all as of September 2026 <Cite id="agibot-world-2026" />.

## RoboMIND: multi-embodiment under one protocol

RoboMIND splits the difference between OXE's federation and DROID's monoculture: 107,000 trajectories across 479 tasks and 96 object classes, collected on four different robots, a Franka Emika Panda, a UR5e, an AgileX dual-arm, and a humanoid with dual dexterous hands, but all under one collection platform and one standardized protocol <Cite id="robomind-2024" />. Cross-embodiment data without the cross-lab inconsistency.

Two details set it apart. It includes 5,000 real-world failure demonstrations with annotated causes, and failure data is nearly absent from every other dataset in this table, which are collections of successes. And it ships a digital twin of the tasks and assets in Isaac Sim, so additional data can be collected and policies evaluated in simulation against the same distribution <Cite id="robomind-2024" />. The license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 on the 2026-08-09 verification record; no currently reachable primary page prints it (the paper, project site and gated Hugging Face card do not as of September 2026), so the term awaits a license-bearing source.

## How to read dataset claims

Convert to hours wherever you can. DROID's 350 hours across 76,000 trajectories works out to about 17 seconds each; AgiBot World Beta's 2,976 hours across a million trajectories is about 11 seconds each. Episode counts flatter datasets of short trajectories, and a million-episode claim can hide less total interaction than a hundred-thousand-episode dataset of long-horizon tasks. The arithmetic behind this is in [the data bottleneck](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck).

Check the license before the leaderboard. DROID ships CC BY 4.0; its deed describes commercial sharing and adaptation subject to its terms, not a guarantee of every permission needed for a use <Cite id="cc-by-4-0-deed" />; and AgiBot World's non-commercial terms are printed by its own release repository; BridgeData V2's official project page states CC BY 4.0 <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />; RoboMIND's previously recorded non-commercial terms remain unverified, and OXE inherits whatever each of its 60 constituents chose. A policy trained on NC data is an NC policy in practice, whatever the license lawyers eventually decide.

Finally, weigh breadth against consistency. OXE's federation maximizes diversity and inherits every constituent's quirks; DROID's monoculture gives up embodiment coverage for a clean action space. The scaling-laws work says environment and task diversity dominates raw volume, and that multi-embodiment pretraining may be optional when task diversity is high <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />. If that result holds, the DROID and RoboMIND bets age better than the OXE bet. The field has not settled it.
