The data bottleneck is the shortage of robot training data compared with the text language models learn from. The largest [public robot datasets](/data-hardware/datasets) hold hundreds to a few thousand hours <Cite id="droid-2024" /> <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />, while Llama 4 Scout trained on about 40 trillion tokens <Cite id="llama-4-model-card-2025" /> and companies report private corpora of 100,000 hours or more from handheld and wearable devices <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" /> <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

Fifty data collectors used 18 robots across 13 institutions over 12 months, and the release adds roughly 16,000 unsuccessful trajectories outside that count <Cite id="droid-2024" />. ## Two data universes, nine orders of magnitude apart

GPT-3 trained on 300 billion tokens in 2020 <Cite id="gpt3-2020" /> and Llama 3 on over 15 trillion in 2024 <Cite id="llama-3-2024" />, a scale the open FineWeb corpus rebuilds from 96 Common Crawl snapshots <Cite id="fineweb-2024" />. Qwen3 trained on 36 trillion tokens <Cite id="qwen3-2025" />. Ken Goldberg of UC Berkeley calls the difference a 100,000-year data gap <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />.

<Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> pools over a million trajectories across 22 robot embodiments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. The chart assigns it no hour estimate. Robot data has no crawl: a paid <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> operator usually drives each hour. AgiBot World publishes 2,976 hours for 1,001,552 trajectories, about 11 seconds each <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.

Toyota Research Institute's Large Behavior Models trained on about 1,700 hours of its own bimanual data, simulation, UMI and Open X-Embodiment <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. π0 pretrained on 10,000 hours of dexterous manipulation from 7 robot configurations, plus Open X-Embodiment, DROID and Bridge <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

## What the scaling laws say: diversity beats density

<Term id="scaling-law">Scaling laws</Term> come from language modelling: loss falls as a power law in model size, dataset size and compute <Cite id="kaplan-scaling-laws-2020" />. A meta-analysis of 327 robotics papers finds power laws there too <Cite id="sartor-robotics-scaling-2024" />.

Lin et al. collected over 40,000 demonstrations and ran over 15,000 real-world rollouts on <Term id="imitation-learning">imitation learning</Term> <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />. Success with new environments and objects follows a rough power law in the number of training environments and objects; demonstrations per environment saturate past about 50. Their recipe, 32 environments with 50 demonstrations each, reached about 90% success with unseen objects; four operators collected it in one afternoon.

<Image id="umi-test-environments-2024" />

On the AgiBot World stack, Shi et al. split diversity into task, embodiment and expert <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />. Task diversity mattered more than demonstrations per task. Models pretrained on high-quality single-embodiment data transferred to other robots and scaled better in fine-tuning than [multi-embodiment](/manipulation/cross-embodiment) models.

Expert diversity hurt: different operator styles make velocities multimodal. Debiasing them gave GO-1-Pro a 15% gain, which the authors equate to 2.5 times more pretraining data <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

## Around the bottleneck: data that does not need a robot

UMI collects without a robot: an operator holds a 3D-printed gripper with a GoPro, and its policies deploy zero-shot on real arms <Cite id="umi-2024" />. Apple's EgoDex used Vision Pro hand tracking to record 829 hours of dexterous manipulation with per-joint 3D poses across 194 tasks <Cite id="egodex-2025" />. Ego4D holds 3,670 hours of egocentric daily-life video from 931 wearers in 74 locations, without action labels <Cite id="ego4d-2022" />. Build AI's Egocentric-100K holds 100,405 hours of head-mounted video of manual labour <Cite id="egocentric-100k-2025" />.

EgoScale trained a [vision-language-action model](/manipulation/foundation-models) on 20,854 hours of action-labelled egocentric human video, over twenty times prior efforts. It found a log-linear scaling law between human data scale and validation loss, which correlated with real-robot performance <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. Human pretraining followed by lightweight aligned human-robot mid-training raised average <Term id="success-rate">success rate</Term> by 54% over a no-pretraining baseline on a 22-DoF dexterous hand. EgoMimic found an extra hour of hand data worth more than an extra hour of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />.

FACT, from Zhi Cao, Jiajun Wu and colleagues at Stanford, the University of Michigan and Microsoft Research (October 2026), was pretrained mostly on about 2,000 hours of human demonstrations recorded with tactile gloves. Fine-tuned on each task, it averaged 41.4% success on seven tasks with two dexterous hands, against 8.6% and 1.4% for T-Rex and π0.5 fine-tuned on the same data. Pretrained without the gloves' force readings, it opened a test-tube cap in none of ten trials instead of eight <Cite id="fact-2026" />.

HiPHI (CoRL 2026) is a motion-capture dataset from Jiahao Ji, Lei Han and colleagues at Noitom Robotics and several universities: 617.5 hours from 132 performers, 245.7 of them with 40 tracked real objects. Retargeted to a Unitree G1, its motions gave the highest simulated tracking success at a matched 3-hour budget against AMASS, LAFAN1, Motion-X++ and BONES-SEED. They led again at 20 hours, a budget LAFAN1 is too short to fill, and policies trained on them ran, crawled and carried a box on a real G1 <Cite id="hiphi-2026" />.

## Company corpora reach half a million hours

Generalist AI reports over 270,000 hours of manipulation data for GEN-0, growing by 10,000 hours a week; post-training results improve predictably with more pretraining data and compute <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />. For GEN-1 it reports over half a million hours from low-cost wearable devices on humans, with no robot data in the base model <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Xiaomi-Robotics-1 pretrained on over 100,000 hours of trajectories from UMI devices and improved with data and model scale <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />.

Sunday trains ACT-1 on glove recordings, converted to robot data with a reported 90% success rate <Cite id="sunday-act1-2025" />. Figure reports zero-shot navigation after training only on egocentric human video <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.

## Generated and simulated data

GRAIL, from Tianyi Xie, Yuke Zhu and colleagues at NVIDIA and UCLA (June 2026), built over 20,000 humanoid interaction sequences from 3D assets and video models. Policies trained only on them reached 84% pick-up success on a Unitree G1 (five objects, ten trials each) and 90% on stairs <Cite id="grail-2026" />. HumanoidMimicGen, from Kevin Lin, Ajay Mandlekar and colleagues at NVIDIA and UT Austin (May 2026), adapts a few demonstrations to new object poses. Co-training on its data raised average success on four real tasks from 0.51 to 0.71 <Cite id="humanoidmimicgen-2026" />.

PRISM, from Zihan Wang, Pieter Abbeel, Guanya Shi, Angjoo Kanazawa and colleagues at Amazon FAR, UC Berkeley and Carnegie Mellon (September 2026), turned a few real videos into hundreds of generated variants. Its policy ran on a real humanoid with no real-world fine-tuning <Cite id="prism-humanoid-2026" />. DexAgent, from Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu and colleagues at Stanford and Columbia (September 2026), turns one egocentric human video into simulated trajectories. Across eleven real tasks, its policies reached 3.5 times the success rate of baselines <Cite id="dexagent-2026" />.

MobileVISTA, from Suzannah Wistreich, Jiajun Wu and colleagues at Stanford and the Toyota Research Institute (October 2026), synthesizes images and actions for shifted base poses from demonstrations recorded at one pose. With 50 demonstrations per task and base offsets within 15 cm, its policy stacked a book in 13 of 25 trials, against at most 2 for Canonical-2D, DP3 and a fine-tuned π0.5. At the original pose it stacked 18 of 25, below their 24 and 25 <Cite id="mobilevista-2026" />.

Dexterity from Supervised Play, from Botao He, Jitendra Malik and colleagues at Amazon FAR and the University of Maryland (October 2026), starts from 15 minutes of human play on a 22-joint hand. A policy cloned from that play collects data for a world model, then proposes actions for model-predictive control. Asked to rotate a cube to a goal orientation in simulation, the system came within 0.6 radians in 91.4% of 128 trials, where PPO needed about 490 times the data to reach 86.7%, and on the real hand it reached 90.0% <Cite id="dsp-2026" />.

Simulation compresses months of interaction into hours but brings the <Term id="sim-to-real-gap">sim-to-real gap</Term> covered in [massively parallel sim RL](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl). GR00T N1 generated simulated trajectories equal to 6,500 hours of human demonstration in 11 hours <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, and co-training with simulation raised real-world performance by 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />. A model pretrained only on the synthetic InternData-A1 matched the official π0 on 49 simulated, 5 real-world and 4 long-horizon dexterous tasks <Cite id="interndata-a1-2025" />.

Sergey Levine of Physical Intelligence argued in July 2025 that real data is indispensable if robot models are to generalize in the physical world as broadly as language models do in the virtual one <Cite id="levine-sporks-2025" />. Closed-loop learning from any of these sources still meets the <Term id="covariate-shift">covariate shift</Term> covered in [behavior cloning foundations](/manipulation/bc-foundations).

## How to read the field's data claims

Convert everything to hours: AgiBot World's million trajectories are 2,976 hours, less than Ego4D's 3,670 hours of passive video <Cite id="ego4d-2022" />. Ask for the breakdown by environment, object, task and operator style; those axes outweigh the hour count <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />. Treat company-reported scale as a claim until the data or an audit is public: AgiBot World's 30% improvement over OXE pretraining has no independent replication <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.
