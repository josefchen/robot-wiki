The data bottleneck is the shortage of robot training data, and it is why recent robot models are pretrained primarily on recordings of people <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Llama 4 Scout was trained on about 40 trillion tokens <Cite id="llama-4-model-card-2025" />, whereas the largest [public robot datasets](/data-hardware/datasets) hold a few thousand hours <Cite id="droid-2024" /> <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.

## Robot data has no web to crawl

<span id="two-data-universes-nine-orders-of-magnitude-apart" />

Language-model text already exists: Qwen3 was trained on 36 trillion tokens <Cite id="qwen3-2025" />, and the open FineWeb corpus holds 15 trillion tokens built from 96 snapshots of the web <Cite id="fineweb-2024" />. Robot data must be made by a [paid](/data-hardware/data-market/) <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> operator driving the robot. This difference has been dubbed the 100,000-year data gap by Ken Goldberg of UC Berkeley <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />.

The DROID dataset took fifty data collectors, 18 robots, 13 institutions and 12 months to collect 350 hours <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

## Scaling laws favor variety over repetition

A <Term id="scaling-law">scaling law</Term> describes the predictable relationship between a model's performance and the amount of data it is trained on, its size, or the compute. Language models follow power laws in each <Cite id="kaplan-scaling-laws-2020" />, and a meta-analysis of 327 robotics papers found them in robotics too <Cite id="sartor-robotics-scaling-2024" />.

In <Term id="imitation-learning">imitation learning</Term>, where robots learn by mimicking demonstrations, adding more environments improves success on new objects, while demonstrations per environment stop helping past about 50. In Lin and colleagues' study, 32 environments with 50 demonstrations each, collected by four operators in one afternoon, led to about 90% success on unseen objects <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />.

<Image id="umi-test-environments-2024" />

On the AgiBot World dataset, increasing the variety of tasks led to better performance than increasing the number of demonstrations per task, whereas increasing the variety of operators degraded performance. Accounting for operator-specific styles improved the performance of GO-1-Pro by 15%, which the authors equate to 2.5 times more pretraining data <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

## Recording people is the main way around

In UMI, an operator holds a 3D-printed gripper fitted with a GoPro camera to collect data, and policies trained on this data transferred to real robot arms without any further training <Cite id="umi-2024" />. Japan's AIRoA-0 was trained on 16,151 hours of data collected with handheld YUBI grippers and averaged 78% success on ten tasks, compared to 27% for the best of four rivals post-trained on the same data <Cite id="airoa-0-2026" />.

| Human corpus | Hours | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apple EgoDex | 829 | Vision Pro hand tracking across 194 tasks <Cite id="egodex-2025" /> |
| Ego4D | 3,670 | Video with no action labels <Cite id="ego4d-2022" /> |
| EgoScale | 20,854 | Egocentric video labeled with actions <Cite id="egoscale-2026" /> |
| Open-HumanWAM (November 2026) | 50,000 | Released corpus; simulated robot success rose log-linearly with hours <Cite id="open-humanwam-2026" /> |
| Build AI Egocentric-100K | 100,405 | Head-mounted video of manual labor <Cite id="egocentric-100k-2025" /> |

LiberAI's Liber-0 was pretrained on more than 2.2 million hours of videos and motion recordings of humans. It was evaluated on 60 tasks without training on robot demonstrations collected specifically for those tasks. The company reports a 36% success rate over 600 trials, and 10.5% on the 20 multi-step tasks that required more than picking and placing <Cite id="liber-0-2026" />.

Scaling laws hold for human data as well: EgoScale pretrains a [vision-language-action model](/manipulation/foundation-models) on human data and observes log-linear scaling of validation loss with the number of hours of human data, which also tracks downstream performance on real robots. With a short stage of aligned human and robot data, that pretraining leads to an average improvement in <Term id="success-rate">success rate</Term> of 54% over no human pretraining on a 22-degree-of-freedom hand <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. EgoMimic found that an hour of human hand data is worth more than an hour of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />.

Touch and whole-body motion can be recorded too. FACT pretrained primarily on about 2,000 hours of tactile-glove demonstrations and reports an average success rate of 41.4% across seven two-handed tasks, compared to 1.4% for π0.5 fine-tuned on the same data <Cite id="fact-2026" />. HiPHI's 617.5 hours of motion capture beat four public motion sets in simulated tracking, and its policies ran, crawled and carried a box on a real Unitree G1 <Cite id="hiphi-2026" />.

Also: weakly labeled video, EPIC-KITCHENS <Cite id="epic-kitchens-2018" />, HowTo100M <Cite id="howto100m-2019" />, COIN <Cite id="coin-video-2019" />, Something-Something <Cite id="something-video-2017" />, Ego-Exo4D <Cite id="ego-exo4d-2023" />; motion sets, AMASS <Cite id="amass-2019" />, LAFAN1 <Cite id="lafan1-2021" />, Motion-X++ <Cite id="motion-x-plus-plus-2025" />.

## Simulation and generation multiply real data

Simulation compresses months to hours, but it brings the <Term id="sim-to-real-gap">sim-to-real gap</Term>, where skills learned in simulation are lost on a real robot ([parallel sim RL](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl)). GR00T N1 collected the equivalent of 6,500 hours of human demonstration data in simulation in 11 hours <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, and co-training with simulation data improved performance on real robots by 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />. A policy pretrained exclusively on synthetic data from InternData-A1 matched the official π0 checkpoint on 49 simulated and 5 real-robot tasks <Cite id="interndata-a1-2025" />.

Generative models can also amplify small amounts of real data, as GenAug did for tabletop manipulation scenes <Cite id="genaug-2023" />. The 2026 methods:

| Method | Starts from | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GRAIL (NVIDIA, UCLA) | 3D assets and video models | Trained only on its sequences: 84% pick-up and 90% stairs on a Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" /> |
| HumanoidMimicGen (NVIDIA, UT Austin) | A few demonstrations, moved to new object poses | Four real tasks: 0.51 to 0.71 success <Cite id="humanoidmimicgen-2026" /> |
| PRISM (Amazon FAR, Berkeley, CMU) | A few real videos, expanded into hundreds | Real humanoid, no real-world fine-tuning <Cite id="prism-humanoid-2026" /> |
| DexAgent (Stanford, Columbia) | One human video | 3.5 times the baselines' success on eleven real tasks <Cite id="dexagent-2026" /> |
| MobileVISTA (Stanford, TRI) | Demonstrations at one base pose | Base moved up to 15 cm: 13 of 25 against at most 2; at the original pose 18 of 25 against 24 and 25 <Cite id="mobilevista-2026" /> |
| Dexterity from Supervised Play (Amazon FAR, Maryland) | 15 minutes of human play on a 22-joint hand | Cube turning: 91.4% in simulation, where PPO needed 490 times the data for 86.7%; 90.0% on the real hand <Cite id="dsp-2026" /> |

## Real data stays necessary

Physical Intelligence's Sergey Levine argues that real data is indispensable if robots are to generalize as broadly as language models <Cite id="levine-sporks-2025" />. A policy trained by copying suffers from <Term id="covariate-shift">covariate shift</Term> once deployed on its own: small mistakes lead it to states not seen in its training data ([behavior cloning foundations](/manipulation/bc-foundations)). Goldberg instead envisions "agentic robotics", where AI agents write and test robot programs in simulation <Cite id="goldberg-agentic-robotics-2026" />.

Three 2026 papers trained real-robot policies for narrow tasks without real-robot demonstrations. Simulation won against as many as 100 real demonstrations per task. In the kitchen scenario of SimVLA, 200 real demonstrations won:

| No real-robot demonstrations | Real test | Real-robot demonstrations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero-HLM: 66.7% from 800 simulated a task; 69% in shifted setups it also simulated | GR00T N1.7 fine-tuned for a Unitree G1, three tasks in a room reconstructed from phone photos | 46.7% from 100 a task; 23% shifted <Cite id="zero-hlm-2026" /> |
| SimVLA: 38 of 100 in the baselines' kitchen; 35 of 80 in an unseen pantry and home | Five tasks, wheeled two-arm robot | π0.5 on 50 a task: 33 and 0; on 200: 58 and 3 <Cite id="simvla-2026" /> |
| Apple HALO: 91% of 360 trials | Six whole-body skills, Unitree G1, six unscanned homes | No baseline <Cite id="apple-halo-2026" /> |

## Count data claims in hours

Dataset size claims should be made in terms of hours of data, not grasps or frames. GraspVLA reports a billion rendered frames <Cite id="graspvla-2025" />; GraspGen, 53 million simulated grasps <Cite id="graspgen-2025" />. What matters more than the hour count is how the data breaks down across environments, objects, tasks and operators <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

The reported 30% performance gain of AgiBot World over Open X-Embodiment pretraining has yet to be replicated independently <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. Dataset sizes reported by companies should be treated as claims until an audit or the data itself becomes public.
