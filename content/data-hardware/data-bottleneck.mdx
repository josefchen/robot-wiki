The data bottleneck is the shortage of robot training data, and it is why recent robot models pretrain mostly on recordings of people <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Llama 4 Scout trained on about 40 trillion tokens <Cite id="llama-4-model-card-2025" />; the largest [public robot datasets](/data-hardware/datasets) hold a few thousand hours <Cite id="droid-2024" /> <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.

## Robot data has no web to crawl

<span id="two-data-universes-nine-orders-of-magnitude-apart" />

Language-model text already exists: Qwen3 trained on 36 trillion tokens <Cite id="qwen3-2025" />, a scale the open FineWeb corpus rebuilds from 96 web snapshots <Cite id="fineweb-2024" />. Robot data must be made by a paid <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> operator driving the robot. Ken Goldberg of UC Berkeley calls the difference a 100,000-year data gap <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />.

DROID took fifty collectors, 18 robots, 13 institutions and 12 months to record 350 hours <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

## Scaling laws favor variety over repetition

A <Term id="scaling-law">scaling law</Term> is a predictable curve linking performance to data, model size or compute. Language models follow power laws in each <Cite id="kaplan-scaling-laws-2020" />, and a meta-analysis of 327 robotics papers found them in robotics too <Cite id="sartor-robotics-scaling-2024" />.

In <Term id="imitation-learning">imitation learning</Term>, where a robot copies demonstrations, more environments raise success on new objects, and demonstrations per environment stop helping past about 50. Lin and colleagues' 32 environments with 50 demonstrations each reached about 90% on unseen objects, collected by four operators in one afternoon <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />.

<Image id="umi-test-environments-2024" />

On AgiBot World data, task variety beat demonstrations per task, and operator variety hurt. Correcting for operators' styles gave GO-1-Pro a 15% gain, which the authors equate to 2.5 times more pretraining data <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

## Recording people is the main way around

With UMI an operator holds a 3D-printed gripper fitted with a GoPro, and the trained policies run on real arms with no further training <Cite id="umi-2024" />. Japan's AIRoA-0, trained on 16,151 hours from handheld YUBI grippers, averaged 78% success on ten tasks against 27% for the best of four rivals post-trained on the same data <Cite id="airoa-0-2026" />.

| Human corpus | Hours | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apple EgoDex | 829 | Vision Pro hand tracking across 194 tasks <Cite id="egodex-2025" /> |
| Ego4D | 3,670 | Video with no action labels <Cite id="ego4d-2022" /> |
| EgoScale | 20,854 | Egocentric video labeled with actions <Cite id="egoscale-2026" /> |
| Open-HumanWAM (November 2026) | 50,000 | Released corpus; simulated robot success rose log-linearly with hours <Cite id="open-humanwam-2026" /> |
| Build AI Egocentric-100K | 100,405 | Head-mounted video of manual labor <Cite id="egocentric-100k-2025" /> |

Human data follows scaling laws too: the validation loss of EgoScale's [vision-language-action model](/manipulation/foundation-models) fell log-linearly with human hours, tracking real-robot performance. With a short aligned human-robot stage, that pretraining raised average <Term id="success-rate">success rate</Term> 54% over none on a 22-degree-of-freedom hand <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. EgoMimic found an hour of hand data worth more than an hour of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />.

Touch and whole-body motion can be recorded too. FACT pretrained mostly on about 2,000 hours of tactile-glove demonstrations and averaged 41.4% on seven two-handed tasks, against 1.4% for π0.5 fine-tuned on the same data <Cite id="fact-2026" />. HiPHI's 617.5 hours of motion capture beat four public motion sets in simulated tracking, and its policies ran, crawled and carried a box on a real Unitree G1 <Cite id="hiphi-2026" />.

Also: weakly labeled video, EPIC-KITCHENS <Cite id="epic-kitchens-2018" />, HowTo100M <Cite id="howto100m-2019" />, COIN <Cite id="coin-video-2019" />, Something-Something <Cite id="something-video-2017" />, Ego-Exo4D <Cite id="ego-exo4d-2023" />; motion sets, AMASS <Cite id="amass-2019" />, LAFAN1 <Cite id="lafan1-2021" />, Motion-X++ <Cite id="motion-x-plus-plus-2025" />.

## Simulation and generation multiply real data

Simulation compresses months into hours but brings the <Term id="sim-to-real-gap">sim-to-real gap</Term>, the loss of skill on a real robot ([parallel sim RL](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl)). GR00T N1 simulated 6,500 hours' worth of human demonstration in 11 hours <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, and co-training with simulation raised real-world performance 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />. A model pretrained only on synthetic InternData-A1 matched the official π0 on 49 simulated and 5 real tasks <Cite id="interndata-a1-2025" />.

Generative models stretch a few real examples, as GenAug did for tabletop scenes <Cite id="genaug-2023" />. The 2026 methods:

| Method | Starts from | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GRAIL (NVIDIA, UCLA) | 3D assets and video models | Trained only on its sequences: 84% pick-up and 90% stairs on a Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" /> |
| HumanoidMimicGen (NVIDIA, UT Austin) | A few demonstrations, moved to new object poses | Four real tasks: 0.51 to 0.71 success <Cite id="humanoidmimicgen-2026" /> |
| PRISM (Amazon FAR, Berkeley, CMU) | A few real videos, expanded into hundreds | Real humanoid, no real-world fine-tuning <Cite id="prism-humanoid-2026" /> |
| DexAgent (Stanford, Columbia) | One human video | 3.5 times the baselines' success on eleven real tasks <Cite id="dexagent-2026" /> |
| MobileVISTA (Stanford, TRI) | Demonstrations at one base pose | Base moved up to 15 cm: 13 of 25 against at most 2; at the original pose 18 of 25 against 24 and 25 <Cite id="mobilevista-2026" /> |
| Dexterity from Supervised Play (Amazon FAR, Maryland) | 15 minutes of human play on a 22-joint hand | Cube turning: 91.4% in simulation, where PPO needed 490 times the data for 86.7%; 90.0% on the real hand <Cite id="dsp-2026" /> |

## Real data stays necessary

Sergey Levine of Physical Intelligence argues that real data is indispensable for robots to generalize as broadly as language models <Cite id="levine-sporks-2025" />. A copied policy meets <Term id="covariate-shift">covariate shift</Term> once it acts alone: small errors carry it into states its data never showed ([behavior cloning foundations](/manipulation/bc-foundations)). Goldberg instead proposes "agentic robotics": AI agents that write and test robot programs in simulation <Cite id="goldberg-agentic-robotics-2026" />.

## Count data claims in hours

Convert every count to hours. GraspGen's 53 million simulated grasps <Cite id="graspgen-2025" /> and GraspVLA's billion rendered frames <Cite id="graspvla-2025" /> are not hours at all. The breakdown by environment, object, task and operator outweighs the hour count <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

Company scale is a claim until the data or an audit is public. AgiBot World's 30% gain over Open X-Embodiment pretraining has no independent replication <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.
