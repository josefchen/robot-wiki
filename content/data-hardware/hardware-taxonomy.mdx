The machines you can point a policy at in 2026 split into more than arms and humanoids. The stack a buyer actually assembles also includes wheelbases, lidars, RGB-D cameras, IMUs, mobile manipulators, and quadrupeds, plus the hands, tactile sensors, and edge compute already in this guide. The price spread is the story. A self-build SO-101 arm costs about \$122 in parts <Cite id="so-arm100-repo-2026" />. A Unitree Go2 quadruped starts at \$1,600 <Cite id="unitree-go2-2026" />. Hello Robot's Stretch 4 ships for \$29,950 <Cite id="hello-robot-stretch-4-2026" />. Boston Dynamics will not sell you an Atlas at any price, and Tesla has no Optimus 3 price to quote because there is no product to buy <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />. Between those poles sits an actual market with dated prices, disclosed <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term>, and sourcing states you can plan around. This module collects that market into one filterable guide. Every number that nobody publishes stays visibly empty rather than estimated. Every row carries a vendor, press, or Wikimedia photo when a public still exists; licence is not a reason a photo is missing.

## Arms, from \$122 to \$70,000

The low end belongs to the LeRobot ecosystem. The SO-101 is a 3D-printed arm with six degrees of freedom, five joints plus a gripper, built from STS3215 servos; the community BOM for one follower arm runs about \$100 in core parts and \$122 including the US accessory list <Cite id="so-arm100-repo-2026" />. Seeed Studio sells the SO-ARM101 Pro line around it: \$295 unassembled, \$299 assembled, both with 12-bit magnetic encoders and a 500 g payload <Cite id="seeed-so-arm101-pro-2026" />. The older Koch v1.1 design still circulates at \$250-\$300 on Dynamixel servos <Cite id="lerobot-pricing-2026" />. The LeRobot software stack runs ACT, π0, and SmolVLA policies on all of them, which is what makes a \$299 kit a research instrument instead of a toy <Cite id="lerobot-docs-2026" />.

A tier up, Trossen Robotics rebranded its ALOHA line as Trossen AI in 2025-2026. The WidowX AI single-arm <Term id="teleoperation">teleop</Term> rig lists at \$4,545.95 with a 1.5 kg payload and 700 mm reach across six degrees of freedom, and the Mobile AI bimanual-on-wheels rig at \$33,695.95, with the Solo AI and Stationary AI at \$11,385.95 and \$23,995.95 between them <Cite id="trossen-ai-2026" />. All of them run 500 Hz CAN FD control with the iNerve board and integrate LeRobot and OpenPI <Cite id="trossen-ai-2026" />. The ALOHA 2 itself, the bimanual rig behind ACT <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> and Mobile ALOHA <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />, runs \$17,000-\$32,000 depending on configuration <Cite id="lerobot-pricing-2026" />. At the top of the category, Pollen Robotics' Reachy 2 humanoid torso goes for about \$70,000 <Cite id="lerobot-pricing-2026" />.

The industrial and research cobots that fill OXE sit in the same table with no public list prices. Universal Robots' e-Series (the UR5e family) and Kinova's Gen3 are quote-only through distributors <Cite id="ur-e-series-2026" /> <Cite id="kinova-gen3-2026" />. UFACTORY's xArm line is orderable on the company site; the catalog page does not publish a USD figure this guide can date <Cite id="ufactory-xarm-2026" />. Two more open teaching arms sit beside them: Elephant Robotics' myCobot 280 M5, a 6-DoF desktop cobot whose product page shows a \$599 new-customer promo rather than a list price this guide will date <Cite id="elephant-mycobot-280-2026" />, and ROBOTIS's OpenMANIPULATOR-X, the DYNAMIXEL-X plus printed-parts kit whose e-Manual likewise carries no USD figure <Cite id="robotis-openmanipulator-x-2026" />.

One arm in the guide carries no price at all: the Franka Emika Panda, the seven-DoF platform behind DROID's 76,000 trajectories <Cite id="droid-2024" />. It remains the research workhorse, and sourcing it is a conversation rather than a checkout.

## Humanoids: shipping beat shouting

Omdia counts about 13,000 humanoids shipped in 2025 <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />. The dispute over who led says everything about the market's maturity: Omdia puts AgiBot first at 5,168 units and a 39% share, while Unitree self-reports more than 5,500 and disputes the ranking <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />. Read both numbers as an order of magnitude.

Unitree owns the priced ladder. The R1 education robot runs \$4,900-\$5,900 across configurations. The G1 lists at \$13,500 for the base model with 23 DoF; the EDU configuration spans 23-43 DoF and is available by quote <Cite id="unitree-g1-2026" />. It is also one of the two validation platforms for NVIDIA's GR00T line, alongside AgiBot's Genie-1 <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />. The H2 ships at \$29,900 with 31 DoF (six per leg, seven per arm, three waist, two head), 360 N·m leg joints, and a 2,070 TOPS onboard chip <Cite id="unitree-h2-2026" />. 1X prices the NEO at \$20,000 outright or \$499 a month against a \$200 refundable deposit, with US deliveries promised by the end of 2026; the order page lists 22 DoF per hand, and a 25-DoF revision was announced on July 9 <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />. Noetix's Bumi opened presale at ¥9,998, about \$1,400 at July 2026 conversion, with delivery still unconfirmed; EngineAI's SE01 launched at ¥88K (about \$12,000), Fourier's GR-3 prices above ¥200K (about \$27,500), and LimX's Oli takes orders from \$21,800 <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />.

The flagship tier publishes specs but not prices. Boston Dynamics' electric Atlas carries 56 DoF with continuous rotation at key joints, a 50 kg instant payload, IP67, and a 4-hour swappable battery, and every 2026 build is already committed to Hyundai and Google DeepMind <Cite id="bd-atlas-2026" />. Figure 03 is in fleet production with an embedded palm camera per hand and 2 kW wireless charging through its feet, and it is not for individual sale <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />. Agility sells Digit as a service with a 16 kg payload and deployments at GXO; UBTECH's Walker S2 hot-swaps its own batteries in about three minutes; AgiBot's A2 lists 40+ DoF; and Tesla's Optimus 3 has neither a price nor a current spec sheet <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />.

The shakeout around them is real. K-Scale Labs shut down in November 2025, Cartwheel Robotics in February 2026, Sanctuary AI pivoted to software in June, and Amazon acquired Fauna Robotics in March <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />. The companies still standing are the ones with shipping robots in this table.

## Hands ship bundled, not shelved

Dexterous hands almost never sell as standalone products; they ride along with the robot. The open exception is the LEAP Hand from CMU: 16 DoF, buildable in about four hours from catalog parts at a cost of \$2,000, roughly one eighth the price of the Allegro Hand it was benchmarked against <Cite id="leap-hand-2023" />. Wonik still sells that Allegro line (V4 through V5 Sense) as a quote-only research hand <Cite id="wonik-allegro-2026" />. Shadow's Dexterous Hand and Inspire-Robots' micro-servo hands are the other two catalog options, both quote-only.

Everywhere else, hand specs surface inside robot listings. The NEO's hands carry 22 DoF each, with the 25-DoF revision on the way <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />. Unitree's Dex3-1, an option on the G1 EDU, has seven active DoF per hand (three thumb, two index, two middle) with an optional tactile array <Cite id="unitree-g1-2026" />. The H2's dexterous hands are EDU-only options in several models <Cite id="unitree-h2-2026" />. Figure 03 integrates a palm camera into each hand but discloses no hand DoF <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />. If you want a hand to mount on your own arm in 2026, LEAP is the only one with a public build cost; Allegro, Shadow, and Inspire are the quote-only catalog.

## Sensors: touch is the missing channel

The datasets that generalist policies train on ship vision and <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term>; DROID's rig is a Franka with three cameras and no touch sensing at all <Cite id="droid-2024" />. AgiBot World is the counterexample worth naming: its humanoids carry visuo-tactile sensors alongside the camera rig, which makes it one of the few million-trajectory corpora with a touch channel <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. The hardware is not the reason the channel stayed rare. A 2025 outlook in IEEE Transactions on Robotics puts the blame on structure: divergent transduction methods with no standardized evaluation framework, durability failures in real conditions, and temperature sensitivity and hysteresis in the sensing materials themselves <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />. Sensors also add cost and failure modes to collection rigs, and few policy architectures take tactile input natively <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />.

Four sensors anchor the accessible tier, and two of them are priced in three digits. GelSight Mini brings optical tactile sensing with high-resolution contact geometry to a commercial product and retails at \$500 <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />. DIGIT, Meta FAIR's compact optical fingertip, is an open design with public files and a broad replication record, and lists at \$355 on the GelSight store <Cite id="digit-sensor-2020" /> <Cite id="gelsight-digit-2026" />. Its successor Digit 360 wraps the fingertip in 360° of optical coverage, resolves forces down to 1 mN across more than eight million taxels, and ships through GelSight under a manufacturing partnership announced in October 2024 <Cite id="meta-fair-touch-2024" />. AnySkin takes the magnetic route: a soft, inexpensive skin whose learned policies transfer across replacement sensor instances without recalibration, the first uncalibrated tactile sensor to report that <Cite id="anyskin-2024" />.

## Compute: closing the loop

NVIDIA built Jetson Thor specifically for this market. The T5000 module delivers 2,070 sparse FP4 TFLOPS with 128 GB of LPDDR5X at 273 GB/s on a 14-core Neoverse-V3AE CPU, in a 40-130 W envelope, rated 7.5x the performance of the AGX Orin it replaces; the T4000 trims that to 1,200 TFLOPS and 64 GB <Cite id="jetson-thor-2026" />. Both are orderable through NVIDIA partners, and neither carries a published module price. The strongest signal that edge inference is the intended deployment path for humanoids is hardware: the 1X NEO's onboard computer is a Jetson Thor running at up to 2,070 FP4 TFLOPS <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />.

Whether that compute closes the control loop is now a measured question. NVIDIA's VLA-Perf study ran π0 end-to-end across hardware and reported 19.0 Hz on Jetson Thor, 32.2 Hz on an RTX 4090, 61.7 Hz on an A100, 162.5 Hz on an H100, and 314.4 Hz on a B100 <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />. Sixteen times the throughput across the range, and the edge module sits at the bottom of it. The bottleneck is the diffusion action expert's denoising steps: cutting them scales throughput almost proportionally, while <Term id="action-chunking">action chunk</Term> size adds negligible overhead <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />. The paper publishes no card prices, so this guide lists none for the GPU rows. The latency budgets these numbers feed are developed in [Real-Time Execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution).

## Wheelbases, lidars, and cameras

A policy that leaves the table needs a base and a sensor suite. TurtleBot 4 is the open ROS 2 teaching platform: an iRobot Create 3 chassis, a Raspberry Pi 4, an OAK-D, and an RPLIDAR A1, sold through Clearpath distributors. The product page does not publish a current list price, so the 2022 launch figures stay out of this table <Cite id="clearpath-turtlebot4-2026" />. Clearpath's Jackal and Husky A300 cover the weatherproof and mid-size research UGVs; both are quote-only <Cite id="clearpath-jackal-2026" /> <Cite id="clearpath-husky-2026" />. Husarion's ROSbot XL and Fictionlab's Leo Rover are the open indoor and outdoor alternatives, also without a dated USD list price on the pages we fetched <Cite id="husarion-rosbot-xl-2026" /> <Cite id="leo-rover-2026" />.

Lidar splits by what a buyer can actually check out. Unitree's 4D LiDAR L2 lists at \$419 on the company shop <Cite id="unitree-lidar-l2-2026" />. Livox Mid-360 is the compact 360-degree unit on a large fraction of low-cost mobile robots; the Mid-360 page still carries a January 2023 sample price of \$749, which this guide does not treat as a current list price <Cite id="livox-mid-360-2026" />. Ouster OS1, Hesai XT16/XT32, and SLAMTEC RPLIDAR A2 are the research defaults around it, all without a USD figure on the product page <Cite id="ouster-os1-2026" /> <Cite id="hesai-xt-2026" /> <Cite id="slamtec-rplidar-a2-2026" />.

RGB-D is still a RealSense and OAK conversation, with Orbbec now in the same drawer. The D435i (global shutter plus an IMU) and the longer-baseline D455 remain the default wrist and scene cameras after the 2025 spinout from Intel; neither product page publishes a price <Cite id="realsense-d435i-2026" />. Luxonis OAK-D lists at \$329 and is the camera on TurtleBot 4 Standard <Cite id="luxonis-oak-d-2026" />. Stereolabs ZED 2 is the scene camera DROID actually used: two ZED 2 units plus a ZED Mini on every Franka cell <Cite id="stereolabs-zed-2-2026" /> <Cite id="droid-2024" />. Orbbec's Gemini 335 is the active-stereo alternative; the product page has no USD list price <Cite id="orbbec-gemini-335-2026" />. IMUs sit one layer down. VectorNav's VN-100 and MicroStrain's 3DM-CV7 stay quote-only <Cite id="vectornav-vn-100-2026" />. Movella sells the Xsens MTi-3 as a development kit for \$550 <Cite id="xsens-mti-3-2026" />.

## Mobile manipulators and quadrupeds

The whole-body platforms that are not humanoids are their own buying decision. LeKiwi puts an SO-101 follower on a three-omni-wheel base; the US bill of materials is \$482 for the 12 V kit and \$499 for the 5 V kit, with a wired base-only build from \$184 <Cite id="lekiwi-bom-2026" />. Hello Robot's Stretch 4, the current open-source mobile manipulator, lists at \$29,950 with a 55 cm reach, a 2.5 kg extended payload, and an 8-hour runtime <Cite id="hello-robot-stretch-4-2026" />. PAL Robotics' TIAGo remains the European research staple and is quote-only <Cite id="pal-tiago-2026" />.

Quadrupeds have a priced floor. Unitree Go2 lists at \$1,600 / \$2,800 / \$4,500 across Air, Pro, and Edu, tax and freight excluded <Cite id="unitree-go2-2026" />. Boston Dynamics Spot and ANYbotics ANYmal are the inspection machines above that floor, both quote-only, and Spot is one of the two embodiments Gemini Robotics names alongside Atlas <Cite id="bd-spot-2026" /> <Cite id="robozaps-humanoids-2026" />.

## How to read these prices

Three rules. First, the as-of date: prices in this guide were observed between June and August 2026, except the LEAP Hand's \$2,000, which is its build cost at 2023 publication, and hardware pricing moved repeatedly during the year. Second, currency: yuan figures are converted at July 2026 rates, and the table note keeps the original amount so the conversion is auditable. Third, honest gaps: a null cell means the maker does not publish the figure. Tesla has released no Optimus 3 spec sheet, NVIDIA publishes no Thor module price, and Boston Dynamics does not sell Atlas. Those cells stay empty on purpose.
