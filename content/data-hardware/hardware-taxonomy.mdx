## Gear ratio sets how a joint behaves

The gear ratio, g, amplifies torque by a factor of g, reduces speed by a factor of g, and multiplies the motor's inertia at the joint by its square <Cite id="wensing-cheetah-actuator-2017" />. Fast [legged robots](/rl-sim2real/legged-locomotion) keep it low:

| Gearing | Ratio | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One planetary stage | 5.8 to 1 | MIT Cheetah legs: impacts push the leg back, and foot forces come from motor currents with no force sensor <Cite id="wensing-cheetah-actuator-2017" /> |
| Two planetary stages | 36 to 1 | Every joint of the AGILOped humanoid <Cite id="agiloped-2025" /> |
| Strain wave | 30 to 160 | Compact, no backlash; one 100 to 1 model takes up to 4.5 newton-meters at the output before its motor side turns <Cite id="harmonic-drive-csf-catalog-2026" /> |

## Touch is the missing sensor

Generalist policies learn mostly from cameras and <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term>, the robot's sense of its own joints. The Franka robots in DROID are equipped with three cameras but do not have tactile sensors <Cite id="droid-2024" />, whereas the humanoid robots in AgiBot World are equipped with cameras and visuo-tactile sensors <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.

Speaking after IROS 2026, Chris Paxton of Agility Robotics argued that touch could matter more for multi-fingered hands than for grippers, since fingers obscure the contacts cameras are meant to observe. Paxton characterized commercially available tendon-driven hands as progress toward making dexterity "an intelligence problem, instead of a hardware design problem" <Cite id="paxton-iros-2026" />.

A 2025 outlook published by IEEE highlights the lack of standardized evaluation protocols for tactile sensors as well as challenges related to durability, hysteresis, and temperature sensitivity <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />.

In simulation, reducing the number of tactile sensors on XHand1 while keeping sensors at frequent contact locations led to object rotation performance comparable to configurations with a larger number of evenly spaced sensors. For grasping and lifting, sensor configurations for the Allegro and Sharpa hands that maximized task performance placed sensors to cover a wide range of potential contacts when given the largest sensor budget <Cite id="taxel-placement-2026" />.

## Onboard compute caps the control rate

The frequency with which policies can take actions is controlled by the robot's onboard computer. VLA-Perf's analytical model predicts that π0 runs 19.0 times a second on Jetson Thor, 32.2 on an RTX 4090, and 314.4 on a B100 GPU <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" /> ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution)).

## Prices are dated, and US sales now face FCC limits

<HardwarePriceLadder className="my-6" />

Since July 28, 2026, mobile robots made abroad, including humanoids and quadrupeds, cannot get FCC authorization for sale in the US because of the FCC's Covered List (but can still be sold if already authorized) <Cite id="fcc-covered-list-robots-2026" />. Husqvarna's September 9 exemptions followed Department of War conditional approval <Cite id="fcc-da-26-957" />. October exemptions <Cite id="fcc-da-26-1130" /> included ATI Motors' Sherpa Mecha <Cite id="fcc-da-26-1181" />.
