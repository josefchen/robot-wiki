## Arms, from \$122 to \$70,000

The SO-101, a 3D-printed arm, has six <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term>, five joints plus a gripper, using STS3215 servos. One follower arm's community BOM totals \$121.94 in the US column <Cite id="so-arm100-repo-2026" />. Seeed Studio sells SO-ARM101 Pro at \$295 unassembled or \$299 assembled, with 12-bit magnetic encoders and a 500 g payload <Cite id="seeed-so-arm101-pro-2026" />. LeRobot runs ACT, π0 and SmolVLA policies on these arms <Cite id="lerobot-docs-2026" />. Pull the arm apart in the figure below, then guess which motor works hardest to hold it out straight.

<ExplainerFigure id="arm" />

Trossen Robotics renamed its ALOHA line Trossen AI. Its Solo AI, Stationary AI and Mobile AI kits list at \$11,385.95, \$23,995.95 and \$33,695.95 and integrate LeRobot and OpenPI <Cite id="trossen-ai-2026" />. The WidowX AI arm, sold as leader and follower for <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term>, lists at \$4,545.95 in base form, with six DoF, a 1.5 kg payload and 700 mm reach <Cite id="trossen-widowx-ai-2026" />.

ALOHA 2 succeeds the bimanual ALOHA rig behind ACT <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> and Mobile ALOHA <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />; its hardware designs are open-source, and the paper gives no price <Cite id="aloha-2-2024" />. Hugging Face, which bought Pollen Robotics in April 2025, sells the Reachy 2 humanoid for \$70,000 <Cite id="hf-pollen-acquisition-2025" />. The seven-DoF Franka Emika Panda collected DROID's 76,000 trajectories <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

## Humanoids: shipping beat shouting

Omdia counts about 13,000 [humanoids shipped](/market-map) in 2025, including 5,168 from AgiBot, 39 percent of the total <Cite id="agibot-omdia-ranking-2026" />, including AgiBot's mobile manipulators <Cite id="agibot-omdia-2026" />. IFR counts about 7,000 full-size humanoids (above 140 cm) sold in 2025, often in applications still requiring human teleoperation <Cite id="ifr-service-robots-2026" />. Unitree's May 2026 IPO prospectus reports over 5,500 humanoids shipped in 2025, excluding wheeled dual-arm robots, claiming the world's highest total <Cite id="unitree-prospectus-2026" />.

Unitree's R1 AIR lists at \$4,900 with 20 DoF and the R1 at \$5,900 with 26 <Cite id="unitree-r1-2026" />. The G1 lists at \$13,500 for its 23 DoF base model; its EDU configuration spans 23 to 43 DoF by quote <Cite id="unitree-g1-2026" />. H2 has [31 DoF](/how-robots-work/#humanoid) (six per leg, seven per arm, three waist, two head), 360 N·m joint torque and a 2,070 TOPS onboard chip <Cite id="unitree-h2-2026" />.

Joint-count comparisons depend on configuration. Fourier lists up to 55 actuators for GR-3 with its optional 12-DoF hands <Cite id="fourier-gr3-docs-2026" />. LimX lists 31 active DoF for Oli with end-effector options listed separately <Cite id="limx-oli-spec-2026" />.

1X sells NEO for \$20,000 or \$499 monthly after a refundable \$200 deposit, with US deliveries from 2026 <Cite id="1x-order-2026" />. In April 2026 it reported capacity for 10,000 NEOs annually and said some buyers would get theirs that year, others later <Cite id="1x-neo-factory-2026" />.

Boston Dynamics' electric Atlas has 56 DoF with continuous-rotation key joints, a 50 kg instant payload, IP67 sealing and a 4-hour swappable battery <Cite id="bd-atlas-2026" />, and reaches 2.3 m <Cite id="bd-atlas-unveil-2026" />. Hyundai Motor Group plans 25,000 Atlas units for its plants over the next few years, plus a US factory able to build 30,000 robots a year <Cite id="bd-rmac-2026" />.

Figure's third-generation humanoid has a camera in each palm, no disclosed hand DoF and 2 kW wireless charging through its feet. Figure builds it for high-volume manufacturing at its BotQ facility <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />, whose first-line capacity is up to 12,000 humanoids a year <Cite id="figure-botq-2025" />. Agility says Digit 5, unveiled September 2026, repeatedly lifts loads up to 22.7 kg and runs 90 minutes on a 9-minute charge, with general availability by 2027's end <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />. Tesla reports installing the first Optimus production lines at Fremont and will collect training data with the first builds <Cite id="tesla-q2-2026-update" />.

## Hands, bundled and standalone

<span id="hands-ship-bundled" />Most [dexterous hands](/frontier/dexterity) ship with a robot: Unitree's Dex3-1, a G1 EDU option, has seven active DoF per hand (three thumb, two index, two middle) and an optional tactile array <Cite id="unitree-g1-2026" />. 1X's order page lists 22 DoF per NEO hand <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />. Its July 9, 2026 hand post counts 25, adding 3 wrist DoF to the 22 fully actuated finger and palm DoF <Cite id="1x-neos-hands-2026" />. Boston Dynamics' October 2026 Atlas hand has four fingers and 13 DoF, with fingertip and palm pressure sensors <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />.

The open LEAP Hand has 16 DoF and costs about \$2,000 in catalog parts, roughly an eighth of the Allegro Hand used for comparison <Cite id="leap-hand-2023" />. RUKA's authors priced Allegro at \$15,000 and built a five-fingered hand for under \$1,300 <Cite id="ruka-2025" />. ORCA is an open 17-DoF tendon-driven hand with tactile sensors, under 2,000 CHF in materials <Cite id="orca-hand-2025" />, and the commercial Shadow Dexterous Hand uses 20 motors for 24 degrees of freedom <Cite id="shadow-dexterous-hand-2026" />. Sharpa's Wave, a human-scale five-finger hand with 22 active DoF and high-resolution tactile sensing, requires a sales enquiry and lists no price <Cite id="sharpa-wave-2026" />. PSYONIC designed its Ability Hand as a prosthesis, opening it to robotics researchers in November 2022 <Cite id="psyonic-ability-hand-2022" />.

Unitree lists standalone Dex5-1 at 20 total DoF, 16 active, with 94 tactile sensors per hand <Cite id="unitree-dex5-1-2026" />.

## Sensors: touch is the missing channel

Generalist policies mostly train on vision and <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term>; DROID's Franka rig has three cameras and no touch sensing <Cite id="droid-2024" />. AgiBot World's humanoids add visuo-tactile sensors to cameras <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. A 2025 outlook in IEEE Transactions on Robotics blames divergent transduction methods with no standardized evaluation, poor durability, hysteresis and temperature sensitivity <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />.

Perception hardware extends beyond ordinary cameras: ENGINEAI lists the SE01 sensors as lidar, depth cameras and infrared cameras <Cite id="engineai-se01-2026" />.

Agility Robotics' Chris Paxton argued on October 12, after IROS 2026, that multi-fingered hands may need touch sensing two-finger grippers can forgo, because their fingers increasingly hide contacts needed for camera vision. He called currently off-the-shelf tendon-driven hands a significant step toward making human-like dexterity "an intelligence problem, instead of a hardware design problem" <Cite id="paxton-iros-2026" />.

GelSight Mini retails at \$500 and Meta FAIR's open DIGIT fingertip at \$350 <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" /> <Cite id="digit-sensor-2020" />. DIGIT's successor, Digit 360, has about 8.3 million taxels and resolves normal and shear forces to 1.01 mN and 1.27 mN <Cite id="digit-360-2024" />. GelSight manufactures it under Meta's partnership announced in October 2024 <Cite id="meta-fair-touch-2024" />. AnySkin, a magnetic skin, is the first uncalibrated tactile sensor reported to carry learned policies across replacement skins <Cite id="anyskin-2024" />.

Optical designs differ in observable contacts. GelSlim 3.0 measures shape, contact-force distributions and incipient slip in a compact finger <Cite id="gelslim3-2021" />. GelTip views a deformable finger from its base, capturing contacts inside and outside the gripper closure <Cite id="geltip-2021" />. DenseTact estimates 3D surface deformation from fisheye camera images <Cite id="densetact-2022" />; AllSight estimates contact position, forces and torsion on a rounded finger <Cite id="allsight-2023" />.

## Compute: closing the loop

NVIDIA's Jetson Thor T5000 delivers 2,070 sparse FP4 TFLOPS with 128 GB of LPDDR5X at 273 GB/s on a 14-core Neoverse-V3AE CPU, within 40-130 W. The T4000 has 1,200 TFLOPS and 64 GB <Cite id="jetson-thor-2026" />. NVIDIA rates Thor's AI compute at 7.5x Jetson Orin's and launched the developer kit at \$3,499 in August 2025 <Cite id="nvidia-jetson-thor-available-2025" />. It now lists the kit at \$5,499, and the T5000 and T4000 modules at \$4,999 and \$2,999 in 1,000-unit volume <Cite id="nvidia-jetson-faq" />.

The 1X NEO uses Jetson Thor at up to 2,070 FP4 TFLOPS <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />. Figure's Helix runs entirely on embedded low-power GPUs, with a 7-9 Hz vision-language model guiding a 200 Hz controller <Cite id="helix-2025" />.

VLA-Perf's roofline model predicts π0 inference at 19.0 Hz on Jetson Thor, 32.2 Hz on RTX 4090, 61.7 Hz on A100, 162.5 Hz on H100 and 314.4 Hz on B100, without network latency. These batch-one analytical predictions use the 2.7B π0 baseline with 3 cameras, 14 action dimensions, chunk size 50 and 10 denoising steps, without measuring the policy on all five GPUs. On B100, raising denoising steps from 10 to 50 raises total latency 2.15×; raising chunk size from 50 to 250 raises it 11% <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />. [Real-Time Execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution) develops these latency budgets.
