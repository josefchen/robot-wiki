## Arms and bimanual rigs

The cheapest entry is a 3D-printed arm. The SO-101 has six <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term> (DoF), five joints plus a gripper, and the community parts list for one follower arm totals \$121.94 in its US column <Cite id="so-arm100-repo-2026" />. Seeed Studio sells the SO-ARM101 Pro at \$295 unassembled or \$299 assembled <Cite id="seeed-so-arm101-pro-2026" />, and Hugging Face's LeRobot runs ACT, π0 and SmolVLA policies on these arms <Cite id="lerobot-docs-2026" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="arm" />

Research rigs cost more. Trossen Robotics, which renamed its ALOHA line Trossen AI, lists its Solo AI, Stationary AI and Mobile AI kits at \$11,385.95, \$23,995.95 and \$33,695.95, with LeRobot and OpenPI integrated <Cite id="trossen-ai-2026" />. Its WidowX AI arm, sold as a leader and follower pair for <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> (a person moves the leader and the follower copies it), lists at \$4,545.95 in base form <Cite id="trossen-widowx-ai-2026" />. ALOHA 2, successor to the bimanual ALOHA rig behind ACT <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> and Mobile ALOHA <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />, has open-source hardware designs and no published price <Cite id="aloha-2-2024" />.

Higher up, Hugging Face, which bought Pollen Robotics in April 2025, sells the Reachy 2 humanoid for \$70,000 <Cite id="hf-pollen-acquisition-2025" />, and DROID's 76,000 trajectories were all collected on the seven-DoF Franka Emika Panda <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

## Humanoids ship in the thousands

Shipment counts depend on who counts. Omdia counts about 13,000 [humanoids shipped](/market-map) in 2025, 5,168 of them (39 percent) from AgiBot <Cite id="agibot-omdia-ranking-2026" />, including its mobile manipulators <Cite id="agibot-omdia-2026" />. The International Federation of Robotics counts about 7,000 full-size humanoids (above 140 cm) sold in 2025, often for work still requiring human teleoperation <Cite id="ifr-service-robots-2026" />. Unitree's May 2026 IPO prospectus reports over 5,500 shipped in 2025, excluding wheeled dual-arm robots, and claims the world's highest total <Cite id="unitree-prospectus-2026" />.

Joint counts depend on configuration: Fourier lists up to 55 actuators for GR-3 counting its optional 12-DoF hands <Cite id="fourier-gr3-docs-2026" />, LimX lists 31 active DoF for Oli with end-effectors separate <Cite id="limx-oli-spec-2026" />, and Boston Dynamics' electric Atlas has 56, some rotating continuously <Cite id="bd-atlas-2026" />, and reaches 2.3 m <Cite id="bd-atlas-ces-2026" />.

Hyundai Motor Group plans 25,000 Atlas units for its plants and a US factory able to build 30,000 robots a year, and in September 2026 Boston Dynamics began training Atlas at a Hyundai plant in Georgia <Cite id="bd-rmac-2026" />. 1X reported capacity for 10,000 NEOs a year in April 2026 <Cite id="1x-neo-factory-2026" />. Figure builds its third-generation humanoid for high-volume manufacturing <Cite id="figure-03-2025" /> at BotQ, whose first line can make up to 12,000 a year <Cite id="figure-botq-2025" />. Agility plans general availability of Digit 5, unveiled in September 2026, by the end of 2027 <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />. Tesla said in July 2026 that its first Fremont Optimus builds would collect training data <Cite id="tesla-q2-2026-update" />, and in October reported production on high-volume lines there <Cite id="tesla-q3-2026-update" />.

## Sensors: touch is the missing channel

Generalist policies learn mostly from cameras and <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term>, the robot's sense of its own joint positions. DROID's Franka rig has three cameras and no touch sensing <Cite id="droid-2024" />; AgiBot World's humanoids add visuo-tactile sensors <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />, and ENGINEAI's SE01 adds lidar, depth and infrared cameras <Cite id="engineai-se01-2026" />.

Agility Robotics' Chris Paxton argued after IROS 2026 that multi-fingered hands may need touch sensing two-finger grippers can forgo, because their fingers increasingly hide the contacts a camera needs to see. He called the tendon-driven hands now sold off the shelf a significant step toward making human-like dexterity "an intelligence problem, instead of a hardware design problem" <Cite id="paxton-iros-2026" />.

A 2025 outlook in IEEE Transactions on Robotics points to sensors built on divergent physical principles with no standardized evaluation, poor durability, hysteresis (readings that depend on past loading) and temperature sensitivity <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />. Robbyant's open LingBot-Touch (November 2026) maps piezoresistive, magnetic and visuotactile signals into one representation <Cite id="lingbot-touch-2026" />, pretrained on nearly 5,000 hours of tactile recordings <Cite id="lingbot-touch-release-2026" />.

## Compute: closing the loop

The onboard computer sets how often a policy can choose its next move. NVIDIA's Jetson Thor T5000 delivers 2,070 sparse FP4 TFLOPS with 128 GB of memory within 40 to 130 W, and the T4000 1,200 TFLOPS with 64 GB <Cite id="jetson-thor-2026" />. NVIDIA rates Thor at 7.5 times Jetson Orin's AI compute and launched the developer kit at \$3,499 in August 2025 <Cite id="nvidia-jetson-thor-available-2025" />; it now lists the kit at \$5,499 and the two modules at \$4,999 and \$2,999 in 1,000-unit volume <Cite id="nvidia-jetson-faq" />.

VLA-Perf, an analytical model, predicts that π0, with three cameras, 50-action chunks and 10 denoising steps, runs 19.0 times a second on Jetson Thor, 32.2 on an RTX 4090 and 314.4 on a B100 data-center GPU, with one request at a time and no network delay. On the B100, raising denoising steps from 10 to 50 raises total latency 2.15 times <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />. [Real-Time Execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution) develops these latency budgets.

Intel runs real-time control, perception and policies together on Core Ultra Series 3 processors, which combine CPU, GPU and NPU <Cite id="intel-robotics-platform" />. Figure's Helix runs on embedded low-power GPUs, a vision-language model working 7 to 9 times a second guiding a 200 Hz controller <Cite id="helix-2025" />.

List prices are dated and configuration-specific; the G1 EDU is priced by quote <Cite id="unitree-g1-2026" />. On July 28, 2026 the FCC added foreign-produced mobile robots, including humanoids and quadrupeds, to its Covered List: listed new models cannot get FCC authorization for US sale, while earlier-authorized models can still be sold <Cite id="fcc-covered-list-robots-2026" />. From October 2026 the FCC began exempting models that won Department of War conditional approval <Cite id="fcc-da-26-1130" />, including ATI Motors' Sherpa Mecha humanoid and OMRON's LD-150 and LD-300 mobile robots <Cite id="fcc-da-26-1181" />.
