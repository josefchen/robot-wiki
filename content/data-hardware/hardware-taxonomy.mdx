## Gear ratio sets how a joint behaves

A gear ratio multiplies motor torque and divides speed by the same number, and multiplies the motor's inertia at the joint by its square <Cite id="wensing-cheetah-actuator-2017" />. Fast [legged robots](/rl-sim2real/legged-locomotion) keep it low:

| Gearing | Ratio | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One planetary stage | 5.8 to 1 | MIT Cheetah legs: impacts push the leg back, and foot forces come from motor currents with no force sensor <Cite id="wensing-cheetah-actuator-2017" /> |
| Two planetary stages | 36 to 1 | Every joint of the AGILOped humanoid <Cite id="agiloped-2025" /> |
| Strain wave | 30 to 160 | Compact, no backlash; one 100 to 1 model takes up to 4.5 newton-meters at the output before its motor side turns <Cite id="harmonic-drive-csf-catalog-2026" /> |

## Touch is the missing sensor

Generalist policies learn mostly from cameras and <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term>, the robot's sense of its own joints. DROID's Franka rig has three cameras and no touch sensing <Cite id="droid-2024" />; AgiBot World's humanoids add visuo-tactile sensors <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.

Agility Robotics' Chris Paxton argued after IROS 2026 that multi-fingered hands may need touch more than grippers do, because their fingers hide the contacts a camera needs to see. He called off-the-shelf tendon-driven hands a step toward making dexterity "an intelligence problem, instead of a hardware design problem" <Cite id="paxton-iros-2026" />.

A 2025 IEEE outlook finds tactile sensors with no standard evaluation, poor durability, hysteresis and temperature sensitivity <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />.

## Onboard compute caps the control rate

The onboard computer sets how often a policy can choose its next move. VLA-Perf's analytical model predicts that π0 runs 19.0 times a second on Jetson Thor, 32.2 on an RTX 4090 and 314.4 on a B100 GPU <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" /> ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution)).

## Prices are dated, and US sales now face FCC limits

<HardwarePriceLadder className="my-6" />

Since July 28, 2026, the FCC's Covered List bars new foreign-produced mobile robots, humanoids and quadrupeds included, from authorization for US sale; earlier-authorized models can still be sold <Cite id="fcc-covered-list-robots-2026" />. From October the FCC began exempting models with Department of War conditional approval <Cite id="fcc-da-26-1130" />, such as ATI Motors' Sherpa Mecha humanoid <Cite id="fcc-da-26-1181" />.
