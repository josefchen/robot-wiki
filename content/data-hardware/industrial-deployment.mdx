Industrial deployment is the installation of robots in factories and warehouses, where each cell has to pay back its cost. IFR counts a record 5 million industrial robots operating worldwide in 2025, after more than 600,000 installations that year, 59 percent of them in China <Cite id="ifr-world-robotics-2026-release" />.

This authored worked example has no measured deployment behind it: its 80,000 USD robot cost is an assumed input, with no sourced arm-price quote, on a 20,000 to 250,000 USD range in 5,000 USD steps. The other defaults are a 2.5 integration multiple, 6-second cycle, 95 percent uptime, 99.9 percent per-pick success, 15-second jam clearing and a 25 USD hourly wage. The model assumes 730 hours a month, a 60-month amortization period and a chosen 24-month verdict horizon, and counts modeled picks per elapsed hour, including downtime. Payback divides cell capital by modeled monthly labour value, without damage or throughput savings.

At 15-second jam clearing, cutting per-pick success from 99.9 to 99 percent moves payback from 11.56 to 11.82 months, an increase of 2.2 percent. At 300 seconds the same cut moves payback from 12.11 to 17.30 months, and modeled output falls 30 percent, from 542.9 to 380.0 picks per elapsed hour. EVST, a vendor that quotes complete cells, publishes no fixed list prices and puts the robot at a third to half of total cell cost <Cite id="evst-cell-cost-2026" />.

IFR's World Robotics 2026, released on September 24, 2026, counts a record operational stock of 5 million industrial robots in 2025, up 9 percent, after more than 600,000 installations that year, up 11 percent. China installed 354,000 units, 59 percent of the total, and the United States, with almost 38,500 installations, passed Japan to become the second-largest market <Cite id="ifr-world-robotics-2026-release" />.

A year earlier, World Robotics 2025 counted 4,663,698 robots in operation and 542,076 installed in 2024, above 500,000 in each year from 2021 <Cite id="ifr-world-robotics-2025" />. China then held 2,027,190, about 4.5 times second-ranked Japan's 450,530, and took 295,045 installations, 54 percent of the total <Cite id="ifr-world-robotics-2025" /> <Cite id="ifr-china-five-year-plan-2026" />.

Strip the installed base down by task and most of it is repetitive motion in a fixed cell, with little or no perception worth the name. OSHA's Technical Manual lists uses including arc and resistance welding, painting and spraying, machine-tool loading and unloading, assembly, materials handling and packaging <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-8"><Cite id="osha-otm-robots" /></span>. In A3's North American order data for 2025, collaborative robots were 7,212 units worth \$241 million, 19.6 percent of the 36,766 robots ordered and 10.7 percent of the \$2.25 billion in order value. A3 began reporting them as a distinct category in Q1 2025 <Cite id="a3-orders-2025" />.

Learned systems enter mostly through logistics. <Term id="automated-storage-and-retrieval">Automated storage and retrieval</Term> puts high-bay racking and shuttles where aisles and forklifts used to be, and AutoStore reports about 2,000 systems in 68 countries <Cite id="autostore-q2-2026" />. <Term id="autonomous-mobile-robot">Autonomous mobile robot</Term> fleets move cases and totes, and transport and logistics robots sold 117,500 units in 2025, 47 percent of professional service robots <Cite id="ifr-service-robots-2026" />. <Term id="goods-to-person">Goods-to-person</Term> stations bring the shelf to a stationary worker, which sets the productivity bar for automated piece picking.

In October 2023 Amazon reported over 750,000 robots working collaboratively with employees; Sequoia was operating at one Houston fulfillment center, and testing Digit for tote recycling was planned <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-24"><Cite id="amazon-sequoia-digit-2023" /></span>. Its fleet overview, updated in June 2026, reports more than one million robots deployed since 2012, a cumulative total and not a simultaneous active-fleet count, with next-generation Proteus still a lab pilot <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-4"><Cite id="amazon-robot-fleet-2026" /></span>. In September 2026 Amazon said its robots work across more than 300 facilities and assist employees with 75 percent of the customer orders it delivers <Cite id="amazon-greenwood-2026" />. Its DeepFleet model was set to improve fleet travel efficiency by 10 percent <Cite id="amazon-million-robots-2025" />.

Amazon's Vulcan picks and stows approximately 75 percent of the types of items stored at its fulfillment centers, at speeds comparable to front-line employees. It hands items it cannot move to employees, and the post reports no pick-success rate or independently validated reliability <Cite id="amazon-vulcan-2026" />. An Amazon Robotics paper on stowing into fabric pods does report outcomes: over 100,000 attempts in a fulfillment center, 85.86 percent succeeded, 9.31 percent were unproductive and 4.83 percent were defects <Cite id="amazon-stow-2025" />.

Symbotic's FY2025 Form 10-K reports about \$22.5 billion of backlog, meaning unperformed obligations under existing contracts and not revenue already earned, at September 27, 2025, against \$2.246922 billion of revenue for the year. Its Walmart agreement, expanded in May 2022, covers all 42 regional distribution centres, and Symbotic had 50 systems in deployment and 48 operational systems under software maintenance and support contracts <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-8"><Cite id="symbotic-10k-2025" /></span>.

In November 2025 Kroger said it would close three robotic warehouses in Wisconsin, Maryland and Florida and was monitoring its five remaining sites <Cite id="kroger-ocado-closures-2025" />. On December 5, 2025 Ocado and Kroger agreed a one-off \$350 million payment that also covers Kroger's decision not to proceed with Charlotte. Ocado expects the closures to cut its FY26 fee revenue by about \$50 million <Cite id="ocado-kroger-rns-2025" />. Ocado Intelligent Automation sells the same grid-robot warehouses outward into healthcare, third-party logistics and industrial parts <Cite id="ocado-oia-2026" />.

## Humanoids in plants

IFR counts about 7,000 [full-size humanoids](/data-hardware/hardware-taxonomy) sold in 2025 and says most applications remain specialized and often require human teleoperation <Cite id="ifr-service-robots-2026" />. Agility says Digit 4 has logged more than 65,000 operational hours and moved 100,000 totes at GXO's Flowery Branch site at about 98 percent accuracy while on-task <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />. Hyundai is training Atlas to sequence automotive parts at its Robotics Metaplant Application Center and expects component assembly by 2030 <Cite id="bd-rmac-2026" />.

Jonathan Hurst, Agility's cofounder and chief robot officer, wrote in IEEE Spectrum in October 2026 that in Digit 4's commercial deployments the robots had to work behind physical barriers, kept apart from people, to meet insurance and regulatory demands, which limited their usefulness <Cite id="hurst-spectrum-2026" />. As a person approaches, Digit 5 is programmed to stop, set down its payload, sit on the floor and cut power to its motors before the person can touch it, which he calls "the only approach that makes sense as a first step" <Cite id="hurst-spectrum-2026" />. Its safety sensors and computers, larger battery and stronger motors added bulk, and Digit 5 can no longer run or jump as earlier versions could <Cite id="hurst-spectrum-2026" />.

Figure's 11-month BMW deployment set an 84-second cycle requirement, a target above 99 percent success per shift and a goal of zero interventions. It reports 1,250+ hours of runtime and 90,000+ parts loaded, without achieved rates against those targets <Cite id="figure-bmw-production-2025" />. Each of these figures comes from the company itself. Unitree's IPO prospectus adds the financial side: first-quarter 2026 revenue growth slowed to 68.49%, from 332.64% a year earlier, and adjusted net profit fell 52.55% year on year <Cite id="unitree-prospectus-2026" />.

## The integrator's multiple

A robot arm is a minority of what a cell costs. EVST puts complete palletising cells at two to three times the arm's price <Cite id="evst-cell-cost-2026" />. Its July 15, 2026 commercial palletising-cell guide lists application-specific end-of-arm tooling, fencing, light curtains or area scanners, interlocked gates, safety-rated PLCs, installation, PLC/HMI integration, commissioning, pattern programming, software licences and any vision-guided pick logic for mixed loads. The surrounding-line work depends on what is already installed <Cite id="evst-cell-cost-2026" />.

<Image id="kuka-bread-palletizing-bakery-2005" />

OSHA's Technical Manual, discussing ANSI/RIA R15.06-2012, says the <Term id="systems-integrator">systems integrator</Term> completes and documents the application's risk assessment before commissioning and gives the results to the employer. Manufacturers or employers may also act as integrators. The employer retains responsibility for a safe workplace, and a risk assessment alone does not establish that the application protects workers <Cite id="osha-otm-robots" />.

In its April 16, 2026 investor outlook, Bessemer predicts that near-term value will accrue to full-stack, vertically integrated players ahead of pure-play foundation-model companies. It says deployment requires domain-specific data collection, target-environment fine-tuning, hardware integration and operational infrastructure. It also stresses expensive data collection and foundation models not yet general enough to work out of the box <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-8"><Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" /></span>.

## The metrics that decide a deployment

<Term id="cycle-time">Cycle time</Term> is the time required to produce a part or complete a process, measured for that operation <Cite id="lei-cycle-time-definition" />. <Term id="takt-time">Takt time</Term> is available production time divided by customer demand, and its purpose is to match production with demand <Cite id="lei-takt-time-definition" />. NASA distinguishes inherent availability, computed from <Term id="mean-time-between-failures">mean time between failures</Term> and mean time to repair without administrative, logistics or preventive-maintenance time, from operational availability, which includes them. Uptime is time in an operable state <Cite id="nasa-availability-prediction-analysis" />.

The calculator above reports capital cost per modeled pick: robot price times the integration multiple, divided by modeled lifetime picks over a fixed amortization period. It does not include running costs. The <Term id="payback-period">payback period</Term> is total system capital cost divided by monthly labour, reduced-damage and throughput value. EVST's guide estimates 12 to 24 months for multi-shift palletising cells and 24 to 36 months for single-shift or lower-throughput operations <Cite id="evst-cell-cost-2026" />.

## The jam rate is the lesson

A separate authored example has no measured intervention rate behind it. At 99 percent per-pick success, with every failure needing a ten-second clearing, overhead is (1 − 0.99) × 10 = 0.1 seconds per attempted pick. Amazon's stow system is designed to stow 80 percent of warehouse items at 300 units per hour. A no-stow turnaway, when a pod leaves with nothing stowed, consumes at least 12 seconds <Cite id="amazon-stow-2025" />.

The <Term id="intervention-rate">intervention rate</Term> and clearing cost together decide whether a cell ships. Physical Intelligence reports that training on deployment data, including expert corrections during autonomous runs, more than doubled throughput and roughly halved the failure rate on some of its hardest tasks <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. Ken Goldberg proposes combining model-based engineering with model-free learning so robots can perform useful work, collect real-world data, and use those data to improve performance and learn adjacent skills. Goldberg does not claim that engineering removes the need for learning, and he expects model-free AI eventually to enable fully general-purpose robots <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />.

[The Reliability Gap](/frontier/reliability-gap) tracks the demo-to-production gap and selected deployment examples. Morgan Stanley's 2026 note on the humanoid industry's "PR problem", as reported by CNBC, describes [investors](/market-map) looking for real-world return on investment over polished demonstrations <Cite id="morgan-stanley-pr-problem-2026" />. Figure says its livestreamed logistics run lasted 200 hours <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />, but it was one task at one site, with [no published success rate](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis).

## Brownfield versus greenfield

A greenfield site is designed around the automation: floor loading, ceiling height, power, network and traffic lanes exist because the system needed them. Amazon's Shreveport fulfillment center was built with 10 times more robotics, and its Sequoia can hold more than 30 million items <Cite id="amazon-shreveport-2024" />. A <Term id="brownfield-deployment">brownfield deployment</Term> must fit the automation into a building that already runs.

OSHA describes safeguards for non-collaborative robot applications during automatic operation: guards such as fences and barriers, interlocked guards and presence-sensing devices. Teaching inside the safeguarded space uses reduced-speed manual mode with an enabling device, and maintenance follows lockout/tagout procedures under 29 CFR 1910.147 or 29 CFR 1910.333 <Cite id="osha-otm-robots" />. Symbotic says it can install its systems in phases while the existing warehouse keeps operating <Cite id="symbotic-10k-2025" />. [Safety and Assurance](/frontier/safety-and-assurance) covers the wider obligations.

## The labour evidence, disagreement intact

Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo find that one more robot per thousand workers cuts the US employment-to-population ratio by about 0.2 percentage points and wages by 0.42 percent. Losses concentrate in commuting zones exposed to industrial automation <Cite id="acemoglu-restrepo-2020" />.

In Germany, robot-driven displacement in manufacturing was fully offset by new jobs in services, and the cost fell mostly on young workers entering the labour force <Cite id="dauth-robots-germany-2021" />. Spanish manufacturers that adopted robots raised output by 20 to 25 percent within four years and created net jobs at a rate of 10 percent <Cite id="koch-robots-firms-2021" />. French adopters also grew employment, at their competitors' expense, and the overall effect on industry employment was negative <Cite id="acemoglu-lelarge-restrepo-2020" />.

The MIT Task Force on the Work of the Future, co-chaired by David Autor and David Mindell with Elisabeth Reynolds as executive director, found in 2020 no compelling evidence of a jobless future. It describes automation displacing some tasks and creating new work, shaped by economic incentives, policy choices and institutions <Cite id="mit-work-future-2020" />.
