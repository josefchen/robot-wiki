## In 2021 physics moved onto the GPU

Isaac Gym (2021) kept physics and the network on one GPU and reported 2 to 3 orders of magnitude over CPU simulators feeding GPU networks <Cite id="isaac-gym-2021" />. Rudin and colleagues scaled from 128 robots, typical for CPUs, to 16,384 on one workstation GPU <Cite id="rudin-2021" />.

## More environments help until something else bottlenecks

On a Franka drawer task, an RTX 5090 workstation nearly matched a server with two data-center GPUs, which Isaac Lab's authors attribute to CPU bottlenecks <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. At a fixed batch size, more robots hurt Rudin's policy past a point <Cite id="rudin-2021" />, and SAPG reports PPO saturating <Cite id="sapg-2024" />.

Newer methods push past that limit.

| Method | Scale |
| --- | --- |
| Beukman and colleagues (2026) | Over a million environments, improving up to one trillion transitions <Cite id="beukman-2026" /> |
| Success Guided Sampling (2026) | Past a million, practicing tasks solved about half the time <Cite id="sgs-2026" /> <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| Parallel Q-Learning (2023) | Off-policy, tens of thousands of environments <Cite id="pql-2023" /> |
| FastTD3 (2025) | Off-policy, large batches for humanoids <Cite id="fasttd3-2025" /> |

## Two software lines now meet in Newton

NVIDIA's line runs from Isaac Gym to Orbit and Isaac Lab. Google DeepMind's builds on MuJoCo <Cite id="mujoco-2012" />, open-sourced in 2022 <Cite id="mujoco-overview-docs-2026" />, whose MJX and MuJoCo Warp versions run on accelerators <Cite id="mjx-docs-2026" /> <Cite id="mjwarp-docs-2026" />. Newton, from Disney Research, Google DeepMind and NVIDIA, uses MuJoCo Warp as its main backend <Cite id="newton-lf-2025" /> <Cite id="newton-repo-2026" />.

| Tool | Released | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brax | 2021, Google | Physics and learning compiled together in JAX; policies in minutes <Cite id="brax-2021" /> |
| Orbit | 2023 | Framework on Isaac Sim, later Isaac Lab <Cite id="orbit-2023" /> <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| MuJoCo Playground | 2025 | Open sim-to-real framework on MuJoCo XLA (MJX) for legged robots, hands and arms <Cite id="mujoco-playground-2025" /> |
| Newton 1.0 | March 2026 | Several solvers behind one API, from rigid bodies to cloth (VBD <Cite id="vbd-2024" />) and continuum materials <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" /> <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" /> |
| mjlab | 2026 | Isaac Lab's environment API on MuJoCo Warp physics <Cite id="mjlab-2026" /> |
| Isaac Lab 3.0 | Early access 16 September, release 20 October 2026 | One task API over PhysX or Newton <Cite id="isaac-lab-3-ea-2026" /> <Cite id="isaac-lab-3-0-2026" /> |
| MuJoCo 4.0 | 6 November 2026 | Speculative contacts stop thin, fast objects passing through each other; 6 to 9 times faster stair climbing and thin-object grasping, CPU only so far <Cite id="mujoco-4-2026" /> |

| System | Reported throughput |
| --- | --- |
| Isaac Lab, eight GPUs | Over 900,000 steps a second for the DextrAH <Cite id="dextrah-g-2024" /> teacher, 1.6 million on a Franka cabinet <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |

Contact models are improving too. Newton handles detailed CAD meshes and hydroelastic contact, which spreads force over a patch instead of a point <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" /> <Cite id="elandt-2019" />. Isaac Sim 6.1 <Cite id="isaac-sim-v6-1-0-2026" /> added that model to its experimental Newton integration <Cite id="isaac-sim-6-1-release-notes-2026" />, the same day as Newton 1.6.0 <Cite id="newton-v1-6-0-2026" />.

Also: Newton grew from Warp's deprecated simulation module <Cite id="nvidia-warp-repo-2026" />.

## Repeatability and gradients are the open problems

Batched execution in Isaac Lab and Genesis adds significant variability between runs and environments, even from identical starts <Cite id="gpusimbench-2026" />; Steel lists run-to-run determinism as a feature <Cite id="newton-v1-8-0-2026" />. In Isaac Lab, <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term> of mass and friction still goes through the CPU <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />.

SHAC, which takes gradients through the simulator, trained more than 17 times faster than the best established RL on muscle-driven locomotion <Cite id="shac-2022" />. Schwarke and colleagues report the first legged sim-to-real transfer learned entirely in a differentiable simulator <Cite id="schwarke-2024" />. Newton's main backend says "Differentiability via Warp is not yet available" <Cite id="mujoco-warp-repo-2026" />.

Also: per-solver gradients in Newton <Cite id="newton-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" />, beta <Term id="system-identification">system identification</Term> in Isaac Sim 6.1 <Cite id="isaac-sim-6-1-release-notes-2026" />, Drake as the "gold standard" for rigorous numerics <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" />.
