## In 2021 physics moved onto the GPU

Isaac Gym (2021) reported 2 to 3 orders of magnitude speedup compared to simulators running on CPUs while sending observations to networks running on GPUs, by running the neural network and physics simulation on one GPU <Cite id="isaac-gym-2021" />. Rudin and colleagues scaled from 128 robots, typical for CPU-based simulation, to 16,384 on one workstation GPU <Cite id="rudin-2021" />.

## More environments help until something else bottlenecks

On a Franka drawer task the authors of Isaac Lab found that a workstation with an RTX 5090 performs almost as well as a server with two data-center GPUs, which they attribute to bottlenecks on the CPU <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. At a fixed batch size, adding robots beyond a certain point had a negative effect on Rudin's policy <Cite id="rudin-2021" />, and SAPG found that PPO performance saturates <Cite id="sapg-2024" />.

Newer methods push past that limit.

| Method | Scale |
| --- | --- |
| Beukman and colleagues (2026) | Over a million environments, improving up to one trillion transitions <Cite id="beukman-2026" /> |
| Success Guided Sampling (2026) | Past a million, practicing tasks solved about half the time <Cite id="sgs-2026" /> <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| Parallel Q-Learning (2023) | Off-policy, tens of thousands of environments <Cite id="pql-2023" /> |
| FastTD3 (2025) | Off-policy, large batches for humanoids <Cite id="fasttd3-2025" /> |

## Two software lines now meet in Newton

NVIDIA's line runs from Isaac Gym to Orbit and Isaac Lab. Google DeepMind's line builds on MuJoCo <Cite id="mujoco-2012" />, which was open-sourced in 2022 <Cite id="mujoco-overview-docs-2026" /> and also has versions known as MJX and MuJoCo Warp that run on accelerators <Cite id="mjx-docs-2026" /> <Cite id="mjwarp-docs-2026" />. The latter is the main backend for Newton, from Disney Research, Google DeepMind and NVIDIA <Cite id="newton-lf-2025" /> <Cite id="newton-repo-2026" />.

| Tool | Released | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brax | 2021, Google | Physics and learning compiled together in JAX; policies in minutes <Cite id="brax-2021" /> |
| Orbit | 2023 | Framework on Isaac Sim, later Isaac Lab <Cite id="orbit-2023" /> <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| MuJoCo Playground | 2025 | Open sim-to-real framework on MuJoCo XLA (MJX) for legged robots, hands and arms <Cite id="mujoco-playground-2025" /> |
| Newton 1.0 | March 2026 | Several solvers behind one API, from rigid bodies to cloth (VBD <Cite id="vbd-2024" />) and continuum materials <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" /> <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" /> |
| mjlab | 2026 | Isaac Lab's environment API on MuJoCo Warp physics <Cite id="mjlab-2026" /> |
| Isaac Lab 3.0 | Early access 16 September, release 20 October 2026 | One task API over PhysX or Newton <Cite id="isaac-lab-3-ea-2026" /> <Cite id="isaac-lab-3-0-2026" /> |
| MuJoCo 4.0 | 6 November 2026 | Speculative contacts stop thin, fast objects passing through each other; 6 to 9 times faster stair climbing and thin-object grasping, CPU only so far <Cite id="mujoco-4-2026" /> |

Throughput is the selling point, and most performance numbers are self-reported by the vendors.

| System | Reported throughput |
| --- | --- |
| Isaac Lab, eight GPUs | Over 900,000 steps a second for the DextrAH <Cite id="dextrah-g-2024" /> teacher, 1.6 million on a Franka cabinet <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| Isaac Lab 3.0 with Newton | Up to 2.5x Isaac Lab 2.3 with PhysX on camera-based dexterity <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| MuJoCo Warp | 252x MJX on locomotion, 475x on manipulation; setup unstated <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" /> |
| Skild AI Steel, in Newton 1.8.0 <Cite id="newton-v1-8-0-2026" /> | 3.8x PhysX over 20 benchmarks, in Skild's own tests <Cite id="skild-steel-2026" /> |

Contact models are improving too. Newton handles detailed CAD meshes and hydroelastic contact, a contact model that results in forces being distributed over a patch instead of at a single point <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" /> <Cite id="elandt-2019" />. Isaac Sim 6.1 <Cite id="isaac-sim-v6-1-0-2026" /> added hydroelastic contact to its experimental Newton integration <Cite id="isaac-sim-6-1-release-notes-2026" /> the same day as Newton 1.6.0 <Cite id="newton-v1-6-0-2026" />.

Also: Newton grew from Warp's deprecated simulation module <Cite id="nvidia-warp-repo-2026" />.

## Repeatability and gradients are the open problems

Batched execution in Isaac Lab and Genesis adds significant variability between runs and environments, even with identical initial conditions <Cite id="gpusimbench-2026" />, while Steel lists run-to-run determinism as a feature <Cite id="newton-v1-8-0-2026" />. In Isaac Lab, <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term> of mass and friction is still done on the CPU <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />.

SHAC differentiates through the simulator and was able to train more than 17 times faster than the best established RL on muscle-driven locomotion <Cite id="shac-2022" />. Schwarke and colleagues report the first sim-to-real transfer for legged locomotion using a policy trained exclusively in a differentiable simulator <Cite id="schwarke-2024" />. For Newton, the main backend states "Differentiability via Warp is not yet available" <Cite id="mujoco-warp-repo-2026" />.

Also: per-solver gradients in Newton <Cite id="newton-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" />, beta <Term id="system-identification">system identification</Term> in Isaac Sim 6.1 <Cite id="isaac-sim-6-1-release-notes-2026" />, Drake as the "gold standard" for rigorous numerics <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" />.
