The locomotion and manipulation asymmetry is the pattern in which simulation-trained reinforcement learning (RL) made legged locomotion robust well before general manipulation. In 2021 an ANYmal quadruped learned to walk on flat ground in under four minutes, and on uneven terrain in twenty, on one workstation GPU <Cite id="rudin-2021" />.

## The asymmetry is in the MDP

Legged RL was mainly limited to simulation until Hwangbo and colleagues transferred simulation-trained policies to ANYmal in 2019 <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. Lee and colleagues' proprioceptive controller, trained on much simpler simulated terrain, proved robust in natural environments <Cite id="lee-2020" />. RMA needs no reference trajectories, trains on a terrain generator and deploys on an A1 without fine-tuning <Cite id="rma-2021" />. Radosavovic and colleagues' causal transformer over proprioceptive history carried the recipe to humanoids zero-shot <Cite id="radosavovic-2023" />, and an off-policy recipe now trains humanoid locomotion in 15 minutes on one RTX 4090 <Cite id="seo-2025" />.

Boston Dynamics says RL helps where conditions are easy to simulate but good strategies are hard to describe <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />.

The 2026 reality-gap survey notes that most simulators assume perfectly rigid bodies while real objects deform, and that perfect fidelity is computationally infeasible <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />. Rudin and colleagues' locomotion policy observes <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term> and 108 terrain-height samples around the base, and because ANYmal's series-elastic actuators are hard to model in simulation, they learn an actuator network <Cite id="rudin-2021" />. For manipulation, the survey says mismatches from simplified contact models are particularly problematic in contact-rich tasks, where precise interaction modeling is crucial <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />. It also lists photorealistic rendering among the open sim-to-real challenges <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />. Tang and colleagues trace RL's difficulty in [robotics](/rl-sim2real/rl-for-robotics) to the cost of interacting with the physical world <Cite id="tang-drl-survey-2025" />.

Locomotion papers read "<Term id="ppo">PPO</Term>, <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term>, <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term>." Manipulation papers often use real demonstrations, which need no contact model. Diffusion Policy beat prior methods by 46.9% on average across 12 tasks <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, and ACT learned to slot a battery at 80 to 90% success from 10 minutes of [demonstrations](/manipulation/bc-foundations) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## Six properties of the two MDPs

Boston Dynamics builds Atlas from rotary actuators that are "much easier to represent well in simulation" <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />. NVIDIA's SimReady asset standard specifies mass, friction, inertia, collision and articulation limits <Cite id="nvidia-simready-2026" />. Isaac Lab calls multi-fingered manipulation harder than parallel-jaw grasping because of its high-dimensional actions and fine control, and names accurate contact modeling, domain randomization and tiled rendering as support <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. Lin and colleagues note that sim-to-real dexterous RL remains largely limited to single-hand or state-based setups <Cite id="lin-humanoid-sim2real-2025" />.

## Contact geometry is where the gap lives

A gait recovers from a wrong contact force on the next step; a jammed peg cannot. Acosta and colleagues call dynamic contact the computational bottleneck for most simulations; three tested simulators missed elastic impacts <Cite id="acosta-2021" />. Rigid-body contact models have a task-specific accuracy ceiling and hard-to-interpret parameters <Cite id="fazeli-2017" />. Factory simulates 1000 nut-and-bolt interactions simultaneously and calls assembly contact a longstanding challenge <Cite id="factory-2022" />.

## Exceptions in manipulation

OpenAI's in-hand policy learned finger gaiting without human demonstrations <Cite id="openai-dactyl-2018" />. Its Rubik's-cube policy, trained with automatic domain randomization for a modified Shadow hand, completed one fixed 43-subgoal scramble in two of ten trials with a sensor cube, and zero of ten with vision for face angles <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" />. Visual Dexterity reorients novel shapes in a median of about seven seconds on hardware under \$5,000 <Cite id="visual-dexterity-2022" />. DextrAH-RGB [transfers](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/) an end-to-end RGB grasping policy <Cite id="dextrah-rgb-2024" />, distilled from a privileged teacher into a stereo-RGB student for a KUKA arm and Allegro hand <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />.

AutoMate's generalist solves 20 assemblies and transfers zero-shot <Cite id="automate-2024" />. Play2Perfect reaches 60% on 0.5 mm-clearance insertions zero-shot <Cite id="play2perfect-2026" />. InsertAnything trained one policy only on a simulated hexagonal insertion, and it succeeded on 95.0% of trials across eight unseen real insertion tasks <Cite id="insertanything-2026" />. In simulation, a single insertion world model trained on up to 90 tasks reached 56% zero-shot success on unseen parts, against 7% for a model-free baseline <Cite id="insertion-world-models-2026" />. SPARR adds a real-world residual, raising success 38.4% over zero-shot transfer <Cite id="sparr-2026" />.

Such results often need per-task engineering. The cube system used customized hardware, tuned torque limits and a calibrated hand simulation <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" />. Zero-shot force-controlled dexterous grasping needed tactile simulation, current-to-torque calibration and actuator models <Cite id="zhao-force-grasp-2026" />. SimToolReal trains one policy over procedural tools that matches specialist policies and transfers zero-shot across 24 real tasks <Cite id="simtoolreal-2026" />. DoorMan's RGB humanoid policy opens doors up to 31.7% faster than teleoperators <Cite id="doorman-2025" />. Boston Dynamics says Atlas learned to lift a fridge with perception done implicitly from proprioception <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

<span id="the-honest-snapshot-comes-from-the-imitation-side" />
## Imitation-trained baselines

Physical Intelligence reports its π0.5 model cleaning kitchens and bedrooms in entirely new homes <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. TRI finds that multi-task pretraining makes policies more successful and teaches new tasks with a fraction of the data <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 reports 32% to 92% success on multi-finger tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />, and [humanoid whole-body control](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc/) gives its full results. RL-100's real-world RL on diffusion policies succeeded in 1000 of 1000 episodes <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />.
