Simulation-trained reinforcement learning (RL) made legged locomotion robust well before general manipulation, because walking is cheap to simulate and contact-rich manipulation is not. In 2021 an ANYmal quadruped learned to walk on flat ground in under four minutes, and on uneven terrain in twenty, on one workstation GPU <Cite id="rudin-2021" />.

## The asymmetry is in the MDP

Legged RL left simulation in 2019, when Hwangbo and colleagues transferred trained policies to ANYmal <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. Lee and colleagues' proprioceptive controller then held up in natural environments <Cite id="lee-2020" />, RMA ran on an A1 without fine-tuning <Cite id="rma-2021" />, and an off-policy recipe now trains humanoid locomotion in 15 minutes on one RTX 4090 <Cite id="seo-2025" />.

Boston Dynamics says RL helps where conditions are easy to simulate but good strategies are hard to describe <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />. Rudin and colleagues' policy observes <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term> and 108 terrain-height samples, and a learned network models ANYmal's series-elastic actuators, which are hard to simulate <Cite id="rudin-2021" />. Contact-rich manipulation is harder: the 2026 reality-gap survey notes that most simulators assume rigid bodies while real objects deform, that perfect fidelity is computationally infeasible, and that simplified contact models are particularly problematic in contact-rich tasks <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />. Tang and colleagues blame the cost of physical interaction for RL's difficulty in [robotics](/rl-sim2real/rl-for-robotics) <Cite id="tang-drl-survey-2025" />.

Locomotion papers combine <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term>, <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term> and a <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term>. Manipulation papers more often learn from real demonstrations, which need no contact model: Diffusion Policy beat prior methods by 46.9% on average across 12 tasks <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, and ACT learned to slot a battery at 80 to 90% success from 10 minutes of [demonstrations](/manipulation/bc-foundations) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## Six properties of the two MDPs

<MdpComparison className="my-6 wide-measure" />

Boston Dynamics builds Atlas from rotary actuators that are "much easier to represent well in simulation" <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />, and NVIDIA's SimReady asset standard specifies mass, friction, inertia, collision and articulation limits <Cite id="nvidia-simready-2026" />. Isaac Lab calls multi-fingered manipulation harder than parallel-jaw grasping <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />, and Lin and colleagues find sim-to-real dexterous RL still largely limited to single-hand or state-based setups <Cite id="lin-humanoid-sim2real-2025" />.

## Contact geometry is where the gap lives

A gait recovers from a wrong contact force on the next step; a jammed peg cannot. Acosta and colleagues call dynamic contact the computational bottleneck for most simulations, and three simulators they tested missed elastic impacts <Cite id="acosta-2021" />. Rigid-body contact models have a task-specific accuracy ceiling <Cite id="fazeli-2017" />, and Factory, which simulates 1000 nut-and-bolt interactions at once, still calls assembly contact a longstanding challenge <Cite id="factory-2022" />.

## Exceptions in manipulation

OpenAI's in-hand policy learned finger gaiting without human demonstrations <Cite id="openai-dactyl-2018" />. Its Rubik's-cube successor, trained with automatic domain randomization, solved one fixed 43-subgoal scramble in two of ten trials with a sensor cube and zero of ten with vision for face angles, on customized hardware with tuned torque limits and a calibrated hand simulation <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" />. Visual Dexterity reorients novel shapes in a median of about seven seconds on hardware under \$5,000 <Cite id="visual-dexterity-2022" />, and DextrAH-RGB [transfers](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/) an end-to-end RGB grasping policy <Cite id="dextrah-rgb-2024" />, distilled from a privileged teacher into a stereo-RGB student for a KUKA arm and Allegro hand <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />.

<CubeTrials className="my-6" />

In assembly, AutoMate's generalist solves 20 assemblies zero-shot <Cite id="automate-2024" />, Play2Perfect reaches 60% on 0.5 mm-clearance insertions <Cite id="play2perfect-2026" />, and InsertAnything, trained only on a simulated hexagonal insertion, succeeded on 95.0% of trials across eight unseen real insertion tasks <Cite id="insertanything-2026" />. SPARR's real-world residual adds 38.4% success over zero-shot transfer <Cite id="sparr-2026" />.

Such results often need per-task engineering: zero-shot force-controlled dexterous grasping needed tactile simulation, current-to-torque calibration and actuator models <Cite id="zhao-force-grasp-2026" />. SimToolReal instead trains one policy over procedural tools that transfers zero-shot across 24 real tasks <Cite id="simtoolreal-2026" />, and Boston Dynamics says Atlas learned to lift a fridge with perception done implicitly from proprioception <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

## Imitation-trained baselines

Physical Intelligence reports π0.5 cleaning kitchens and bedrooms in entirely new homes <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, and TRI finds that multi-task pretraining raises success and cuts the data a new task needs <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 reports 32% to 92% success on multi-finger tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />; [humanoid whole-body control](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc/) has the full results.
