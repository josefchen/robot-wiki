Levine and colleagues name its central difficulty: a learned value function can overrate actions the data never tested, and no live environment corrects it <Cite id="offline-rl-tutorial-2020" />.

## The fixed-data bargain

<Term id="offline-reinforcement-learning">Offline RL</Term> learns from a static set of transitions `(observation, action, reward, next observation, termination)` produced by one or more behavior policies <Cite id="offline-rl-tutorial-2020" />. Value learning bootstraps, so error from actions outside the data accumulates through the Bellman backup <Cite id="bear-2019" />. For robots, support also spans controller mode, action magnitude, contact regime and task stage, and Real-ORL, built on over 6,500 trajectories from 800 robot hours, found that offline RL and imitation prefer different action spaces <Cite id="real-orl-2022" />.

## Audit the dataset before choosing a method

### Behavior coverage

Map each episode's source (expert, novice, scripted controller, older policy or exploration) and its actions by task stage. D4RL builds its datasets from hand-designed controllers, human demonstrators, multitask data and policy mixtures <Cite id="d4rl-2020" />.

### Outcome coverage

The reward must separate successes, partial progress and failures, though a completion label can suffice: COG chained four robot skills under a +1/0 reward, from prior data that never solved the new task <Cite id="cog-2020" />.

### Transition integrity

Check timestamps, episode boundaries, terminations and action units. Mishandled time limits cause state aliasing and invalidate experience replay <Cite id="time-limits-2017" />.

### Deployment overlap

Judge support in the policy's input representation and in physical operating variables, where a camera or control-rate change can outweigh the task label. PTR learned new tasks in a new domain on a real WidowX from as few as 10 demonstrations after offline RL pretraining <Cite id="ptr-2022" />. The [datasets guide](/data-hardware/datasets) and the [robot-learning stack](/data-hardware/robot-learning-stack) cover selection and provenance.

## Four ways to remain close to evidence

Offline-RL methods differ in how they keep improvement away from unsupported values, and Park and colleagues find the policy-extraction step often matters more than the value objective <Cite id="park-bottleneck-2024" />.

### Conservative value estimation

Conservative Q-Learning penalizes high values on actions outside the dataset while preserving observed ones <Cite id="cql-2020" />. RankQ's authors argue that such pessimism anchors policies to suboptimal data, and their action ranking raised a VLA's real cube-stacking success from 43.1% to 88.9% <Cite id="rankq-2026" />.

### Avoiding unseen-action queries

Implicit Q-Learning fits a state value by expectile regression over dataset actions and extracts a policy by advantage weighting, so value fitting never queries a new action <Cite id="iql-2022" />.

### Regularizing the policy toward behavior

TD3+BC adds a cloning term and state normalization to an off-policy actor-critic and matches more elaborate algorithms <Cite id="td3-bc-2021" />.

### Discretizing and scaling the value function

QT-Opt trained a Q-function on over 580,000 real grasp attempts and reached 96% success on unseen objects <Cite id="qt-opt-2018" />. With datasets up to 1000 times the usual size, many offline RL algorithms still saturate well short of maximum performance, and long horizons form a fundamental barrier <Cite id="horizon-reduction-2025" />.

## When behavior cloning is the better tool

Behavior cloning is the default for clean expert demonstrations: it fits the action labels and has no bootstrapped critic. Robomimic found recurrent cloning a strong baseline on human demonstrations, where batch-RL methods struggled <Cite id="robomimic-2021" />, and Kumar and colleagues favor offline RL for sparse rewards, suboptimal or noisy data, and long horizons with composable trajectories <Cite id="offline-rl-vs-bc-2022" />.

Neither method repairs missing support. Over 240 hours of demonstrations and over 3,000 rollouts, Robo-ValueRL ties success to value reliability, and reliable values let offline RL scale better than quality-agnostic cloning <Cite id="robo-valuerl-2026" />. ## A six-step evaluation protocol

Do not choose checkpoints by the Q-values they maximize. Paine and colleagues found offline RL algorithms not robust to hyperparameters, yet able to rank policies reliably once the algorithm and Q-estimation method are controlled <Cite id="paine-hparam-2020" />.

### 1. Lock the data split by deployment factor

Hold out environments, objects, operators or periods to match the intended generalization; random transition splits leak adjacent frames. Kumar and colleagues adapt cross-validation to tune offline RL without online evaluation <Cite id="offline-rl-workflow-2021" />.

### 2. Establish three baselines

Use cloning, a recurrent cloner when history matters, and the simplest regularized offline-RL method. RvS's conditioned two-layer MLP competes with TD and transformer methods but is weak on random data <Cite id="rvs-2021" />.

### 3. Report support-aware diagnostics

Report action distance from the data, critic disagreement, saturation rate and per-source performance; EDAC, for one, penalizes out-of-distribution actions through Q-ensemble uncertainty <Cite id="edac-2021" />.

### 4. Use simulation only where it is predictive

Simulation ranks close checkpoints only where its link to hardware is measured, as SIMPLER did with paired real evaluations <Cite id="simpler-2024" />. SCORE limits simulated RL to the support of a real-data policy, raising eight real dexterous tasks from 37.8% to 89.9% average success, against 59.5% for the best baseline <Cite id="score-2026" />.

<Image id="simpler-visual-matching-2024" />

### 5. Run blinded physical trials

Fix the protocol before policy identities are known, randomize conditions, and count interventions, collisions and resets, as TRI did <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

### 6. Keep a fixed canary set

Run one repeatable physical suite for every candidate and keep the episode IDs; the TriFinger benchmark offers a reproducible real-hardware setup <Cite id="trifinger-offline-2023" />.

## From a fixed dataset to controlled online improvement

RLPD mixes offline data into off-policy online learning without separate conservative pretraining <Cite id="rlpd-2023" />. Conservative critics fine-tune poorly, so Cal-QL calibrates CQL with a one-line change and leads on 9 of 11 benchmarks <Cite id="cal-ql-2023" />. Retaining offline data in replay is a safe default, though WSRL shows it is unnecessary with a well-designed online method <Cite id="wsrl-2024" />.

Keep action bounds and command validation outside the learned policy, define interventions and resets, and compare each promotion with the frozen offline checkpoint. PA-RL fine-tuned the 7B OpenVLA with Cal-QL from 40% to 70% real-world success in 40 minutes <Cite id="pa-rl-2024" />. LWD applies offline-to-online RL to a pretrained VLA across a fleet of 16 dual-arm robots and reports 95% average success over eight tasks <Cite id="lwd-2026" />. The [RL fine-tuning guide](/manipulation/rl-finetuning) covers this phase.

## A compact go/no-go checklist

Run an offline-RL experiment only when actions, rewards, terminations and timing are trustworthy, the data holds behavior that cloning should not imitate uniformly, and deployment lies inside the data's support, since generalization to out-of-support states is often a big barrier <Cite id="park-bottleneck-2024" />.
