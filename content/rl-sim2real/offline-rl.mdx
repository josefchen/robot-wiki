<Term id="offline-reinforcement-learning">Offline reinforcement learning</Term> trains a robot policy from a fixed log of past experience, with no new trials. It promises to reuse robot data that already exists. Its central difficulty is that a learned estimate of how good an action is can overrate actions the data never tried, and nothing corrects it <Cite id="offline-rl-tutorial-2020" />.

## The fixed-data bargain

The log is a list of transitions: what the robot observed, what it did, the reward, what it saw next and whether the episode ended, recorded from one or more earlier policies <Cite id="offline-rl-tutorial-2020" />. Values are learned by bootstrapping, each estimate built from the next, so an error on an action outside the data spreads through every estimate that depends on it <Cite id="bear-2019" />. For robots, "outside the data" also covers controller mode, action size, contact type and task stage. Real-ORL, built on over 6,500 trajectories from 800 robot hours, found that offline RL and imitation prefer different action spaces <Cite id="real-orl-2022" />.

## What makes a dataset usable

Four properties decide whether a log can support offline RL. The first is behavior coverage: knowing who produced each episode, such as an expert, a scripted controller or an older policy. D4RL builds its benchmark datasets from such mixtures <Cite id="d4rl-2020" />.

The second is outcome coverage, rewards that separate success, partial progress and failure. Completion labels can be enough: COG chained picking, placing, drawer opening and grasping under a reward of 1 only on completion, from prior data that never solved the new task <Cite id="cog-2020" />. Learned reward models help: Robometer, trained on over a million trajectories including failures, supplies dense rewards for offline RL on mixed-quality data <Cite id="robometer-2026" />; in RoboReward's simulated tests reward accuracy correlated with downstream RL performance at 0.83 <Cite id="roboreward-2026" />; and NEEDLEWORK adds verified short bridges between recorded motions <Cite id="needlework-2026" />.

The third is clean transitions, with correct timestamps, episode boundaries and units; mishandled time limits make different states look identical and invalidate replay <Cite id="time-limits-2017" />. The fourth is overlap with deployment, where a new camera or control rate can matter more than the task name. With it, offline RL pretraining let PTR learn new tasks on a real WidowX arm from as few as 10 demonstrations <Cite id="ptr-2022" /> ([datasets](/data-hardware/datasets), [robot-learning stack](/data-hardware/robot-learning-stack)).

## Four ways to stay close to the evidence

Methods differ in how they avoid trusting unsupported values, and Park and colleagues found that how a policy is extracted from the values often matters more than how the values are learned <Cite id="park-bottleneck-2024" />.

| Approach | Example | Idea and result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pessimism | CQL (2020) | Pushes down values of actions missing from the data <Cite id="cql-2020" /> |
| Ranking instead of pessimism | RankQ (2026) | Ranks actions so the policy is not anchored to poor data; a VLA's real cube stacking rose from 43.1% to 88.9% <Cite id="rankq-2026" /> |
| Never query unseen actions | IQL (2022) | Learns values only from actions in the data <Cite id="iql-2022" /> |
| Values over action chunks | CO-RFT (2025), Q-chunking (2025) | Learn values for short sequences of actions; CO-RFT fine-tuned a VLA from 30 to 60 demonstrations <Cite id="co-rft-2025" /> <Cite id="q-chunking-2025" /> |
| Pull toward the data | TD3+BC (2021) | Adds an imitation term to ordinary RL and matches more complex methods <Cite id="td3-bc-2021" /> |
| One-step actor | Flow Q-Learning (2025) | Trains a fast one-step policy beside an expressive one <Cite id="flow-q-learning-2025" /> |
| Scale the value function | QT-Opt (2018), Q-Transformer (2023) | QT-Opt reached 96% grasp success on unseen objects from over 580,000 real attempts <Cite id="qt-opt-2018" />; Q-Transformer predicts values one action dimension at a time <Cite id="q-transformer-2023" /> |

With datasets up to 1,000 times the usual size, many offline algorithms still saturate well below the best possible performance, and long tasks are a fundamental barrier <Cite id="horizon-reduction-2025" />.

## When behavior cloning is the better tool

For clean expert demonstrations, behavior cloning, which simply fits the demonstrated actions, is usually the better default. Robomimic found a cloning baseline with memory strong on human demonstrations, where offline RL methods struggled <Cite id="robomimic-2021" />. Kumar and colleagues favor offline RL for sparse rewards, suboptimal or noisy data, and long tasks that can be stitched from pieces of different trajectories <Cite id="offline-rl-vs-bc-2022" />.

<TrajectoryStitching className="my-6" />

Neither method repairs missing data. Across 240 hours of demonstrations and over 3,000 rollouts, Robo-ValueRL tied success to reliable values, which let offline RL scale better than cloning that ignores data quality <Cite id="robo-valuerl-2026" />. SeeQ shortens what its values must predict by learning them per annotated subtask <Cite id="seeq-2026" />. Physical Intelligence's RECAP conditions a VLA on how much better than average each action was <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />, yet a November 2026 chess study on an SO-101 arm found that this style of conditioning, filtered cloning on the robot's own rollouts and residual RL gave no measurable gain over plain cloning <Cite id="chess-vla-adaptation-2026" />. The [behavior-cloning guide](/manipulation/bc-foundations) covers the baseline.

## Evaluating without trusting the critic

A learned value function cannot grade itself, so checkpoints chosen for the highest predicted values mislead. Paine and colleagues found offline RL sensitive to its settings, yet able to rank policies reliably when the algorithm and value-estimation method are held fixed <Cite id="paine-hparam-2020" />. Kumar and colleagues adapted cross-validation to tune offline RL without online trials <Cite id="offline-rl-workflow-2021" />, with data held out by environment, object or operator, since random splits leak neighboring frames.

Simple baselines matter. A small conditioned network, RvS, competes with more complex methods but is weak on random data <Cite id="rvs-2021" />, and EDAC uses an ensemble of value estimates to penalize uncertain actions <Cite id="edac-2021" />. Simulation helps only where its link to hardware has been measured, as in SIMPLER's paired real evaluations <Cite id="simpler-2024" />. SCORE keeps simulated RL within what a real-data policy would do and raised eight real dexterous tasks from 37.8% to 89.9% average success, against 59.5% for the best baseline <Cite id="score-2026" />.

<Image id="simpler-visual-matching-2024" />

Physical trials remain the final test: protocols fixed before policy identities are known, randomized conditions and counted interventions, as TRI did <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />, and repeatable hardware suites such as TriFinger's <Cite id="trifinger-offline-2023" />.

## From a fixed dataset to online improvement

Offline RL is increasingly a warm start for online learning. RLPD mixes offline data into online learning without separate pessimistic pretraining <Cite id="rlpd-2023" />. Pessimistic critics fine-tune poorly, so Cal-QL calibrates CQL with a one-line change and led on 9 of 11 benchmarks <Cite id="cal-ql-2023" />. WSRL showed that keeping the offline data during fine-tuning is unnecessary with a well-designed online method <Cite id="wsrl-2024" />, and another study found that naively pretrained values often add little over random initialization <Cite id="dong-q-pretrain-2026" />. Offline logs can also guide exploration: a bonus for reaching states with many options helped on five simulated task and dataset pairs and matched the baseline elsewhere <Cite id="offline-empowerment-2026" />.

On real robots, PA-RL fine-tuned OpenVLA with Cal-QL from 40% to 70% success in 40 minutes <Cite id="pa-rl-2024" />, and Q2RL extracts values from a cloned policy with a few interactions, then switches between cloned and RL actions <Cite id="q2rl-2026" />. LWD reports 95% average success over eight tasks on 16 two-armed robots <Cite id="lwd-2026" />, Value as Memory gained 32.8% over matched offline training on long tasks <Cite id="value-as-memory-2026" />, and BORA updates only a small residual network on a frozen VLA from online rollouts, corrections and offline data <Cite id="bora-2026" /> ([RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning)).

## When offline RL is worth trying

Offline RL earns its complexity when actions, rewards, endings and timing in the log are trustworthy, when the data holds behavior that should not all be imitated equally, and when deployment stays within what the data covers, since behavior in unseen states is often the larger barrier <Cite id="park-bottleneck-2024" />.
