## Domain randomization makes reality one more variation

<Term id="domain-randomization">Domain randomization</Term> trains on many perturbed simulators so that the real world looks like one more variation. In 1995 Jakobi and colleagues found that transfer depended on the simulation's noise level <Cite id="jakobi-1995" />. Tobin and colleagues randomized textures, lighting and camera pose, and a network trained only on those images located objects to about 1.5 cm in real webcam images <Cite id="tobin-2017" />.

Peng and colleagues randomized the physics instead, from link mass to sensor noise, over [that range of worlds](/how-robots-work/#worlds). A recurrent policy trained only in simulation then pushed a puck to targets with a real Fetch arm <Cite id="peng-2018" />, and later theory showed why memory of recent history matters <Cite id="chen-dr-theory-2021" />.

OpenAI's robot hand randomized friction and appearance and transferred to the physical hand <Cite id="openai-dactyl-2018" />. Its Rubik's-cube successor widened the ranges as the policy succeeded, a <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" />.

<Image id="mujoco-playground-franka-block-2025" />

Isaac Lab, the [GPU-parallel training framework](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl), ships randomization as a configurable curriculum <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. Poorly tuned ranges "can lead to overly conservative policies" <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />.

## Teacher-student distillation: train with privileges, deploy without

<Term id="teacher-student-distillation">Teacher-student distillation</Term> splits learning in two. A teacher learns with privileged simulator information, such as exact terrain, and a student that sees only the robot's sensors learns to imitate it. Learning by Cheating used this for driving <Cite id="learning-by-cheating-2019" />, after asymmetric actor-critic gave full state only to the critic <Cite id="asymmetric-ac-2017" />.

Lee and colleagues' student read two seconds of <Term id="proprioception">proprioceptive</Term> history and walked ANYmal through mud, snow and rubble <Cite id="lee-2020" />.

| The student sees | Task | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Depth camera | Stairs and stepping stones | <Cite id="agarwal-vision-2022" /> |
| Stereo camera | Grasping with a KUKA arm and Allegro hand | <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| Camera | Rebar insertion at 1.4 mm clearance | 91.3% of real rollouts <Cite id="rebarsim-2026" /> |
| Camera | Humanoid carrying while walking | 54 cycles, on 64 GPUs <Cite id="viral-2025" /> |

RMA adds an estimator of current conditions. On the A1 it updates 10 times a second from half a second of history, so the robot adapts in under a second with no fine-tuning <Cite id="rma-2021" />. UP-OSI earlier fed dynamics parameters predicted from recent history into one universal policy <Cite id="up-osi-2017" />.

## System identification: measure the gap instead

Randomization averages over model error; <Term id="system-identification">system identification</Term> measures it. Hwangbo and colleagues fitted a neural network to real data from ANYmal's elastic actuators. With ideal or analytical actuator models, policies could not take a single step without falling <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />.

| Work | What it identifies | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tan et al. | An actuator model, paired with randomization | <Cite id="tan-quadruped-2018" /> |
| Active exploration | Test motions that reveal parameters fastest | 42 to 63% better than baselines on locomotion <Cite id="spi-active-2025" /> |
| Miller et al. | Spot's simulator, tuned against hardware data | <Cite id="miller-spot-2025" /> |
| Fey et al. | Complex actuation without torque sensors | <Cite id="fey-athletic-2025" /> |
| HALO | An unknown payload's mass on a humanoid | <Cite id="halo-payload-2026" /> |
| CUE | Contact dynamics, probed where the simulation is least accurate | 72% lower billiard-ball error than the best baseline in simulation; ran on a real pool table <Cite id="cue-dynamics-2026" /> |

ASAP corrects the simulator with what the robot did. It records a Unitree G1 running policies trained on <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> human motion, learns a correction added before each simulator step, and fine-tunes the policy against the corrected simulator. Tracking error fell from 61.2 to 50.2 mm on a kick and from 159 to 112 mm on LeBron James's "Silencer" celebration <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

HumanoidNDM fine-tunes G1 tracking online without motion capture, through a dynamics model learned from the robot's own sensing <Cite id="humanoid-ndm-2026" />. Swift's champion-level drone racing also combined simulated RL with real data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

## Real-to-sim: rebuild the scene, keep the physics

Real-to-sim methods rebuild the deployment scene in simulation, usually as photo-realistic Gaussian splats, and keep a standard physics engine. Diffusion policies trained only on SplatSim's simulated demonstrations reached 86.25% real success on four UR5 tasks, against 97.5% with real data <Cite id="splatsim-2024" />.

| System | What it builds | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RialTo (2024) | Digital twins from small amounts of real data, for RL | Over 67% more robust imitation policies <Cite id="rialto-2024" /> |
| PolaRiS (2025) | Test environments from short video scans | Tracks real generalist-policy performance better than benchmarks <Cite id="polaris-2025" /> |
| Soft-body splats (2025) | Deformable twins from real videos | Simulated rollouts correlate strongly with real ones <Cite id="softbody-splat-eval-2025" /> |
| SimFoundry (2026) | Automated scene generation | 0.911 mean correlation with real results, 7 tasks, 5 policy types <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" /> |
| ZeroBot (2026) | Meshes from a single image | 87% real success after 119 seconds of training on average <Cite id="zerobot-2026" /> |
| AutoRSR (2026) | Assets from scanned workspaces | 73.3 to 86.7% real success, comparable to π0.5 on 150 demonstrations <Cite id="autorsr-2026" /> |
| RoboRender (2026) | Photorealistic video from simulated trajectories | 71% average, about 3.6 times plain visual randomization <Cite id="roborender-2026" /> |
| ForceTwin (2026) | Friction and springs of doors and other articulated objects | 87% goal completion on nine object and robot pairs <Cite id="forcetwin-2026" /> |
| GRAIL (2026) | Generated scenes for humanoids; a CoRL 2026 best-paper finalist <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" /> | 84% real pick-up success on a Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" /> |

NVIDIA's Newton 1.0 renders meshes and splats in one camera <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" />. A rebuilt twin can still mislead: a ring-toss model placed 90% of rings on the real UR5 but 30% in RoboGSim <Cite id="robogsim-2024" />. In a 3D-scanned lab, navigation agents' simulated and real success correlated at only 0.18, and tuned simulation parameters raised it to 0.844 <Cite id="kadian-2019" />.

RoboSimStudio lets people and coding agents edit a simulation's solver, contact model and physical parameters. Agents built a two-armed sponge-wiping simulation from a smartphone video in about an hour, and a policy improved there completed the wipe on the real robot <Cite id="robosimstudio-2026" />.

## Data mix and hardware also decide transfer

Mixing simulated with real data raised real-world performance by 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />, mainly by aligning how the policy represents both <Cite id="lei-cotraining-2026" />. RL in simulation anchored on real data raised real success by 24% for OpenVLA and 20% for π0.5 <Cite id="rl-co-2026" />. The action space "plays a crucial role in reducing the sim-to-real gap" <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />.

| Lever | Example | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stronger regularization | Regularized actor-critic | More robust transfer than PPO <Cite id="smm-ac-2026" /> |
| Simulation-aware updates and an action integrator | IndustReal | Assembly transferred to hardware <Cite id="industreal-2023" /> |
| An identified motor-energy model | Bjelonic et al. | Three main and ten further legged robots, no dynamics randomization <Cite id="bjelonic-sim2real-2025" /> |
| High-frequency control and targeted randomization | DeepMind soccer humanoid | <Cite id="haarnoja-soccer-2023" /> |
| Actuators shaped to act like the simulator's | Actuator reality shaping | <Cite id="actuator-reality-shaping-2026" /> |
| Joint gains picked from actuator models | ZEST | Skills onto Atlas zero-shot <Cite id="zest-2026" /> |
| A model that matches the hardware | Atlas | Fewer fidelity issues, per Boston Dynamics <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" /> |
