## Domain randomization makes reality one more variation

<Term id="domain-randomization">Domain randomization</Term> refers to randomly perturbing aspects of the simulator during training such that the real environment becomes one more instance of perturbed dynamics or appearance. In 1995 Jakobi and colleagues observed that sim-to-real transfer was dependent on the amount of noise added in simulation <Cite id="jakobi-1995" />. Tobin and colleagues randomized texture, illumination and camera pose, and showed that an object localization network trained only on those images could localize objects to about 1.5 cm in real webcam images <Cite id="tobin-2017" />.

Peng and colleagues randomized the physics instead, from link mass to sensor noise, over [that range of worlds](/how-robots-work/#worlds). A recurrent policy was learned purely in simulation and then pushed a puck to targets with a real Fetch arm <Cite id="peng-2018" />. Subsequent theoretical work showed why it's important for the policy to maintain a memory of recent history <Cite id="chen-dr-theory-2021" />.

Randomizing the appearance and friction of OpenAI's robot hand allowed a policy trained in simulation to transfer to the physical hand <Cite id="openai-dactyl-2018" />. In their follow-up work on learning to solve a Rubik's cube, OpenAI increases the randomization ranges as the policy improves, a <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" />.

<Image id="mujoco-playground-franka-block-2025" />

Isaac Lab, the [GPU-parallel training framework](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl), includes domain randomization as a configurable curriculum <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. Poorly tuned ranges "can lead to overly conservative policies" <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />.

## Teacher-student distillation: train with privileges, deploy without

<Term id="teacher-student-distillation">Teacher-student distillation</Term> splits learning in two. A teacher learns with privileged simulator information, such as exact terrain, and is then distilled into a student policy that only has access to onboard sensing. This idea was used for driving in Learning by Cheating <Cite id="learning-by-cheating-2019" />, after asymmetric actor-critic, where only the critic is allowed to see the true state <Cite id="asymmetric-ac-2017" />.

Lee and colleagues used a student policy with two seconds of <Term id="proprioception">proprioceptive</Term> input history to walk ANYmal over mud, snow and rubble <Cite id="lee-2020" />.

| The student sees | Task | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Depth camera | Stairs and stepping stones | <Cite id="agarwal-vision-2022" /> |
| Stereo camera | Grasping with a KUKA arm and Allegro hand | <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| Point cloud | COWBOY steers a mobile base, arm and hand to grasp objects | Averaged 91% successful grasps across 24 real scenes <Cite id="cowboy-grasping-2026" /> |
| Camera | Rebar insertion at 1.4 mm clearance | 91.3% of real rollouts <Cite id="rebarsim-2026" /> |
| Camera | Humanoid carrying while walking | 54 cycles, on 64 GPUs <Cite id="viral-2025" /> |

RMA estimates relevant properties of the current environment from sensory history. On the A1, the environment estimator receives half a second of history and updates 10 times per second, allowing the robot to adapt in less than a second without fine-tuning <Cite id="rma-2021" />. UP-OSI earlier learned to predict parameters of a dynamics model from recent history and used them to condition one universal policy <Cite id="up-osi-2017" />.

## System identification: measure the gap instead

Another approach is to directly quantify model errors through <Term id="system-identification">system identification</Term> instead of averaging them out through randomization. Hwangbo and colleagues use a neural network learned from ANYmal's real data to model its elastic actuator dynamics. They show that RL policies trained with ideal or analytical actuator models fail to even take a step on the real robot without falling <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />.

| Work | What it identifies | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tan et al. | An actuator model, paired with randomization | <Cite id="tan-quadruped-2018" /> |
| Active exploration | Test motions that reveal parameters fastest | 42 to 63% better than baselines on locomotion <Cite id="spi-active-2025" /> |
| Miller et al. | Spot's simulator, tuned against hardware data | <Cite id="miller-spot-2025" /> |
| Fey et al. | Complex actuation without torque sensors | <Cite id="fey-athletic-2025" /> |
| HALO | An unknown payload's mass on a humanoid | <Cite id="halo-payload-2026" /> |
| CUE | Contact dynamics, probed where the simulation is least accurate | 72% lower billiard-ball error than the best baseline in simulation; ran on a real pool table <Cite id="cue-dynamics-2026" /> |

ASAP corrects the simulator with what the robot did. It records a Unitree G1 running policies trained on <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> human motion, and uses these data to learn a correction term that is added before each simulation step. The motion tracking policy is then fine-tuned in the corrected simulator. Tracking error fell from 61.2 mm to 50.2 mm on a kick motion and from 159 mm to 112 mm on LeBron James's "Silencer" celebration <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

HumanoidNDM fine-tunes G1 humanoid tracking online without using motion capture by learning a dynamics model using onboard sensing <Cite id="humanoid-ndm-2026" />. Swift's champion-level drone racing also combined simulated RL with real data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

## Real-to-sim: rebuild the scene, keep the physics

Real-to-sim focuses on reconstructing real scenes (typically as photo-realistic Gaussian splats) in simulators with conventional physics engines. Diffusion policies trained purely on simulated demonstrations in SplatSim transfer to the real world, achieving a success rate of 86.25% on four manipulation tasks using a UR5 arm (97.5% when trained with real data) <Cite id="splatsim-2024" />.

| System | What it builds | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RialTo (2024) | Digital twins from small amounts of real data, for RL | Over 67% more robust imitation policies <Cite id="rialto-2024" /> |
| PolaRiS (2025) | Test environments from short video scans | Tracks real generalist-policy performance better than benchmarks <Cite id="polaris-2025" /> |
| Soft-body splats (2025) | Deformable twins from real videos | Simulated rollouts correlate strongly with real ones <Cite id="softbody-splat-eval-2025" /> |
| SimFoundry (2026) | Automated scene generation | 0.911 mean correlation with real results, 7 tasks, 5 policy types <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" /> |
| ZeroBot (2026) | Meshes from a single image | 87% real success after 119 seconds of training on average <Cite id="zerobot-2026" /> |
| AutoRSR (2026) | Assets from scanned workspaces | 73.3 to 86.7% real success, comparable to π0.5 on 150 demonstrations <Cite id="autorsr-2026" /> |
| RoboRender (2026) | Photorealistic video from simulated trajectories | 71% average, about 3.6 times plain visual randomization <Cite id="roborender-2026" /> |
| ForceTwin (2026) | Friction and springs of doors and other articulated objects | 87% goal completion on nine object and robot pairs <Cite id="forcetwin-2026" /> |
| GRAIL (2026) | Generated scenes for humanoids; a CoRL 2026 best-paper finalist <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" /> | 84% real pick-up success on a Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" /> |

NVIDIA's Newton 1.0 simulator renders meshes and Gaussian splats from one camera <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" />. A rebuilt twin can still mislead: a ring-toss policy only achieved a 30% success rate in RoboGSim, compared to 90% on the real UR5 arm <Cite id="robogsim-2024" />. When tested in a lab environment reconstructed via 3D scanning, the correlation between real and simulated success rates for navigation policies was only 0.18, improving to 0.844 after simulator parameter tuning <Cite id="kadian-2019" />.

In RoboSimStudio, both humans and coding agents can modify the solver, contact model and physical parameters of simulators. Agents were able to recreate a two-armed sponge-wiping task in simulation from a smartphone video in about an hour, and a policy improved there completed the wipe on the real robot <Cite id="robosimstudio-2026" />.

## Data mix and hardware also decide transfer

Blending simulated trajectories with real ones improved downstream policy performance in the real world by 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />, primarily because it encourages compatible policy representations <Cite id="lei-cotraining-2026" />. RL training in simulation anchored on real data improves real success by 24% and 20% for OpenVLA and π0.5 respectively <Cite id="rl-co-2026" />. Other work has emphasized the importance of designing the action space, noting that it "plays a crucial role in reducing the sim-to-real gap" <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />.

| An identified motor-energy model | Bjelonic et al. | Three main and ten further legged robots, no dynamics randomization <Cite id="bjelonic-sim2real-2025" /> |
| High-frequency control and targeted randomization | DeepMind soccer humanoid | <Cite id="haarnoja-soccer-2023" /> |
| Actuators shaped to act like the simulator's | Actuator reality shaping | <Cite id="actuator-reality-shaping-2026" /> |
| Joint gains picked from actuator models | ZEST | Skills onto Atlas zero-shot <Cite id="zest-2026" /> |
| A model that matches the hardware | Atlas | Fewer fidelity issues, per Boston Dynamics <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" /> |
