Sim-to-real transfer is getting a policy trained in simulation to work on the physical robot. Its four method families are <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term>, <Term id="teacher-student-distillation">teacher-student distillation</Term>, <Term id="system-identification">system identification</Term> and real-to-sim reconstruction. Aljalbout and colleagues' survey covers all four, splitting methods that reduce the gap from those that overcome it <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />.

## Domain randomization: train on a distribution of simulators

Jakobi and colleagues found in 1995 that sim-to-real correspondence depended on how much noise the simulation used, with very good results at realistic levels <Cite id="jakobi-1995" />. Tobin and colleagues randomized textures, lighting and camera pose for tabletop object localization. After a small hyperparameter search, their best network localized objects to about 1.5 cm, a summary over 480 webcam images of eight geometric objects in object-only, distractor and partial-occlusion conditions. Most runs used ImageNet initialization, though random initialization nearly matched it given enough simulated data, and a Fetch robot used the detector with a motion planner. Their abstract frames the real world as "just another variation" once the simulator varies enough <Cite id="tobin-2017" />.

RoboRender, from Jiajun Wu, Li Fei-Fei (Stanford) and colleagues (October 2026), turns simulated trajectories into photorealistic videos for policy training; its policies average 71% real-world success across pick-and-place, articulated-object and mobile manipulation, about 7.1 times raw simulation renderings and 3.6 times conventional visual domain randomization <Cite id="roborender-2026" />.

Peng and colleagues randomized link mass, joint damping, puck mass, friction and damping, table height, controller gains, action timing and observation noise, maximizing expected return over that dynamics distribution. A recurrent policy trained only in simulation pushed a puck with a seven-DoF Fetch arm. Real trials used motion capture, 200 control steps per episode, start and target positions randomized within a 30 cm square, and success within 7 cm at the end. The authors report substantial joint-trajectory mismatch after limited calibration and ablate their randomization choices <Cite id="peng-2018" />.

OpenAI's 2018 hand policy randomized friction coefficients and object appearance and transferred to the physical robot <Cite id="openai-dactyl-2018" />. Its Rubik's-cube successor added the <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> version: automatic domain randomization widens ranges as the policy succeeds <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" />. Isaac Lab, the framework from the [previous module](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl), ships ADR as a configurable curriculum with reference configs in its dexterous-manipulation examples. In Isaac Lab, simulation state is GPU-resident, but mass, friction, contact offsets and joint armature change through PhysX CPU APIs. Most parameters randomize at runtime, mesh scale and collider type only before simulation starts, and CPU parameter writes can occur beyond episode resets <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />.

SPI-Active notes that poorly tuned randomization "can lead to overly conservative policies" <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />, and Chen and colleagues bound the gap between a randomized policy and the real-world optimum, stressing history-dependent policies <Cite id="chen-dr-theory-2021" />. Boston Dynamics reports that Atlas's fridge policy, trained for 50 to 70 pound loads, moved a fridge of over 100 pounds <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

## Teacher-student distillation: privilege in, proprioception out

Teacher-student training splits sensing from control: a teacher learns from privileged simulator state, then a student with deployable sensing imitates it. Learning by Cheating used this two-stage form for driving <Cite id="learning-by-cheating-2019" />, after asymmetric actor-critic had given only the critic full state <Cite id="asymmetric-ac-2017" />.

Lee and colleagues distilled a privileged RL teacher, which saw simulator-only terrain and contact information, into a temporal convolutional student via its actions and latent features. The student uses two seconds of <Term id="proprioception">proprioceptive</Term> history from joint encoders and an IMU, plus the command and current state, with no terrain map. Trained on rigid procedural terrain, one controller per robot generation took ANYmal-B and ANYmal-C robots through mud, snow, rubble and vegetation without environment-specific tuning <Cite id="lee-2020" />.

Agarwal and colleagues distilled a teacher trained on a cheap depth variant into a depth-camera student <Cite id="agarwal-vision-2022" />, and Isaac Lab's DextrAH-RGB example distills a privileged KUKA and Allegro teacher into a stereo-RGB network <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. VIRAL's authors report that scaling to tens of GPUs, up to 64, made teacher and student training reliable, and their RGB humanoid ran up to 54 loco-manipulation cycles <Cite id="viral-2025" />.

RebarSim, from Abhishek Gupta (University of Washington) and colleagues (September 2026), distills an RL teacher trained over procedurally generated rebar into a student that sees multi-view RGB and proprioception. The student seated real factory rebars at 1.4 mm clearance in 91.3% of zero-shot rollouts, and removing appearance randomization or the DAgger mixture sharply lowered success <Cite id="rebarsim-2026" />.

RMA trains a base policy and an environment-factor encoder jointly with RL, then regresses an adaptation module on simulated state-action histories to predict the latent extrinsics. At deployment the base policy maps current state, previous action and estimated extrinsics to desired joint positions. The module reads 50 state-action steps (0.5 seconds) and updates asynchronously at about 10 Hz on the A1, while the base policy runs at 100 Hz on the latest estimate, with no online gradient updates. The paper reports adaptation in fractions of a second without real-world fine-tuning, and failures after large perturbations or multiple leg obstructions. Its latent encodes behavior-relevant conditions without guaranteeing each physical parameter <Cite id="rma-2021" />. UP-OSI had earlier fed dynamics parameters predicted from recent history into a universal policy <Cite id="up-osi-2017" />.

Isaac Lab calls the failure mode an information gap due to input mismatch: the student must reconstruct unobserved states from partial observations such as images, hardest under heavy camera occlusion <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. The teacher-student panel illustrates this with authored terrain and noise, so its errors rise by construction.

## System identification: measure the gap instead

Randomization averages over model error, and system identification measures it. Hwangbo and colleagues fitted an actuator network for ANYmal's series-elastic actuators to real excitation data and used it in simulation to train policies. It predicts torque from joint-position errors and velocities now and 0.01 and 0.02 seconds earlier. Policies trained with ideal or analytical actuator models failed to take a single step without falling <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. Tan and colleagues paired an identified actuator model and simulated latency with randomization, perturbations and a compact observation space <Cite id="tan-quadruped-2018" />.

Newer methods identify parameters directly. SPI-Active samples them in parallel with Fisher-information-driven excitation and beats baselines by 42 to 63% in locomotion tasks <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />. Miller and colleagues score Spot simulation against hardware with Wasserstein distance and maximum mean discrepancy and tune it with CMA-ES <Cite id="miller-spot-2025" />. HALO calibrates a humanoid and then its payload in differentiable MuJoCo XLA <Cite id="halo-payload-2026" />. ZEST selects joint gains from approximate analytical armature values for closed-chain actuators and adds a refined actuator model; its policies, trained entirely in simulation, deploy zero-shot on Atlas, the Unitree G1 and Spot <Cite id="zest-2026" />.

Marco Hutter (ETH Zurich) and colleagues apply identification to the objects a robot handles in ForceTwin (September 2026). A person probes a door or another articulated object with a handheld force-sensing gripper, and the system fits inertia, friction, damping and a neural residual for configuration-dependent forces such as door closers. It nearly halves the inertial-parameter error of a vision-language-model prior, and whole-body door-traversal policies trained in its twins were deployed on real robots <Cite id="forcetwin-2026" />.

ASAP pretrains phase-conditioned tracking policies in IsaacGym on <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> human motions, runs them on a Unitree G1, and records trajectories with motion capture and onboard sensors. A second PPO policy maps simulated state and recorded action to a delta action added before the simulator step, rewarded for matching the recorded real trajectory, so no residual labels are needed. The tracking policy is fine-tuned with the frozen delta model in simulation, and only the tracking policy is deployed. For the hardware study the delta covers only four ankle DoFs of the 23-DoF model <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-40"><Cite id="asap-2025" /></span>.

Evaluation separates IsaacGym-to-IsaacSim and IsaacGym-to-Genesis tests, with open-loop replay and closed-loop tracking over 43 motion sequences grouped by difficulty, from the G1 study. SysID and delta dynamics are simulation baselines, and ASAP does not win every metric: in the easy IsaacSim closed-loop group its mean global joint-position error is 106 mm against SysID's 105 mm. On hardware, compared only with Vanilla, the error falls from 61.2 to 50.2 mm on kicking and from 159 to 112 mm on LeBron James's "Silencer". Pretraining uses basic domain randomization, and the random-action-noise ablation is a Genesis test <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

ASAP reports better tracking on the held-out "Silencer" motion and better out-of-distribution replay with more data, without establishing coverage of arbitrary unseen skills. Its stated hardware limits are motor overheating and damage, motion-capture dependence and the data needed for a full 23-DoF delta model <Cite id="asap-2025" />. Swift's champion-level drone racing likewise combined simulation RL with physical-world data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

## Real-to-sim: rebuild the scene, keep the physics

SplatSim replaces mesh rendering with Gaussian splats reconstructed from the deployment scene, while PyBullet supplies physics. Across four UR5 tasks with 40 trials each, diffusion policies trained on its simulated demonstrations reached 86.25% zero-shot real success, against 97.5% for real-data policies, with training augmentations included <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-32"><Cite id="splatsim-2024" />.</span>

The setup uses a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and two RealSense D455 cameras. Splat preparation needs manual robot segmentation, CAD-derived link bounds and ICP alignment, and robot kinematics and simulated object poses drive rendering. The setup figure lists RGB plus <Term id="end-effector">end-effector</Term> pose as inputs, while the problem statement says the policy uses only RGB at test time <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-32"><Cite id="splatsim-2024" />.</span>

RoboGSim's August 2025 revision chains a Gaussian Reconstructor, Digital Twins Builder, Scene Composer and Interactive Engine. Multi-view images and robot MDH parameters feed reconstruction, mesh assets and measured layout connect it to Isaac Sim, and a synthesizer composes novel views, objects, scenes and trajectories. Novel-pose rendering and trajectory replay are evaluated separately. Closed-loop policies act on splat renders while Isaac Sim handles kinematics and collisions. On UR5 ring-toss, ten trials with up to three grasps each, the real-data model placed 90% on the real robot but 30% in RoboGSim, so its scores cannot replace real-robot tests <Cite id="robogsim-2024" />.

Newer twins go beyond static appearance. RialTo builds digital twins from small amounts of real data and runs RL in them, raising imitation-policy robustness by over 67% <Cite id="rialto-2024" />. Zhang and colleagues build soft-body splat twins from video whose rollouts correlate strongly with real execution <Cite id="softbody-splat-eval-2025" />. PolaRiS turns short video scans into interactive environments that track real generalist-policy performance better than existing benchmarks <Cite id="polaris-2025" />. SimFoundry builds sim-ready twins from a video and edits them into digital cousins; across 7 tasks and 5 policy architectures its simulated evaluations correlated with real results at a mean Pearson coefficient of 0.911 <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />. GRAIL generates humanoid interaction data from 3D assets and video-model priors, and visual policies trained only on it reached 84% success on object pick-up and 90% on stair-climbing on a real Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" />. NVIDIA's Newton 1.0 post describes a Warp-based tiled camera sensor that ray-traces both triangle meshes and Gaussian splats <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-24"><Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" /></span>.

A rebuilt twin can still mislead. Kadian and colleagues measured a sim-versus-real success correlation of 0.18 in a 3D-scanned lab because agents exploited collision dynamics, and tuning simulation parameters raised it to 0.844 <Cite id="kadian-2019" />.

## Levers behind successful transfer

The survey also covers co-training with real-world data, noting its benefit for robotic foundation models such as GR00T N1. It says the action space "plays a crucial role in reducing the sim-to-real gap" across navigation, locomotion and manipulation, citing joint-velocity control for manipulation, and that high-frequency impedance controllers can improve manipulation transfer <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />. Co-training has since become a headline recipe. Maddukuri and colleagues report that simulation data raises real-world performance by 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />, and Lei and colleagues identify structured representation alignment as its primary effect <Cite id="lei-cotraining-2026" />.

Deployed systems lean on such levers. Haarnoja and colleagues' soccer humanoid transferred through high-frequency control, targeted dynamics randomization and training perturbations <Cite id="haarnoja-soccer-2023" />. IndustReal's assembly transfer used simulation-aware policy updates and a deployment-time action integrator <Cite id="industreal-2023" />. Actuator reality shaping makes physical actuators match the simulator's idealized dynamics <Cite id="actuator-reality-shaping-2026" />, and Boston Dynamics reports fewer fidelity issues because Atlas's model and hardware are extremely close <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

## Where each family breaks

The two interactives construct their costs; measured costs come from the sources. Poorly tuned randomization can yield overly conservative policies <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />. Students depend on reconstructing hidden state <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> and on compute <Cite id="viral-2025" />. ASAP's broader skill coverage and affordable real-robot data collection remain open <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-4"><Cite id="asap-2025" /></span>. Rebuilt twins can reward simulator exploits <Cite id="kadian-2019" />.
