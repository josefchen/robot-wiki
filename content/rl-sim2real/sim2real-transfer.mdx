Sim-to-real transfer is getting a policy trained in simulation to work on the physical robot, through four families of methods: <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term>, <Term id="teacher-student-distillation">teacher-student distillation</Term>, <Term id="system-identification">system identification</Term> and real-to-sim reconstruction. Aljalbout and colleagues' survey covers all four, splitting methods that reduce the gap from those that overcome it <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />.

## Domain randomization: train on a distribution of simulators

Jakobi and colleagues found in 1995 that sim-to-real correspondence depended on how much noise the simulation used <Cite id="jakobi-1995" />. Tobin and colleagues randomized textures, lighting and camera pose for tabletop object localization; their best network localized objects to about 1.5 cm over 480 webcam images of eight geometric objects, though most runs used ImageNet initialization (random initialization nearly matched it given enough simulated data), and their abstract frames the real world as "just another variation" once the simulator varies enough <Cite id="tobin-2017" />.

Peng and colleagues randomized link mass, joint damping, friction, controller gains, action timing and observation noise, maximizing expected return over [that dynamics distribution](/how-robots-work/#worlds). A recurrent policy trained only in simulation pushed a puck with a seven-DoF Fetch arm, with start and target positions randomized within a 30 cm square <Cite id="peng-2018" />.

<Image id="mujoco-playground-franka-block-2025" />

OpenAI's 2018 hand policy randomized friction coefficients and object appearance and transferred to the physical robot <Cite id="openai-dactyl-2018" />. Its Rubik's-cube successor added a <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> version, automatic domain randomization, which widens ranges as the policy succeeds <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" />.

Isaac Lab, the [GPU-parallel training framework](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl), ships ADR as a configurable curriculum, but mass, friction, contact offsets and joint armature change through PhysX CPU APIs. Most parameters randomize at runtime, mesh scale and collider type only before simulation starts <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. SPI-Active notes that poorly tuned randomization "can lead to overly conservative policies" <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />.

## Teacher-student distillation: privilege in, proprioception out

Teacher-student training splits sensing from control: a teacher learns from privileged simulator state, then a student with deployable sensing imitates it. Learning by Cheating used this two-stage form for driving <Cite id="learning-by-cheating-2019" />, after asymmetric actor-critic had given only the critic full state <Cite id="asymmetric-ac-2017" />.

Lee and colleagues distilled a privileged RL teacher, which saw simulator-only terrain and contact information, into a temporal convolutional student via its actions and latent features. The student uses two seconds of <Term id="proprioception">proprioceptive</Term> history, plus the command and current state, and one controller per robot generation took ANYmal-B and ANYmal-C robots through mud, snow, rubble and vegetation without environment-specific tuning <Cite id="lee-2020" />. Isaac Lab's DextrAH-RGB example distills a privileged KUKA and Allegro teacher into a stereo-RGB network <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />, and VIRAL's authors report that scaling to tens of GPUs, up to 64, made teacher and student training reliable for an RGB humanoid that ran up to 54 loco-manipulation cycles <Cite id="viral-2025" />.

RMA trains a base policy and an environment-factor encoder jointly with RL, then regresses an adaptation module on simulated state-action histories to predict the latent extrinsics. The module reads 50 state-action steps (0.5 seconds) and updates asynchronously at about 10 Hz on the A1, while the base policy runs at 100 Hz on the latest estimate, with no online gradient updates. The paper reports adaptation in fractions of a second without real-world fine-tuning, and failures after large perturbations or multiple leg obstructions; its latent encodes behavior-relevant conditions without guaranteeing each physical parameter <Cite id="rma-2021" />. UP-OSI had earlier fed dynamics parameters predicted from recent history into a universal policy <Cite id="up-osi-2017" />.

Isaac Lab calls the failure mode an information gap due to input mismatch: the student must reconstruct unobserved states from partial observations such as images, hardest under heavy camera occlusion <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />. The teacher-student panel illustrates this with authored terrain and noise, so its errors rise by construction.

## System identification: measure the gap instead

Randomization averages over model error, and system identification measures it. Hwangbo and colleagues fitted an actuator network for ANYmal's series-elastic actuators to real excitation data, predicting torque from joint-position errors and velocities now and 0.01 and 0.02 seconds earlier; policies trained with ideal or analytical actuator models failed to take a single step without falling <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. Tan and colleagues paired an identified actuator model and simulated latency with randomization <Cite id="tan-quadruped-2018" />. SPI-Active samples parameters in parallel with Fisher-information-driven excitation and beats baselines by 42 to 63% in locomotion tasks <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />, and Miller and colleagues tune Spot simulation against hardware with CMA-ES <Cite id="miller-spot-2025" />.

ASAP pretrains phase-conditioned tracking policies in IsaacGym on <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> human motions, runs them on a Unitree G1, and records trajectories with motion capture and onboard sensors. A second PPO policy learns a delta action added before the simulator step, the tracking policy is fine-tuned with the frozen delta model in simulation, and only the tracking policy is deployed. For the hardware study the delta covers only four ankle DoFs of the 23-DoF model <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

Evaluation separates IsaacGym-to-IsaacSim and IsaacGym-to-Genesis tests, with open-loop replay and closed-loop tracking over 43 motion sequences, and ASAP does not win every metric: in the easy IsaacSim closed-loop group its mean global joint-position error is 106 mm against SysID's 105 mm. On hardware, compared only with Vanilla, the error falls from 61.2 to 50.2 mm on kicking and from 159 to 112 mm on LeBron James's "Silencer" <Cite id="asap-2025" />. ASAP reports better tracking on the held-out "Silencer" motion without establishing coverage of arbitrary unseen skills, and its stated hardware limits are motor overheating and damage, motion-capture dependence and the data needed for a full 23-DoF delta model <Cite id="asap-2025" />. Swift's champion-level drone racing likewise combined simulation RL with physical-world data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

## Real-to-sim: rebuild the scene, keep the physics

SplatSim replaces mesh rendering with Gaussian splats reconstructed from the deployment scene, while PyBullet supplies physics. Across four UR5 tasks with 40 trials each, diffusion policies trained on its simulated demonstrations reached 86.25% zero-shot real success, against 97.5% for real-data policies, with training augmentations included <Cite id="splatsim-2024" />.

The setup uses a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and two RealSense D455 cameras, and splat preparation needs manual robot segmentation, CAD-derived link bounds and ICP alignment. The setup figure lists RGB plus <Term id="end-effector">end-effector</Term> pose as inputs, while the problem statement says the policy uses only RGB at test time <Cite id="splatsim-2024" />.

RoboGSim's August 2025 revision chains a Gaussian Reconstructor, Digital Twins Builder, Scene Composer and Interactive Engine, where mesh assets and measured layout connect it to Isaac Sim. Novel-pose rendering and trajectory replay are evaluated separately. Closed-loop policies act on splat renders while Isaac Sim handles kinematics and collisions. On UR5 ring-toss, ten trials with up to three grasps each, the real-data model placed 90% on the real robot but 30% in RoboGSim, so its scores cannot replace real-robot tests <Cite id="robogsim-2024" />.

Newer twins go beyond static appearance: RialTo runs RL in digital twins built from small amounts of real data, raising imitation-policy robustness by over 67% <Cite id="rialto-2024" />, and PolaRiS turns short video scans into environments that track real generalist-policy performance better than existing benchmarks <Cite id="polaris-2025" />. SimFoundry's simulated evaluations correlated with real results at a mean Pearson coefficient of 0.911 across 7 tasks and 5 policy architectures <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />, and NVIDIA's Newton 1.0 post describes a tiled camera sensor that ray-traces both triangle meshes and Gaussian splats <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" />.

A rebuilt twin can still mislead. Kadian and colleagues measured a sim-versus-real success correlation of 0.18 in a 3D-scanned lab because agents exploited collision dynamics, and tuning simulation parameters raised it to 0.844 <Cite id="kadian-2019" />.

## Levers behind successful transfer

The survey says the action space "plays a crucial role in reducing the sim-to-real gap", citing joint-velocity control for manipulation, and that co-training with real-world data benefits robotic foundation models such as GR00T N1 <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />. Maddukuri and colleagues report that simulation data raises real-world performance by 38% on average <Cite id="sim-real-cotraining-2025" />, and Lei and colleagues identify structured representation alignment as its primary effect <Cite id="lei-cotraining-2026" />.

Deployed systems lean on such levers: Haarnoja and colleagues' soccer humanoid transferred through high-frequency control, targeted dynamics randomization and training perturbations <Cite id="haarnoja-soccer-2023" />. Actuator reality shaping makes physical actuators match the simulator's idealized dynamics <Cite id="actuator-reality-shaping-2026" />, and Boston Dynamics reports fewer fidelity issues because Atlas's model and hardware are extremely close <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.
