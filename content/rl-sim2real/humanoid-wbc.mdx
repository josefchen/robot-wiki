Humanoid whole-body control is the control of every actuated joint of a humanoid, from legs to hands, as one coupled problem. A dominant paradigm treats <Term id="whole-body-control">whole-body control</Term> as reinforcement-learned tracking of human motion <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> to the robot <Cite id="omniretarget-2025" />, though retargeted motion can be physically infeasible <Cite id="h2o-2024" />.

## The tracking lineage

DeepMimic adapted standard RL to imitate motion clips, including retargeted ones, for animated characters and an Atlas robot model <Cite id="deepmimic-2018" />.

<Image id="motion-capture-session-hyderabad-2026" />

PHC tracks motion and recovers from falls for simulated avatars, with no physical robot: its controller imitates 98.9% of 11,313 filtered AMASS training clips, and PMCP trains new primitives on progressively harder failed clips <Cite id="phc-2023" />.

On the Unitree H1, H2O's privileged motion-filtering imitator is "similar to PHC" and follows PULSE <Cite id="pulse-2023" />. A second policy learns whole-body tracking from the cleaned retargeted set with domain randomization and follows human keypoints estimated from an RGB camera, but the deployed <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> system, a joint-target policy over a PD controller, also uses robot-side motion capture for linear velocity <Cite id="h2o-2024" />.

OmniH2O's student, distilled from a privileged RL teacher, tracks head-and-hand goals from proprioceptive history with no explicit global-linear-velocity input, though root odometry still maps goals into the robot frame <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-18"><Cite id="omnih2o-2024" /></span>. A visual policy trained on teleoperated demonstrations can drive the tracker, evaluated on four of six recorded tasks at ten runs each, and the authors report no safety guarantees for extreme disturbances or out-of-distribution goals <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-32"><Cite id="omnih2o-2024" /></span>.

HumanPlus estimates the operator's body and hand poses with one RGB camera, and its Humanoid Shadowing Transformer, trained with PPO on retargeted motion in simulation, runs zero-shot on a customized H1 from IMU and joint encoders. Two head-mounted cameras record the robot's view during shadowing, and supervised <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> then trains a Humanoid Imitation Transformer from binocular images and proprioception; occlusion, fixed retargeting and limited locomotion remain constraints <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />.

ExBody2 tracks local keypoints with separate velocity tracking, because drifting global keypoint targets can accumulate tracking errors. It filters references by an initial policy's lower-body tracking error and distills a privileged PPO teacher into a history-based student that walks, squats and dances on Unitree G1, though transitions between specialists remain unresolved <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-5"><Cite id="exbody2-2024" /></span>. ASAP, the delta-action method in the [sim-to-real module](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer), fine-tunes against a simulator corrected from real G1 rollouts for jumps and sports-style motions <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-24"><Cite id="asap-2025" /></span>.

KungfuBot trains one policy per processed reference motion and tightens the tracking reward's error tolerance as training proceeds, never loosening it; its quantitative hardware test is ten Tai Chi trials with the G1 root fixed for evaluation <Cite id="kungfubot-2025" />. GMT instead trains a single tracking controller, distilled from a soft mixture-of-experts teacher whose gating network combines expert actions; its baselines and ablations run in simulation, and it neither gets up after falls nor targets slopes and stairs <Cite id="gmt-2025" />. Learned get-up policies lift a real G1 from lying face up or face down <Cite id="humanup-2025" />.

BeyondMimic learns cartwheels, spin-kicks, flip-kicks and sprinting with one setup and shared hyperparameters and transfers zero-shot to hardware <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" />. ZEST trains motion-imitation policies with RL from motion capture, monocular video and animation, and deploys them zero-shot on Atlas, the Unitree G1 and Spot <Cite id="zest-2026" />.

## Three decompositions ship in 2026

HOVER distills full-body kinematic motion imitation across control modes into one policy <Cite id="hover-2024" />. Figure, NVIDIA and Google DeepMind each shipped whole-body control in 2026 with the learning boundary in a different place.

### A unified motion-tracking policy: Figure Helix 02 S0

Figure announced Helix 02 on 27 January 2026. It keeps the tracking decomposition: S2 reasons over scenes and language and emits latent goals; S1, a 200 Hz visuomotor transformer, maps head and palm cameras, fingertip tactile sensors and full-body <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term> to whole-body joint targets; and S0, a 10M-parameter controller at 1 kHz, turns joint state and base motion into actuator commands. S0 trained on over 1,000 hours of joint-level retargeted human motion and sim-to-real RL across more than 200,000 parallel environments, and the headline demo is a 4-minute dishwasher unload and reload <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Figure says it later conditioned S0 on camera perception <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />, and reports that Helix 2.5, pretrained from random initialization on its Index human-behavior dataset, succeeded on 56% of blind zero-shot trials in 30 unseen homes, against 9% when trained from scratch <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

### A VLA above a learned controller: GR00T + GEAR-SONIC

NVIDIA keeps the VLA and whole-body controller as separate learned systems, and released GR00T N1.7 as early access on 18 April 2026 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-7-release-2026" />. GR00T N1.7 is a 3B-parameter VLA with a Cosmos-Reason2-2B backbone and a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action head; with the `UNITREE_G1_SONIC` embodiment it predicts latent action tokens that the GEAR-SONIC controller, running at 50 Hz, decodes into joint commands for legs, arms and hands <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="groot-wbc-2026" />. SONIC (Science Robotics) scales tracking from 1.2M to 42M parameters on 700 hours of motion capture, with one token space serving VR teleoperation and VLAs <Cite id="sonic-2025" />. Code is under Apache 2.0 and weights under the NVIDIA Open Model License, and every checkpoint loads the gated Cosmos-Reason2-2B backbone <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

Qiayuan Liao, Pieter Abbeel, Koushil Sreenath and colleagues at UC Berkeley (October 2026) train a tracker with analytic gradients through short rollouts of a differentiable world model fit to logged simulator transitions <Cite id="supermimic-2026" />. Their SuperMimic passed the return of the released SONIC checkpoint after 3 hours on one H100 GPU, where SONIC trained for 7 days on 128 L40 GPUs, and failed on 25 of its 129,514 training motions against 179 for SONIC <Cite id="supermimic-2026" />. The authors note that the GPU models differ and that SuperMimic trained on the public part of SONIC's data, about 2.3 times less motion <Cite id="supermimic-2026" />.

### One VLA, feet to fingertips: Gemini Robotics 2

Google DeepMind puts everything in one model: its 30 July 2026 announcement reports that Gemini Robotics 2 controls full humanoids "from feet to fingertips", fetching and shelving a watering can on Apptronik's Apollo 2. One checkpoint runs Apollo 2 with SharpaWave hands, Apollo 2 with Inspire hands and the Franka Duo with Robotiq grippers, and DeepMind reports adapting to new bi-arm robots in a few hours, with fewer than 200 examples in most cases <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## Gemini Robotics 2 results

DeepMind's whole-body and gripper rates average several tasks per skill category; multi-finger rates are per task. Whole-body picking on Apollo 2 with Inspire hands averages 76.3% from a shelf, 68.4% from a table and 45.7% from the floor, against 89.6% for precise gripper insertion on the Franka Duo. Multi-finger dexterity on the SharpaWave hand spans 92% for unscrewing a bulb to 32% for a dustpan, and DeepMind says multi-finger dexterous manipulation "remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## What tracking does not solve

A tracker does not choose its own motion. Zhang and colleagues pair a terrain-aware diffusion motion generator with a tracker fine-tuned on its imperfect references, crossing boxes, hurdles and stairs on a G1 <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" />. VIRAL's simulation-trained RGB policy ran continuous loco-manipulation for up to 54 cycles on a G1 <Cite id="viral-2025" />, and Boston Dynamics reports that a policy trained for 50 to 70 pound loads moved a fridge of over 100 pounds <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

Yutang Lin, Guanya Shi and C. Karen Liu at Amazon FAR, Carnegie Mellon and Stanford (October 2026) plan in the latent command space of a frozen behavior foundation model, which closes the balance loop at 50 Hz while their FIBER planner replans at 5 Hz <Cite id="fiber-2026" />. FIBER projects separately trained policies for box pickup, door opening and grasping into that space and trains one perceptive planner on their rollouts; across 128 simulated variations per task it succeeded 42.0% of the time, against 11.6% for replaying the projected latents, 3.5% for a kinematic planner and 95.4% for the task experts <Cite id="fiber-2026" />. Trained only in simulation, FIBER succeeded in 6, 4 and 2 of 10 real G1 trials on the three tasks <Cite id="fiber-2026" />.

Zeji Yi, Guanya Shi and colleagues at Carnegie Mellon and Amazon FAR (October 2026) split collision avoidance between a perception-conditioned diffusion policy that proposes whole-body motions and an RL controller that tracks them and keeps balance <Cite id="swap-2026" />. Their SWAP labels training data in hindsight, placing obstacles only where an executed motion did not sweep <Cite id="swap-2026" />. Across five simulated tasks it averaged 88.3% success with test-time steering, 72.4% without it and 17.4% when the diffusion output drove the joints directly <Cite id="swap-2026" />. On a real G1, with motion capture for localization and the moving obstacles, it succeeded in 18 of 25 trials; the authors trace the failures to LiDAR occlusion and tracking mismatch <Cite id="swap-2026" />.

HITTER pairs a planner that predicts the ball and picks the strike with an RL whole-body controller, and reached up to 106 consecutive table-tennis shots against a human <Cite id="hitter-2025" />. Chris Paxton of Agility Robotics wrote in October 2026 that many such systems still see the ball through external motion trackers, which leaves onboard perception as the limit <Cite id="paxton-robot-sports-2026" />.

Retargeting is lossy: artifacts such as foot sliding and self-penetration significantly reduce tracking-policy robustness, particularly for dynamic or long sequences <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />, and noisy, inconsistent transferred references motivated a 2026 robustness method with a fall-recovery curriculum <Cite id="robust-tracking-2026" />.

Nicholas Ioannidis, Xue Bin Peng and colleagues at Simon Fraser University, FieldAI and NVIDIA (October 2026) replace the weighted objectives of retargeting with a priority order: foot placement first, then contacts with objects or other characters, then the remaining pose objectives <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. On two-person interactions from Inter-X their PriMoRetarget kept 78.8% of source hand contacts, and PPO policies trained on its output tracked the motions on a simulated Unitree G1 93.6% of the time <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. The strongest of three baselines, PHC, reached 35.2% and 72.8%; GMR and OmniRetarget scored lower <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. Collaborative manipulation by several robots stayed harder, at 62.6% tracking success <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />.

Chaitanya Chawla, Kartik Sharma, Guanya Shi and colleagues at Carnegie Mellon (October 2026) check reachability during generation: their EmbodyHOI steers a frozen generator of human hand-object motion with a differentiable inverse-kinematics cost for the target robot while it samples <Cite id="embodyhoi-2026" />. On the Unitree G1 this cut the wrist error left after retargeting from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm, and simulated policies trained on the guided references succeeded on 79% of 50 randomly drawn tasks, against 68% for unguided references <Cite id="embodyhoi-2026" />. Fixing the arms with inverse kinematics after generation dropped success on a four-task suite to 32.8%, against 79.7% with guidance, which costs 32 times an unguided sample <Cite id="embodyhoi-2026" />.
