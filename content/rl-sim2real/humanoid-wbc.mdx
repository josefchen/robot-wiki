## How motion tracking works

A person's movement already shows how to shift weight while stepping and reaching. In motion tracking, people are recorded with motion capture, each clip is mapped onto the robot's skeleton, and <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term> in simulation trains a policy to follow the clip without falling. H2O first dropped clips that a simulated imitator could not follow, since some ask for moves the robot cannot make <Cite id="h2o-2024" /> <Cite id="pulse-2023" />.

DeepMimic (2018) used the method on animated characters and a simulated Atlas <Cite id="deepmimic-2018" />. PHC (2023) imitated 98.9% of 11,313 clips from AMASS, an archive that unifies 15 motion-capture datasets <Cite id="amass-2019" />, and its simulated avatars recovered from falls <Cite id="phc-2023" />. The payoff is a reusable motor layer: one HOVER policy takes a walking velocity or arm joint angles without retraining <Cite id="hover-2024" />, and a human operator or a vision-language-action model can drive the same SONIC controller <Cite id="sonic-2025" />.

## From animation to real robots

From 2024 labs moved trackers onto real humanoids, mostly Unitree's H1 and G1. An early use was <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term>: H2O let a person drive an H1 by moving in front of an ordinary camera <Cite id="h2o-2024" />. HumanPlus used such shadowing to collect demonstrations, then trained the robot by <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> to put on a shoe and walk or fold a sweatshirt alone, at 60 to 100% success from up to 40 demonstrations per task <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />.

Most systems train by <Term id="teacher-student-distillation">teacher-student distillation</Term>: a teacher learns in simulation with information a real robot cannot sense, and a student learns to copy it from the robot's own joint readings <Cite id="omnih2o-2024" /> <Cite id="exbody2-2024" /> <Cite id="gmt-2025" />. The remaining question was breadth. KungfuBot trained one policy per motion; its hardware test was ten Tai Chi trials with the robot's base held fixed <Cite id="kungfubot-2025" />; BeyondMimic ran cartwheels, flip-kicks and sprints from one shared setup on real hardware without fine-tuning <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" />.

## Three ways to split the work

A tracker keeps the robot upright but does not decide what to do. On the simulated HumanoidBench tasks, a hierarchy built on robust low-level skills such as walking beat standard reinforcement learning <Cite id="humanoidbench-2024" />, and LeVERB and WholeBodyVLA put a vision-language model above a learned controller <Cite id="leverb-2025" /> <Cite id="wholebodyvla-2025" />. In 2026 Figure, NVIDIA and Google DeepMind shipped whole-body systems that draw the line between deciding and balancing in three places.

### Figure Helix 02: three layers

In Figure's Helix 02 (January 2026), S2 interprets the scene and the instruction; S1 turns camera views, fingertip touch and body sensing into whole-body joint targets 200 times a second; S0, a network of 10 million parameters, sends motor commands 1,000 times a second. S0 learned from over 1,000 hours of retargeted human motion in more than 200,000 parallel simulated worlds, and its showcase is a four-minute dishwasher unload and reload <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Figure later gave S0 camera input <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />. Helix 2.5, pretrained on Figure's Index dataset of human behavior, succeeded on 56% of blind trials in 30 unfamiliar homes, against 9% when trained from scratch <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

### NVIDIA GR00T and SONIC: a VLA steering a separate controller

GR00T N1.7, in early access since 18 April 2026 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-7-release-2026" />, is a 3-billion-parameter <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> (VLA) whose <Term id="latent-action">latent action</Term> tokens NVIDIA's separate GEAR-SONIC controller decodes 50 times a second into joint commands for legs, arms and hands <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="groot-wbc-2026" />. SONIC, scaled from 1.2 million to 42 million parameters on 700 hours of motion capture, improved steadily with more compute and more varied data <Cite id="sonic-2025" />.

GR00T 2 (November 2026) keeps the split: its 2-billion-parameter Turbo version predicts future video and actions together and steers SONIC from the G1's onboard computer, at 124 milliseconds per prediction. NVIDIA reports it served popcorn in all 48 trials and cleared a table in 93% <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

### Google DeepMind Gemini Robotics 2: one model, feet to fingertips

Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 (30 July 2026) controls Apptronik's Apollo 2 "from feet to fingertips", fetching and shelving a watering can, and the same checkpoint runs Apollo 2 with a second hand type and a two-armed Franka Duo with grippers. DeepMind reports adapting to new two-armed robots in a few hours, usually from fewer than 200 examples <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Gemini Robotics 2.5 (10 November 2026) reports 30 to 100% higher success than earlier baselines across 200 tasks, and 96% kitting car parts in a proof of concept on Mercedes-Benz production lines <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />.

## Whole-body skills still trail gripper skills

On Apollo 2 with Inspire hands, picking objects averaged 76.3% success from a shelf, 68.4% from a table and 45.7% from the floor, against 89.6% for precise insertion with grippers on the Franka Duo. These rates average several tasks per category. Single-task rates on the SharpaWave hand ranged from 92% for unscrewing a light bulb to 32% for using a dustpan, and DeepMind says multi-finger manipulation "remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## A tracker needs a planner

A tracker only follows motion. Something else must invent motion that fits the task, and letting that slower model drive the joints works badly. In SWAP (Carnegie Mellon and Amazon FAR, October 2026), a diffusion model proposed collision-free motions; steered at run time and carried out by an RL tracker, they succeeded 88.3% of the time over five simulated tasks, against 17.4% when the diffusion output drove the joints directly; a real G1, with motion capture for localization, succeeded in 18 of 25 trials <Cite id="swap-2026" />. FIBER plans 5 times a second in the command space of a frozen controller that balances 50 times a second. Across 128 simulated variations per task it reached 42.0%, against 11.6% for replaying the experts' projected commands and 95.4% for the experts themselves; trained only in simulation, it managed 6, 4 and 2 of 10 real G1 trials on box pickup, door opening and grasping <Cite id="fiber-2026" />.

Other planners work from human video <Cite id="hdmi-2025" />, from the terrain ahead <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" />, from skills written as code by AI agents <Cite id="metatron-2026" /> or from the same human poses the tracker learned from <Cite id="workhorse-2026" />. OneSteer steers its tracker with task costs and passed under a 1.1 m bar in four of five real attempts using onboard LiDAR <Cite id="onesteer-2026" />. For precise hand work, ResGAC adds compliant force control and a learned correction, reaching 90% on a real standing peg-in-hole task against 50% for SONIC <Cite id="resgac-2026" />.

Perception is the next limit. Camera-based policies trained only in simulation kept walking and handling objects for up to 54 cycles <Cite id="viral-2025" /> or walked to and grasped objects in unseen scenes 73.3% of the time <Cite id="fetchman-2026" />, and an Atlas policy trained for 50 to 70 pound loads, sensing them through its own body, moved a fridge of over 100 pounds <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />. HITTER returned up to 106 consecutive table-tennis shots against a human <Cite id="hitter-2025" /> and LATENT sustained tennis rallies <Cite id="latent-tennis-2026" />, though Chris Paxton of Agility Robotics noted in October 2026 that many such systems still see the ball through external motion trackers <Cite id="paxton-robot-sports-2026" />.

## Retargeting loses information

Copying human joint angles onto a robot with different proportions leaves its feet floating or sinking into the floor. Such artifacts significantly reduce tracking robustness, especially for fast or long motions <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />, and noisy references led to training with a fall-recovery curriculum <Cite id="robust-tracking-2026" />.

<RetargetingGap className="my-6" />

Two October 2026 methods fix retargeting itself. PriMoRetarget places the feet first, then contacts, then the rest of the pose. On two-person interactions it kept 78.8% of hand contacts, and a simulated G1 trained on its output tracked 93.6% of motions, against 35.2% and 72.8% for the strongest baseline, PHC; collaboration between several robots stayed harder, at 62.6% <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. EmbodyHOI checks during generation that the robot can reach each hand position, cutting G1 wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm and raising simulated task success from 68% to 79%, at 32 times the sampling cost <Cite id="embodyhoi-2026" />.

## Where the field is heading

Training trackers is getting cheaper. UC Berkeley's SuperMimic (October 2026) learns through a differentiable <Term id="world-model">world model</Term>; it passed the released SONIC checkpoint's score after 3 hours on one GPU, where SONIC trained for 7 days on 128, though on different GPU models and with about 2.3 times less motion data <Cite id="supermimic-2026" />. ∇Mimic, differentiating through the simulator, reached the best reported world-model score on 40 LAFAN1 motion clips in 20 minutes instead of 1.2 hours, in simulation <Cite id="nabla-mimic-2026" />, and LooperMuscle closed much of the gap to standard <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> training in about 45 minutes, where PPO needs about 6 hours <Cite id="loopermuscle-2026" />.

The open design question is where balance belongs. Figure and NVIDIA keep [balance](/how-robots-work/#upright) and contact beneath the foundation model, behind tracked joint targets or a latent command <Cite id="helix-02-2026" /> <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />. DeepMind puts them inside one policy <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.
