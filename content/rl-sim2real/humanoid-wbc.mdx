## Motion tracking turns human clips into a motor layer

In motion tracking, reference motions are recorded from humans and mapped onto the robot's skeleton, and then the robot imitates them without falling, using <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term> in simulation. For H2O, motions which could not be tracked by the simulated imitator were first discarded <Cite id="h2o-2024" /> <Cite id="pulse-2023" />.

PHC imitated 98.9% of 11,313 clips from AMASS (15 motion-capture datasets) <Cite id="amass-2019" /> <Cite id="phc-2023" />. One HOVER policy can control walking velocity or arm joint angles without retraining <Cite id="hover-2024" />. The same SONIC controller can be steered by either a human or a vision-language-action model <Cite id="sonic-2025" />.

Also: DeepMimic on animated characters <Cite id="deepmimic-2018" />.

## Trackers reached real humanoids in 2024

H2O allowed a Unitree H1 to be piloted by a human moving in front of a commodity camera, a form of <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> <Cite id="h2o-2024" />. HumanPlus recorded that shadowing and used <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> on up to 40 demonstrations per task, achieving task success rates ranging from 60 to 100% <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />.

Most trackers follow a <Term id="teacher-student-distillation">teacher-student distillation</Term> setup, where the teacher policy is trained with privileged information not available to the robot and a student policy is trained to imitate the teacher policy using joint readings <Cite id="omnih2o-2024" /> <Cite id="exbody2-2024" /> <Cite id="gmt-2025" />. With one shared setup and no fine-tuning, BeyondMimic achieves skills including cartwheels, flip-kicks and sprints on real hardware <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" />.

## Three companies split deciding from balancing

A tracker will keep the robot upright but won't make decisions. On simulated HumanoidBench, a hierarchical method built on top of robust skills outperforms flat reinforcement learning <Cite id="humanoidbench-2024" />. In 2026, Figure, NVIDIA and Google DeepMind drew the line between decision making and balancing differently.

Also: language models over a controller, LeVERB <Cite id="leverb-2025" /> and WholeBodyVLA <Cite id="wholebodyvla-2025" />.

### Figure Helix 02: three layers

In Helix 02 (January 2026), S2 is in charge of understanding the scene and the instruction, S1 generates whole-body joint targets 200 times a second based on the output from S2, and S0 controls the motors 1,000 times a second following the commands from S1. S0 is a neural network with 10 million parameters, trained with more than 1,000 hours of human motion retargeted to the robot in over 200,000 parallel simulated environments <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Helix 2.5, pretrained on human-behavior data, achieved a 56% success rate on blind evaluations in 30 previously unseen homes, compared to 9% for models trained from scratch <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

Also: S0 with camera input <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />.

### NVIDIA GR00T and SONIC: a VLA steering a separate controller

GR00T N1.7 is a <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> (VLA) with 3 billion parameters that has been made available in early access since 18 April 2026 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-7-release-2026" />. GR00T N1.7 generates sequences of <Term id="latent-action">latent action</Term> tokens, which are decoded by NVIDIA's separate whole-body controller, GEAR-SONIC, 50 times a second into joint commands <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="groot-wbc-2026" />. SONIC was trained on 700 hours of motion capture and saw continual performance gains as its size was scaled up from 1.2 million to 42 million parameters <Cite id="sonic-2025" />.

GR00T 2 (November 2026) maintains the split: its 2-billion-parameter Turbo variant steers SONIC from the G1's onboard computer and served popcorn in all 48 trials <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

### Google DeepMind Gemini Robotics 2: one model, feet to fingertips

Google DeepMind says the same Gemini Robotics 2 checkpoint (30 July 2026) controls Apptronik's Apollo 2 "from feet to fingertips", as well as a two-armed Franka Duo. It learns to control a new two-armed robot in a few hours, from fewer than 200 examples in most cases <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Gemini Robotics 2.5 (10 November 2026) kitted car parts, achieving 96% in a proof of concept with Mercedes-Benz <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />.

## Whole-body skills still trail gripper skills

When tasked with picking an object from a shelf, table or floor, Apollo 2 managed average success rates of 76.3%, 68.4% and 45.7% respectively when equipped with Inspire hands, versus 89.6% with Franka grippers performing a precise insertion task. Success rates on single tasks with the SharpaWave hand ranged from 92% (unscrewing a light bulb) to 32% (using a dustpan). DeepMind says multi-finger manipulation "remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## A tracker needs a planner

A tracker needs a planner to design its motions, and the planner works best through the tracker. In SWAP, letting the planner drive the joints directly dropped success from 88.3% to 17.4% <Cite id="swap-2026" />.

| Method | How it plans | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SWAP (2026) | Diffusion proposes collision-free motions; an RL tracker executes | 88.3% over five simulated tasks <Cite id="swap-2026" /> |
| FIBER (2026) | 5 Hz plans over a frozen 50 Hz controller | 42.0% in simulation, against 11.6% replayed and 95.4% for experts <Cite id="fiber-2026" /> |
| OneSteer (2026) | Task costs steer the tracker | Passed under a 1.1 m bar in 4 of 5 real tries <Cite id="onesteer-2026" /> |
| ResGAC (2026) | Compliant force control plus a learned correction | 90% on real peg-in-hole, against 50% for SONIC <Cite id="resgac-2026" /> |
| Whole-Body Dexterity (2026) | A human steers a G1 that braces a basketball or box against its body | Completed 45 of 50 trials, against 9 with direct motion retargeting and 2 with SONIC <Cite id="wbd-2026" /> |

Also: planners from human video <Cite id="hdmi-2025" />, the terrain ahead <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" />, agent-written skill code <Cite id="metatron-2026" /> and human poses <Cite id="workhorse-2026" />.

Perception is the next limit. Many demos of sports-related tasks still use external motion tracking systems to sense the position of the ball, as pointed out by Chris Paxton of Agility Robotics <Cite id="paxton-robot-sports-2026" />. Visuomotor policies trained only in simulation walked to and picked up objects in new environments 73.3% of the time <Cite id="fetchman-2026" />.

Also: 54 camera-only work cycles <Cite id="viral-2025" />, 106-shot table tennis <Cite id="hitter-2025" />, tennis rallies <Cite id="latent-tennis-2026" />, Atlas moving a 100-pound fridge <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

## Retargeting loses information

Since the human and robot proportions are different, copying the human joint angles would result in unrealistic robot motions (the robot's feet floating in the air or penetrating the ground). A [motion retargeter](/rl-sim2real/motion-retargeting/) aims to remove such artifacts. These errors are especially problematic for tracking long or fast motions <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />. Motions with noise in the reference led one team to use a curriculum in which the robot learned to recover from falls <Cite id="robust-tracking-2026" />.

<RetargetingGap className="my-6" />

Two October 2026 methods fix retargeting. PriMoRetarget places the feet, then contacts, then the pose. A simulated Unitree G1 trained with motions retargeted by PriMoRetarget tracks 93.6% of motions, in comparison to the best baseline's 72.8% <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. EmbodyHOI checks reach during generation, cutting G1 wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm at 32 times the sampling cost <Cite id="embodyhoi-2026" />.
