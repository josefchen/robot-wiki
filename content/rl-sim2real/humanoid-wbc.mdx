## Motion tracking turns human clips into a motor layer

Motion tracking maps recorded human clips onto the robot's skeleton and uses <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term> in simulation to follow them without falling. H2O first dropped clips that a simulated imitator could not follow <Cite id="h2o-2024" /> <Cite id="pulse-2023" />.

PHC imitated 98.9% of 11,313 clips from AMASS, an archive of 15 motion-capture datasets <Cite id="amass-2019" /> <Cite id="phc-2023" />. One HOVER policy takes a walking velocity or arm joint angles without retraining <Cite id="hover-2024" />, and a person or a vision-language-action model can drive the same SONIC controller <Cite id="sonic-2025" />.

Also: DeepMimic on animated characters <Cite id="deepmimic-2018" />.

## Trackers reached real humanoids in 2024

H2O let a person drive a Unitree H1 by moving in front of an ordinary camera, a form of <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> <Cite id="h2o-2024" />. HumanPlus recorded that shadowing and used <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> to reach 60 to 100% success from up to 40 demonstrations per task <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />.

Most trackers use <Term id="teacher-student-distillation">teacher-student distillation</Term>: a teacher learns with information the robot cannot sense, and a student copies it from joint readings <Cite id="omnih2o-2024" /> <Cite id="exbody2-2024" /> <Cite id="gmt-2025" />. BeyondMimic ran cartwheels, flip-kicks and sprints from one shared setup on real hardware without fine-tuning <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" />.

| ExBody2 (2024) | G1 | Walks, squats and dances <Cite id="exbody2-2024" /> |

## Three companies split deciding from balancing

A tracker keeps the robot upright but does not choose what to do. On simulated HumanoidBench, a hierarchy over robust skills beat flat reinforcement learning <Cite id="humanoidbench-2024" />. In 2026 Figure, NVIDIA and Google DeepMind each drew the line between deciding and balancing in a different place.

Also: language models over a controller, LeVERB <Cite id="leverb-2025" /> and WholeBodyVLA <Cite id="wholebodyvla-2025" />.

### Figure Helix 02: three layers

In Helix 02 (January 2026), S2 reads the scene and the instruction, S1 outputs whole-body joint targets 200 times a second, and S0 drives the motors 1,000 times a second. S0, a 10-million-parameter network, learned from over 1,000 hours of retargeted human motion in more than 200,000 parallel simulated worlds <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Helix 2.5, pretrained on human-behavior data, succeeded in 56% of blind trials in 30 unfamiliar homes, against 9% from scratch <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

Also: S0 with camera input <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />.

### NVIDIA GR00T and SONIC: a VLA steering a separate controller

GR00T N1.7 is a 3-billion-parameter <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> (VLA), in early access since 18 April 2026 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-7-release-2026" />. NVIDIA's separate GEAR-SONIC controller decodes its <Term id="latent-action">latent action</Term> tokens 50 times a second into joint commands <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="groot-wbc-2026" />. SONIC kept improving as it grew from 1.2 million to 42 million parameters on 700 hours of motion capture <Cite id="sonic-2025" />.

GR00T 2 (November 2026) keeps the split: its 2-billion-parameter Turbo model steers SONIC from the G1's onboard computer and served popcorn in all 48 trials <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

### Google DeepMind Gemini Robotics 2: one model, feet to fingertips

Gemini Robotics 2 (30 July 2026) controls Apptronik's Apollo 2 "from feet to fingertips", and the same checkpoint runs a two-armed Franka Duo. It adapts to a new two-armed robot in a few hours, usually from fewer than 200 examples <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Gemini Robotics 2.5 (10 November 2026) kitted car parts at 96% in a Mercedes-Benz proof of concept <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />.

## Whole-body skills still trail gripper skills

On Apollo 2 with Inspire hands, picking averaged 76.3% from a shelf, 68.4% from a table and 45.7% from the floor, against 89.6% for precise insertion with Franka grippers. Single tasks on the SharpaWave hand ranged from 92% for unscrewing a light bulb to 32% for using a dustpan. DeepMind says multi-finger manipulation "remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## A tracker needs a planner

A planner must invent the motion a tracker follows, and it works best through the tracker. In SWAP, letting the planner drive the joints directly dropped success from 88.3% to 17.4% <Cite id="swap-2026" />.

| Method | How it plans | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SWAP (2026) | Diffusion proposes collision-free motions; an RL tracker executes | 88.3% over five simulated tasks <Cite id="swap-2026" /> |
| FIBER (2026) | 5 Hz plans over a frozen 50 Hz controller | 42.0% in simulation, against 11.6% replayed and 95.4% for experts <Cite id="fiber-2026" /> |
| OneSteer (2026) | Task costs steer the tracker | Passed under a 1.1 m bar in 4 of 5 real tries <Cite id="onesteer-2026" /> |
| ResGAC (2026) | Compliant force control plus a learned correction | 90% on real peg-in-hole, against 50% for SONIC <Cite id="resgac-2026" /> |

Also: planners from human video <Cite id="hdmi-2025" />, the terrain ahead <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" />, agent-written skill code <Cite id="metatron-2026" /> and human poses <Cite id="workhorse-2026" />.

Perception is the next limit. Many sports demos still see the ball through external motion trackers, Chris Paxton of Agility Robotics noted <Cite id="paxton-robot-sports-2026" />. Camera policies trained only in simulation walked to and grasped objects in unseen scenes 73.3% of the time <Cite id="fetchman-2026" />.

Also: 54 camera-only work cycles <Cite id="viral-2025" />, 106-shot table tennis <Cite id="hitter-2025" />, tennis rallies <Cite id="latent-tennis-2026" />, Atlas moving a 100-pound fridge <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

## Retargeting loses information

Human joint angles copied onto a robot with different proportions leave its feet floating or sunk into the floor. The artifacts hurt tracking most on fast or long motions <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />, and noisy references led one team to train with a fall-recovery curriculum <Cite id="robust-tracking-2026" />.

<RetargetingGap className="my-6" />

Two October 2026 methods fix retargeting: PriMoRetarget places the feet, then contacts, then the pose. A simulated G1 trained on its output tracked 93.6% of motions, against 72.8% for the best baseline <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. EmbodyHOI checks reach during generation, cutting G1 wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm at 32 times the sampling cost <Cite id="embodyhoi-2026" />.
