Humanoid whole-body control is the control of every actuated joint of a humanoid, from legs to hands, as one coupled problem. A dominant paradigm treats <Term id="whole-body-control">whole-body control</Term> as reinforcement-learned tracking of human motion <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> to the robot <Cite id="omniretarget-2025" />, though retargeted motion can be physically infeasible <Cite id="h2o-2024" />.

## The tracking lineage

In character animation, DeepMimic adapted standard RL to imitate motion clips, including retargeted ones, for characters including an Atlas robot model <Cite id="deepmimic-2018" />. AMASS unified 15 optical mocap datasets, over 40 hours and 11,000 motions, in one body parameterization <Cite id="amass-2019" />.

PHC tracks motion and recovers from falls for simulated avatars, with no physical robot. Its rotation-based controller imitates 98.9% of 11,313 filtered AMASS training clips, counting a failure when mean joint deviation exceeds 0.5 m. PMCP freezes learned primitives, trains new ones on progressively harder failed clips, adds a recovery primitive and learns a multiplicative composer. The paper shows reset-free avatar control from webcam pose estimates and language-generated motion, and lists highly dynamic clips, noisy real-time estimates and recovery artifacts as limitations <Cite id="phc-2023" />.

On the Unitree H1, H2O's privileged motion-filtering imitator is "similar to PHC" and follows PULSE, which distills a universal motion imitator into a latent representation <Cite id="pulse-2023" />. A separate policy learns whole-body tracking from the cleaned retargeted set with domain randomization, then follows human keypoints estimated in real time from an RGB camera. The deployed <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> system also uses robot-side motion capture for linear velocity, with a PD controller under the 19-dimensional joint-target policy <Cite id="h2o-2024" />.

OmniH2O uses kinematic motion goals as a shared interface on the H1. A student distilled from a privileged RL teacher tracks head-and-hand position goals from proprioceptive history, with no explicit global-linear-velocity input, though root odometry still maps goals into the robot frame <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-18"><Cite id="omnih2o-2024" /></span>. Language passes through a text-to-motion model, and a vision-language model picks motion primitives from camera images because it is too slow for direct motor control. A visual policy trained on teleoperated demonstrations feeds the tracker motion goals and hand commands, evaluated on four of six recorded tasks at ten runs each. The authors report no safety guarantees for extreme disturbances or out-of-distribution goals <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-32"><Cite id="omnih2o-2024" /></span>.

HumanPlus estimates the operator's body and hand poses with one RGB camera. Its Humanoid Shadowing Transformer, trained with PPO on retargeted motion in simulation, deploys zero-shot on a customized H1, reading IMU and joint encoders and outputting joint setpoints for PD control. Two head-mounted cameras record the robot's view during shadowing, and supervised <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> then trains a Humanoid Imitation Transformer from binocular images and proprioception. Occlusion, fixed retargeting and limited locomotion remain constraints <Cite id="humanplus-2024" />. TWIST retargets mocap clips and trains one whole-body teleoperation controller with RL plus behavior cloning, covering manipulation, legged manipulation and locomotion <Cite id="twist-2025" />.

ExBody had the upper body imitate a reference while the legs only followed a commanded velocity <Cite id="exbody-2024" />. ExBody2 reports that drifting global keypoint targets can accumulate tracking errors, so it tracks local keypoints with separate velocity tracking. It filters references by an initial policy's lower-body tracking error and distills a privileged PPO teacher into a history-based student that walks, squats and dances on Unitree G1. Its generalist and specialist policies trade coverage against precision, and transitions between specialists remain unresolved <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-5"><Cite id="exbody2-2024" /></span>. ASAP, the delta-action method in the [sim-to-real module](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer), learns a correction model from real G1 rollouts and fine-tunes against the corrected simulator, producing jumps and sports-style motions <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-24"><Cite id="asap-2025" /></span>.

KungfuBot trains one policy per processed reference motion. Its adaptive rule tightens the tracking reward's error tolerance from a moving average of tracking error, never loosening it, while curricula lower the early-termination threshold and raise regularization. Its quantitative hardware test is ten Tai Chi trials with the G1 root fixed for evaluation <Cite id="kungfubot-2025" />. Its successor KungfuBot2 is one unified controller with an orthogonal mixture-of-experts, stable over minute-long sequences <Cite id="kungfubot2-2025" />. GMT also trains a single tracking controller, re-clipping long motions and resampling by completion and tracking error. A soft mixture-of-experts teacher, whose gating network combines expert actions, is distilled into a student through DAgger. Its baselines and ablations run in simulation, and it neither gets up after falls nor targets slopes and stairs <Cite id="gmt-2025" />. Learned get-up policies lift a real G1 from lying face up or face down <Cite id="humanup-2025" />.

BeyondMimic learns cartwheels, spin-kicks, flip-kicks and sprinting with one setup and shared hyperparameters, composes them through guided latent diffusion and transfers zero-shot to hardware <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" />. The 2026 robustness work aggregates noisy, inconsistent references with a multi-head cross-attention command encoder and adds a fall-recovery curriculum <Cite id="robust-tracking-2026" />. ZEST trains motion-imitation policies with RL from motion capture, monocular video and animation, and deploys them zero-shot on Atlas, the Unitree G1 and Spot without contact labels or state estimators <Cite id="zest-2026" />. ResGAC, from Guanya Shi (Carnegie Mellon), Koushil Sreenath and colleagues (October 2026), adds residual RL to geometric admittance control on a real Unitree G1 and reports 90% success on a standing peg-in-hole task, against 50% for SONIC <Cite id="resgac-2026" />.

## Three decompositions ship in 2026

HOVER treats full-body kinematic motion imitation as a common abstraction across control modes, distilled into one policy <Cite id="hover-2024" />. Figure, NVIDIA and Google DeepMind each shipped whole-body control in 2026 with the learning boundary in a different place.

### A unified motion-tracking policy: Figure Helix 02 S0

Figure announced Helix 02 on 27 January 2026. It keeps the tracking decomposition, and Figure reports that its S2 reasons over scenes and language and emits latent goals. S1, a 200 Hz visuomotor transformer, reads head and palm cameras, fingertip tactile sensors and full-body <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term> and outputs whole-body joint targets. S0, a 10M-parameter controller at 1 kHz, turns joint state and base motion into actuator commands. S0 trained on over 1,000 hours of joint-level retargeted human motion and sim-to-real RL across more than 200,000 parallel environments, and Figure says it replaces 109,504 lines of hand-engineered C++. The headline demo is a 4-minute dishwasher unload and reload of 61 loco-manipulation actions <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Figure says it later conditioned S0, which had reasoned only about the robot's own body, on camera perception <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />. Figure reports that Helix 2.5, pretrained from random initialization on its Index human-behavior dataset, succeeded on 56% of blind zero-shot trials in 30 unseen homes, against 9% when trained from scratch. Helix 02 had started from a pretrained vision-language model <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

### A VLA above a learned controller: GR00T + GEAR-SONIC

NVIDIA keeps the VLA and whole-body controller as separate learned systems. It released GR00T N1.7 as early access on 18 April 2026 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-7-release-2026" />. GR00T N1.7 is a 3B-parameter VLA with a Cosmos-Reason2-2B backbone and a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action head. With the `UNITREE_G1_SONIC` embodiment it predicts latent action tokens that the GEAR-SONIC controller, running at 50 Hz, decodes into joint commands for legs, arms and hands, so one language-conditioned policy coordinates locomotion and manipulation <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="groot-wbc-2026" />. SONIC, published in Science Robotics, scales tracking from 1.2M to 42M parameters on 700 hours of motion capture and 21k GPU hours, with one token space serving VR teleoperation and VLAs <Cite id="sonic-2025" />. LeVERB learns a latent vision-language verb vocabulary from rendered kinematic demonstrations for an RL whole-body controller <Cite id="leverb-2025" />. WholeBodyVLA learns latent actions from action-free egocentric video <Cite id="wholebodyvla-2025" />. The GR00T repository licenses code under Apache 2.0 and model weights under the NVIDIA Open Model License, and every checkpoint loads the gated Cosmos-Reason2-2B backbone <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

Workhorse, from Pieter Abbeel, Koushil Sreenath (UC Berkeley) and colleagues (October 2026), pairs a visual planner with an RL whole-body tracker, both trained on robot-free human poses without retargeting; in a simulated copy of the demonstration room it completes box sorting in 77% of episodes, and 64% under 40 N·s pushes <Cite id="workhorse-2026" />.

### One VLA, feet to fingertips: Gemini Robotics 2

Google DeepMind removed the boundary. Its 30 July 2026 announcement reports that Gemini Robotics 2 controls full humanoids "from feet to fingertips": on Apptronik's Apollo 2 it walks to fetch a watering can and shelve it. It drives the five-fingered, 22-degree-of-freedom SharpaWave hand and parallel grippers on a Franka Duo. One checkpoint runs Apollo 2 with SharpaWave hands, Apollo 2 with Inspire hands and the Franka Duo with Robotiq grippers. DeepMind reports adapting to new bi-arm robots in a few hours, with fewer than 200 examples in most cases <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## Gemini Robotics 2 results

DeepMind's whole-body and gripper rates average several tasks per skill category; multi-finger rates are per task. Whole-body picking on Apollo 2 with Inspire hands averages 76.3% from a shelf, 68.4% from a table and 45.7% from the floor. Precise gripper insertion on the Franka Duo averages 89.6%. Multi-finger dexterity on the SharpaWave hand spans 92% for unscrewing a bulb to 32% for a dustpan, and DeepMind says multi-finger dexterous manipulation "remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## What tracking does not solve

Tracking reproduces motion without choosing the task. Zhang and colleagues pair a terrain-aware diffusion motion generator with a tracker fine-tuned in closed loop on its imperfect references, crossing boxes, hurdles and stairs on a G1 <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" />. HDMI learns from monocular video to co-track robot and object states, with 67 consecutive door traversals and six real loco-manipulation tasks <Cite id="hdmi-2025" />. Simulation-trained visual loco-manipulation now reaches hardware: VIRAL's RGB policy ran continuous loco-manipulation for up to 54 cycles on a G1 <Cite id="viral-2025" />, and FetchMan's policy walked to and grasped targets in unseen scenes at 73.3% success, zero-shot <Cite id="fetchman-2026" />. Boston Dynamics reports that it trained Atlas's fridge move with RL from a simple animation, and that a policy trained for 50 to 70 pound loads moved a fridge of over 100 pounds <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />.

HITTER pairs a planner that predicts the ball and picks the strike with an RL whole-body controller, and reached up to 106 consecutive table-tennis shots against a human <Cite id="hitter-2025" />. LATENT learns tennis strokes from fragments of human motion and sustained multi-shot rallies with people on a Unitree G1 <Cite id="latent-tennis-2026" />. Chris Paxton of Agility Robotics wrote in October 2026 that many such systems still see the ball through external motion trackers, which leaves onboard perception as the limit <Cite id="paxton-robot-sports-2026" />.

Retargeting is lossy. Artifacts such as foot sliding and self-penetration significantly reduce tracking-policy robustness, particularly for dynamic or long sequences <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />. Noisy, inconsistent transferred references motivate the 2026 robustness work <Cite id="robust-tracking-2026" />.

Figure and NVIDIA [keep balance](/how-robots-work/#upright) and contact beneath the foundation model, behind tracked joint targets or a latent token <Cite id="helix-02-2026" /> <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />. DeepMind puts them inside one policy, whose whole-body pick categories average 45.7 to 76.3% against 89.6% for gripper insertion tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.
