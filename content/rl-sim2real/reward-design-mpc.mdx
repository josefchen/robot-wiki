In <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term>, robots learn what they are rewarded for, and thus much of the engineering goes into crafting the reward <Cite id="rudin-2021" />. In model-predictive control (MPC), planning is done online using a model of the physics, with no learning involved <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />.

## The reward is the product

The walking reward proposed by Rudin and colleagues is composed of nine weighted terms, from tracking the body velocity to feet air time, and prescribes no gait <Cite id="rudin-2021" />. The October 2021 `legged_gym` code has nine out of fifteen reward weights different from zero <Cite id="legged-gym-repo-2021" />. When the learned policy does not behave as expected in some new situation, the fix is "a slow and iterative cycle of reward and environment redesign" <Cite id="walk-these-ways-2022" />.

## Three ways cut the weight tuning

Constraints, adaptive weights and shared setups each shift some of this burden away from the reward weights.

| Approach | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constraints | Three rewards, 11 constraints | Seven robots with only the torque weight changed; ran on Raibo and Mini Cheetah <Cite id="rewards-constraints-2024" /> |
| Staged constraints | Hand-set stages with own rewards and costs | Go1 back-flips, side-rolls, two-hand walking <Cite id="stagewise-cmorl-2024" /> |
| Adaptive weights | ROGER | Shifts weight to penalties near constraint limits <Cite id="gain-adaptation-2025" /> |
| Language-model preferences | MAPL | Scores objectives from trajectory comparisons <Cite id="mapl-2026" /> |
| Shared structure | MPBRS | One set of weights across nine humanoids <Cite id="mpbrs-2026" /> |
| One tracking setup | BeyondMimic | Many agile humanoid skills, shared settings <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" /> |
| No per-skill shaping | ZEST | Skills moved onto Atlas <Cite id="zest-2026" /> |
| Minimal rewards | FastSAC | Humanoid walking <Cite id="seo-2025" /> |

A planner can replace the shaped reward. Schuck, Hutter and colleagues had sample-based MPC generate demonstrations in simulation, then trained RL on sparse task rewards alone. The policies surpassed their MPC teacher and ran on an arm-equipped Spot and a Unitree G1 <Cite id="smpc-sparse-rl-2026" />.

## Language models write the reward

Eureka treats reward design as a search over code. Given a task description and the code for an RL environment, a language model generates reward functions written in Python. Eureka then uses these reward functions to train policies using <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term>, and a fitness function scores them to inform the next round of reward functions <Cite id="eureka-2024" />.

Across 29 tasks in simulation, Eureka finds rewards that outperform the original shaped reward function in 83% of tasks, with an average normalized improvement of 52% <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-[48px]"><Cite id="eureka-2024" /></span>.

<EurekaLoop className="my-6" />

People still write the fitness function, and it can diverge from what they intend. That gap is specification gaming, where agents succeed at the given objective but do not exhibit the intended behavior. DeepMind researchers have identified about 60 examples <Cite id="specification-gaming-2020" />. Reward functions that are specified only in terms of the task are susceptible to specification gaming <Cite id="fey-athletic-2025" />.

| System | The model writes | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RDA | Reward revisions, after a vision-language model summarizes failures | <Cite id="rda-2026" /> |
| DrEureka | Rewards and randomization ranges | A quadruped walks on a yoga ball <Cite id="dreureka-2024" /> |
| ARPG | The whole problem definition | Runnable code can still define unlearnable tasks <Cite id="arpg-2026" /> |

## A curriculum keeps training at the edge of ability

<Term id="reward-shaping">Reward shaping</Term> is about what to optimize, whereas a <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> is about the order in which difficulty is presented to the agent. Shaping has one safe form: a bonus that can be written as a difference of some potential of the states is the only form that provably does not change the optimal policy <Cite id="ng-reward-shaping-1999" />.

In a terrain curriculum, robots are promoted onto harder tiles when they are successful on the current tile and demoted back down when they fail <Cite id="rudin-2021" />. Automatic Domain Randomization increases the amount of randomization applied in simulation as the agent's performance passes a success threshold, an idea from OpenAI's robot hand that Isaac Lab ships <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" /> <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />.

| Curriculum | What grows | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margolis et al. | Commanded speed | One of two main parts of the Mini Cheetah controller <Cite id="margolis-rapid-2022" /> |
| Eurekaverse | Parkour courses a language model writes | <Cite id="eurekaverse-2024" /> |
| Skild self-play | Opponents: recent copies of itself | A soccer policy scored on goals <Cite id="skild-self-play-2026" /> |
| BVER | Starts out from the goal and goals out from the start, like [bidirectional RRT](/classical/motion-planning/) | 95% on a 0.4 m box in about 65% fewer iterations <Cite id="bver-2026" /> |
| Success Guided Sampling | Setups at the edge of ability | Camera-based assembly transferred zero-shot <Cite id="sgs-2026" /> |

## The classical baseline got stronger

Classical legged control uses a simplified robot dynamic model in its <Term id="mpc">MPC</Term> layer. For example, MIT Cheetah 3 cast its MPC layer as a convex optimization problem and planned its ground contact forces 0.5 s into the future, solving it in less than 1 ms <Cite id="di-carlo-2018" />. Today, whole-body MPC that plans using the full robot dynamic model runs on hardware.

| Controller | What it showed |
| --- | --- |
| Sleiman et al. (2021) | Walking and manipulation as one problem, onboard; pushed and pulled a heavy door <Cite id="sleiman-wbmpc-2021" /> |
| DIAL-MPC | Full quadruped dynamics with no training; beat RL by 50% on climbing <Cite id="dial-mpc-2024" /> |
| Whole-body MPPI | Parallel MuJoCo rollouts for real walking, box climbing and pushing <Cite id="mppi-whole-body-2024" /> |
| RAI Institute | An arm-equipped Spot rallies at table tennis with people <Cite id="rai-iros-2026" /> |
| Zhang et al. iLQR | Unitree quadrupeds and an H1 at about 50 Hz, with motion capture <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" /> |
| Humanoid iLQR-MPC | A real G1 survived 245 N forward and 175 N sideways pushes <Cite id="ilqr-mpc-humanoid-2026" /> |

Since MPC is solved at every time step with respect to the measured system state, some feedback is already included, which compensates for model errors to some extent. Boston Dynamics keeps MPC for model-based control in Spot, but uses a learned policy to avoid solving multiple MPCs in parallel <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />. Its Atlas work with TRI drives language-conditioned policies through an MPC-based teleoperation interface <Cite id="bd-atlas-lbm-2025" />.

## The case for each side

<MpcComparison className="my-6" />

Learning-based control wins on reach and speed: simulation-trained controllers enabled ANYmal to navigate natural terrain using one forward pass of a neural network for each control decision <Cite id="lee-2020" /> and to complete a 2.2 km hike through alpine terrain <Cite id="miki-2022" />. A reinforcement learning policy enabled Spot to run at over 5.2 m/s, triple the top speed of the robot's stock controller <Cite id="miller-spot-2025" />.

Boston Dynamics trains an RL policy that controls Atlas to lift a fridge, where the load is sensed through the robot's own proprioception <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />. In drone racing, RL outperformed optimal control because it "optimizes a better objective" and handles model error with <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term> <Cite id="racing-rl-vs-oc-2023" />. Language-conditioned [humanoid whole-body control](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc/) comes from the learned side too <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

On the other hand, model-based approaches do not require any task data: a whole-body iLQR baseline controller runs in real time on quadruped and humanoid hardware <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />. Grandia and colleagues optimize the entire set of ANYmal's joints with foothold limits given by elevation maps <Cite id="grandia-2022" />, and sampling MPC discovers gaits such as trotting and galloping on a CPU <Cite id="schramm-2025" />. Friction limits are included as hard constraints in convex MPC, instead of being penalized in the reward <Cite id="di-carlo-2018" />.

The two sides share tools: Newton's hydroelastic contact draws on Drake's, TRI collaborates with Newton on solvers, and Newton serves as a physics engine in Isaac Lab <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" /> <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" />. Work on a real Franka arm points out that contact sensitivity remains a main challenge for parallel sampling MPC <Cite id="dierking-2026" />.

## The debate landed on hybrids

Hybrids run in both directions: either a planning algorithm is used to train or steer a policy, or a learned policy tunes a planning algorithm.

| Hybrid | How | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MPC-guided policy search | MPC with full state trains a network | A simulated quadrotor at a fraction of MPC's cost <Cite id="mpc-gps-2015" /> |
| DTC | RL follows a model-based planner's footholds | More robust on slippery or soft ground <Cite id="dtc-2023" /> |
| Differentiable simulation | The physics supplies gradients | Zero-shot transfer to a real quadruped <Cite id="schwarke-2024" /> |
| Sumo | A sampling planner steers a pretrained RL policy | Spot uprighted a tire heavier than its rated lift <Cite id="sumo-2026" /> |
| Learned references | RL adjusts a model-based controller's references | Survival under disturbance from 50.35% to 77.29%, in simulation <Cite id="structured-references-2026" /> |
