## The reward is the product

Rudin and colleagues' walking reward summed nine weighted terms, from velocity tracking to feet air time, and prescribed no gait <Cite id="rudin-2021" />. The October 2021 `legged_gym` code sets nine of its fifteen reward weights to nonzero values <Cite id="legged-gym-repo-2021" />. When a policy fails somewhere new, the fix is "a slow and iterative cycle of reward and environment redesign" <Cite id="walk-these-ways-2022" />.

## Three ways cut the weight tuning

Constraints, adaptive weights and shared setups each move effort out of the weights.

| Approach | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constraints | Three rewards, 11 constraints | Seven robots with only the torque weight changed; ran on Raibo and Mini Cheetah <Cite id="rewards-constraints-2024" /> |
| Staged constraints | Hand-set stages with own rewards and costs | Go1 back-flips, side-rolls, two-hand walking <Cite id="stagewise-cmorl-2024" /> |
| Adaptive weights | ROGER | Shifts weight to penalties near constraint limits <Cite id="gain-adaptation-2025" /> |
| Language-model preferences | MAPL | Scores objectives from trajectory comparisons <Cite id="mapl-2026" /> |
| Shared structure | MPBRS | One set of weights across nine humanoids <Cite id="mpbrs-2026" /> |
| One tracking setup | BeyondMimic | Many agile humanoid skills, shared settings <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" /> |
| No per-skill shaping | ZEST | Skills moved onto Atlas <Cite id="zest-2026" /> |
| Minimal rewards | FastSAC | Humanoid walking <Cite id="seo-2025" /> |

A planner can replace the shaped reward. Schuck, Hutter and colleagues had sample-based MPC generate demonstrations in simulation, then trained RL on sparse task rewards alone. The policies surpassed their MPC teacher and ran on an arm-equipped Spot and a Unitree G1 <Cite id="smpc-sparse-rl-2026" />.

## Language models write the reward

Eureka treats reward design as a search over code. A language model reads the task and the environment code and writes Python reward functions. <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> trains a policy on each, and a fitness function scores them to steer the next round <Cite id="eureka-2024" />.

Across 29 simulated tasks, Eureka's rewards beat the original shaped reward on 83%, with 52% average normalized improvement <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-[48px]"><Cite id="eureka-2024" /></span>.

<EurekaLoop className="my-6" />

People still write the fitness function, and it can diverge from what they intend. That gap is specification gaming, meeting the literal objective without the intended outcome; DeepMind researchers collected about 60 examples <Cite id="specification-gaming-2020" />, and task-only rewards invite it <Cite id="fey-athletic-2025" />.

| System | The model writes | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RDA | Reward revisions, after a vision-language model summarizes failures | <Cite id="rda-2026" /> |
| DrEureka | Rewards and randomization ranges | A quadruped walks on a yoga ball <Cite id="dreureka-2024" /> |
| ARPG | The whole problem definition | Runnable code can still define unlearnable tasks <Cite id="arpg-2026" /> |

## A curriculum keeps training at the edge of ability

<Term id="reward-shaping">Reward shaping</Term> sets what to optimize; a <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> sets the order of difficulty. Shaping has one safe form: a bonus written as a difference of potentials between states leaves the best policy unchanged, and only that form guarantees it <Cite id="ng-reward-shaping-1999" />.

A terrain curriculum promotes robots that succeed on a tile to harder terrain and demotes those that fail <Cite id="rudin-2021" />. Automatic Domain Randomization widens the simulated conditions as success crosses a threshold, an idea from OpenAI's robot hand that Isaac Lab ships <Cite id="openai-rubiks-cube-2019" /> <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" />.

| Curriculum | What grows | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margolis et al. | Commanded speed | One of two main parts of the Mini Cheetah controller <Cite id="margolis-rapid-2022" /> |
| Eurekaverse | Parkour courses a language model writes | <Cite id="eurekaverse-2024" /> |
| Skild self-play | Opponents: recent copies of itself | A soccer policy scored on goals <Cite id="skild-self-play-2026" /> |
| BVER | Starts out from the goal and goals out from the start, like [bidirectional RRT](/classical/motion-planning/) | 95% on a 0.4 m box in about 65% fewer iterations <Cite id="bver-2026" /> |
| Success Guided Sampling | Setups at the edge of ability | Camera-based assembly transferred zero-shot <Cite id="sgs-2026" /> |

## The classical baseline got stronger

Classical legged control simplifies the model inside its <Term id="mpc">MPC</Term> layer. MIT Cheetah 3 posed its dynamics as a convex problem, planning ground forces 0.5 s ahead and solving in under 1 ms <Cite id="di-carlo-2018" />. Whole-body MPC, which plans with the full robot model, now runs on hardware.

| Controller | What it showed |
| --- | --- |
| Sleiman et al. (2021) | Walking and manipulation as one problem, onboard; pushed and pulled a heavy door <Cite id="sleiman-wbmpc-2021" /> |
| DIAL-MPC | Full quadruped dynamics with no training; beat RL by 50% on climbing <Cite id="dial-mpc-2024" /> |
| Whole-body MPPI | Parallel MuJoCo rollouts for real walking, box climbing and pushing <Cite id="mppi-whole-body-2024" /> |
| RAI Institute | An arm-equipped Spot rallies at table tennis with people <Cite id="rai-iros-2026" /> |
| Zhang et al. iLQR | Unitree quadrupeds and an H1 at about 50 Hz, with motion capture <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" /> |
| Humanoid iLQR-MPC | A real G1 survived 245 N forward and 175 N sideways pushes <Cite id="ilqr-mpc-humanoid-2026" /> |

MPC re-solves against the measured state every step, so feedback absorbs part of any model error. Boston Dynamics keeps Spot's model-based controller and uses a learned policy so it need not run several MPC instances in parallel <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />. Its Atlas work with TRI drives language-conditioned policies through an MPC-based teleoperation interface <Cite id="bd-atlas-lbm-2025" />.

## The case for each side

<MpcComparison className="my-6" />

Learning wins on reach and speed. Simulation-trained controllers took ANYmal through natural terrain at one network pass per step <Cite id="lee-2020" /> and up a 2.2 km alpine hike <Cite id="miki-2022" />. An RL policy ran Spot at over 5.2 m/s, triple the default controller's top speed <Cite id="miller-spot-2025" />.

Boston Dynamics uses RL to have Atlas lift a fridge, sensing the load through its body <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />. In drone racing RL beat optimal control because it "optimizes a better objective" and handles model error with <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term> <Cite id="racing-rl-vs-oc-2023" />. Language-conditioned [humanoid whole-body control](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc/) comes from the learned side too <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

Model-based control needs no task data: a whole-body iLQR baseline runs in real time on quadruped and humanoid hardware <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />. Grandia and colleagues optimize all of ANYmal's joints against foothold limits from elevation maps <Cite id="grandia-2022" />, and sampling MPC discovers gaits from trotting to galloping on a CPU <Cite id="schramm-2025" />. Convex MPC enforces friction limits as hard constraints, where a reward penalty only discourages violations <Cite id="di-carlo-2018" />.

The two sides share tools. Newton's hydroelastic contact draws on Drake's, TRI works with Newton on solvers, and Newton is a physics backend for Isaac Lab <Cite id="state-of-simulation-2026" /> <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" />. Contact sensitivity remains a main challenge for parallel sampling MPC on a real Franka arm <Cite id="dierking-2026" />.

## The debate landed on hybrids

Hybrids run in both directions: a planner trains or steers a policy, or a policy tunes a planner.

| Hybrid | How | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MPC-guided policy search | MPC with full state trains a network | A simulated quadrotor at a fraction of MPC's cost <Cite id="mpc-gps-2015" /> |
| DTC | RL follows a model-based planner's footholds | More robust on slippery or soft ground <Cite id="dtc-2023" /> |
| Differentiable simulation | The physics supplies gradients | Zero-shot transfer to a real quadruped <Cite id="schwarke-2024" /> |
| Sumo | A sampling planner steers a pretrained RL policy | Spot uprighted a tire heavier than its rated lift <Cite id="sumo-2026" /> |
| Learned references | RL adjusts a model-based controller's references | Survival under disturbance from 50.35% to 77.29%, in simulation <Cite id="structured-references-2026" /> |
