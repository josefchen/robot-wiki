## Each quadruped result removed hand engineering

In 2019 Hwangbo and colleagues replaced idealized actuators in simulation with a neural network model fit to match the behavior of ANYmal's real motors. Policies trained with idealized or analytical motor models couldn't take one step on ANYmal without falling <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />.

<ActuatorGap className="my-6" />

In 2020 Lee and colleagues showed that quadruped locomotion policies could be deployed blind: a student trained only in simulation on rigid terrain, given as input two seconds of <Term id="proprioception">proprioceptive</Term> history, allowed ANYmal to traverse mud, snow, rubble and vegetation <Cite id="lee-2020" />. RMA was deployed on Unitree's A1 quadruped with no fine-tuning <Cite id="rma-2021" />; the [sim-to-real module](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer) covers such training.

Miki and colleagues augmented Lee and colleagues' method with access to a terrain map, using a learned gating mechanism to fall back on proprioception when the terrain map is misleading. This allowed ANYmal to complete the 2.2 km, 120 m elevation gain hike up Etzel in 78 minutes (76 estimated by the planner) without falling <Cite id="miki-2022" />.

Also: terrain under the hind feet from one front camera <Cite id="agarwal-vision-2022" />.

Training became fast. Rudin and colleagues trained flat-ground locomotion in less than four minutes on one GPU, and locomotion on uneven terrain for 4,096 simulated ANYmals in less than twenty minutes <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-4"><Cite id="rudin-2021" /></span>. They also introduced a learning <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> inspired by games: simulated robots that walk over a terrain tile are moved to a more difficult tile, and robots that fail are moved to an easier tile. The environment has been open-sourced as `legged_gym` <Cite id="legged-gym-repo-2021" />.

Also: 20 minutes on one consumer GPU, then a lunar-analog site <Cite id="space-quadruped-rl-2026" />.

## A gait is a timing pattern

A gait is a pattern of footfall timing: when each leg lands relative to the others, and its duty factor, the fraction of a stride it spends down. Animals of different sizes move alike when their Froude numbers (speed squared divided by gravity times hip height) are the same <Cite id="alexander-1984" />. Walk These Ways commands a gait as three foot-pair offsets plus a frequency: (0.5, 0, 0) trots, (0, 0.5, 0) bounds, (0, 0, 0) pronks <Cite id="walk-these-ways-2022" />.

<Image id="horse-in-motion-muybridge-1878" />

Hand-designed controllers set the timing; learned ones find it. In Park, Wensing and Kim's work on the MIT Cheetah 2, the ground time was set to stride length divided by speed, and the robot reached 6.4 m/s on a treadmill <Cite id="park-2017-bounding" />. Learned controllers gave the Mini Cheetah its fastest recorded speed (3.9 m/s) <Cite id="margolis-rapid-2022" /> and Cassie five gaits from periodic reward terms <Cite id="siekmann-2020" />.

## Humanoids inherited the quadruped recipe

Humanoids took the quadruped recipe and added human motion as a reference. Where early work centered around model-based control, the community has since transitioned to large-scale RL and, more recently, generative models <Cite id="gu-humanoid-review-2026" />. H2O tracks <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> motion-capture clips to achieve real-time whole-body <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> of a Unitree H1 from human motion captured by a camera <Cite id="h2o-2024" />. ASAP adapts its simulation to better align with reality using rollouts collected on Unitree's G1 <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

| Method | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Transformer over recent sensing (2023) | A real humanoid, zero-shot <Cite id="radosavovic-2023" /> |
| Short and long histories (2024) | Cassie ran a 400 m dash <Cite id="li-cassie-2024" /> |
| Deep RL soccer (2023) | Walked 181% and turned 302% faster than a scripted baseline <Cite id="haarnoja-soccer-2023" /> |
| Hiking in the Wild (2026) | Depth and body sensing to joints at up to 2.5 m/s <Cite id="hiking-wild-2026" /> |
| Humanoid Parkour (2024) | Vision-based whole-body skills, no motion priors <Cite id="humanoid-parkour-2024" /> |
| Noetix E1 (2026) | Succeeded in 29 of 30 single-obstacle trials and four of five mixed-course trials without fine-tuning on the physical robot <Cite id="noetix-e1-parkour-2026" /> |
| Diffusion reference motion (2026) | G1 crossed boxes, hurdles and stairs <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" /> |

Walk Like This (Disney Research Zurich and ETH Zurich, October 2026) takes style from a reference clip and speed from a joystick. A diffusion model generates the motion, an RL policy tracks it, and a Unitree G1 ran about 4 hours zero-shot across styles without a failure <Cite id="walk-like-this-2026" />.

Reward design mattered little for a simulated MIT Humanoid running. Different reward formulations led to similar performance with <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> <Cite id="ppo-2017" /> fixed, over ten runs per design, and potential-based reward shaping was less sensitive to its weights <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-[64px]"><Cite id="mit-humanoid-rewards-2023" /></span>.

## Industry ships learned controllers

Spot now runs a learned policy in addition to its model-based controller (which once evaluated dozens of <Term id="mpc">model-predictive control</Term> plans in under a millisecond). Boston Dynamics says Spot falls less with the learned controller, even on extremely slick surfaces, and that they evaluate policies by running them on an internal fleet for over 2,000 hours per week <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />.

Also: model-based control with MuJoCo inside the optimizer <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />.

Atlas learns its skills, mostly in simulation.

| When | Atlas |
| --- | --- |
| March 2025 | RAI Institute: tracked human motion, about 150 million simulator runs per maneuver, zero-shot <Cite id="rai-atlas-rl-2025" /> |
| August 2025 | Large Behavior Models with Toyota Research Institute, whole robot at 30 Hz <Cite id="bd-atlas-lbm-2025" /> |
| 5 January 2026 | Product Atlas at CES; 2026 fleets committed to Hyundai and Google DeepMind <Cite id="bd-atlas-ces-2026" /> |
| 2026 | "Millions of hours" of parallel simulation, rotary actuators only <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" /> |
| 2026 | ZEST: skills from motion capture, video and animation, zero-shot <Cite id="zest-2026" /> |
| November 2026 | Wheeled testbed learned to roll during a side dash <Cite id="bd-atlas-on-a-roll-2026" /> |

The controller for Agility's Digit contains under one million parameters and trains in three or four days despite simulating decades of experience; the resulting policies transfer zero-shot to the real robot <Cite id="agility-wbc-2025" />. Agility's Digit 5 (15 September 2026) benefits from more than 65,000 hours of real-world experience and lifts up to 50 lb (22.7 kg) <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />.

Also: Agility on Nasdaq <Cite id="agility-nasdaq-2026" />, Figure's S0 climbing stairs from camera input <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />.

## Contact remains the hard part

Contact-rich manipulation is harder to simulate, because dynamic contact is the bottleneck of most simulators <Cite id="acosta-2021" />; the [locomotion and manipulation comparison](/rl-sim2real/why-rl-locomotion) explains why. Language models now write the remaining hand design: Eureka's rewards outperformed human experts on 83% of 29 environments <Cite id="eureka-2024" />, and DrEureka resulted in a quadruped that can walk on a yoga ball <Cite id="dreureka-2024" />.

Feet can manipulate too. In October 2026 ETH Zurich's ANYmal D solved a 0.4 m, 4.6 kg Rubik's Cube, pinning it with one foot while the other turned a layer. It completed all three hardware solves without intervention, each about 30 steps over 8 to 9 minutes <Cite id="rubiks-quadruped-2026" />.
