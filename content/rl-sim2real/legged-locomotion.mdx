<Term id="legged-locomotion">Legged locomotion</Term> is the control of walking and running robots, and the area where sim-to-real reinforcement learning reached shipping products such as Spot <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />. ETH Zurich's ANYmal line went from simulation-trained policies on hardware in 2019 <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" /> to a 2.2 km alpine hike in 2022 <Cite id="miki-2022" />.

## The quadruped lineage

Hwangbo and colleagues learned an actuator network for ANYmal's series-elastic actuators. It predicts joint torque from current and past joint-position errors and velocities, trained on data from exciting the real legs and disturbing the robot by hand. It stands in for actuation in the simulator. Policies trained with ideal or analytical actuator models failed to take a single step without falling. A separate recovery policy flipped ANYmal upright from nine tested configurations, including a nearly upside-down pose and two resting on its own legs, after the authors relaxed joint-velocity constraints following the first hardware attempt <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />.

Lee and colleagues trained a privileged RL teacher to supervise the actions and latent features of a temporal convolutional student. The deployed student reads two seconds of <Term id="proprioception">proprioceptive</Term> history from joint encoders and an IMU, plus the command and current state, with no terrain map. Trained on rigid procedural terrain, ANYmal-B and ANYmal-C controllers crossed mud, snow, rubble and vegetation without environment-specific tuning. The authors warn that a robot commanded off a cliff will walk off it <Cite id="lee-2020" />. RMA, a base policy plus an adaptation module, deployed on the A1 robot without any fine-tuning <Cite id="rma-2021" />; the [sim-to-real module](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer) covers the distillation mechanics.

Miki and colleagues fed elevation-map height samples and proprioception to a GRU belief encoder whose learned attention gate controls how much exteroception enters. The student imitates a privileged teacher and reconstructs terrain from noisy observations. With covered sensors on stairs and slippery platforms it fell back on proprioception and revised its terrain estimate after contact. ANYmal C used two Robosense Bpearl LiDARs or four RealSense D435 cameras, with mapping at 20 Hz and the policy at 50 Hz. The Etzel hike covered 2.2 km and 120 m of climb in 78 minutes against a planner's 76, with no falls but stops to reattach a shoe and swap batteries. The authors list possible falls at occluded cliffs or stepping stones <Cite id="miki-2022" />.

Rudin and colleagues trained flat-terrain walking in under four minutes on one workstation GPU. Their deployment policy used 4,096 ANYmal robots, batches of 98,304 transitions (24 steps per robot) and 1,500 updates in under twenty minutes on an i9-11900k CPU and RTX A6000 GPU. Their game-inspired terrain <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> promotes robots that cross a tile, demotes those covering under half the commanded distance, and loops hardest-level solvers back to a random level <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-4"><Cite id="rudin-2021" /></span>. The released `legged_gym` repository (October 2021) is the Isaac Gym environment used to train ANYmal on rough terrain <Cite id="legged-gym-repo-2021" />.

<Image id="anymal-anybotics-2022" />

ANYmal Parkour's high-level policy selects walking, jumping, climbing and crouching skills, crossing obstacles at up to two meters per second <Cite id="anymal-parkour-2023" />. An end-to-end policy with a single front-facing depth camera took a quadruped over stairs, curbs, stepping stones and gaps <Cite id="agarwal-vision-2022" />. Choi and colleagues put a cheap granular-media model in the training loop, with a controller that identifies terrain from feel, and their Raibo ran on beach sand at 3.03 m/s with feet fully buried <Cite id="choi-2023" />.

## A gait is a timing pattern

A gait is a footfall-timing pattern: each leg's phase offset in the stride and its duty factor $\beta$, the fraction of the cycle on the ground. Walk These Ways commands a gait as three timing offsets between foot pairs plus a stepping frequency: (0.5, 0, 0) trots, (0, 0.5, 0) bounds and (0, 0, 0) pronks <Cite id="walk-these-ways-2022" />. Alexander linked dynamically similar movement in mammals of different sizes to equal Froude numbers $u^2/gh$ <Cite id="alexander-1984" />.

In the MIT Cheetah 2 design, Park, Wensing and Kim plan stance time from stride length and speed, $T_{st} = L/v_d$, keep swing duration fixed, and modulate duty cycle by vertical impulse scaling. Stride length grows with speed only up to 3 m/s, so this is one controller's schedule. Roll instability ended the 6.4 m/s top-speed treadmill run, and at the highest speeds the robot partly leaned on the side wall. The minimum cost of transport, 0.47, came at 4 m/s <Cite id="park-2017-bounding" />. Margolis and colleagues report record agility for the MIT Mini Cheetah with a learned controller, sustaining speeds up to 3.9 m/s <Cite id="margolis-rapid-2022" />. Learned gaits can be specified as well as discovered: periodic reward composition yields standing, walking, hopping, running and skipping on Cassie <Cite id="siekmann-2020" />.

## From quadrupeds to humanoids

On Unitree's H1, H2O turns RGB-camera human pose estimates into real-time whole-body <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term>. It fits and <Term id="retargeting">retargets</Term> AMASS motions, filters those a privileged imitator cannot track, and trains a tracking policy with domain randomization that outputs 19 joint-position targets to a PD controller. The robot side also uses motion capture for linear velocity <Cite id="h2o-2024" />.

On the G1 with fixed wrists, ASAP trains an ankle delta-action model from real rollouts, freezes it in simulation for fine-tuning, and deploys the policy without it. The paper shows jumps and Ronaldo-, LeBron- and Kobe-inspired motions, but its quantitative hardware comparison covers only kicking and LeBron's "Silencer" against Vanilla <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

A fully learning-based humanoid controller, a causal transformer over the history of proprioceptive observations and actions, deployed zero-shot <Cite id="radosavovic-2023" />. Cassie's dual-history controller ran a 400-meter dash <Cite id="li-cassie-2024" />, and a miniature soccer humanoid walked 181% faster and turned 302% faster than a scripted baseline <Cite id="haarnoja-soccer-2023" />. A vision-based humanoid parkour policy without motion priors jumps onto a 0.42 m platform and leaps 0.8 m gaps <Cite id="humanoid-parkour-2024" />. Hiking in the Wild maps raw depth and proprioception to joint actions without external state estimation, at up to 2.5 m/s <Cite id="hiking-wild-2026" />. Zhang, Hutter and colleagues pair a diffusion model that generates terrain-aware reference motions in real time with an RL tracker; on a Unitree G1 with onboard perception it crossed boxes, hurdles and stairs <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" />. A 2026 Science Robotics review traces humanoid locomotion control from model-based methods through RL in large-scale simulation to generative models <Cite id="gu-humanoid-review-2026" />.

Jeon and colleagues compared rewards for a simulated 18-DoF MIT Humanoid running with arms fixed and ten leg joints controlled, holding <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term>-Clip <Cite id="ppo-2017" /> and IsaacGym fixed. They tested baseline rewards alone, plus direct shaping, and plus potential-based shaping of orientation, height and joint regularization, scoring accumulated baseline reward. Ten runs per case converged to similar performance within 1,000 iterations. Potential-based terms converged only marginally faster but were less sensitive across a 0.1 to 10 times weight sweep. The practical variant set the shaping discount to 1 against PPO's 0.99, which the authors say sacrifices policy invariance <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-[64px]"><Cite id="mit-humanoid-rewards-2023" /></span>.

## Industrial adoption

Boston Dynamics says Spot's earlier controller evaluated dozens of <Term id="mpc">MPC</Term> horizons with distinct step-trajectory references in under a millisecond and picked the highest-scoring output. Its 2024 account adds a learned policy, keeps the model-based controller for what it does well and drops the parallel MPC instances. The company reports Spot falls less, including on extremely slick or irregular surfaces, without a numerical fall rate, and ships the policy by software update. The company says a separate research architecture climbs boxes over 70 cm <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />.

Boston Dynamics benchmarks policies in simulation, then on an internal Spot robustness fleet running 24/7 for over 2,000 cumulative hours a week across indoor and outdoor terrain, stairs, floor materials and simulated weather. Falls reproducible in simulation are recreated for training or evaluation, and policies pass thousands of simulated and on-robot hours before customers <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />. Whole-body MPC now runs in real time on legged hardware with iLQR and MuJoCo dynamics <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />.

On 19 March 2025 the RAI Institute described an Atlas policy that tracks retargeted human motion, each maneuver built from about 150 million simulator runs and transferred zero-shot <Cite id="rai-atlas-rl-2025" />. Boston Dynamics later says Atlas practiced "for millions of hours in simulations in parallel" on GPUs, with only two body actuator types, all rotary and easier to represent in simulation <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" />. ZEST, a motion-imitation framework trained entirely in simulation, gave Atlas multi-contact skills such as an army crawl and breakdancing from motion capture and deployed them zero-shot <Cite id="zest-2026" />.

In August 2025, Boston Dynamics and Toyota Research Institute reported Large Behavior Models on Atlas. The 450M-parameter diffusion transformer is trained with <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term> on proprioception, images and a language prompt, using curated teleoperated demonstrations from hardware and simulation. It controls the full robot at 30 Hz from 30 Hz images, predicting 48 actions (1.6 seconds) per inference and most often executing 24 (0.8 seconds at 1x speed). Its teleoperation system is built on MPC, and the neural policies share its control interface <Cite id="bd-atlas-lbm-2025" />. Boston Dynamics unveiled the product version of Atlas at CES on 5 January 2026, began production immediately and committed its 2026 fleets to Hyundai's Robotics Metaplant Application Center and Google DeepMind <Cite id="bd-atlas-ces-2026" />.

Agility reports that Digit's whole-body control model, an LSTM with fewer than one million parameters, trains in Isaac Sim for decades of simulated time over three or four days and transfers zero-shot <Cite id="agility-wbc-2025" />. Figure reports that its S0 whole-body policy, now conditioned on camera perception and trained with RL across thousands of randomized terrains, traverses real-world stairs <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />.

## Limits of the lineage

Walking is not manipulation. Acosta and colleagues call dynamic contact the computational bottleneck for most simulations <Cite id="acosta-2021" />, and the [first module in this domain](/rl-sim2real/why-rl-locomotion) shows why manipulation's MDP resists simulation. Motion tracking needs feasible references, and naive retargeting can break them <Cite id="h2o-2024" />. Eureka's LLM-written rewards beat human experts on 83% of 29 environments <Cite id="eureka-2024" />, and DrEureka writes both reward functions and domain-randomization distributions, getting a quadruped to walk atop a yoga ball <Cite id="dreureka-2024" />.
