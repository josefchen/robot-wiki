## Each quadruped result removed hand engineering

In 2019 Hwangbo and colleagues replaced the simulator's ideal motors with a neural network fitted to ANYmal's real actuators. Policies trained on ideal or analytical motor models could not take one step without falling <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />.

<ActuatorGap className="my-6" />

In 2020 Lee and colleagues showed that a robot could walk blind. A student reading two seconds of <Term id="proprioception">proprioceptive</Term> history, trained only on rigid simulated terrain, took ANYmal through mud, snow, rubble and vegetation <Cite id="lee-2020" />. RMA deployed on the A1 with no fine-tuning <Cite id="rma-2021" />; the [sim-to-real module](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer) covers such training.

Miki and colleagues added the terrain map back behind a learned gate that falls back on body sensing when the map misleads. The Etzel hike covered 2.2 km and 120 m of climb in 78 minutes, against a planner's estimate of 76, with no falls <Cite id="miki-2022" />.

Also: terrain under the hind feet from one front camera <Cite id="agarwal-vision-2022" />.

Training became fast. Rudin and colleagues trained flat-ground walking in under four minutes on one GPU, and an uneven-terrain policy for 4,096 simulated ANYmals in under twenty <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-left-4"><Cite id="rudin-2021" /></span>. Their game-inspired <Term id="curriculum-learning">curriculum</Term> sends robots that cross a tile to harder terrain and those that fall short to easier, and `legged_gym` released the environment <Cite id="legged-gym-repo-2021" />.

Also: 20 minutes on one consumer GPU, then a lunar-analog site <Cite id="space-quadruped-rl-2026" />.

## A gait is a timing pattern

A gait is a pattern of footfall timing: when each leg lands relative to the others, and its duty factor, the fraction of a stride it spends down. Animals of different sizes move alike when their Froude number, speed squared over gravity times hip height, is equal <Cite id="alexander-1984" />. Walk These Ways commands a gait as three foot-pair offsets plus a frequency: (0.5, 0, 0) trots, (0, 0.5, 0) bounds, (0, 0, 0) pronks <Cite id="walk-these-ways-2022" />.

<Image id="horse-in-motion-muybridge-1878" />

Hand-designed controllers set the timing; learned ones find it. Park, Wensing and Kim set the MIT Cheetah 2's ground time to stride length over speed, and it reached 6.4 m/s on a treadmill <Cite id="park-2017-bounding" />. Learned controllers gave the Mini Cheetah a record 3.9 m/s <Cite id="margolis-rapid-2022" /> and Cassie five gaits from periodic reward terms <Cite id="siekmann-2020" />.

## Humanoids inherited the quadruped recipe

Humanoids took the quadruped recipe and added human motion as a reference; the field moved from model-based control to RL at scale and now to generative models <Cite id="gu-humanoid-review-2026" />. H2O tracks <Term id="retargeting">retargeted</Term> motion-capture clips for real-time whole-body <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> of a Unitree H1 from a camera <Cite id="h2o-2024" />. ASAP corrects its simulator with real G1 rollouts <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

| Method | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Transformer over recent sensing (2023) | A real humanoid, zero-shot <Cite id="radosavovic-2023" /> |
| Short and long histories (2024) | Cassie ran a 400 m dash <Cite id="li-cassie-2024" /> |
| Deep RL soccer (2023) | Walked 181% and turned 302% faster than a scripted baseline <Cite id="haarnoja-soccer-2023" /> |
| Hiking in the Wild (2026) | Depth and body sensing to joints at up to 2.5 m/s <Cite id="hiking-wild-2026" /> |
| Humanoid Parkour (2024) | Vision-based whole-body skills, no motion priors <Cite id="humanoid-parkour-2024" /> |
| Diffusion reference motion (2026) | G1 crossed boxes, hurdles and stairs <Cite id="wbc-motion-gen-2026" /> |

Walk Like This (Disney Research Zurich and ETH Zurich, October 2026) takes style from a reference clip and speed from a joystick. A diffusion model generates the motion, an RL policy tracks it, and a Unitree G1 ran about 4 hours zero-shot across styles without a failure <Cite id="walk-like-this-2026" />.

<Image id="cassie-oregon-state-2017" />

Reward design mattered little for a simulated MIT Humanoid running. With <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> <Cite id="ppo-2017" /> fixed, ten runs per design converged to similar performance, and potential-based shaping was less sensitive to its weights <span className="max-sm:[&_[role=tooltip]]:-translate-x-[64px]"><Cite id="mit-humanoid-rewards-2023" /></span>.

## Industry ships learned controllers

Spot now runs a learned policy beside its model-based controller, which once evaluated dozens of <Term id="mpc">model-predictive control</Term> plans in under a millisecond. Boston Dynamics reports fewer falls, including on extremely slick surfaces, and tests policies on an internal fleet running over 2,000 hours a week <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />.

Also: model-based control with MuJoCo inside the optimizer <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />.

Atlas learns its skills, mostly in simulation.

| When | Atlas |
| --- | --- |
| March 2025 | RAI Institute: tracked human motion, about 150 million simulator runs per maneuver, zero-shot <Cite id="rai-atlas-rl-2025" /> |
| August 2025 | Large Behavior Models with Toyota Research Institute, whole robot at 30 Hz <Cite id="bd-atlas-lbm-2025" /> |
| 5 January 2026 | Product Atlas at CES; 2026 fleets committed to Hyundai and Google DeepMind <Cite id="bd-atlas-ces-2026" /> |
| 2026 | "Millions of hours" of parallel simulation, rotary actuators only <Cite id="bd-atlas-hard-work-2026" /> |
| 2026 | ZEST: skills from motion capture, video and animation, zero-shot <Cite id="zest-2026" /> |
| November 2026 | Wheeled testbed learned to roll during a side dash <Cite id="bd-atlas-on-a-roll-2026" /> |

Agility's Digit controller, under one million parameters, trains for decades of simulated time in three or four days and transfers zero-shot <Cite id="agility-wbc-2025" />. Digit 5 (15 September 2026) builds on more than 65,000 hours of operation and lifts up to 50 lb (22.7 kg) <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />.

Also: Agility on Nasdaq <Cite id="agility-nasdaq-2026" />, Figure's S0 climbing stairs from camera input <Cite id="figure-s0-2026" />.

## Contact remains the hard part

Contact-rich manipulation is harder to simulate, because dynamic contact is the bottleneck of most simulators <Cite id="acosta-2021" />; the [locomotion and manipulation comparison](/rl-sim2real/why-rl-locomotion) explains why. Language models now write the remaining hand design: Eureka's rewards beat human experts on 83% of 29 environments <Cite id="eureka-2024" />, and DrEureka got a quadruped walking on a yoga ball <Cite id="dreureka-2024" />.

Feet can manipulate too. In October 2026 ETH Zurich's ANYmal D solved a 0.4 m, 4.6 kg Rubik's Cube, pinning it with one foot while the other turned a layer. It completed all three hardware solves without intervention, each about 30 steps over 8 to 9 minutes <Cite id="rubiks-quadruped-2026" />.
