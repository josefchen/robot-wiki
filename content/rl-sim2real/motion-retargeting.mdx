<Term id="retargeting">Motion retargeting</Term> is the problem of converting human motion, either obtained with motion capture or estimated from video, into joint trajectories for a robot whose proportions and joint limits differ from the performer's. For humanoids, the output is the kinematic reference a [tracking policy](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc/) learns to follow with reinforcement learning <Cite id="omniretarget-2025" />. Imperfections in that reference make the policy fail more often <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />.

## What a retargeter solves

H2O establishes a correspondence between 12 human and humanoid joints. Human and robot bodies are aligned in a common rest pose and the SMPL body shape <Cite id="smpl-2015" /> is fitted to the Unitree H1 <Cite id="unitree-h1-2026" />. It then minimizes the 12 joint-position differences with Adam <Cite id="h2o-2024" />.

GMR defines a weighted correspondence between key human and robot bodies in terms of position and orientation. The two rest poses are aligned and the upper and lower body are scaled separately. A differential inverse kinematics problem with joint limits is then solved. In the first stage body orientations and end-effector positions are matched, and in the second stage the positions of all key bodies are matched, starting each frame from the previous one. To remove floating and ground penetration, the height of the lowest body is subtracted <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />.

## The errors it leaves

A kinematic fit does not take into account dynamics and, unless specified in the objective, contact. Typical failures are foot sliding, ground penetration, self-collisions, object penetration, discontinuities in joint positions between consecutive frames, and hand contacts the robot no longer makes.

In the GMR study, PHC's <Cite id="phc-2023" /> retargeted versions of two dancing sequences penetrated the ground, by 60 cm in one case. The retargeted stop-and-go running sequence from ProtoMotions <Cite id="protomotions-repo-2024" /> suffered from leg self-intersections, and GMR's own output had discontinuities in joint positions on one 10-second segment, less than 2% of the dataset. BeyondMimic <Cite id="beyondmimic-2025" /> policies trained on either PHC dance clip achieved a 0% success rate in IsaacSim rollouts without domain randomization, in comparison to 100% for policies trained on GMR's data. Policies trained on Unitree's closed-source retargets performed even better, with the lowest values on all three tracking-error metrics <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />.

## Four families

**Kinematic optimisation.** H2O and GMR only consider matching the motion to the human, without accounting for objects or terrain. GMR's repository describes retargeting "in real time on CPU" <Cite id="gmr-repo-2025" />.

**Learned retargeting.** NMR learns to predict G1 motions from SMPL motions using a CNN-Transformer architecture. It trains on around 30,000 motion pairs, where the G1 motions are made physically consistent using RL tracking policies trained per motion cluster. On 82 AMASS sequences, its output had no frame with a joint step above 0.5 rad (compared to 56 for GMR), and 431 self-collision frames (compared to 947) <Cite id="nmr-retargeting-2026" />. UMR learns only the correspondence: a point-cloud network predicts where each human surface point should be mapped to on the robot in a T-pose. A constrained Gauss-Newton solver then fits every frame under joint limits and floor clearance. G1 policies trained on its references achieved a success rate of 99.28% on a carrying task, compared to 82.89% with OmniRetarget's, but performed worse on a chair task, 75.24% compared to 79.67% <Cite id="umr-retargeting-2026" />.

**Interaction-preserving retargeting.** OmniRetarget builds an interaction mesh with tetrahedrons connecting the key joints with points sampled from the object and environment, and minimizes the Laplacian deformation between the meshes of the human and the robot. Collision avoidance, joint and velocity limits and foot sticking are hard constraints; a foot is in stance when its source horizontal velocity falls below 1 cm/s. G1 policies trained on its data with five reward terms and no curriculum performed loco-manipulation and parkour skills of up to 30 seconds <Cite id="omniretarget-2025" />. PriMoRetarget considers a prioritization rather than weighting of objectives <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />; how well a G1 tracks its two-person retargets is on the [whole-body control page](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc/#retargeting-loses-information).

**Filter, then let RL absorb the rest.** H2O filters AMASS motions by learning a privileged imitation policy on every retargeted clip and keeps the motions that can be tracked. Out of 10k AMASS sequences <Cite id="amass-2019" />, H2O is able to track roughly 8.5k <Cite id="h2o-2024" />. OmniH2O builds on H2O's retargeting and augments its motions with variations that lock the lower body into either a squatting or standing pose <Cite id="omnih2o-2024" />. ASAP filters earlier: motions are reconstructed from human video using TRAM <Cite id="tram-2024" />, tracked using MaskedMimic <Cite id="maskedmimic-2024" /> in IsaacGym <Cite id="isaac-gym-2021" />, and only kept if they can be tracked. They are then retargeted in two stages. ASAP's later [delta-action correction](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/) accounts for dynamics in the simulator and does not alter the references <Cite id="asap-2025" />.

## Where the numbers disagree

Foot skating on LAFAN1 <Cite id="lafan1-2021" />: OmniRetarget reports zero for its own output <Cite id="omniretarget-2025" />, whereas PriMoRetarget measures that output sliding at 9.4 cm/s, the highest among the three methods it tests, compared to 1.2 cm/s for the mocap data <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. Downstream tracking performance on OMOMO <Cite id="omomo-2023" />: OmniRetarget reports a 50.83% success rate for GMR, whereas PriMoRetarget reports 79.8% for GMR <Cite id="omniretarget-2025" /> <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />. Since the papers have defined stance, selected motions and trained trackers differently, their numbers cannot rank methods across papers.

## What to use when

- One performer and no objects: GMR (MIT), configured for Unitree, Booster, Fourier and other humanoids <Cite id="gmr-repo-2025" />.
- Objects or terrain: OmniRetarget, implemented in holosoma (Apache-2.0) from Amazon FAR, with an MIT-licensed dataset <Cite id="holosoma-repo-2025" /> <Cite id="omniretarget-dataset-2025" />.
- Two humans in contact: PriMoRetarget reports better contact preservation than GMR, OmniRetarget and PHC (but code is not publicly available as of 14 November 2026) <Cite id="primoretarget-2026" />.
- Any source: use a simulated tracker to filter out bad clips before training, either after (H2O) or before (ASAP, MIT code) retargeting <Cite id="h2o-2024" /> <Cite id="asap-2025" /> <Cite id="asap-repo-2025" />.
