Reinforcement learning (RL) trains a robot by trial and error: it acts, gets a numerical reward, and adjusts its <Term id="policy">policy</Term> to earn more <Cite id="sutton-barto-2018" />. In robotics the price of each try shapes the method: nearly free in GPU simulation <Cite id="isaac-gym-2021" /> <Cite id="rudin-2021" />, costly on a real machine.

<Image id="daydreamer-a1-push-recovery-2022" />

## Every try costs time on hardware

An RL method is limited by how many environment steps it can afford. Simulation steps thousands of robots at once and resets them; hardware spends real time and wear on every step and needs a reset per episode <Cite id="rudin-2021" /> <Cite id="haarnoja-walk-2019" />.

<SampleEfficiencyLedger className="my-6" />

Reviews call stable, sample-efficient real-world RL a priority <Cite id="tang-drl-survey-2025" /> and list problems that games rarely pose <Cite id="ibarz-2021" />.

## On-policy discards data; off-policy reuses it

An <Term id="on-policy">on-policy</Term> method learns only from data its current policy just produced, then discards it; PPO takes several update passes over each batch <Cite id="ppo-2017" />. An <Term id="off-policy">off-policy</Term> method keeps every try in a <Term id="replay-buffer">replay buffer</Term>. Most build on <Term id="q-learning">Q-learning</Term>, which learns from the best next action, even one never taken <Cite id="sutton-barto-2018" />.

<ReplayReuse className="my-6" />

| Algorithm | What it changed |
| --- | --- |
| DDPG (2016) | Off-policy actor-critic learning for continuous actions <Cite id="ddpg-2016" /> |
| TD3 (2018) | Curbed DDPG's overestimated action values <Cite id="td3-2018" /> |
| SAC (2018) | Rewards acting as randomly as the task allows, for steadier learning <Cite id="sac-2018" /> |

### Simulation made PPO the default

With thousands of simulated robots, tries are cheap and stability is scarce <Cite id="sac-2018" />. Rudin and colleagues trained ANYmal with 4,096 simulated robots in under twenty minutes on one GPU, using [parallel simulation](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl) <Cite id="rudin-2021" />.

<Image id="isaac-gym-allegro-envs-2021" />

Off-policy methods used data better but ran slower by the clock, so most GPU-simulation work used PPO. That gap has closed <Cite id="pql-2023" />.

| Method | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Parallel Q-Learning | Beat PPO in wall-clock time <Cite id="pql-2023" /> |
| FastTD3, FastSAC | Humanoid walking for real robots in 15 minutes on one gaming GPU <Cite id="fasttd3-2025" /> <Cite id="seo-2025" /> |
| SAC (2026) | Matched PPO on seven legged robots with PPO's own rewards <Cite id="rsl-rl-sac-2026" /> |
| FlashSAC | Beat PPO on more than 60 tasks in 10 simulators <Cite id="flashsac-2026" /> |

## Real robots count tries in hours

<Term id="sample-efficiency">Sample efficiency</Term>, the experience a method needs, decides whether real-robot learning takes an afternoon or a season. Grasping takes far longer than walking.

| Robot | Task | Experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minitaur | Walking | 160,000 steps, about two hours with resets <Cite id="haarnoja-walk-2019" /> |
| Unitree A1 | Roll over, stand, walk, in a learned world model | One hour, no simulator <Cite id="daydreamer-2022" /> |
| Quadruped (A Walk in the Park) | Walking, with a tuned controller | 20 minutes <Cite id="walk-in-the-park-2022" /> |
| 11.5-ton excavator | Sub-centimeter path tracking | 40 minutes <Cite id="excavator-mbrl-2026" /> |
| Seven arms (QT-Opt) | Grasping | 580,000 attempts, about 800 robot hours <Cite id="qt-opt-2018" /> |
| 6 to 14 arms | Supervised grasp prediction (2016) | About 800,000 attempts over two months <Cite id="levine-hand-eye-2016" /> |

## Offline RL learns from a fixed log

<Term id="offline-reinforcement-learning">Offline reinforcement learning</Term> learns from a log of past experience, so it can reuse robot [datasets](/data-hardware/datasets) <Cite id="offline-rl-tutorial-2020" />. Its danger is optimism: actions the log never shows get wrongly high values, and no fresh data corrects them <Cite id="offline-rl-tutorial-2020" />. BCQ showed this breaks standard off-policy algorithms on fixed data <Cite id="bcq-2018" />.

| Fix | How it stays near the data |
| --- | --- |
| CQL | Pushes down the values of unseen actions <Cite id="cql-2020" /> |
| IQL | Never evaluates them <Cite id="iql-2022" /> |
| TD3+BC | Adds an imitation term <Cite id="td3-bc-2021" /> |

Q-Transformer beat earlier offline and imitation methods on a large real-robot suite <Cite id="q-transformer-2023" />.

Also: offline pretraining that speeds up later learning, PTR <Cite id="ptr-2022" />, Cal-QL <Cite id="cal-ql-2023" />, RLPD <Cite id="rlpd-2023" />.

### On demonstrations, behavior cloning often wins

On human demonstrations, [behavior cloning](/manipulation/bc-foundations) with memory of recent steps was strong in robomimic, while BCQ and CQL did poorly <Cite id="robomimic-2021" />. Offline RL wins when the data covers the optimal policy; on purely expert data the two match in the worst case <Cite id="offline-rl-vs-bc-2022" />.

Newer real-robot results favor RL. Policies fine-tuned on RL-generated data beat the same policies trained on human demonstrations by up to 40% <Cite id="rldg-2024" />. RECAP, offline RL for a VLA model, more than doubled throughput on some of the hardest tasks <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

## Hindsight relabeling learns from failure

If reward comes only on success, a robot that never succeeds learns nothing. [Reward shaping](/rl-sim2real/reward-design-mpc) is one answer; <Term id="hindsight-experience-replay">hindsight experience replay</Term> (HER) stores each attempt again as if a state reached later had been the goal <Cite id="her-2017" />.

<HindsightRelabel className="my-6" />

With HER, DDPG learned pushing, sliding and pick-and-place on a simulated Fetch arm where DDPG alone failed, and the pick-and-place policy worked on a real Fetch without fine-tuning <Cite id="her-2017" />.

## Real episodes need resets

A simulated episode restarts itself <Cite id="autonomous-rl-2022" />; a real one ends wherever the robot is <Cite id="leave-no-trace-2017" />.

| Method | Approach | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zhu et al. | Raw sensors, no instrumented rewards, no human resets | A D'Claw hand trained unattended <Cite id="real-world-rl-ingredients-2020" /> |
| Leave No Trace | A learned reset policy that aborts before irreversible states | <Cite id="leave-no-trace-2017" /> |
| VaPRL | Practice from chosen starting points | Fewer interventions in simulated tasks <Cite id="vaprl-2021" /> |
| MTRF | <Term id="reset-free-learning">Reset-free</Term> chaining of tasks | Valve and pipe tasks on a hand, no manual resets <Cite id="reset-free-rl-2021" /> |
| FIND | A vision-language agent picks the next task from the scene | Eight real tasks from 55% to 71.9%; 456 episodes in six hours with 30 interventions <Cite id="find-2026" /> |

## Real-robot RL reaches near-perfect reliability

The recipe starts from a few demonstrations, learns off-policy on the robot and lets a person step in <Cite id="serl-2024" /> <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />. HIL-SERL reports 100% success in 100 trials per task, including whole IKEA assembly, after one to two and a half hours of training <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" /> <Cite id="hil-serl-scirobotics-2025" />. RL-100 reports 1,000 successes in 1,000 episodes across eight tasks, from pouring to box folding <Cite id="rl-100-2025" /> <Cite id="rl-100-scirobotics-2026" />.

| System | Reported result |
| --- | --- |
| SERL (2024) | Circuit-board assembly and cable routing in 25 to 50 minutes per policy <Cite id="serl-2024" /> |
| ConRFT (2025) | 96.3% on eight tasks after 45 to 90 minutes of online VLA fine-tuning <Cite id="conrft-2025" /> |
| Q2RL (2026) | Pipe assembly and kitting in 1 to 2 hours online <Cite id="q2rl-2026" /> |
| LWD (2026) | One policy on 16 two-armed robots, 95% over eight tasks <Cite id="lwd-2026" /> |
| ReF-HIL (2026) | 90% success after 18 to 63 minutes of active training <Cite id="ref-hil-2026" /> |
| ACCRUE (2026) | 31.0% to 68.5% less training time on assembly sequences <Cite id="accrue-2026" /> |

Also: Stork on learning beyond imitation <Cite id="stork-beyond-imitation-2026" />.

## Generalist policies are the next target

Korniak and colleagues improved one generalist policy online across many tasks at once, with gains on tasks left out of online training <Cite id="generalist-rl-improvement-2026" />. [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning) covers online PPO for π0 and π0.5 <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" /> and residual specialists <Cite id="pld-2026" />. DROID <Cite id="droid-2024" /> and Open X-Embodiment <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" /> offer demonstrations without rewards, so offline RL on them needs added labels.
