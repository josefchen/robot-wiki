Calibration is about making a robot's commands and sensor readings consistent with reality by modeling, measuring, identifying and correcting errors in the system <Cite id="roth-mooring-ravani-1987" />. Any mistakes that remain after calibration will be baked into a learned policy.

## A wrong link anywhere moves the grasp

From the pixel that the policy deems to be a mug to the gripper that grabs it, there are many transformations and dynamics including <Term id="camera-intrinsics">camera intrinsics</Term>, depth information, how the camera is mounted, robot joint zero positions, tool dimensions, timing and actuator response. If the camera transform is wrong, the arm will reach in the wrong place even though FoundationPose was tracking the object <Cite id="foundationpose-2024" />.

Also: [visual servoing](/classical/perception) <Cite id="espiau-1992" />.

## The camera model must match the lens and the depth sensor

Camera intrinsics describe how to associate a pixel in an image with a ray in space. These can be estimated using Zhang's method <Cite id="zhang-2000-calibration" /> by imaging a flat calibration pattern from a handful of different angles. Changes to focus or cropping require refitting intrinsics; quality of the fit is judged by reprojection error on held-out calibration images <Cite id="reproducible-intrinsics-2026" />.

The depth error of stereo methods increases with the square of the distance <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />. Highly reflective objects cause saturation in images <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />. The Kinect's depth error reaches about 4 cm at its maximum operating range <Cite id="khoshelham-kinect-2012" />.

Also: fisheye lenses <Cite id="kannala-brandt-2006" />, single-image AnyCalib <Cite id="anycalib-2025" />, Kinect depth distortion <Cite id="herrera-depth-color-2012" />.

## Hand-eye calibration solves AX = XB

Hand-eye calibration is the problem of estimating the location of a camera mounted on a gripper. Shiu and Ahmad formulated the problem as $AX = XB$, where $A$ and $B$ are transformations due to movements of the robot arm and the camera, respectively, and $X$ is the unknown camera mount <Cite id="shiu-ahmad-1989" />. This problem has a closed-form solution presented by Tsai and Lenz <Cite id="tsai-lenz-1989" />.

<Image id="baxter-gripper-wrist-camera" />

It requires at least three poses about non-parallel axes <Cite id="opencv-hand-eye-docs-2026" />. Accuracy levels off at about 12 to 15 poses <Cite id="moveit-hand-eye-tutorial-2026" />.

## Real arms drift from their nominal kinematics

CAD models do not accurately capture real-world [kinematics](/classical/kinematics) due to manufacturing errors and joint flexion <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />. A humanoid robot was calibrated using 31 poses, which reduced the RMS error by a factor of 2.3 <Cite id="humanoid-geometric-calibration-2025" />. OmniCalib showed that CAD camera values had errors up to 10.56 mm <Cite id="omnicalib-2026" />.

Also: humanoid joint offsets <Cite id="humanoid-joint-offset-2026" />.

## A late frame misses by speed times delay

A frame 50 ms late shows the gripper where it was: if the gripper moves at 10 cm per second, the grasp position would be off by 5 mm. A RealSense rig was measured to have 55 ms of camera delay <Cite id="realtime-vla-v2-2026" />. Compensating for camera delay increased policy success rate by 8.1 points <Cite id="synchec-2026" />.

## Actuator response must be measured

In the absence of measured actuator response, policy error and tracking error are indistinguishable. Hwangbo and colleagues learned ANYmal's actuator dynamics from measurements <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. Actively selecting such data (SPI-Active) improved performance over baselines by 42 to 63% in locomotion transfer <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />.

Also: identified inertias <Cite id="atkeson-an-hollerbach-1986" />, Franka Panda dynamics <Cite id="gaz-panda-dynamics-2019" />, periodic excitation <Cite id="swevers-excitation-1997" />.

## Calibrations drift, so store them with their evidence

Most public datasets lack extrinsics; CalibAll recovered them for about 97,000 episodes <Cite id="calib-all-2025" />. Save each calibration alongside other relevant information (robot and camera serial numbers, settings, software version, residuals). Recalibrate after collisions, tool changes, or remounts.

Also: DROID's per-session checkerboards <Cite id="droid-2024" />.
