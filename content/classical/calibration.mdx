Robot calibration estimates the parameters that make a robot's measurements and commands agree with the physical world. Classical kinematic calibration has four steps: model, measure, identify and correct <Cite id="roth-mooring-ravani-1987" />. Learned policies inherit every calibration error.

## The calibration chain

Between the pixel where a policy sees a mug and the gripper reaching for it lie <Term id="camera-intrinsics">camera intrinsics</Term>, depth alignment, the camera mount (extrinsics), joint zeroes, link and tool dimensions, payload, timing and actuator response. An error in any link misplaces the grasp: FoundationPose tracks novel-object poses with or without a model <Cite id="foundationpose-2024" />, but a wrong camera transform still sends the hand astray, and even [visual servoing](/classical/perception) <Cite id="espiau-1992" /> needs consistent frames.

## Camera intrinsics

Intrinsics turn a pixel into a ray through focal lengths, the image center and lens distortion. Zhang's method recovers them from a flat pattern held at a few unknown angles <Cite id="zhang-2000-calibration" />, and Kannala and Brandt's generic model covers wide-angle and fisheye lenses <Cite id="kannala-brandt-2006" />. Refit after any focus, resolution or crop change, and judge it by held-out reprojection error, which automatic image filtering cut by 25% in a 2026 study <Cite id="reproducible-intrinsics-2026" />. AnyCalib estimates pixel rays from one image and fits pinhole, fisheye and other models to them <Cite id="anycalib-2025" />.

## Depth is a measurement model

Stereo depth error grows with the square of distance, and repeating patterns make matches ambiguous <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />; shiny surfaces can saturate the image <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />. Kinect's random error grows from a few millimeters to about 4 cm at maximum range <Cite id="khoshelham-kinect-2012" />, and a joint depth-and-color calibration with a depth-distortion model beat the manufacturer's <Cite id="herrera-depth-color-2012" />.

## Hand-eye calibration

Hand-eye calibration finds where the camera sits relative to the gripper or base. Shiu and Ahmad cast it as $AX = XB$, relating known arm motions $A$ to the camera's observed motions $B$ through the unknown mount $X$ <Cite id="shiu-ahmad-1989" />. Tsai and Lenz gave an efficient closed-form solution, rotation first <Cite id="tsai-lenz-1989" />, Park and Martin derived closed-form exact and least-squares solutions <Cite id="park-martin-1994" />, and Daniilidis's dual quaternions solve rotation and translation together <Cite id="daniilidis-1999" />.

<Image id="baxter-gripper-wrist-camera" />

OpenCV needs at least three poses with non-parallel rotation axes and strongly recommends many more <Cite id="opencv-hand-eye-docs-2026" />; MoveIt reports accuracy leveling off after about 12 to 15 samples <Cite id="moveit-hand-eye-tutorial-2026" />.

Markerless methods, which recognize the robot itself, now rival calibration targets. DREAM's single-image accuracy matches multi-frame offline calibration <Cite id="dream-2019" />, Kalib solves from a tracked robot point and its forward-kinematics positions <Cite id="kalib-2024" />, and Hydra reaches 5 mm task-space accuracy against 7 mm for classical methods <Cite id="hydra-hand-eye-2025" />. DRHeC reports 88.9% grasp success on a UR5e, 46.3 points above rendering-based EasyHeC <Cite id="drhec-2026" /> <Cite id="easyhec-2023" />, and AnchorHEC (November 2026) calibrates wrist and fixed cameras together, with 86.4% less simulated translation error than reconstruction baselines <Cite id="anchorhec-2026" />.

## Kinematic and tool calibration

Manufacturing errors and joint flex shift real arms from nominal [kinematics](/classical/kinematics) <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />. Nguyen and colleagues calibrated a humanoid from 31 chosen postures and cut its RMS error 2.3-fold against the manufacturer's model <Cite id="humanoid-geometric-calibration-2025" />. OmniCalib found CAD wrist-camera extrinsics off by up to 10.56 mm and 1.74 degrees, and writes back only corrections its observability checks support <Cite id="omnicalib-2026" />. Double-support stances cut one humanoid's held-out foot-height errors from 8.03 to 1.43 mm <Cite id="humanoid-joint-offset-2026" />.

## Time calibration

Kalibr estimates time offsets together with spatial transforms <Cite id="kalibr-2013" />, and IEEE 1588 syncs networked clocks to sub-microsecond accuracy <Cite id="ieee-1588-2019" />. On one RealSense D435 and Airbot Play rig, Realtime-VLA V2 measured camera, joint-reading and motion delays of 55, 50 and 150 ms <Cite id="realtime-vla-v2-2026" />. SyncHeC (November 2026) estimates hand-eye pose and camera latency together from one 6.5 s motion, and compensating the offset raised policy success by 8.1 points over 1,800 matched trial pairs <Cite id="synchec-2026" />.

## Dynamics and actuator calibration

In sim-to-real work this is system identification. Atkeson, An and Hollerbach identified load and link inertias by least squares and beat CAD-derived predictions <Cite id="atkeson-an-hollerbach-1986" />, and Gaz and colleagues first identified the Franka Panda's dynamics, joint friction and physically feasible parameters <Cite id="gaz-panda-dynamics-2019" />. Swevers and colleagues design periodic excitation motions as finite Fourier series, so measurements can be averaged over cycles <Cite id="swevers-excitation-1997" />.

Hwangbo and colleagues found actuator dynamics central to ANYmal's sim-to-real gap and learned an actuator network from real measurements <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. SPI-Active, choosing identification data actively, beat baselines by 42 to 63% in locomotion transfer <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />. Without measured response, policy error and tracking error look the same.

## Drift and ownership

DROID's per-session checkerboard calibrations were not always accurate, and the dataset provides camera-to-base calibrations for about 36,000 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />. Most public datasets lack extrinsics; CalibAll recovered them for about 97,000 episodes across 16 datasets <Cite id="calib-all-2025" />. Store each calibration with hardware serials, camera settings, tool, software version, date, procedure and residuals, and recalibrate after a remount, a collision, or a tool or firmware change.
