Robot calibration is the estimation of the parameters that make a robot's measurements and commands refer to the same physical world. Classical kinematic calibration has four steps: modeling, measurement, identification and correction <Cite id="roth-mooring-ravani-1987" />. A learning system adds cameras, depth, clocks and actuators to the chain.

## The calibration chain

A manipulation system carries <Term id="camera-intrinsics">camera intrinsics</Term>, depth alignment, camera extrinsics, joint zeroes and link parameters, tool and payload parameters, timestamps and latency, and actuator response. Each stage feeds the next. FoundationPose estimates and tracks novel-object poses with or without a model <Cite id="foundationpose-2024" />, yet a wrong hand-eye transform still sends the gripper to the wrong place. HALO first calibrates a humanoid's nominal model from real data, then identifies an unknown payload's mass distribution <Cite id="halo-payload-2026" />.

## Camera intrinsics

Intrinsics map pixels to rays through focal lengths, principal point and lens distortion. Zhang's method recovers them from a planar pattern seen at a few unknown orientations, with a closed-form solution and nonlinear refinement <Cite id="zhang-2000-calibration" />. Kannala and Brandt's generic model covers conventional, wide-angle and fisheye lenses <Cite id="kannala-brandt-2006" />. AnyCalib estimates intrinsics from one in-the-wild image for pinhole, Brown-Conrady and Kannala-Brandt models <Cite id="anycalib-2025" />. Intrinsics need revalidation after any change of focus, resolution or crop.

## Depth is a measurement model

Stereo depth error scales with the square of distance, and repetitive structures make matches ambiguous <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />. The D400 datasheet lists false depth from repetitive patterns and saturation from specular reflections <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />. Kinect random error grows from a few millimeters to about 4 cm at maximum range <Cite id="khoshelham-kinect-2012" />, and a joint depth-color calibration with a depth-distortion model beat the manufacturer's calibration <Cite id="herrera-depth-color-2012" />. Learned camera depth models reach nearly simulation-level depth accuracy for manipulation <Cite id="camera-depth-models-2025" />.

## Hand-eye calibration

Hand-eye calibration finds the gripper-to-camera transform for a wrist camera, or base-to-camera for a fixed one. Shiu and Ahmad cast it as $AX = XB$ <Cite id="shiu-ahmad-1989" />. Tsai and Lenz's solution takes about ${}100 + 64N$ arithmetic operations for $N$ robot stations, and a few milliseconds <Cite id="tsai-lenz-1989" />. Park and Martin derived closed-form exact and least-squares solutions on the Euclidean group <Cite id="park-martin-1994" />, and Daniilidis solved rotation and translation together with dual quaternions <Cite id="daniilidis-1999" />. AprilTag targets give a full 6-DoF pose from one image <Cite id="apriltag-2011" />.

OpenCV needs at least two motions with non-parallel rotation axes, so three poses, and strongly recommends many more <Cite id="opencv-hand-eye-docs-2026" />. MoveIt's tutorial reports that accuracy plateaus after about 12 or 15 samples in most cases <Cite id="moveit-hand-eye-tutorial-2026" />. Validation belongs in robot coordinates, because reprojection error alone does not close that loop.

Markerless methods now rival targets. DREAM's single-image accuracy is comparable to multi-frame offline calibration <Cite id="dream-2019" />. EasyHeC uses differentiable rendering and automatic joint-space exploration <Cite id="easyhec-2023" />, and Kalib needs only the kinematic chain and a tracked reference point <Cite id="kalib-2024" />. Hydra reaches 5 mm task-space accuracy against 7 mm for classical methods <Cite id="hydra-hand-eye-2025" />. DRHeC's authors report 88.9% grasp success on a UR5e, 46.3 points above EasyHeC <Cite id="drhec-2026" />.

## Kinematic and tool calibration

Manufacturing errors and joint compliance shift an assembled arm from its nominal [kinematics](/classical/kinematics) <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />. A tool center point is checked by touching one fixed point from several orientations. Fitting every parameter without an observability check lets errors trade off. Nguyen and colleagues calibrated a humanoid from 31 chosen postures and cut RMS error 2.3-fold against the manufacturer's model <Cite id="humanoid-geometric-calibration-2025" />. OmniCalib found CAD wrist-camera extrinsics off by up to 10.56 mm and 1.74 degrees, and writes back only corrections its observability checks support <Cite id="omnicalib-2026" />.

## Time calibration

A camera frame at time $t$ paired with joint state at $t + \Delta t$ creates a pose error proportional to speed. Kalibr estimates time offsets jointly with spatial transforms, to a fraction of the smallest measurement period <Cite id="kalibr-2013" />. IEEE 1588 clock synchronization reaches sub-microsecond accuracy across networked devices <Cite id="ieee-1588-2019" />. On a RealSense D435 and Airbot Play rig, Realtime-VLA V2 measured camera, proprioception and motion delays of 55, 50 and 150 ms <Cite id="realtime-vla-v2-2026" />.

## Dynamics and actuator calibration

Sim-to-real work calls this system identification. Atkeson, An and Hollerbach identified load and link inertial parameters by least squares, beating CAD-derived predictions <Cite id="atkeson-an-hollerbach-1986" />. Swevers and colleagues optimize finite-Fourier-series excitation for parameter uncertainty <Cite id="swevers-excitation-1997" />. Gaz and colleagues first identified the Franka Panda's dynamics, joint friction and feasible parameters <Cite id="gaz-panda-dynamics-2019" />.

Hwangbo and colleagues found actuator dynamics central to ANYmal's transfer gap and learned an actuator network from real measurements <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. SPI-Active chooses identification data actively and beats baselines by 42 to 63% in locomotion transfer <Cite id="spi-active-2025" />. Bjelonic and colleagues transferred policies to 13 legged robots without randomizing dynamics <Cite id="bjelonic-sim2real-2025" />. A dataset that stores commands without measured response cannot separate policy error from tracking error.

## Calibration versus visual servoing

Visual servoing puts the vision sensor inside the control loop and drives image features toward a target through a task function <Cite id="espiau-1992" />. Its interaction model, velocity commands and safety geometry still need consistent conventions.

## Drift and ownership

Calibrations drift. DROID's per-session checkerboard calibrations were not always accurate, and the dataset provides camera-to-base calibrations for about 36,000 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />. Most public datasets lack extrinsics, and CalibAll recovered them for about 97,000 episodes across 16 datasets <Cite id="calib-all-2025" />. Each calibration should be stored with hardware serials, camera settings, tool, software version, date, procedure and residuals, and redone after a remount, collision, tool or firmware change.
