Kinematics is the geometry of robot motion. <Term id="forward-kinematics">Forward kinematics</Term> finds the hand from the joint angles; <Term id="inverse-kinematics">inverse kinematics</Term> finds joint angles for a hand pose, and may find none, one or many <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />.

<PlanarFkArm className="my-6" />

## Forward kinematics has one answer, found by multiplication

[The arm is a chain](/how-robots-work/#arm) of rigid links, and one matrix $T$ places each link's frame relative to the last, with rotation $R$ and translation $p$ <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />:

$$
T = \begin{bmatrix} R & p \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Multiplying one per joint, base to tool, gives the gripper's pose <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />:

$$
T_0^{n}(q) = T_0^1(q_1)\,T_1^2(q_2)\,\cdots\,T_{n-1}^{n}(q_n)
$$

## Four numbers per joint describe an arm

The <Term id="denavit-hartenberg-parameters">Denavit-Hartenberg convention</Term> uses four numbers per joint: how far it turns ($\theta_i$) and slides ($d_i$), and how far away ($a_i$) and how tilted ($\alpha_i$) the next axis is <Cite id="denavit-hartenberg-1955" /> <Cite id="lavalle-2006" />.

<DhParameterTable className="my-6" />

They misbehave when neighboring axes are nearly parallel <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />; Brockett's product of exponentials, a screw motion per joint, changes smoothly instead <Cite id="brockett-1984" /> <Cite id="okamura-park-1996" />.

<Image id="puma-560-nasa-ames" />

Most robots ship as a URDF file giving each joint an origin and an axis <Cite id="urdf-dataset-2023" />, like the [3D playground](/playground)'s SO-101 arm <Cite id="so-arm100-repo-2026" />.

Also: modified DH <Cite id="corke-dh-2007" />, URDF 1.2 <Cite id="urdfdom-versions-2026" />, Pinocchio 4.1 <Cite id="pinocchio-4-1-2026" />.

## The Jacobian maps joint speed to hand speed

The <Term id="jacobian">Jacobian</Term> $J(q)$ turns joint speeds into hand speed; column $i$ is the motion joint $i$ alone gives the hand <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" /> <Cite id="siciliano-robotics-2009" />:

$$
\dot{x} = J(q)\,\dot{q}
$$

Its transpose maps a hand force $F$ to joint torques, $\tau = J^{T}F$ <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />; Whitney's 1969 resolved-rate control inverted it <Cite id="whitney-1969" />.

## At a singularity the arm loses a direction

Fully stretched, an arm cannot push its hand farther out: the Jacobian loses rank, and nearby joint speeds blow up <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />.

A seven-joint arm spends spare freedom on goals like avoiding joint limits through the pseudoinverse $J^{+}$ <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />. Near a singularity the pseudoinverse turns unstable <Cite id="buss-ik-survey-2009" />, so damped least squares trades a little hand error for small joint motions <Cite id="wampler-1986" />.

Also: manipulability <Cite id="yoshikawa-1985" />, task priority <Cite id="siciliano-slotine-1991" />, singularity-robust inverses <Cite id="nakamura-hanafusa-1986" />.

## Inverse kinematics has many answers, or none

Inverse kinematics seeks joints $q$ whose forward kinematics $k(q)$ hits a target pose $x_d$. A cup may be reachable elbow up, elbow down, endlessly with seven joints, or not at all <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="reaching" />

Six-joint arms whose wrist axes meet at a point have a formula <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />. Any six-joint revolute arm reduces to one polynomial, solved in 11 ms on average <Cite id="raghavan-roth-1993" /> <Cite id="manocha-canny-1994" />. The general method iterates on the gap $\Delta x$ to the target <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />:

$$
\Delta q = J^{+}(q)\,\Delta x
$$

| Solver | Kind | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IKFast (2010) | Generated formula | About 6 microseconds, against about 10 ms for most numerical solvers <Cite id="ikfast-diankov-2010" /> |
| TRAC-IK (2015) | Numerical | Solves far more often than the KDL library, which reports false failures on humanoids <Cite id="trac-ik-2015" /> |
| mink | Numerical, on MuJoCo | Best joint speed within limits, each step <Cite id="mink-ik-2026" /> |
| PyRoki (2025) | Least squares, with retargeting and planning | CPU, GPU or TPU <Cite id="pyroki-2025" /> |
| cuRobo (2023) | GPU | Over 7,000 collision-free queries per second <Cite id="curobo-2023" /> |
| cuRoboV2 (2026) | GPU | 99.6% collision-free on a humanoid with 48 degrees of freedom; prior methods failed entirely <Cite id="curobo-v2-2026" /> |
| IKFlow (2021) | Learned sampler | 2,000 solutions in under 10 ms, within 10 mm and 2 degrees <Cite id="ikflow-2021" /> |

## Learned policies inherit this geometry

ACT's [action chunks](/manipulation/action-chunking) hold 14 joint targets for two arms <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />; OpenVLA predicts a 7-number hand action <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Across 13,000 real rollouts, relative actions helped, joint space favored stability and hand space favored generalization <Cite id="action-space-design-2026" />.

Quaternions and Euler angles are discontinuous for networks; 5D and 6D rotations are continuous <Cite id="rotation-continuity-2018" />.

Also: Diffusion Policy <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, GR00T N1 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, GR00T N1.7 <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

## Coordinate frames are an API

A hand [action](/manipulation/action-spaces) must name its frame, units, rotation format and whether it is relative. REP 103 makes ROS frames right-handed, x forward, y left, z up <Cite id="rep-103-2010" />; REP 105 separates the drifting `odom` frame from the jumping `map` <Cite id="rep-105-2010" />.

Geometry must match across machines. LeRobot [calibrates](/classical/calibration) SO-101 arms to read the same values in the same pose, so a network trained on one works on another <Cite id="lerobot-so101-docs-2026" />. The GELLO <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> leader copies its target arm's structure <Cite id="gello-2023" />.

Also: tf <Cite id="tf-library-2013" />, humanoid retargeting <Cite id="gmr-retargeting-2025" />.
