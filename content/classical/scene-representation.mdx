Scene representation is the form in which a robot stores what it knows about its surroundings so that planners, controllers and other consumers can query it <Cite id="cadena-2016" />. Representations range from point clouds and occupancy grids to signed-distance fields, meshes and neural fields such as radiance fields and Gaussian splats <Cite id="cadena-2016" /><Cite id="neural-fields-survey-2024" />.

## The ladder, and what each rung can answer

Three queries separate the rungs: whether a cell is free for a planner, which way a surface faces for a contact solver, and what the scene looks like from a viewpoint the sensor never occupied. Cadena and colleagues judge representations by storage size, construction cost and usefulness for the task <Cite id="cadena-2016" />.

An occupancy map can hold an occluded region as explicitly unknown, as OctoMap does for free and unknown space alongside occupied space <Cite id="octomap-2013" />. Learned reconstructors fill such regions instead: CUT3R can infer unseen regions of a scene by probing virtual, unobserved views <Cite id="cut3r-2025" />.

### Point clouds and occupancy grids

A <Term id="point-cloud">point cloud</Term> is an unordered set of 3D points, and PointNet requires invariance to the order in which they arrive <Cite id="pointnet-2017" />. A region without points is either empty or unobserved, and the store cannot say which.

Moravec and Elfes projected wide-angle sonar readings from different sensors and robot positions onto a 2D <Term id="occupancy-grid">occupancy grid</Term>. Each reading marked a probably empty beam interior and an uncertain reflecting point near the measured range, and overlapping readings refined these constraints. Zero represented unknown occupancy, and negative and positive values probably empty and probably occupied cells <Cite id="moravec-elfes-1985" />. OctoMap carries the three states into 3D in a compressed octree <Cite id="octomap-2013" />.

### Signed-distance fields

Curless and Levoy fuse range images into a cumulative weighted <Term id="signed-distance-field">signed distance function</Term> and recover the surface at its zero crossing, truncating distance ramps and weights near observed points. Their hole-filling construction marks empty space with negative $D_{min}$ and unseen space with positive $D_{max}$, both at zero weight <Cite id="curless-levoy-1996" />. KinectFusion uses the opposite sign: positive toward visible free space and negative on the non-visible side <Cite id="kinectfusion-2011" />.

KinectFusion fused 11-bit, 640×480 Kinect depth frames at 30 Hz, tracking each frame against the global model by coarse-to-fine ICP and mapping at the sensor rate on a commodity GPU. A large plane filling most of the view can cause drift or tracking failure. Its projective TSDF caps visible-space values at the truncation distance $\mu$, leaves non-visible points beyond $\mu$ unmeasured and is exact at the surface, so fusion yields an approximate pseudo-Euclidean distance. Normals come from numerical derivatives near the zero level set, and ray marching finds surfaces by trilinear interpolation <Cite id="kinectfusion-2011" />.

Trajectory optimizers need obstacle distances. Voxblox builds TSDFs faster than OctoMap builds its maps, then derives Euclidean signed distance fields (ESDFs) from them incrementally, more accurately than from occupancy <Cite id="voxblox-2016" />. On GPUs, nvblox reports up to 177x faster surface reconstruction and 31x faster distance-field computation <Cite id="nvblox-2023" />. Voxel hashing stores surface data densely only where measurements are observed <Cite id="voxel-hashing-2013" />.

### Meshes, and then neural fields

A mesh adds connectivity, giving each face a normal and a renderable surface, but leaves holes where nothing was observed, and filling them invents geometry <Cite id="curless-levoy-1996" />.

NeRF fits a <Term id="neural-radiance-field">neural radiance field</Term> to a static scene from posed RGB photographs, intrinsics and scene bounds. Its MLP predicts density from position and colour from position and viewing direction, and differentiable volume rendering lets image error drive optimization <Cite id="nerf-2020" />. Without regularization, a radiance field can fit its training images perfectly "for an arbitrary incorrect geometry", and Zhang and colleagues show that NeRF's MLP structure helps avoid this shape-radiance ambiguity <Cite id="nerfpp-2020" />. Instant NGP cut optimization cost with a multiresolution hash encoding, smaller networks and fully fused CUDA kernels. It rendered one scene at 1080p in 5 seconds over 128 samples and a large 360-degree scene at 10 frames per second on one RTX 3090 <Cite id="instant-ngp-2022" />. <Term id="gaussian-splatting">3D Gaussian splatting</Term> optimizes Gaussian positions, anisotropic covariances, opacities and spherical-harmonic appearance from calibrated photographs, then rasterizes by alpha-blending sorted splats. It reports real-time 1080p rendering, with results on an A6000 GPU <Cite id="3dgs-2023" />. A 2024 survey of over 200 robotics papers covers four neural-field frameworks: occupancy networks, signed distance fields, radiance fields and splats <Cite id="neural-fields-survey-2024" />.

DUSt3R predicts dense pointmaps from a pair of RGB images without intrinsics or poses, in the first image's frame and at unknown scale, trained with geometric supervision from pretrained CroCo weights. For an image collection, it then optimizes the alignment of pairwise pointmaps in 3D <Cite id="dust3r-2024" />. MASt3R adds a dense local-feature head so matching rests on the same 3D prediction <Cite id="mast3r-2024" />. Feed-forward models now cover whole collections. VGGT infers cameras, point maps, depth maps and point tracks from one, a few or hundreds of views in under one second <Cite id="vggt-2025" />. MapAnything accepts optional intrinsics, poses or depth and regresses metric geometry and cameras <Cite id="mapanything-2025" />. π³ drops the reference view, an anchor its authors call a source of instability <Cite id="pi3-2025" />. Depth Anything 3 reports average camera pose accuracy 44.3% higher than VGGT <Cite id="depth-anything-3-2025" />.

## A radiance field is geometry for rendering

The [world-models taxonomy](/world-models/taxonomy) treats a splat reconstruction as a learned renderer: appearance is learned and physics is not. In the original 3DGS, learned opacity is an image-space alpha-compositing weight with no free, occupied or unknown label, and the stored primitive is a volumetric Gaussian <Cite id="3dgs-2023" />. The paper names mesh reconstruction as an open direction <Cite id="3dgs-2023" />. SuGaR then aligned Gaussians with surfaces and extracted meshes by Poisson reconstruction <Cite id="sugar-2023" />. 2D Gaussian Splatting collapses the volume into oriented planar disks for view-consistent geometry, since 3D Gaussians fail to represent surfaces accurately <Cite id="2dgs-2024" />.

Simulators pair splats for appearance with separate physics. SplatSim renders with splats while PyBullet supplies physics <Cite id="splatsim-2024" />. Its authors report zero-shot real-world transfer of diffusion policies on four rigid-body tasks with a UR5 and Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, after manual robot segmentation and ICP alignment <Cite id="splatsim-2024" />. In RoboGSim's closed-loop evaluator, a policy acts on splat-rendered images while Isaac Sim handles inverse kinematics and physical interaction, from multi-view images, supplied MDH parameters and mesh assets <Cite id="robogsim-2024" />. PhysTwin combines spring-mass simulation, generative shape models and Gaussian splats for deformable objects <Cite id="phystwin-2025" />.

Other fields serve a planner directly. Dex-NeRF renders transparency-aware depth from learned density for a grasp planner, where a RealSense camera failed to compute depth for most transparent objects tested <Cite id="dex-nerf-2021" />. Distilled feature fields add semantics from 2D foundation models to 3D geometry for language-guided 6-DoF grasping <Cite id="f3rm-2023" />. OccWorld predicts how a 3D occupancy grid evolves and plans competitively without instance or map supervision <Cite id="occworld-2023" />.

ParticleSplat, from Deepak Pathak (Carnegie Mellon) and colleagues (September 2026), encodes posed multi-view images into latent particles that decode to 3D Gaussians, trained with a novel-view synthesis objective. Object masks emerge without labels, moving a particle moves its object, and the representation improved downstream manipulation <Cite id="particlesplat-2026" />.

## SLAM: the system that builds the map

Simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) builds such maps from sensor data while the robot moves without knowing its exact pose. Cadena and colleagues separate a sensor-dependent front end, which abstracts measurements and associates them with state variables, from a back end that performs inference and feeds information back for loop-closure detection and validation <Cite id="cadena-2016" />. [State estimation](/classical/state-estimation) covers the inference machinery.

Front ends are feature-based or direct. Monocular ORB-SLAM (2015) uses ORB features for tracking, local mapping, relocalization and loop closing, triangulating matched features and running local bundle adjustment. Its DBoW2 vocabulary proposes relocalization and loop candidates that need geometric validation <Cite id="orb-slam-2015" />. DBoW2's binary bag of words takes 22 ms per frame on a 26,300-image sequence <Cite id="dbow2-2012" />. ORB-SLAM3 supports stereo, RGB-D and visual-inertial modes with pinhole or fisheye cameras and MAP-based IMU initialization. It starts a new map after persistent tracking loss and merges maps only after geometric and covisibility checks, plus gravity-direction checks for mature visual-inertial maps. Low texture remains the main failure case, and slow motion can delay inertial initialization <Cite id="orb-slam3-2021" />.

Direct Sparse Odometry skips the detector and optimizes photometric error over sampled pixels jointly with geometry <Cite id="dso-2018" />. Features are robust to brightness change, while a direct method can sample any region with intensity gradient, including near-featureless walls, and gains from calibrating exposure, vignetting and camera response <Cite id="dso-2018" />. Learned systems keep classical machinery: DROID-SLAM iterates camera pose and per-pixel depth through a dense bundle-adjustment layer <Cite id="droid-slam-2021" />.

Revisiting mapped landmarks can reduce odometric drift and in some cases correct it, and visual-inertial methods drift less than wheel odometry <Cite id="cadena-2016" />. Monocular ORB-SLAM validates a <Term id="loop-closure">loop closure</Term> geometrically, then spreads its error with similarity alignment and Essential Graph optimization <Cite id="orb-slam-2015" />. <Term id="place-recognition">Place recognition</Term> asks whether the current image matches a stored place, and motion information can also inform that belief. Viewpoint, illumination and seasonal changes alter one place's appearance, and different places can look alike, which is perceptual aliasing <Cite id="lowry-2016-place-recognition" />. AnyLoc builds place recognition on off-the-shelf self-supervised features, works from urban to underwater and subterranean settings without retraining, and reports up to 4x higher performance than prior approaches <Cite id="anyloc-2023" />.

Perceptual aliasing can create false-positive associations that corrupt back-end estimates, while false negatives discard useful measurements. Robust losses and loop-closure validation mitigate outliers, though failures remain that depend on initialization <Cite id="cadena-2016" />. Graduated non-convexity solvers tolerate 70 to 80% outliers, without a guarantee of global optimality <Cite id="gnc-2019" />.

In the back end, Square Root SAM estimates the trajectory and landmark map jointly by MAP inference, which under Gaussian models with known data associations is sparse nonlinear least squares. It solves successive linearized systems by QR or Cholesky, and variable ordering controls fill-in <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />. The iSAM2 method adds incremental reordering and relinearization on a Bayes tree. Thresholded updates trade some accuracy for speed, and a large loop closure can cost as much as a batch solve <Cite id="kaess-2012" />. Smoothing suits loop closure because relinearizing the past requires the past to stay in the graph. Cadena and colleagues contrast sparse landmark maps with dense models suited to obstacle avoidance, and cite octrees and voxel hashing as efficient volumetric storage. Two rooms can look identical to a 2D laser yet differ to a camera. Severe illumination changes defeat bag-of-words methods, and active range cameras work in dark, untextured scenes but face range and external-light limits <Cite id="cadena-2016" />.

The iMAP system learns a scene-specific MLP from scratch at room scale from a handheld RGB-D stream. It tracks at 10 Hz and maps at 2 Hz on a desktop CPU and GPU, retaining keyframes for replay <Cite id="imap-2021" />. NICE-SLAM uses hierarchical feature grids with pretrained geometry decoders, reconstructs a multi-room apartment and performs no loop closure <Cite id="nice-slam-2022" />. Its 47 ms tracking and 130 ms mapping times use small pixel samples on an RTX 3090 <Cite id="nice-slam-2022" />. SplaTAM maps with 3D Gaussians from an RGB-D camera and uses a silhouette mask to determine whether areas were previously mapped <Cite id="splatam-2023" />. Gaussian Splatting SLAM runs monocular SLAM live at 3 fps with Gaussians as the only representation <Cite id="monogs-2023" />. Systems on learned 3D priors report higher frame rates: MASt3R-SLAM runs at 15 FPS with loop closure and second-order global optimization <Cite id="mast3r-slam-2024" />. SLAM3R reconstructs from monocular video at 20+ FPS without solving camera parameters <Cite id="slam3r-2024" />. VGGT-SLAM aligns submaps with 15-degree-of-freedom homographies, the projective ambiguity of uncalibrated cameras <Cite id="vggt-slam-2025" />.

## The deployed answer: costmaps and two planners

A navigation stack plans over a <Term id="costmap">costmap</Term>: occupancy from the map and live sensors, inflated by the robot's footprint. Lu, Hershberger and Smart's layered costmaps keep an ordered list of semantically separate layers and accumulate them into a master 2D costmap. Each update gathers the layers' bounds, then lets each layer write the master grid within them; a configuration setting decides whether sensed obstacles overwrite static-map costs <Cite id="layered-costmaps-2014" />. Cartographer, a 2D LIDAR SLAM system, maps and closes loops in real time at 5 cm resolution on a backpack platform <Cite id="cartographer-2016" />.

Navigation2, built on ROS 2, uses a layered costmap. Its global planner computes a shortest route to the goal, and a separate controller computes a local path and control signals, each a plugin in an asynchronous server <Cite id="nav2-2020" />. Its Smac Planner provides cost-aware A\*, Hybrid-A\* and State Lattice planners for Ackermann, legged and large non-circular robots <Cite id="smac-planner-2024" />. Its `track_unknown_space` option defaults to false, which treats unknown space as free; keeping the unknown state requires enabling it <Cite id="nav2-costmap-2d-2026" />. The planning algorithms are those of [motion planning](/classical/motion-planning) on a 2D grid.

## A representation is a service contract

[Perception](/classical/perception) locates objects in the current frame. Consumers issue different queries: localization needs stable landmarks, collision checking needs occupied volume and a conservative update rule, grasping needs surface pose and identity, and rendering needs appearance. Hydra stacks rungs in one real-time system, building a local ESDF, extracting a topological map of places from it and segmenting places into rooms <Cite id="hydra-2022" />. ConceptFusion fuses pixel-aligned open-set features into 3D maps through traditional SLAM <Cite id="conceptfusion-2023" />. ConceptGraphs builds open-vocabulary, graph-structured 3D scene representations because per-point feature maps do not scale well to larger environments <Cite id="conceptgraphs-2023" />.

SceneLM, from Jitendra Malik (UC Berkeley), Marco Pavone and colleagues (September 2026), keeps the whole map as a structured text list of objects that one vision-language model edits after each image. On language-grounded retrieval and localization benchmarks it performed competitively with complete mapping pipelines, with a map 6 to 12 times more compact, and it ran online on an edge device aboard a quadruped <Cite id="scenelm-2026" />.

A deployed map publishes its frame, timestamp, resolution, unknown-state convention, update rate and invalidation rule. In MoveIt 2, the planning scene holds the robot's current state, its model and the world representation for collision checking, and a monitor maintains it from parameters and topic subscriptions <Cite id="moveit-planning-scene-2026" />. Attached or removed objects must reach that view before motion; the [ROS 2 guide](/classical/ros2-for-ml-engineers) covers transforms and the planning scene. A scene representation answers what is present now, while a world model must answer how state changes under an action, as the [simulator comparison](/world-models/world-models-vs-simulators) sets out.
