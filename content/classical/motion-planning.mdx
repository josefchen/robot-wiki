Motion planning finds a collision-free route from a robot's posture to a goal, a sequence of joint angles that [kinematics](/classical/kinematics) turns into poses <Cite id="lavalle-2006" />. Sampling planners connect random postures; trajectory optimizers bend a whole motion until it is smooth and clear <Cite id="ratliff-2009" /><Cite id="schulman-2013" />.

## In configuration space the robot is a point

In <Term id="configuration-space">configuration space</Term>, one point $q$ lists every joint value: a seven-joint arm is a point in 7 dimensions. Postures that touch an obstacle form $\mathcal{C}_{obs}$, the rest is free space $\mathcal{C}_{free} = \mathcal{C} \setminus \mathcal{C}_{obs}$, and planning becomes finding a curve through free space <Cite id="lozano-perez-1983" />.

Mapping that space exactly is intractable: the generalized mover's problem is PSPACE-hard <Cite id="reif-1979" />, even for the basic version with unbounded <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term> <Cite id="lavalle-2006" />. Sampling planners skip the map and only ask a collision checker about particular postures and short paths <Cite id="lavalle-2006" />.

Constraints make sampling harder: a robot holding a tray level lives on a thin slice of its space that random samples almost never hit <Cite id="kingston-constraints-2018" />. MCVAMP projects many samples onto that slice at once with CPU vector instructions <Cite id="mcvamp-2026" />, and ReVAMP samples it directly through analytic inverse kinematics <Cite id="revamp-2026" />.

Also: Canny's roadmap <Cite id="solovey-complexity-2020" />.

## Sampling planners grow graphs through free space

The probabilistic roadmap (PRM) builds a graph of free postures once and answers many queries by graph search <Cite id="kavraki-1996" />. The rapidly-exploring random tree (RRT) grows one tree from the start: it extends the nearest node toward a random point and keeps the branch if it is free <Cite id="lavalle-1998" />. Frontier nodes are nearest to the most empty space, so the tree is pulled outward.

<ExplainerFigure id="path" />

RRT-Connect grows trees from start and goal until they meet <Cite id="rrt-connect-2000" />, and led a 2023 comparison on six manipulation problems <Cite id="orthey-review-2023" />. These planners are probabilistically complete: if a solution exists, the chance of finding it approaches one as sampling continues <Cite id="lavalle-2006" />. Karaman and Frazzoli proved it for RRT and a simplified PRM <Cite id="karaman-frazzoli-2011" />.

Also: kinodynamic RRT <Cite id="lavalle-kuffner-2001" />, FLASK <Cite id="flask-2026" />, OMPL <Cite id="ompl-2012" />, OMPL 2.0 <Cite id="ompl-2-2026" />.

## Parallel hardware keeps sampling ahead

Kinematics and collision checks take much of a planner's time, and both parallelize. On a GPU, sampling solved about 90% of feasible 14-joint dual-arm problems in 2 seconds, while no search or optimization planner solved a third <Cite id="prismplan-2026" />.

| --- | --- | --- |
| VAMP (2023) | CPU vector instructions | Speedups over 500x on 7 to 14 joint robots <Cite id="vamp-2023" />; an OMPL backend at up to 25 kHz <Cite id="ompl-release-notes-2026" /> |
| pRRTC (2025) | GPU, parallel tree growth and collision checks <Cite id="prrtc-2025" /> | 98% of Panda problems in PrismPlan's 2-second test <Cite id="prismplan-2026" /> |
| ReVAMP (2026) | CPU vector instructions | Constrained plans in microseconds to milliseconds, up to 20 dimensions <Cite id="revamp-2026" /> |

| cuRobo (2023) | GPU | Hard problems in 50 ms on average, 60 times faster than prior trajectory optimization <Cite id="curobo-2023" /> |

## RRT\* rewires its way to the optimal path

RRT's best path converges, almost surely, to a suboptimal one even in empty space <Cite id="karaman-frazzoli-2011" />. RRT\* rewires: each new node takes the cheapest free parent among its neighbors, which switch to it when that shortens their route, within a radius <Cite id="karaman-frazzoli-2011" />

$$
r_n = \min\!\left\{\eta,\; \gamma\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/d}\right\},
$$

for $n$ nodes in $d$ dimensions, with step cap $\eta$ and $\gamma$ above a bound set by the free space's volume. The best cost then converges to the optimum with probability one, for order $n\log n$ work <Cite id="karaman-frazzoli-2011" />. In 2020 Solovey and colleagues found a gap in the original proof; the corrected proof suggests a larger radius, scaling as $(\log n/n)^{1/(d+1)}$ <Cite id="solovey-rrt-star-2020" />.

| Planner | How it improves paths faster |
| --- | --- |
| FMT\* (2013) | Grows a tree over a fixed batch of samples in cost order <Cite id="fmt-star-2013" /> |
| Informed RRT\* (2014) | After a first solution, samples only the ellipsoid that could hold a shorter path <Cite id="gammell-2014" /> |
| BIT\* (2017) | Orders batches of candidate connections by the cost they could give <Cite id="bit-star-2017" /> |
| AO-RRT-Connect (2025) | Adds asymptotic optimality to RRT-Connect, keeping its fast first answer <Cite id="aorrtc-2025" /> |

## Trajectory optimization pushes a whole motion clear

<Term id="trajectory-optimization">Trajectory optimization</Term> treats the whole motion as the unknown and lowers a cost for jerkiness, obstacle proximity and the task. CHOMP and TrajOpt can start from a guess that passes through an obstacle and push it out, with no guarantee of success <Cite id="ratliff-2009" /><Cite id="schulman-2013" />. Graphs of Convex Sets poses planning as a mixed-integer program whose tight relaxation usually rounds to a globally optimal trajectory <Cite id="gcs-2022" />.

CHOMP's cost over the waypoints $\xi$ is a smoothness term plus an obstacle term <Cite id="ratliff-2009" />:

$$
\mathcal U(\xi)=f_{\mathrm{prior}}(\xi)+f_{\mathrm{obs}}(\xi)
$$

Each gradient step spreads smoothly along the whole path. On six joints of a Barrett WAM arm, CHOMP solved 99 of 105 problems <Cite id="ratliff-2009" />.

TrajOpt solves a sequence of convex subproblems inside a trust region, with penalties that grow until hard constraints hold. Its collision penalty $\max(d_{\mathrm{safe}}-\operatorname{sd},\,0)$ covers the swept volume between waypoints, so it catches collisions between them <Cite id="schulman-2013" />.

<Image id="atlas-vrc-simulation-2013" />

Its 2013 simulated benchmark ran 198 PR2 arm problems and 96 full-body ones <Cite id="schulman-2013" />.

Deployments run in two stages: a sampling planner finds a feasible path, then shortcutting, the cheapest cleanup, joins path points with shorter free segments <Cite id="ratliff-2009" /> <Cite id="hauser-shortcut-2010" />.

Also: Hamiltonian Monte Carlo CHOMP <Cite id="chomp-ijrr-2013" />, gradient-free STOMP <Cite id="stomp-2011" />, TrajOpt on Atlas <Cite id="trajopt-ijrr-2014" />, cuRobo on a gantry <Cite id="industrial-curobo-2025" />, MR-POP <Cite id="mr-pop-2026" />.

## Learned planners trade guarantees for speed

Motion Policy Networks trained on over 3 million problems in over 500,000 [environments](/world-models/generative-sim) <Cite id="mpinets-2022" />, yet neural planners often fail in unseen settings <Cite id="neural-motion-planner-survey-2026" />. Deep Reactive Policy switched its expert from AIT\* to GPU-based cuRobo to scale to 10 million trajectories <Cite id="deep-reactive-policy-2025" />.

Ariadne learns from valid postures alone. On two seven-joint arms it averaged 53.3% success against 44.7% for RRT, training one planner per scene <Cite id="ariadne-2026" />.

Also: Neural Motion Planning <Cite id="neural-mp-2024" />, receding-horizon MPC <Cite id="mayne-2000" />, Diffusion Policy <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, [MPC against learned policies](/rl-sim2real/reward-design-mpc).

## A plan is only as safe as its execution

A deployable planner needs the robot model, current state, collision geometry, limits and a [controller](/classical/control) that can follow the result; a path checked against a stale scene is unsafe. The [ROS 2 guide](/classical/ros2-for-ml-engineers) maps these onto the runtime.

A planner's path has no clock. Time-Optimal Trajectory Generation finds the fastest timing within velocity and acceleration limits <Cite id="totg-2012" />, as MoveIt 2 advises <Cite id="moveit-trajectory-processing-2026" />, and jerk-limited Ruckig <Cite id="ruckig-2021" /> can smooth its output <Cite id="moveit-time-parameterization-2026" />.

Payloads change what is executable. cuRoboV2 reports 99.7% success under a 3 kg payload against 72 to 77% for baselines <Cite id="curobo-v2-2026" />. cuRoboV2-Fast plans those motions in 4.4 ms on an RTX 5090 <Cite id="curobo-v2-fast-2026" />.

Validators fail too. MoveIt Pro 10.1.0 fixed a bug that passed a colliding trajectory as free <Cite id="moveit-pro-10-1-2026" />. Release 10.2.0 re-validates every plan, cutting residual tunneling 7 to 12 times on PickNik's benchmark <Cite id="moveit-pro-10-2-2026" />.
