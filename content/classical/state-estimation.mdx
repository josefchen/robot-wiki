State estimation is the computation of a robot's state, such as its [configuration](/classical/kinematics) or velocity over ground, from noisy, biased and intermittent sensor readings. Probabilistic estimators report a belief, so each estimate carries its uncertainty <Cite id="thrun-2005" />. [Planning](/classical/motion-planning) and [control](/classical/control) assume that this state is known.

## The Bayes filter

A belief $bel(x_t)$ is a probability distribution over the state, kept current by two alternating steps. The predict step pushes the belief through the process model, given the control $u_t$ <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
\overline{bel}(x_t) = \int p(x_t \mid u_t, x_{t-1}) \, bel(x_{t-1}) \, dx_{t-1}
$$

The update step folds in the measurement $z_t$ through the sensor model $p(z_t \mid x_t)$ and sharpens the belief where the measurement points <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
bel(x_t) = \eta \, p(z_t \mid x_t) \, \overline{bel}(x_t)
$$

with normalizer $\eta$. This recursion is the Bayes filter, which Thrun, Burgard and Fox call the basis of virtually every technique in their book <Cite id="thrun-2005" />. Each tractable filter adds assumptions or approximations that make the integral computable <Cite id="thrun-2005" />. The bootstrap filter of Gordon, Salmond and Smith drops the linearity and Gaussian-noise assumptions and applies to any state transition or measurement model <Cite id="gordon-salmond-smith-1993" />. Monte Carlo localization used samples to localize a mobile robot without knowing its starting location <Cite id="dellaert-mcl-1999" />.

## The Kalman filter

In Kalman's 1960 filter the recursion reduces to matrix algebra. The <Term id="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</Term> assumes linear process and measurement models and Gaussian noise <Cite id="kalman-1960-filter" />:

$$
x_t = A_t x_{t-1} + B_t u_t + w_t, \qquad z_t = C_t x_t + v_t
$$

with $w_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q_t)$ and $v_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_t)$; Probabilistic Robotics swaps the two symbols. A Gaussian belief then stays Gaussian, so the recursion tracks only a mean and covariance. Predict <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
\bar{\mu}_t = A_t \mu_{t-1} + B_t u_t, \qquad \bar{\Sigma}_t = A_t \Sigma_{t-1} A_t^{\top} + Q_t
$$

Update, through the Kalman gain $K_t$ <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
K_t = \bar{\Sigma}_t C_t^{\top} \left( C_t \bar{\Sigma}_t C_t^{\top} + R_t \right)^{-1}
$$

$$
\mu_t = \bar{\mu}_t + K_t \left( z_t - C_t \bar{\mu}_t \right), \qquad \Sigma_t = \left( I - K_t C_t \right) \bar{\Sigma}_t
$$

The gain is prior uncertainty over prior-plus-sensor uncertainty. A filter that trusts its model gets a small gain and mostly ignores the sensor; one that trusts its sensor gets a gain near one. Under linear-Gaussian assumptions the recursion is the minimum-variance estimator, and Kalman's projection-based derivation makes it the best linear estimator with white noise even without Gaussian noise <Cite id="kalman-1960-filter" />. Kalman and Bucy's 1961 continuous-time filter derives a Riccati equation for the covariance of the optimal filtering error <Cite id="kalman-bucy-1961" />.

McGee and Schmidt's 1985 NASA history credits Schmidt's Ames group with adapting the filter to nonlinear circumlunar navigation in the early 1960s. By early 1961 simulations supported the approach, though operation on available flight computers was unverified. The report separates this work from Battin's Apollo studies at MIT and Potter's square-root implementation used in Apollo. Primary Apollo navigation used ground radar, with an onboard backup <Cite id="mcgee-schmidt-1985" />.

## The extended Kalman filter

Real robots are nonlinear: joint angles enter through sines and cosines, so the models become $x_t = g(x_{t-1}, u_t) + w_t$ and $z_t = h(x_t) + v_t$. The extended Kalman filter keeps the Gaussian belief, linearizes both models about the current estimate with their <Term id="jacobian">Jacobians</Term> $G_t = \partial g / \partial x$ and $H_t = \partial h / \partial x$, and propagates the covariance through the tangent system <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
\bar{\Sigma}_t = G_t \Sigma_{t-1} G_t^{\top} + Q_t, \qquad K_t = \bar{\Sigma}_t H_t^{\top} \left( H_t \bar{\Sigma}_t H_t^{\top} + R_t \right)^{-1}
$$

The mean moves through the true maps $g$ and $h$; only the covariance takes the linearized route <Cite id="thrun-2005" />. The Ames group moved from linearizing about a nominal reference trajectory to relinearizing about the *current estimated state*, which they hoped would bring "substantial advantages". An accidentally off-nominal simulation converged after initial overshoots, and the report names this modification the extended Kalman filter. It also discusses instability from round-off, inadequate statistical models and nonlinearities <Cite id="mcgee-schmidt-1985" />.

Linearization holds near the estimate, which is the uncertain quantity. Where the nonlinearity is strong over the region the covariance covers, $\Sigma$ stops describing the true error, and the filter grows overconfident and can diverge <Cite id="mcgee-schmidt-1985" /><Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />. Julier and Uhlmann summarize 35 years of experience: the EKF is difficult to implement and tune, and reliable only for systems almost linear on the time scale of the updates <Cite id="julier-uhlmann-2004" />. Li and Mourikis proved that standard EKF visual-inertial odometry is inconsistent because of how its Jacobians are computed, so it underestimates its uncertainty <Cite id="li-mourikis-2013" />. Smith, Self, and Cheeseman's stochastic map propagated first-order covariance estimates of spatial relationships and required small angular errors, because it linearizes inherently nonlinear relationships <Cite id="smith-1990" />.

The unscented Kalman filter of Julier and Uhlmann keeps the Gaussian and pushes deterministically chosen sample points through the true nonlinearity <Cite id="julier-uhlmann-1997" />. Particle filters drop the Gaussian assumption and carry the belief as a set of particles <Cite id="thrun-2005" />. Invariant filters on Lie groups remove the dependence on the estimate: in the contact-aided invariant EKF, neither the linearized error dynamics nor the linearized observation model depends on the current state estimate <Cite id="hartley-inekf-2019" />. The equivariant filter EqVIO is a consistent visual-inertial estimator with lower linearization error <Cite id="eqvio-2022" />. GTSAM 4.3.0, released September 18, 2026, ships EKF and invariant-EKF infrastructure, equivariant filtering and EqVIO support <Cite id="gtsam-4-3-2026" />.

## Factor graphs: keep the whole trajectory

Rauch, Tung and Striebel related filtered and smoothed estimates in 1965 and showed numerically that smoothing reduces errors <Cite id="rauch-tung-striebel-1965" />. In EKF-based SLAM, marginalizing old poses commits their linearized information to a Gaussian approximation that cannot be relinearized later. Full smoothing keeps the trajectory and can relinearize, though nonlinear optimization is not guaranteed to reach the global minimum <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" /><Cite id="cadena-2016" />. The posterior over the trajectory $X = \{x_0, \dots, x_T\}$ is a product of factors, one per measurement or motion constraint <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />:

$$
p(X \mid Z) \propto \prod_i \phi_i(X_i)
$$

Drawn as a bipartite graph of variable and factor nodes, this is a <Term id="factor-graph">factor graph</Term>. Kschischang, Frey, and Loeliger's 2001 paper unified inference on such graphs under the sum-product algorithm <Cite id="kschischang-2001" />. With Gaussian factors, the most probable trajectory solves one sparse nonlinear least-squares problem <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />:

$$
X^{\star} = \arg\min_X \sum_i \left\lVert h_i(X_i) - z_i \right\rVert_{\Sigma_i}^2
$$

Dellaert and Kaess's 2006 Square Root SAM jointly estimates the <Term id="slam">SLAM</Term> trajectory and landmark map, assuming known data associations, Gaussian models, a uniform landmark prior and a fixed initial frame. It solves successive linearized systems, by QR on the measurement Jacobian or Cholesky on the information matrix, and variable ordering sets fill-in and cost <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />. The iSAM method updates only the part of the factorization a new measurement touches <Cite id="kaess-2008" />. iSAM2 adds a Bayes tree with incremental reordering and relinearization: an update re-eliminates the affected cliques and their ancestors, then reattaches unaffected subtrees. Thresholds trade accuracy for computation, and a large loop closure can cost as much as a batch solve <Cite id="kaess-2012" />.

GTSAM is a BSD-licensed C++ library that implements sensor fusion for robotics and computer vision using factor graphs <Cite id="gtsam-2026" />. Version 4.3 moved to C++17 and added experimental CUDA nonlinear optimization and several legged-state-estimation implementations <Cite id="gtsam-4-3-2026" />. Building on Lupton and Sukkarieh's preintegration, Forster and colleagues' on-manifold version collapses anywhere from a few to hundreds of IMU readings between two keyframes into one factor <Cite id="forster-2017" />.

Cadena and colleagues describe a classical SLAM period from 1986 to 2004, with EKF, Rao-Blackwellised particle-filter and maximum-likelihood approaches, and an algorithmic-analysis period from 2004 to 2015 <Cite id="cadena-2016" />. In Rao-Blackwellized grid mapping, each particle carries its own map <Cite id="grisetti-gmapping-2007" />. The survey treats MAP estimation, often over factor graphs, as the de facto standard but notes EKF systems with state-of-the-art performance. The gap shrinks when EKF linearization is accurate, sliding windows are used and inconsistency is addressed, and graphs still face unbounded growth and information loss under sparsification <Cite id="cadena-2016" />. The MSCKF sliding-window filter keeps 3D feature positions out of the state and is optimal up to linearization errors <Cite id="msckf-2007" />. OKVIS argues that nonlinear optimization offers superior accuracy while staying tractable through sparsity <Cite id="okvis-2014" />. [Scene representation and mapping](/classical/scene-representation) covers what a robot keeps from the estimate.

## Where this meets the learned stack

Learned components mostly enter classical estimators as measurements with uncertainty. Backprop KF optimizes a filter as a deterministic computation graph, so convolutional networks can read raw images <Cite id="backprop-kf-2016" />. TLIO regresses 3D displacement and its uncertainty from IMU data and fuses both into a stochastic cloning EKF <Cite id="tlio-2020" />. AirIO pairs a learned inertial model with an uncertainty-aware EKF for aggressive UAV flight <Cite id="airio-2025" />. MAC-VO learns a metrics-aware keypoint covariance and uses it to weight pose graph optimization <Cite id="mac-vo-2024" />.

A September 2026 paper from Linus Kramer, William Talbot, Olga Vysotska and Marco Hutter is an example. For robots on construction sites, they replace the observation model of a Monte Carlo localization particle filter with a network that matches LiDAR scans against the building mesh, trained only on scans simulated inside that mesh. On real-world datasets it outperformed diffusion-based and ScanContext++ baselines at 18 ms per call <Cite id="mesh-mcl-construction-2026" />.

Tartan IMU, pretrained on over 100 hours of multi-platform data, reports a 36% ATE improvement over specialized models and learns online at 200 FPS <Cite id="tartan-imu-2025" />. GAIT estimates legged-robot state without an explicit contact estimator and outperforms contact-aided model-based methods on a Unitree Go1 <Cite id="gait-2026" />. MASt3R-SLAM builds on a learned two-view reconstruction prior and keeps loop closure and second-order global optimization <Cite id="mast3r-slam-2024" />.

[Latent-dynamics world models](/world-models/latent-dynamics) act as learned belief states. PlaNet's latent dynamics model has deterministic and stochastic transition components trained with variational inference <Cite id="planet-2018" />. DreamerV3 learns a model of the environment and improves its behaviour by imagining future scenarios <Cite id="dreamerv3-nature-2025" />.

## Timing is part of the state

Fusing visual and inertial measurements requires each measurement's precise sampling time <Cite id="li-mourikis-temporal-2014" />. Samples with the same receipt time can describe different instants, and most consumer cameras use rolling shutters that capture each pixel row at a slightly different time <Cite id="li-rolling-shutter-2013" />. The camera-IMU offset can be a state variable, locally identifiable except in a few degenerate motions <Cite id="li-mourikis-temporal-2014" />. OpenVINS calibrates the camera-to-IMU time offset in its sliding-window filter <Cite id="openvins-2020" />, and Kalibr estimates time offsets to a fraction of the smallest measurement period <Cite id="kalibr-2013" />.

Persistent directional residuals in per-sensor innovations point to calibration or timing bias instead of random noise. GrandTour, which its authors call the largest open-access legged-robotics dataset, supplies RTK-GNSS and total-station ground truth <Cite id="grandtour-2026" />. The [calibration guide](/classical/calibration) covers temporal residuals, and the [ROS 2 guide](/classical/ros2-for-ml-engineers) covers timestamped interfaces.
