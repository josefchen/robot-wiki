State estimation turns noisy, biased and intermittent sensor readings into a robot's state, such as its [configuration](/classical/kinematics) or speed, plus its uncertainty <Cite id="thrun-2005" />. [Planning](/classical/motion-planning) and [control](/classical/control) assume that state is known.

## Every estimator predicts, then corrects

A robot's wheels say it has rolled 10 m down a corridor, but wheels slip, so its belief spreads. Then its laser sees a doorway known to be at 9.6 m, and the belief snaps tight. The predict step pushes the belief $bel(x_t)$ through a motion model, given the command $u_t$ <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
\overline{bel}(x_t) = \int p(x_t \mid u_t, x_{t-1}) \, bel(x_{t-1}) \, dx_{t-1}
$$

The update weighs the prediction by a sensor model $p(z_t \mid x_t)$, with $\eta$ rescaling the result to a probability <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
bel(x_t) = \eta \, p(z_t \mid x_t) \, \overline{bel}(x_t)
$$

The integral is rarely computable, so each practical filter approximates it <Cite id="thrun-2005" />. The bootstrap particle filter carries the belief as a cloud of samples and works with any motion or sensor model <Cite id="gordon-salmond-smith-1993" />.

## The Kalman filter is exact for linear models

<Image id="apollo-guidance-computer-dsky" />

The 1960 <Term id="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</Term> assumes linear motion and sensor models with Gaussian noise <Cite id="kalman-1960-filter" />:

$$
x_t = A_t x_{t-1} + B_t u_t + w_t, \qquad z_t = C_t x_t + v_t
$$

$A_t$ and $B_t$ say how the state evolves under the command, $C_t$ what the sensor reads, and the noises $w_t$ and $v_t$ have covariances $Q_t$ and $R_t$. A Gaussian belief stays Gaussian, so the filter tracks only a mean $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma$. The update blends prediction and measurement through the Kalman gain $K_t$ <Cite id="thrun-2005" />:

$$
K_t = \bar{\Sigma}_t C_t^{\top} \left( C_t \bar{\Sigma}_t C_t^{\top} + R_t \right)^{-1}, \qquad \mu_t = \bar{\mu}_t + K_t \left( z_t - C_t \bar{\mu}_t \right)
$$

The gain is the prediction's uncertainty over the total: a trusted model gets a small gain, a precise sensor a gain near one. Under linear-Gaussian assumptions this is the minimum-variance estimator, and with white non-Gaussian noise the best linear one <Cite id="kalman-1960-filter" />. Also: Kalman and Bucy's continuous-time version <Cite id="kalman-bucy-1961" />, the square-root version that flew in Apollo <Cite id="mcgee-schmidt-1985" />.

## The extended Kalman filter linearizes, and can grow overconfident

Real robots are nonlinear: joint angles enter through sines and cosines, so the models become $x_t = g(x_{t-1}, u_t) + w_t$ and $z_t = h(x_t) + v_t$. The extended Kalman filter (EKF) replaces $g$ and $h$ at each step by their <Term id="jacobian">Jacobians</Term> at the current estimate. The mean moves through the true models; only the uncertainty takes the straight-line route <Cite id="thrun-2005" />.

Where the model curves sharply across the uncertainty, $\Sigma$ stops describing the true error, and the filter grows overconfident and can diverge <Cite id="mcgee-schmidt-1985" /><Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />. Julier and Uhlmann found the EKF reliable only for systems almost linear over one update <Cite id="julier-uhlmann-2004" />. Li and Mourikis proved standard EKF visual-inertial odometry inconsistent: its Jacobians make it underestimate its uncertainty <Cite id="li-mourikis-2013" />.

| Design | How it avoids the problem |
| --- | --- |
| Unscented Kalman filter | Pushes a few chosen sample points through the true model <Cite id="julier-uhlmann-1997" /> |
| Particle filter | Drops the Gaussian altogether <Cite id="thrun-2005" /> |
| Contact-aided invariant EKF | For legged robots, linearized error and observation models that do not depend on the estimate <Cite id="hartley-inekf-2019" /> |
| EqVIO | The same symmetry idea for visual-inertial odometry <Cite id="eqvio-2022" /> |
| Iterated EKF | Relinearizes several times per update; runs FAST-LIO2 at up to 100 Hz outdoors <Cite id="fast-lio2-2021" /> |

## Smoothers keep the whole trajectory

A filter forgets: once it passes a pose, that pose is frozen into a Gaussian, so a later place recognition cannot fix the path in between. Smoothing uses later measurements to improve earlier estimates, and Rauch, Tung and Striebel showed in 1965 that it reduces errors <Cite id="rauch-tung-striebel-1965" />. It can also relinearize, though nonlinear optimization has no guarantee of a global minimum <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" /><Cite id="cadena-2016" />.

The posterior over the trajectory $X$ factors into one term per constraint, $p(X \mid Z) \propto \prod_i \phi_i(X_i)$. Drawn as a graph, this is a <Term id="factor-graph">factor graph</Term> <Cite id="kschischang-2001" />. With Gaussian noise, the best trajectory fits every measurement $z_i$, weighted by its uncertainty $\Sigma_i$, as sparse least squares <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />:

$$
X^{\star} = \arg\min_X \sum_i \left\lVert h_i(X_i) - z_i \right\rVert_{\Sigma_i}^2
$$

Dellaert and Kaess's Square Root SAM solves it for the <Term id="slam">SLAM</Term> trajectory and landmark map together; the order of variable elimination sets its cost <Cite id="dellaert-kaess-2006" />.

| Tool | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| iSAM | Updates only the part a new measurement touches <Cite id="kaess-2008" /> |
| iSAM2 | Reorders and relinearizes incrementally on a tree; a large loop closure can cost a full solve <Cite id="kaess-2012" /> |
| GTSAM | The open library <Cite id="gtsam-2026" />; version 4.3 (September 2026) adds experimental GPU optimization and legged-robot estimators <Cite id="gtsam-4-3-2026" /> |
| IMU preintegration | Collapses a few to hundreds of IMU readings between keyframes into one factor <Cite id="forster-2017" /> |
| LIO-SAM | LiDAR odometry, IMU preintegration and loop closures in one iSAM2 graph <Cite id="lio-sam-2020" /> |
| LAG-IO (2026) | Body and leg IMUs plus joint encoders in a sliding window, for quadrupeds <Cite id="lag-io-2026" /> |

## Filters and smoothers now compete closely

Factor-graph optimization is the de facto standard, yet EKF systems match it when linearization is accurate, windows slide and inconsistency is handled <Cite id="cadena-2016" />. The MSCKF keeps 3D features out of its state and is optimal up to linearization errors <Cite id="msckf-2007" />; OKVIS finds nonlinear optimization more accurate and tractable through sparsity <Cite id="okvis-2014" />.

Also: Smith, Self and Cheeseman's stochastic map <Cite id="smith-1990" />, Rao-Blackwellized grid mapping with one map per particle <Cite id="grisetti-gmapping-2007" />.

## Learned parts enter as measurements

Learning mostly enters as a measurement with its own uncertainty, which the filter or graph weighs like any other sensor.

| System | What is learned | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TLIO (2020) | Displacement and its uncertainty from IMU data | Fused in an EKF <Cite id="tlio-2020" /> |
| MAC-VO (2024) | Per-keypoint uncertainty | Weights pose optimization <Cite id="mac-vo-2024" /> |
| AirIO (2025) | IMU correction for aerial robots | Paired with an uncertainty-aware EKF <Cite id="airio-2025" /> |
| Backprop KF (2016) | The whole filter | Trained end to end from raw observations <Cite id="backprop-kf-2016" /> |
| Mesh MCL (2026) | Particle-filter observation model matching LiDAR scans to a building mesh, trained on simulated scans | Beat diffusion-based and ScanContext++ baselines on real construction sites, 18 ms per call <Cite id="mesh-mcl-construction-2026" /> |
| ContactFlowFilter (2026) | Flow-matching proposal for contact estimation | 0.36 cm real-world error for one contact, 0.69 ms per step <Cite id="contact-flow-filter-2026" /> |
| GAIT (2026) | Legged-robot state without an explicit contact estimator | Beat contact-aided model-based methods on a Unitree Go1 <Cite id="gait-2026" /> |

Learned estimators can train without ground truth. Qiu, Abbeel, Shi and colleagues trained inertial odometry with no velocity or position labels, refining targets with a pose graph. On six datasets its velocity error came within 0.07 m/s of a supervised twin <Cite id="ssio-2026" />.

An ETH Zurich group read humanoid joint angles from video with a decoder trained only on 128,000 synthetic images. On three real robots its error was 18 to 32% below the best baseline <Cite id="humanoidpose-2026" />.

Also: [latent-dynamics world models](/world-models/latent-dynamics) as learned belief states, PlaNet <Cite id="planet-2018" />, DreamerV3 <Cite id="dreamerv3-nature-2025" />.

## Timing is part of the state

Visual-inertial fusion needs each measurement's exact sampling time, and the camera-IMU offset can itself be estimated as a state <Cite id="li-mourikis-temporal-2014" />. Most consumer cameras have rolling shutters that capture each pixel row at a slightly different moment <Cite id="li-rolling-shutter-2013" />. Also: OpenVINS's in-filter offset calibration <Cite id="openvins-2020" />, Kalibr's offsets to a fraction of the shortest measurement period <Cite id="kalibr-2013" />.

Compute is timing too. On a drone's computer, the LiDAR odometry most accurate on a workstation silently dropped up to two-thirds of its input while its readings looked normal <Cite id="matos-lio-benchmark-2026" />.

Residuals that keep pointing one way signal calibration or timing bias, since random noise averages out. Confirming it takes ground truth, such as the RTK-GNSS and total-station references of GrandTour, which its authors call the largest open legged-robot dataset <Cite id="grandtour-2026" />. The [calibration guide](/classical/calibration) covers temporal residuals.
