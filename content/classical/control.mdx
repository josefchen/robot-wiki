## PID corrects the error now, its history and its trend

With error $e(t) = r(t) - y(t)$ between the reference $r$ and the measurement $y$, PID commands <Cite id="fbs-2020" />

$$
u(t) = K_p\, e(t) + K_i \int_0^{t} e(\tau)\, d\tau + K_d\, \frac{de}{dt}
$$

The first term pushes against the error now, the second erases a steady offset, and the third damps overshoot. Ziegler and Nichols's 1942 tuning rule pushes the loop to oscillation, then backs off <Cite id="ziegler-nichols-1942" />. Of over 11,000 process controllers surveyed, 97% were PID <Cite id="fbs-2020" />.

A pole balanced upright by a motor obeys <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />

$$
\ddot{\theta} = \frac{g}{\ell} \sin\theta + \frac{u + \tau_{bias}}{m \ell^2}
$$

where $\theta$ is the lean, $m$ and $\ell$ the pole's mass and length, $u$ the motor torque and $\tau_{bias}$ a payload's torque. Near upright the loop holds only when $K_p > m g \ell$: the motor must out-push gravity <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />.

## LQR prices error against effort

The linear quadratic regulator (LQR) takes a linear model $\dot{x} = Ax + Bu$ and finds the feedback $u = -Kx$ that minimizes <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />

$$
J = \int_0^{\infty} \left( x^{\top} Q x + u^{\top} R u \right) dt
$$

$Q$ prices state error and $R$ prices effort. The guarantee is local: swinging the pole up needs energy shaping, with LQR catching it at the top <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />.

## MPC re-plans at every step

<Term id="mpc">Model-predictive control</Term> plans the next $N$ inputs from the measured state at every step, within the model $f$ and the limits $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{X}$ <Cite id="mayne-2000" />:

$$
\min_{u_0, \dots, u_{N-1}} \; \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left( x_k^{\top} Q x_k + u_k^{\top} R u_k \right) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k), \; u_k \in \mathcal{U}, \; x_k \in \mathcal{X}
$$

Only the first input is applied before the next solve, so fresh measurements absorb model error <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" /><Cite id="di-carlo-2018" />. Torque bounds and friction cones enter the optimization directly <Cite id="neunert-2018" />.

On the MIT Cheetah 3, convex MPC solved each problem in under a millisecond <Cite id="di-carlo-2018" />. DIAL-MPC beat RL policies by 50% on quadruped climbing, with no training <Cite id="dial-mpc-2024" />.

Also: refinery origins <Cite id="garcia-1989" /> <Cite id="qin-badgwell-2003" />, whole-body MPC on HRP-2 <Cite id="koenemann-2015" />, acados <Cite id="acados-2019" />, Crocoddyl <Cite id="crocoddyl-2019" />, MuJoCo MPC <Cite id="mjpc-2022" />, [MPC against learned policies](/rl-sim2real/reward-design-mpc).

## A whole-body QP turns every goal into torques each tick

Khatib wrote the dynamics in terms of the hand's motion, mapped to joints by the [kinematic](/classical/kinematics) <Term id="jacobian">Jacobian</Term> <Cite id="khatib-1987" />. Sentis and Khatib ranked contact and joint limits first, posture in the leftover <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term> <Cite id="sentis-khatib-2005" />.

Weighted controllers solve one quadratic program (QP) per tick:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\ddot{q},\, \tau,\, f} \quad & \sum_i w_i \, \lVert A_i \ddot{q} - b_i \rVert^2 \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & M(q)\, \ddot{q} + h(q, \dot{q}) = S^{\top} \tau + J_c(q)^{\top} f \\
& \tau_{min} \le \tau \le \tau_{max}, \qquad f \in \mathcal{F}
\end{aligned}
$$

Each task is a weighted error over accelerations $\ddot{q}$, torques $\tau$ and contact forces $f$; physics is the constraint, and $\mathcal{F}$ keeps forces inside friction cones <Cite id="kuindersma-2015" />.

Learned and model-based <Term id="whole-body-control">whole-body control</Term> both ship. Figure's Helix 02 handles balance and contact at 1 kHz <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />, and Boston Dynamics pairs Atlas's MPC with VR teleoperation to collect data <Cite id="bd-atlas-lbm-2025" />.

Also: hierarchical QP <Cite id="escande-2014" />, fast dynamics algorithms <Cite id="featherstone-2008" />, [learned whole-body control](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc).

## Impedance control makes contact a design variable

Under stiff position control, an arm wiping a tabletop that sits 2 mm higher than modeled collides hard. Hogan's <Term id="impedance-control">impedance control</Term> commands how force relates to motion, so the arm acts like a mass on a spring and damper <Cite id="hogan-1985" />.

<Term id="admittance-control">Admittance control</Term> is the mirror image: measure force at the wrist, command motion. A backdrivable arm realizes impedance directly; a stiff geared arm with a force sensor runs admittance in software.

<Term id="hybrid-force-position-control">Hybrid force/position control</Term> controls force along constrained axes and position along free ones <Cite id="mason-1981" /> <Cite id="raibert-craig-1981" />.

Learned policies now set compliance.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Adaptive Compliance Policy | Compliance varying in space and time, from demonstrations | Over 50% better than visuomotor baselines <Cite id="acp-2024" /> |
| Diffusion impedance policy | Stiffness and damping online, on a KUKA LBR iiwa | <Cite id="geiger-impedance-2025" /> |
| FLORE | Force-reactive humanoid actions | 80% success, against 26.7% position-only <Cite id="flore-2026" /> |
| ForceTwin | An object's inertia, friction and springs, from human probing | 87% of goals, against 60% with vision-language priors <Cite id="forcetwin-2026" /> |

Rules still won Intrinsic's 2026 workcell challenge: 90% of top teams inserted the connector with hand-written routines and force feedback <Cite id="intrinsic-ai-for-industry-challenge-2026" />.

Also: stiffness control <Cite id="salisbury-1980" />, blended impedance and admittance <Cite id="ott-2010" />.

## The hardware decides what force control is possible

A stiff geared arm with no force sensor cannot regulate force, which limits [dexterity](/frontier/dexterity). <Term id="series-elastic-actuator">Series elastic actuation</Term> adds a spring for shock tolerance and stable force control <Cite id="pratt-williamson-1995" />.

<Image id="baxter-rethink-innorobo-2015" />

<Term id="backdrivability">Backdrivability</Term> fixes what the hardware can attempt. Franka exposes 1 kHz torque control with Cartesian impedance <Cite id="franka-fci-docs" />, and Universal Robots offers a force mode <Cite id="ur-force-mode-docs" />.

Near people, force has limits; the lab below takes its limit from measured pain thresholds <Cite id="han-force-pain-2024" />. The 2025 ISO 10218 editions absorb the collaborative-robot tables of ISO/TS 15066 <Cite id="iso-10218-1-2025" /><Cite id="iso-10218-2-2025" /><Cite id="iso-ts-15066" /><Cite id="hartmann-iso-2026" />.

## The controller under a policy is part of its interface

A learned [action](/manipulation/action-spaces) reaches the motors through a lower controller whose type, rate and limits are part of the interface. Action type changes learning, robustness and motion quality <Cite id="peng-action-space-2016" />, and VICES lets an RL policy pick its own compliance <Cite id="martin-martin-2019" />.

Berkeley Humanoid runs its policy at 50 Hz, its estimator at 1 kHz and its PD loop at 25 kHz <Cite id="berkeley-humanoid-2024" />. A 3-billion-parameter VLA spends 46 ms reading its inputs <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />, so deadlines, bounds and safety filters sit outside the model <Cite id="safety-filter-2023" />.

## Model error is the open problem

MPC keeps a [sim-to-real](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer) gap even when it re-solves against the [measured state](/classical/state-estimation) <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />. Fixes learn a residual model <Cite id="ishihara-2024" /> or estimate a carried payload's inertia online <Cite id="foster-wbmpc-payload-2026" />, and Boston Dynamics added RL to Spot's MPC stack <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />.
