Control uses feedback to make a robot follow a reference, such as a [motion plan](/classical/motion-planning), despite model error and disturbances: measure the state, compare it with the reference, correct <Cite id="fbs-2020" />. Feedback loops run beneath any learned policy, from motor drivers to whole-body torque control.

## PID: three ways to use an error

For an error $e(t) = r(t) - y(t)$ between reference and measured output, a PID controller commands <Cite id="fbs-2020" />

$$
u(t) = K_p\, e(t) + K_i \int_0^{t} e(\tau)\, d\tau + K_d\, \frac{de}{dt}
$$

Ziegler and Nichols called the three actions proportional response, automatic reset and pre-act. Their 1942 tuning rules drive the loop into oscillation with proportional gain alone, then back off from the critical gain and period by fixed fractions <Cite id="ziegler-nichols-1942" />.

Integral action removes the steady-state offset that a constant disturbance leaves under proportional control, and it needs anti-windup when the actuator saturates. Åström and Murray quote a survey in which 97% of over eleven thousand regulatory controllers in refining, chemicals and pulp and paper used PID <Cite id="fbs-2020" />.

A torque-driven inverted pendulum, the canonical underactuated plant, obeys <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />

$$
\ddot{\theta} = \frac{g}{\ell} \sin\theta + \frac{u + \tau_{bias}}{m \ell^2}
$$

with $\theta$ measured from upright and $\tau_{bias}$ a constant torque from an off-center payload. Linearized about upright, the loop is stable only when $K_p > m g \ell$ <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />.

## State space and LQR

For a linear plant $\dot{x} = Ax + Bu$ under state feedback $u = -Kx$, the linear quadratic regulator (LQR) picks the gain that minimizes a quadratic cost trading state error against control effort <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />:

$$
J = \int_0^{\infty} \left( x^{\top} Q x + u^{\top} R u \right) dt
$$

The optimal gain is $K = R^{-1} B^{\top} P$, where $P$ solves the algebraic Riccati equation <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />:

$$
A^{\top} P + P A - P B R^{-1} B^{\top} P + Q = 0
$$

A heavier angle penalty in $Q$ stiffens the controller and a heavier torque penalty in $R$ softens it; for the linearized pendulum, the LQR gain is a PD controller. The guarantee is local: swinging the pole up from hanging takes an energy-shaping controller, with LQR catching it at the top <Cite id="tedrake-underactuated" />.

## MPC: re-solve the plan at every step

<Term id="mpc">Model-predictive control</Term> solves a short-horizon optimal control problem at every control step, from the measured state and subject to the constraints <Cite id="mayne-2000" />:

$$
\min_{u_0, \dots, u_{N-1}} \; \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left( x_k^{\top} Q x_k + u_k^{\top} R u_k \right) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k), \; u_k \in \mathcal{U}, \; x_k \in \mathcal{X}
$$

Only the first input is applied before the next solve, so feedback absorbs part of the model error, and torque limits, joint limits and friction cones enter the optimization directly.

MPC grew out of 1970s and 1980s power-plant and refinery practice, where slow, constrained, multivariable plants defeated PID <Cite id="garcia-1989" /><Cite id="qin-badgwell-2003" />. On the MIT Cheetah 3, Di Carlo and colleagues' convex MPC solved each QP in under a millisecond at 20 to 30 Hz, and one gain set served every gait from standing to gallop <Cite id="di-carlo-2018" />.

On legged hardware, DIAL-MPC optimizes full-order quadruped dynamics in real time without training and beats RL policies by 50% on climbing tasks <Cite id="dial-mpc-2024" />. [Reward design and the MPC debate](/rl-sim2real/reward-design-mpc) compares these controllers with learned policies.

## Whole-body QP: the bottom of the stack

A legged or humanoid robot's whole-body controller turns desired accelerations and contact forces into joint torques. Khatib's 1987 operational-space formulation writes the dynamics in task space <Cite id="khatib-1987" />:

$$
\Lambda(x)\, \ddot{x} + \mu(x, \dot{x}) + p(x) = F
$$

with $\Lambda$ the task-space inertia and $\mu$, $p$ the Coriolis and gravity terms, mapped to joints through the [kinematic](/classical/kinematics) <Term id="jacobian">Jacobian</Term>. Sentis and Khatib extended it to a task hierarchy, with contact and joint-limit constraints first and posture in the remaining <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term> <Cite id="sentis-khatib-2005" />, and hierarchical QP solves such priorities at control rate <Cite id="escande-2014" />. Weighted controllers instead solve one quadratic program per tick:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\ddot{q},\, \tau,\, f} \quad & \sum_i w_i \, \lVert A_i \ddot{q} - b_i \rVert^2 \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & M(q)\, \ddot{q} + h(q, \dot{q}) = S^{\top} \tau + J_c(q)^{\top} f \\
& \tau_{min} \le \tau \le \tau_{max}, \qquad f \in \mathcal{F}
\end{aligned}
$$

Its variables are joint accelerations, torques and contact forces; the rigid-body dynamics are the equality constraint, $\mathcal{F}$ holds the friction cones, and each task is one weighted row of the objective.

Learned and model-based <Term id="whole-body-control">whole-body control</Term> both ship. Figure reports that Helix 02's System 0 runs at 1 kHz and handles balance, contact and coordination across the whole body <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />, while Boston Dynamics reports that the teleoperation system behind Atlas's large behavior models pairs Atlas's model-predictive controller with a VR interface <Cite id="bd-atlas-lbm-2025" />. [Humanoid whole-body control](/rl-sim2real/humanoid-wbc) covers how learned policies relate to the QP.

## Impedance control: making contact a design variable

A stiff position-controlled arm driven into a surface 2 mm nearer than modeled turns that error into stored collision energy. Hogan's 1985 <Term id="impedance-control">impedance control</Term> instead commands the relationship between force and motion, so the arm presents a programmable mass-spring-damper: at a contact, a controller commands force or velocity, and the environment fixes the other <Cite id="hogan-1985" />.

<Term id="admittance-control">Admittance control</Term> is the dual: measure force with a wrist sensor and command motion. A backdrivable direct-drive arm realizes an impedance directly, while a stiff geared arm with a force-torque sensor runs admittance in software.

Mason's and Raibert and Craig's <Term id="hybrid-force-position-control">hybrid force/position control</Term> builds a task frame from the contact geometry and controls force along the constrained axes and position along the free ones, never both on one axis <Cite id="mason-1981" /> <Cite id="raibert-craig-1981" />. Polishing, for instance, controls force normal to the surface and position along it, which needs the contact geometry in advance.

Learned policies now set impedance: Adaptive Compliance Policy learns spatially and temporally varying compliance from human demonstrations and reports over 50% improvement over state-of-the-art visuomotor policies <Cite id="acp-2024" />. A diffusion-based impedance learner co-authored by Hogan adapts stiffness and damping online on a torque-controlled KUKA LBR iiwa <Cite id="geiger-impedance-2025" />. Intrinsic, which ran the 2026 AI for Industry Challenge, reports that 90% of the top teams in its real-workcell phase inserted the connector with rules-based routines, such as spiral searches, and force-torque feedback <Cite id="intrinsic-ai-for-industry-challenge-2026" />.

ForceTwin, from Marco Hutter (ETH Zurich) and colleagues (September 2026), fits an articulated object's inertia, friction, damping and spring or door-closer forces from a person probing it with a handheld force-sensing gripper. As the feedforward model for impedance control on a Spot and a Franka FR3, it completed 87% of goals across nine object-robot pairs, against 60% with vision-language-model priors and 57% with kinematics-only twins <Cite id="forcetwin-2026" />.

A non-backdrivable, position-controlled geared arm without a force sensor has no force channel: position error, gear ratio and the surface set the contact force, so a joint-position policy on it cannot regulate force in the contact-rich tasks of [dexterity](/frontier/dexterity). <Term id="series-elastic-actuator">Series elastic actuation</Term> adds a deliberate spring for shock tolerance and more accurate, stable force control <Cite id="pratt-williamson-1995" />.

Franka's interface exposes a 1 kHz torque-level layer with Cartesian impedance controllers <Cite id="franka-fci-docs" />, while Universal Robots' URScript force mode takes a task frame, compliant axes, target forces and position-deviation limits <Cite id="ur-force-mode-docs" />. <Term id="backdrivability">Backdrivability</Term> is an actuator decision that fixes which of these schemes the hardware can attempt.

The contact lab's transient force limit comes from published biomechanical measurements <Cite id="han-force-pain-2024" />. ISO/TS 15066:2016 holds the original collaborative-robot tables <Cite id="iso-ts-15066" />. ISO republished ISO 10218-1 and ISO 10218-2 in February 2025, withdrawing the 2011 editions <Cite id="iso-10218-1-2025" /><Cite id="iso-10218-2-2025" />, and a 2026 comparison describes the 2025 series as fully absorbing ISO/TS 15066 <Cite id="hartmann-iso-2026" />.

## The policy-to-controller contract

A learned action reaches the motors through a lower controller, so request type, rate, horizon, frame and saturation behavior belong to the interface. Peng and van de Panne showed that the action parameterization, from torques to target joint angles, affects learning, robustness and motion quality <Cite id="peng-action-space-2016" />, and the same Cartesian delta behaves differently in contact under a position controller and under an impedance controller.

Deployed stacks are multi-rate: Berkeley Humanoid runs its RL policy at 50 Hz, its state estimator at 1 kHz and its PD controller at 25 kHz <Cite id="berkeley-humanoid-2024" />.

Model latency is part of the loop: a 3-billion-parameter VLA spends 46 ms on KV-cache prefill alone on an RTX 4090 <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />. Deadline handling and hard bounds therefore sit outside the model, holding or stopping the robot when inference is late or an action is out of bounds. Data-driven controllers often lack well-understood guarantees, which motivates separate safety filters <Cite id="safety-filter-2023" />. The [action-space guide](/manipulation/action-spaces) covers interface choice.

## Where this meets the learned stack

MPC keeps a [sim-to-real](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer) gap even when it re-solves against the [measured state](/classical/state-estimation) <Cite id="mujoco-ilqr-2026" />. That gap and the computational burden still limit whole-body MPC on real humanoids, and Ishihara and colleagues patch the model with a learned residual network <Cite id="ishihara-2024" />. Boston Dynamics added reinforcement learning to Spot's existing MPC stack <Cite id="bd-spot-rl-2024" />.
