Grasp planning chooses where a hand touches an object and how hard it pushes, so the object stays held when knocked <Cite id="bicchi-kumar-2000" />. Its central test, force closure, asks whether the contacts can resist a push or twist from any direction <Cite id="murray-li-sastry-1994" />.

## A fingertip pushes only inside its friction cone

A contact model says which forces a fingertip can transmit <Cite id="salisbury-roth-1983" />. A frictionless contact pushes only along the surface normal; with friction, Coulomb's law lets the sideways force $f^t$ reach $\mu$ times the pressing force $f^n$ before the finger slips <Cite id="murray-li-sastry-1994" />:

$$
\lVert f^t \rVert \le \mu \, f^n
$$

The allowed forces form a <Term id="friction-cone">friction cone</Term> of half-angle $\arctan \mu$ around the inward normal, often approximated by a pyramid to keep the analysis linear <Cite id="murray-li-sastry-1994" /> <Cite id="prattichizzo-trinkle-2016" />. A soft fingertip can also twist about the normal, up to a limit set by how hard it presses <Cite id="murray-li-sastry-1994" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="grip" />

Cutkosky's taxonomy separates power grasps, which wrap the object, from precision grasps held in the fingertips <Cite id="cutkosky-1989" />. Simplified force-closure analysis favors power grasps <Cite id="graspqp-2025" />.

## The grasp wrench space holds every wrench the contacts can make

A wrench stacks a force and a torque into one vector. Given the object's pose from [perception](/classical/perception), each contact force $f_i$ at position $r_i$ pushes and twists the object <Cite id="murray-li-sastry-1994" />:

$$
w_i = \begin{bmatrix} f_i \\ r_i \times f_i \end{bmatrix}
$$

The <Term id="grasp-wrench-space">grasp wrench space</Term> is the convex hull of the wrenches along every friction cone's edges, at unit pressing force <Cite id="roa-suarez-2015" />. Testing whether it contains the origin is a linear program <Cite id="liu-closure-lp-1999" />.

Also: convex closure tests with linear matrix inequalities <Cite id="han-trinkle-li-2000" />.

## Force closure needs the origin strictly inside the hull

A grasp has <Term id="force-closure">force closure</Term> when its contacts can resist any external wrench <Cite id="nguyen-1988" />: the hull must contain a small ball $B_\varepsilon(0)$ around the origin <Cite id="murray-li-sastry-1994" />. An origin on the boundary leaves one disturbance unresisted, and uncertain friction or contact position can break closure <Cite id="zheng-qian-2005" />.

Without friction, <Term id="form-closure">form closure</Term> needs four contacts for any planar object but a circle, and twelve for any spatial object without rotational symmetry <Cite id="markenscoff-1990" />. With friction, three fingers suffice in the plane and four in space <Cite id="markenscoff-1990" /> <Cite id="murray-li-sastry-1994" />.

Two fingers have an exact test. Nguyen's <Term id="antipodal-grasp">antipodal grasp</Term> theorem says a two-finger planar grasp with friction is force closure if and only if the line between the contacts lies inside both friction cones <Cite id="nguyen-1988" />. On a square at $\mu = 0.7$, a top-right pair fails: its line sits 45 degrees from each normal, outside cones of half-angle 35 degrees.

Also: form versus force closure <Cite id="bicchi-1995" />, placing contacts <Cite id="mishra-1987" />, differentiable closure tests <Cite id="differentiable-force-closure-2021" />.

## The epsilon metric scores the weakest direction

Ferrari and Canny's quality $\varepsilon$ is the radius of the largest ball around the origin inside the grasp wrench space <Cite id="ferrari-canny-1992" />, the distance to its nearest face <Cite id="roa-suarez-2015" />. With unit pressing forces, it is the worst-case disturbance the grasp absorbs per unit of finger force.

The nominal score predicts real outcomes poorly. In FIRMGrasp's shake test, the nominal margin ranked a successful grasp above a failed one with probability 0.53, barely better than a coin flip <Cite id="firmgrasp-2026" />. The grasp most robust to pose error is usually not the one with the highest $\varepsilon$ <Cite id="weisz-allen-2012" />.

Also: pyramid approximation <Cite id="pokorny-kragic-2013" />, wrench subsets <Cite id="kirkpatrick-mishra-yap-1992" />, combined metrics <Cite id="rubert-grasp-metrics-2017" />, task wrench ellipsoids <Cite id="li-sastry-1988" />, differentiable FRoGGeR <Cite id="frogger-2023" />.

## Learned grasping is trained on these tests

Dex-Net 2.0 labeled 6.7 million synthetic depth images good when the expected $\varepsilon$ under pose and friction uncertainty exceeded 0.002 <Cite id="dexnet-2-2017" />. Executing the most robust [reachable](/classical/kinematics), [collision-free](/classical/motion-planning) candidate, one variant succeeded in 94% of 100 trials on 40 novel household objects <Cite id="dexnet-2-2017" />.

| System | Reported result |
| --- | --- |
| Dex-Net 4.0 (2019) | Cleared bins of up to 25 novel objects with over 95% reliability, over 300 mean picks per hour <Cite id="dexnet-4-2019" /> |
| Contact-GraspNet (2021) | Trained on 17 million simulated grasps; over 90% success on unseen objects in clutter <Cite id="contact-graspnet-2021" /> |
| AnyGrasp (2022) | Cleared bins of over 300 unseen objects, 93.3% success, over 900 mean picks per hour <Cite id="anygrasp-2022" /> |
| DexGraspNet 2.0 (2024) | 427 million dexterous grasps; 90.7% zero-shot in real clutter <Cite id="dexgraspnet-2-2024" /> |
| GraspGen-X (2026) | 2 billion grasps on procedural grippers; zero-shot to novel real grippers <Cite id="graspgen-x-2026" /> |
| OmniDex (2026) | Benchmark of about half a billion dexterous grasps over 2.6 million scenes <Cite id="omnidex-2026" /> |
| T(R,O) Grasp++ (2026) | Benchmark of about half a billion grasps, eight hands, about 50,000 objects <Cite id="tro-grasp-plus-2026" /> |

The analytic checks still catch what end-to-end models miss. In simulation, AnyGrasp's grasps failed force closure 61.3% of the time, while pipelines that sampled antipodal grasps on estimated shapes reached 1.6 to 2 times its success <Cite id="pose-shape-grasp-2026" />.

Even learned [diffusion policies](/manipulation/diffusion-policy) depend on data pipelines that solve contact mechanics: BODex synthesizes over 49 grasps per second on one RTX 3090 <Cite id="bodex-2024" />. A 2026 [controller](/classical/control) keeps a median 94% of the quality margin during execution, at 0.09 ms per solve <Cite id="grasp-distance-fields-2026" />.

Also: filtering synthesized grasps in high-fidelity simulation <Cite id="dexevolve-2026" />.
