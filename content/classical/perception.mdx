Perception for manipulation is the pipeline that turns camera and depth data into object poses in the robot's own frame. Calibration, depth sensing, detection, segmentation and pose estimation feed it, and DOPE's pose network was accurate enough for a real robot to grasp known household objects in clutter <Cite id="dope-2018" />. [Grasp planning](/classical/grasp-planning) then computes grasps against that pose.

## Calibration: what a pixel means

A camera maps a 3D point onto a 2D sensor, and its <Term id="camera-intrinsics">intrinsics</Term> describe that mapping: focal length in pixels on each axis, the principal point and lens distortion. Zhang's 2000 method shows the camera a planar target at several unmeasured orientations. It models radial distortion and gives a closed-form solution followed by maximum-likelihood nonlinear refinement. Zhang contrasts it with classical techniques that need expensive equipment such as two or three orthogonal planes <Cite id="zhang-2000-calibration" />.

### Hand-eye calibration: what a pixel means to the robot

Extrinsics place the camera relative to something else. <Term id="hand-eye-calibration">Hand-eye calibration</Term> finds the flange-to-camera transform for a wrist camera, or base-to-camera for a fixed one. Shiu and Ahmad cast it as $AX = XB$, where $A$ is a robot motion, $B$ the camera's observed motion of a static target and $X$ the unknown transform <Cite id="shiu-ahmad-1989" />. Tsai and Lenz gave an efficient closed-form technique that solves rotation first, then translation <Cite id="tsai-lenz-1989" />.

Park and Martin derived closed-form exact and least-squares solutions with Lie theory <Cite id="park-martin-1994" />. Horaud and Dornaika's form $MY = M'YB$ leaves the camera's intrinsic and extrinsic parameters implicit <Cite id="horaud-dornaika-1995" />. Daniilidis's dual quaternions solve rotation and translation simultaneously by singular value decomposition <Cite id="daniilidis-1999" />. OpenCV's hand-eye calibration defaults to the Tsai and Lenz method, needs at least two motions with non-parallel rotation axes, so three poses, and strongly recommends many more <Cite id="opencv-hand-eye-docs-2026" />.

In simulation and UR5e experiments, simultaneous methods resisted rotation noise better and separate methods handled translation noise better. A wider rotation span improved separate methods and degraded simultaneous ones, while a wider translation range did the opposite <Cite id="enebuse-2022" />. The instrument's hand-eye term is a ray-to-plane offset at separation $d$ for a ray inclined by $\theta$ to the plane's normal, about 1.7 mm at 10 cm and 17 mm at 1 m for one degree:

$$
e_\theta(d) = d \, \tan \theta
$$

## Depth sensing: what fails, and on what

Stereo triangulates features matched between two images, so a featureless surface gives it nothing to match. Intel's RGBD depth cameras add an active texture projector, a technique classical stereo used as far back as 1984, to generate texture that makes matching unambiguous <Cite id="keselman-2017-realsense" />. The March 2026 RealSense D400 datasheet lists ±2% Z-accuracy up to 2 m for D410/D415 and D43x, over an 80% region of interest at HD resolution. Range limits differ by model: up to 4 m for D450/D455/D455f/D456 and 0.5 m for D401/D405. Accuracy compares valid pixels with ground truth over 80% of the depth field of view, and factory KPIs reflect typical conditions. The D405 is tested on a textured target in about 250 lux room light, the others on a texture-less white target at the default 150 mW laser power and auto exposure <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />. RealSense has been independent of Intel since 11 July 2025 <Cite id="realsense-spinout-2025" />.

RealSense's D415/D435 tuning guide says D400 depth error scales with the square of distance. It advises moving closer while staying outside the minimum operating distance, MinZ. It describes resolution and range trade-offs when changing MinZ, and notes that focus and differing left and right views limit very-near matching <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />.

Learned models now estimate depth from images, alone or with a sparse sensor. FoundationStereo trains on a synthetic set of 1M stereo pairs for zero-shot stereo matching <Cite id="foundationstereo-2025" />. Depth Pro predicts metric monocular depth without camera intrinsics, producing a 2.25-megapixel map in 0.3 seconds on a standard GPU <Cite id="depth-pro-2024" />. Prompt Depth Anything uses a low-cost LiDAR as the prompt for metric depth at up to 4K resolution <Cite id="prompt-depth-anything-2024" />.

For industrial 3D scanning, the PhoXi 3D Scanner L page lists 0.200 mm calibration accuracy and 0.190 mm temporal noise (both 1 σ), an 870 to 2150 mm scanning range and 250 to 2750 ms scanning time. These are separate specification fields for one model <Cite id="photoneo-phoxi-l-2026" />.

Time of flight casts its own modulated near-infrared light and measures its round trip per pixel. Multi-path interference lets one pixel integrate light that arrived by several routes. The Azure Kinect DK documentation lists five causes of invalid depth: outside the IR illumination mask, saturated IR signal, low IR signal, filter outlier and multi-path interference. Invalid pixels carry depth zero. Ambiguous depth arises when light reflects from one wall onto another in a corner, and where foreground and background mix at object edges. Fast motion can increase edge invalidation <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />.

### The failure taxonomy that decides how a cell is built

- Transparent surfaces refract light, so the sensor ranges whatever lies behind them. ClearGrasp starts from the observation that transparent objects "often appear as noisy or distorted approximations of the surfaces that lie behind them". It infers surface normals, transparent-surface masks and occlusion boundaries, then *replaces* the returned depth <Cite id="cleargrasp-2020" />. TransCG supplies 57,715 real RGB-D images from 130 scenes for transparent-object depth completion <Cite id="transcg-2022" />.
- Specular reflections can saturate D400 images, while the D400f column says "Saturation mitigated" <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />. Azure Kinect lists saturated IR signal as its own invalidation cause <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />. DREDS simulates an active stereo sensor over 130K photorealistic images, and a restoration network trained on it generalizes to real depth datasets such as ClearGrasp's <Cite id="dreds-2022" />.
- Dark surfaces and low light weaken the signal. Azure Kinect invalidates a pixel whose IR signal is too weak to generate depth <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />. RealSense ties D4xx depth quality to the input images and warns of grainy low-light images, underexposure and overexposure. For its projected-pattern example, reducing background light with the projector on can raise the pattern's signal-to-noise ratio <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />.
- RealSense names fences and wire grids as scenes where left-right correspondence can become ambiguous. Its D4 processor compares the best and second-best match scores, and raising DSSecondPeakThreshold rejects more matches as zero depth <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />.
- Azure Kinect notes that a surface invalidated by multi-path from one camera view can reappear from another <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />.

The 2% figure is measured on a texture-less white target with the projector at default power <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />, and the PhoXi page publishes no target condition for its 0.200 mm figure <Cite id="photoneo-phoxi-l-2026" />. Accuracy for transparent or specular surfaces is not disclosed in either datasheet.

Depth yields a <Term id="point-cloud">point cloud</Term>, an unordered set that a network must process without depending on input order. PointNet uses a shared per-point encoder and a symmetric pooling function <Cite id="pointnet-2017" />. PointNet++ applies PointNet within radius neighbourhoods around sampled centroids, in coordinates relative to each centroid, building local features at growing scales. Density-adaptive variants combine scales where small neighbourhoods hold too few samples <Cite id="pointnet-plus-plus-2017" />.

## Detection: from pixels to candidate objects

Classical detectors return boxes over a class list fixed at training time. Mask R-CNN extends Faster R-CNN with a branch that predicts an object mask in parallel with box recognition <Cite id="mask-rcnn-2017" />. Open-vocabulary detection removes the fixed list. Grounding DINO adds language-vision fusion to the DINO detector's feature enhancer, query selection and decoder, and accepts category names or referring expressions. It performs weakly on referring expressions without REC training data. Its Swin-L model reports 52.5 AP on COCO 2017 validation after pretraining on O365, OpenImage and GoldG without COCO training images <Cite id="grounding-dino-2024" />.

OWLv2 self-trains on web image-text pairs at over 1B examples. With an L/14 model this raised AP on LVIS rare classes, which had no human box annotations, from 31.2% to 44.6% <Cite id="owlv2-2023" />. Grounding DINO 1.5 Edge reaches 75.2 FPS with TensorRT at 36.2 zero-shot AP on LVIS-minival <Cite id="grounding-dino-1-5-2024" />.

DINOv2 learns image and patch features by self-supervised pretraining on the curated LVD-142M dataset. Its transfer evaluations freeze the encoder and train linear heads for classification and segmentation, and linear or DPT heads for depth <Cite id="dinov2-2023" />. DINOv3 adds Gram anchoring against the degradation of dense feature maps during long training <Cite id="dinov3-2025" />.

## Segmentation: the grounding layer

A box around a mug also contains the table behind it, so a grasp planner needs the surface region that <Term id="semantic-segmentation">per-pixel labelling</Term> provides. Segment Anything (SAM, 2023) makes segmentation *promptable*. Its default ViT-H model was trained on SA-1B, reported as 1.1 billion automatically generated masks on 11 million images. Point, box and mask prompts condition the decoder, and an ambiguous point can return several candidate masks for a part or a whole object <Cite id="segment-anything-2023" />.

In SAM's single-point test across 23 datasets, the prompt is the ground-truth mask's center and the output SAM's most confident mask, which beats RITM's mIoU on 16. Free-form text was a separately trained, CLIP-conditioned proof of concept, and the geometric-prompt model has no demonstrated text capability. Fine structures and boundaries remain failure cases. The prompt step of about 50 ms on a browser CPU assumes a precomputed embedding, and the heavy image encoder prevents overall real-time performance <Cite id="segment-anything-2023" />. SAM 3 (2025) takes concept prompts, short noun phrases or image exemplars, and returns masks and identities for every matching instance. Its data engine produced 4M unique concept labels, and its authors report doubling the accuracy of existing systems on image and video concept segmentation <Cite id="sam3-2025" />.

SAM 2 extends prompting to video with a streaming memory of earlier predictions and prompted frames, and its October 2024 paper reports results for SAM 2.1. Its simulated interactive tests on nine video datasets use Hiera-B+ at resolution 1024 and three clicks per interacted frame. There the authors report better accuracy with over three times fewer interactions than SAM+XMem++ and SAM+Cutie; actual user effort was not measured. On images, the SA-1B-only Hiera-B+ model scores 58.9 one-click mIoU on SA-23 against SAM ViT-H's 58.1. It processes 130.1 images per second against 21.7, about six times the throughput on one A100 with image batches of 10, bfloat16 and compiled encoders. The image evaluation spans 37 datasets <Cite id="sam2-2024" />. Objects are inferred independently, and failures persist after shot changes or long occlusions, in crowded scenes and on thin, fast-moving details <Cite id="sam2-2024" />.

Grounded SAM feeds Grounding DINO's open-set boxes to SAM and reports 48.7 mean AP on the zero-shot SegInW benchmark <Cite id="grounded-sam-2024" />. CNOS segments unseen objects by matching DINOv2 descriptors of SAM proposals against references built from CAD models, surpassing prior methods by 19.8% AP on BOP's seven core datasets <Cite id="cnos-2023" />.

<Term id="promptable-segmentation">Promptable segmentation</Term> supplies object masks to MOKA and ReKep, two [keypoint interfaces](/manipulation/hierarchical) with different perception stacks. MOKA uses GroundedSAM masks to propose marked candidate points, then prompts a pretrained VLM to select grasp, function and target keypoints, waypoint regions and motion attributes. Calibrated depth lifts the points into the robot frame, and a separate grasp sampler and motion pipeline produce actions; the prompted method trains no robot policy, though the paper separately studies distilling successful trajectories into one <Cite id="moka-2024" />.

ReKep proposes semantic keypoints from DINOv2 features within SAM masks and calibrated RGB-D geometry. A large vision-language model writes Python sub-goal and path constraints from a marked image and an instruction, and a solver repeatedly plans end-effector motion from tracked keypoints. The solver penalizes constraint violations without guaranteeing physical feasibility, and rigidity assumptions and tracking failures remain limitations. Some evaluations use human annotations or disable tracking <Cite id="rekep-2024" />.

RoboPoint instruction-tunes a VLM to predict image-space affordance points from synthetic object and free-space data mixed with real-image VQA and LVIS detection data. It needs no external detector at test time, though its robot pipeline adds depth, an end-effector offset and a motion planner <Cite id="robopoint-2024" />.

Dense Object Nets learns a descriptor for each RGB pixel, self-supervised by matches from static RGB-D reconstructions, and demonstrates correspondence across viewpoints and moderately deformable configurations. Class-consistent training generalizes across sufficiently similar hats, shoes and mugs, while instance-specific training distinguishes objects. Learning a new object takes about 20 minutes, including a handful of scenes; optimization runs 3500 steps in about 13 minutes on one 1080 Ti or Titan Xp, and a static-scene scan takes about 70 seconds. The setup uses a calibrated Kuka IIWA LBR, a wrist-mounted Primesense Carmine 1.09 and a Schunk two-finger gripper, and the gripper's 6-DoF orientation needs further information <Cite id="dense-object-nets-2018" />.

Dex-Net 2.0 scores parallel-jaw grasp candidates. It trains on 6.7 million synthetic datapoints from 1500 object models, each pairing a grasp-aligned depth crop with an analytic robust-epsilon quality label. The planner ranks antipodal candidates, checks reachability and table collision, and works on single-view depth of an isolated object on a planar worksurface, assuming rigid objects and known camera intrinsics. Missing depth on thin parts and collisions remain failure modes, and its pile demonstration separates objects before grasping <Cite id="dexnet-2-2017" />.

## 6-DoF pose estimation, and how to read a claim about it

<Term id="pose-estimation">Pose estimation</Term> turns a mask into a rigid body with a position and orientation, and methods divide by what they assume about the object. Instance-level methods train on specific objects. PoseCNN localizes each object's centre, predicts its distance and regresses a rotation quaternion with a loss that handles symmetric objects. It introduced YCB-Video, 21 objects across 92 videos and 133,827 frames <Cite id="posecnn-2018" />. DOPE, trained only on synthetic domain-randomized and photorealistic data, performs competitively with a network trained on real and synthetic data <Cite id="dope-2018" />.

Category-level methods such as NOCS estimate the pose and size of unseen instances in known categories with no exact CAD model at training or test time <Cite id="nocs-2019" />. Novel-object methods drop per-object training. MegaPose uses render-and-compare against a CAD model supplied at test time <Cite id="megapose-2022" />. FoundationPose works from a CAD model or a few reference images and reports results comparable to instance-level methods <Cite id="foundationpose-2024" />. SAM-6D builds proposals with SAM, then treats pose as partial-to-partial point matching on cluttered RGB-D images <Cite id="sam6d-2023" />. Any6D needs one RGB-D anchor image to estimate both the 6D pose and size of an unknown object <Cite id="any6d-2025" />. SAM 3D predicts geometry, texture and layout from one image and reports at least a 5:1 win rate in human preference tests <Cite id="sam3d-2025" />.

To read a pose result, check its evaluation protocol. Hinterstoisser and colleagues evaluate a model $\mathcal M$ with ground-truth pose $(R,T)$ and estimate $(\tilde R,\tilde T)$ by averaging distances between corresponding transformed model points <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />:

$$
m = \operatorname{avg}_{x \in \mathcal M}
\lVert (Rx+T)-(\tilde R x+\tilde T) \rVert
$$

An estimate is correct when $m \leq k_m d$, where $d$ is the model's diameter and $k_m$ a chosen coefficient; their LINEMOD evaluation uses $k_m=0.1$ <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />. For the <Term id="add-s-metric">symmetry-aware pose error</Term>, each ground-truth-transformed point is matched to its nearest estimated-pose point before averaging <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />:

$$
m = \operatorname{avg}_{x_1 \in \mathcal M}
\min_{x_2 \in \mathcal M}
\lVert (Rx_1+T)-(\tilde R x_2+\tilde T) \rVert
$$

They apply this rule to objects that are ambiguous, or look ambiguous from some views, naming "cup", "bowl", "box" and "glue" <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />.

The BOP Challenge 2023 report scores 6-DoF localization differently. Each dataset score averages the VSD, MSSD and MSPD recalls, each over several thresholds with a strict test $e < \theta_e$, and $AR_C$ averages seven core datasets (LM-O, T-LESS, ITODD, HB, YCB-V, TUD-L, IC-BIN) on a 0 to 100 scale. VSD uses the visible surface, while MSSD and MSPD use pre-identified symmetries. Seen and unseen tracks share the method, with object identities and instance counts supplied at test time. Its 2D-detection $AP_C$ averages over objects first <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="bop-challenge-2023" />. The 2020 report, which supplies the threshold grids, marks where deep-network methods caught up with the point-pair-feature methods that had dominated earlier editions <Cite id="bop-2020" />.

From 2017 to 2023, seen-object accuracy rose from 56.9 to 85.6 $AR_C$, a relative gain of over 50 percent. The endpoints are Vidal's point-pair-feature method with ICP and no training images, and GPose2023 with object-specific networks trained on PBR and real images <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="bop-challenge-2023" />. For unseen objects, GenFlow-MultiHypo16 led 2023's Task 4 with 67.4 $AR_C$, against 69.8 for the best 2020 seen-object entry, CosyPose, and the report calls these comparable across distinct tracks. Their average image times were 34.58 and 13.74 seconds on unmatched hardware. GenFlow uses one network across datasets with CNOS-fastSAM detections and recurrent-flow refinement, while CosyPose uses dataset-specific networks with DeepIM and ICP refinement. Task 4 still supplies mesh models for onboarding, capped at 5 minutes per object on one GPU with no real images of the objects, and GenFlow still fails under heavy occlusion <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="bop-challenge-2023" />. CosyPose refines cameras and object poses jointly by object-level bundle adjustment over all views <Cite id="cosypose-2020" />.

The BOP 2024 report moved the frontier. Its best unseen-object method, FreeZeV2.1, is 22% more accurate on BOP-Classic-Core than GenFlow and 4% behind GPose2023, at 24.9 versus 2.7 seconds per image. Co-op takes 0.8 seconds per image and is 13% more accurate than GenFlow. The 2024 edition added model-free tasks onboarded from reference videos, and the report names 2D detection as the main bottleneck for unseen objects <Cite id="bop-2024" />.

## Visual servoing: image-based and position-based control

<Term id="visual-servoing">Visual servoing</Term> uses vision inside the control loop. Hutchinson, Hager and Corke's 1996 tutorial divides such systems into position-based and image-based classes <Cite id="hutchinson-hager-corke-1996" />. Espiau, Chaumette and Rives's 1992 paper applies a task-function framework to image-based control, with a task function designed for regulation that need not be the raw feature difference. Its interaction matrix relates feature rates to the camera's translational and rotational velocity in the camera frame. Convergence depends on task and model conditions, and neglecting target motion can leave a tracking error <Cite id="espiau-1992" />.

For a fixed goal and motionless target, Chaumette and Hutchinson's Part I writes $\dot{s} = L_s v_c$, with $v_c$ the camera's six-component spatial velocity. Image-based visual servoing (IBVS) uses image features, and position-based visual servoing (PBVS) uses estimated pose. The IBVS point-feature interaction matrix still uses depth and camera intrinsics, estimated from current depths or depths at the desired pose <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2006" />.

Redundant-feature IBVS is locally asymptotically stable when the interaction matrices have full rank and the estimated pseudoinverse times the true matrix meets a positivity condition. Coarse estimates can change the camera path without changing the final pose, while poor ones can make the system unstable. Large displacements can meet local minima or singularities, and PBVS points can leave the field of view. In PBVS, pose-estimation errors affect final accuracy <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2006" />.

Part II (2007) covers interaction-matrix estimation, hybrid and partitioned control, optimization, switching, trajectory planning and target tracking. Its switching and planning methods address visibility, occlusions and joint limits, and its joint-space formulations cover eye-in-hand and eye-to-hand systems <br className="lg:hidden" /><Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2007" />. Bateux and colleagues fine-tuned a CNN to estimate the relative pose between current and desired images and kept a pose-based control law <Cite id="bateux-2018" />.

Learned servoing can also aim the camera. EyeRobot 2.0, from Ken Goldberg (UC Berkeley) and colleagues (October 2026), swivels the two eye viewpoints of a single stereo camera to fixate a 3D point. A gaze-servoing policy and a fixation selector are both trained with RL on real data. Without wrist cameras, a standard policy on passive stereo fell from 52% to 27% real-world success. EyeRobot 2.0 beat passive stereo by 40% in real trials and, when a grasped object hid the wrist views, more than doubled the success of policies that had them, 48% against 22% <Cite id="eyerobot-2-2026" />.

## Separate model error from geometric error

Hand-eye accuracy depends on noise and motion range, so Enebuse and colleagues advise considering those conditions when benchmarking or calibrating <Cite id="enebuse-2022" />. A detector can keep stable image coordinates while the robot misses because the camera transform drifted, and a depth model can improve pixel metrics while grasps fail because invalid values cluster on the target material. Each stage is evaluated in its native output before the geometry that consumes it. The [calibration guide](/classical/calibration) defines the geometric checks, rerun after camera remounts, lens changes, time-source changes and resizing updates. The [ROS 2 guide](/classical/ros2-for-ml-engineers) shows where images, transforms and timestamps meet at runtime.

## What the end-to-end models replaced

End-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) policies absorb the explicit pipeline into weights. Gemini Robotics is a VLA model that controls robots directly, built on an embodied-reasoning model whose capabilities include object detection, pointing, trajectory and grasp prediction, multi-view correspondence and 3D bounding boxes <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. Such a policy needs no detector class or CAD model for a new object. It also has no hand-eye residual to re-solve when it misses a grasp by 8 mm.

Explicit geometric intermediates persist in some frontier systems. The [keypoint interfaces](/manipulation/hierarchical) kept geometry explicit while a learned model produced it. MolmoAct encodes observations into depth-aware perception tokens and generates editable trajectory traces before predicting low-level actions <Cite id="molmoact-2025" />. Google DeepMind reports that Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 improves pointing, counting and success detection, and acts as a robot's high-level reasoning model that calls VLAs as tools <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-1-6-2026" />.
