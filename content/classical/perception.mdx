Perception for manipulation turns camera and depth data into object poses in the robot's frame, so [grasp planning](/classical/grasp-planning) can compute grasps against them. Done well, it lets a robot grasp known household objects in clutter <Cite id="dope-2018" />.

<Image id="realsense-d435-depth-camera" />

## Errors add up across the pipeline

<PerceptionErrorBudget className="my-6" />

Camera placement, depth and pose errors add up at the gripper, and the placement term grows with distance. Margins are small: one reported assembly task had 0.5 mm of contact clearance <Cite id="play2perfect-2026" />.

## Calibration fixes what a pixel means

A camera's <Term id="camera-intrinsics">intrinsics</Term> describe how it projects the world onto its sensor: focal length in pixels, image center and lens distortion <Cite id="hartley-zisserman-2004" />. Zhang's 2000 method recovers them from a flat printed pattern shown at a few unmeasured angles <Cite id="zhang-2000-calibration" />.

### Hand-eye calibration places the camera on the robot

<Term id="hand-eye-calibration">Hand-eye calibration</Term> finds where the camera sits on the robot. Shiu and Ahmad cast it as $AX = XB$: move the arm by $A$, watch a fixed target shift by $B$, solve for the mount $X$ <Cite id="shiu-ahmad-1989" />.

| Solver | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Tsai and Lenz (1989) | Closed form, rotation first, then translation <Cite id="tsai-lenz-1989" /> |
| Park and Martin (1994) | Exact and least-squares solutions from Lie theory <Cite id="park-martin-1994" /> |
| OpenCV | Defaults to Tsai and Lenz; needs two motions about non-parallel axes, and many more are better <Cite id="opencv-hand-eye-docs-2026" /> |

Small rotation errors become large misses: an error $\theta$ shifts a point at distance $d$ by about $d \tan \theta$, so one degree costs 17 mm at 1 m. Tsai and Lenz's error analysis gives 0.4 mm from 3 milliradians at 5 inches <Cite id="tsai-lenz-1989" />.

## Every depth sensor fails on some surface

Stereo triangulates features matched between two images, so a blank wall defeats it; RealSense adds a projector that paints texture onto the scene <Cite id="keselman-2017-realsense" />. Time-of-flight cameras time modulated infrared light at each pixel and fail where light arrives by several paths, as in a corner <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />.

| Sensor | Principle | Listed specification | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RealSense D415, D43x | Active stereo | ±2% depth accuracy to 2 m, on a texture-less white target <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" /> | Error grows with the square of distance <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" /> |
| Photoneo PhoXi L | Structured light | 0.200 mm calibration accuracy, 870 to 2150 mm range, 250 to 2750 ms per scan <Cite id="photoneo-phoxi-l-2026" /> | No target condition given for the accuracy figure |
| Azure Kinect DK | Time of flight | Five documented causes of invalid (zero) depth <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" /> | Production ended August 2023, SDK support August 2024 <Cite id="azure-kinect-sdk-retirement-2024" /> |

Neither the RealSense nor the PhoXi documents state accuracy on transparent or shiny surfaces <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" /> <Cite id="photoneo-phoxi-l-2026" />. Those are where depth breaks:

| Surface | What goes wrong |
| --- | --- |
| Transparent | Reads as a noisy copy of the surface behind; ClearGrasp rebuilds depth from predicted normals, masks and edges <Cite id="cleargrasp-2020" /> |
| Shiny | Saturates the image; the D400f lists this as mitigated <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" /> |
| Dark | Signal too weak, so Azure Kinect discards the pixel <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" /> |
| Fences, wire grids | Two equally good stereo matches, rejected as zero depth <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" /> |

A surface lost from one viewpoint can reappear from another <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />. Also: RealSense's 2025 spin-out from Intel <Cite id="realsense-spinout-2025" />, Cognex's 2026 deal to buy it for about \$500 million <Cite id="cognex-realsense-2026" />.

Learned models now estimate depth from ordinary images.

| Model | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Depth Pro | Metric depth from one image with unknown intrinsics, 2.25 megapixels in 0.3 s on a standard GPU <Cite id="depth-pro-2024" /> |
| FoundationStereo | Zero-shot stereo, trained on varied synthetic pairs with a single-image depth prior <Cite id="foundationstereo-2025" /> |
| Fast-FoundationStereo | Over 10 times faster at close to the same zero-shot accuracy <Cite id="fast-foundationstereo-2025" /> |
| Prompt Depth Anything | Sparse readings from low-cost LiDAR as a metric prompt <Cite id="prompt-depth-anything-2024" /> |

Depth yields a <Term id="point-cloud">point cloud</Term>, an unordered set of 3D points. PointNet ignores the order by encoding each point with shared weights and pooling symmetrically <Cite id="pointnet-2017" />; PointNet++ repeats this in growing neighborhoods <Cite id="pointnet-plus-plus-2017" />.

## Detectors now find any object a word can name

Classical detectors box a class list fixed at training; Mask R-CNN adds a mask to each box <Cite id="mask-rcnn-2017" />. Grounding DINO accepts any phrase, such as "the red mug on the left"; its largest model scores 52.5 AP on COCO without COCO training images <Cite id="grounding-dino-2024" />.

Also: OWLv2's self-labeled web training <Cite id="owlv2-2023" />, Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro and Edge <Cite id="grounding-dino-1-5-2024" />, DINOv2's frozen features that transfer with simple heads <Cite id="dinov2-2023" />, DINOv3's sharper dense features <Cite id="dinov3-2025" />.

## Segmentation gives the grasp planner exact pixels

A box around a mug also holds the table behind it, so the grasp planner needs <Term id="semantic-segmentation">per-pixel labels</Term>. Segment Anything (SAM, 2023) made masks promptable: click a point or draw a box and get a mask. It trained on 1.1 billion masks over 11 million images <Cite id="segment-anything-2023" />.

| Model | Adds |
| --- | --- |
| SAM 2 | Video, with a streaming memory <Cite id="sam2-2024" /> |
| SAM 3 | Every instance matching a noun phrase or example image <Cite id="sam3-2025" /> |
| SAM 3.1 (March 2026) | 16 objects per pass, 32 frames per second on one H100, up from 16 <Cite id="sam3-1-2026" /> |
| Grounded SAM | Grounding DINO boxes as SAM prompts <Cite id="grounded-sam-2024" /> |
| CNOS | Unseen objects, by matching DINOv2 features of SAM masks to CAD renders <Cite id="cnos-2023" /> |

<Term id="promptable-segmentation">Promptable segmentation</Term> also feeds [keypoint interfaces](/manipulation/hierarchical), where a vision-language model (VLM) picks points and classical code acts on them.

| System | Points from | Action from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MOKA | A VLM choosing among marks on GroundedSAM masks | Depth lifts the points; a grasp sampler and motion planner act, with no trained policy <Cite id="moka-2024" /> |
| ReKep | DINOv2 features inside SAM masks | VLM-written Python constraints, solved without guaranteed feasibility <Cite id="rekep-2024" /> |
| RoboPoint | A tuned VLM, no detector | Predicted affordance points <Cite id="robopoint-2024" /> |
| Dense Object Nets | A self-supervised descriptor per pixel | The same spot across views and across similar hats, shoes and mugs <Cite id="dense-object-nets-2018" /> |

## Pose estimators need less of the object every year

<Term id="pose-estimation">Pose estimation</Term> turns a mask into a position and an orientation, six numbers in all.

| Method | Needs for a new object | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PoseCNN (2018) | Training on that object | Finds the center, predicts distance, regresses rotation, handles symmetry <Cite id="posecnn-2018" /> |
| DOPE (2018) | Training on that object, synthetic data only | Competitive with a network trained on real and synthetic data <Cite id="dope-2018" /> |
| NOCS (2019) | Training on its category, no exact model | Pose and size of unseen instances <Cite id="nocs-2019" /> |
| SAM-6D (2023) | An object model | SAM masks matched to the model as point clouds <Cite id="sam6d-2023" /> |
| MegaPose (2022) | A CAD model at test time | Render and compare <Cite id="megapose-2022" /> |
| FoundationPose (2024) | A CAD model or a few reference images | Results comparable to instance-level methods <Cite id="foundationpose-2024" /> |
| Any6D (2025) | One RGB-D view, no CAD model | Pose from a single anchor view <Cite id="any6d-2025" /> |
| SAM 3D (2025) | One image | Infers geometry, texture and layout, including unseen surfaces <Cite id="sam3d-2025" /> |

A pose claim means what its metric means. Hinterstoisser and colleagues place the object's model points at the true pose $(R,T)$ and at the estimate $(\tilde R,\tilde T)$ and average the gaps <Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />:

$$
m = \operatorname{avg}_{x \in \mathcal M}
\lVert (Rx+T)-(\tilde R x+\tilde T) \rVert
$$

An estimate counts as correct when $m$ is under 0.1 of the object's diameter. A cup looks the same at many rotations, so the <Term id="add-s-metric">symmetry-aware pose error</Term> matches each point to its nearest neighbor <Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />. CosyPose handles symmetry across several images <Cite id="cosypose-2020" />.

The BOP benchmark scores pose methods on seven core datasets from 0 to 100 <Cite id="bop-challenge-2023" />. Unseen objects now score close to seen ones; speed still separates them.

| BOP result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Seen objects, 2017 to 2024 | 56.9 to 86.0 <Cite id="bop-2025-challenge" /> |
| Unseen objects, best 2024 entry, onboarded from CAD in 5 minutes on one GPU | 82.5 <Cite id="bop-2025-challenge" /> |
| Time per image, best unseen method against GPose2023 | 24.9 s against 2.7 s; 2D detection is the bottleneck <Cite id="bop-2024" /> |
| Metallic, symmetric, shiny bin-picking parts (XYZ-IBD, a 2025 industrial track) | Marked drop from household objects <Cite id="xyz-ibd-2025" /> |

Also: BOP 2020's physically based synthetic training images <Cite id="bop-2020" />.

## Visual servoing steers by the image

<Term id="visual-servoing">Visual servoing</Term> closes the loop on vision: the robot keeps moving until the image looks right. Image-based servoing (IBVS) drives image features to where they should appear; position-based servoing (PBVS) drives an estimated pose to its goal <Cite id="hutchinson-hager-corke-1996" />. Espiau, Chaumette and Rives tied feature motion to camera velocity, $\dot{s} = L_s v_c$ <Cite id="espiau-1992" />; that matrix needs depth and intrinsics <Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2006" />.

Large moves can trap IBVS in local minima or singularities; PBVS can lose features from view, and its pose error sets the final accuracy <Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2006" />. Also: switching and planning fixes <Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2007" />, a CNN pose estimate inside a PBVS law <Cite id="bateux-2018" />.

Learned gaze can aim the camera. EyeRobot 2.0 swivels a stereo camera's views to fixate a 3D point and beat passive stereo by 40%. When a held object hid the wrist views, it succeeded 48% of the time against 22% for wrist-camera policies <Cite id="eyerobot-2-2026" />.

## Separate model error from geometric error

When the robot misses the mug, the detector may be fine and the camera mount may have moved. Hand-eye accuracy depends on sensor noise and on how widely the arm moved during calibration <Cite id="enebuse-2022" />. Check each stage on its own: the [calibration guide](/classical/calibration) lists checks after a remount, and the [ROS 2 guide](/classical/ros2-for-ml-engineers) shows where images, transforms and timestamps meet.

## End-to-end models absorb the pipeline

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies fold the pipeline into their weights: Gemini Robotics runs on a reasoning model that detects, points and predicts grasps and 3D boxes <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. Such a policy needs no CAD model for a new object, but when it misses a grasp by 8 mm there is no hand-eye estimate to re-solve. MolmoAct keeps geometry visible, drawing editable trajectory traces before it acts <Cite id="molmoact-2025" />.

Also: Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6, a planner that calls VLAs as tools <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-1-6-2026" />.
