Perception for manipulation turns camera and depth data into object poses in the robot's own frame, through calibration, depth sensing, detection, segmentation and pose estimation, and [grasp planning](/classical/grasp-planning) computes grasps against those poses. DOPE's pose network was accurate enough for a real robot to grasp known household objects in clutter <Cite id="dope-2018" />.

## Calibration: what a pixel means

A camera maps a 3D point onto a 2D sensor, and its <Term id="camera-intrinsics">intrinsics</Term> describe that mapping: focal length in pixels on each axis, the principal point and lens distortion. Zhang's 2000 method shows the camera a planar target at several unmeasured orientations. It models radial distortion and gives a closed-form solution followed by maximum-likelihood nonlinear refinement. Zhang contrasts it with classical techniques that need expensive equipment such as two or three orthogonal planes <Cite id="zhang-2000-calibration" />.

### Hand-eye calibration: what a pixel means to the robot

Extrinsics place the camera relative to something else. <Term id="hand-eye-calibration">Hand-eye calibration</Term> finds the flange-to-camera transform for a wrist camera, or base-to-camera for a fixed one. Shiu and Ahmad cast it as $AX = XB$, where $A$ is a robot motion, $B$ the camera's observed motion of a static target and $X$ the unknown transform <Cite id="shiu-ahmad-1989" />. Tsai and Lenz gave an efficient closed-form technique that solves rotation first, then translation <Cite id="tsai-lenz-1989" />.

Park and Martin derived closed-form exact and least-squares solutions with Lie theory <Cite id="park-martin-1994" />. OpenCV's hand-eye calibration defaults to the Tsai and Lenz method, needs at least two motions with non-parallel rotation axes, so three poses, and strongly recommends many more <Cite id="opencv-hand-eye-docs-2026" />.

The instrument's hand-eye term is a ray-to-plane offset at separation $d$ for a ray inclined by $\theta$ to the plane's normal, about 1.7 mm at 10 cm and 17 mm at 1 m for one degree:

$$
e_\theta(d) = d \, \tan \theta
$$

## Depth sensing: what fails, and on what

Stereo triangulates features matched between two images, so a featureless surface gives it nothing to match. Intel's RGBD depth cameras add an active texture projector to make matching unambiguous <Cite id="keselman-2017-realsense" />.

The March 2026 RealSense D400 datasheet lists ±2% Z-accuracy up to 2 m for D410/D415 and D43x over an 80% region of interest at HD resolution. Accuracy compares valid pixels with ground truth over 80% of the depth field of view, and factory KPIs reflect typical conditions; these models are tested on a texture-less white target at the default 150 mW laser power and auto exposure <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />.

RealSense's D415/D435 tuning guide says D400 depth error scales with the square of distance. It advises moving closer while staying outside the minimum operating distance, MinZ. It describes resolution and range trade-offs when changing MinZ, and notes that focus and differing left and right views limit very-near matching <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />.

Learned models now estimate depth from images, alone or with a sparse sensor: Depth Pro predicts metric monocular depth without camera intrinsics, a 2.25-megapixel map in 0.3 seconds on a standard GPU <Cite id="depth-pro-2024" />.

For industrial 3D scanning, the PhoXi 3D Scanner L page lists 0.200 mm calibration accuracy and 0.190 mm temporal noise (both 1 σ), an 870 to 2150 mm scanning range and 250 to 2750 ms scanning time. These are separate specification fields for one model <Cite id="photoneo-phoxi-l-2026" />.

Time of flight casts its own modulated near-infrared light and measures its round trip per pixel. Multi-path interference lets one pixel integrate light that arrived by several routes. The Azure Kinect DK documentation lists five causes of invalid depth: outside the IR illumination mask, saturated IR signal, low IR signal, filter outlier and multi-path interference. Invalid pixels carry depth zero. Ambiguous depth arises when light reflects from one wall onto another in a corner, and where foreground and background mix at object edges. Fast motion can increase edge invalidation <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />. Microsoft announced the end of Azure Kinect DK production in August 2023, and support for its Sensor SDK ended in August 2024 <Cite id="azure-kinect-sdk-retirement-2024" />.

### The failure taxonomy that decides how a cell is built

- ClearGrasp starts from the observation that transparent objects "often appear as noisy or distorted approximations of the surfaces that lie behind them". It infers surface normals, transparent-surface masks and occlusion boundaries, then replaces the returned depth <Cite id="cleargrasp-2020" />.
- Specular reflections can saturate D400 images, and the D400f column says "Saturation mitigated" <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />. Azure Kinect invalidates saturated IR pixels <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />.
- Dark surfaces and low light weaken the signal. Azure Kinect invalidates a pixel whose IR signal is too weak to generate depth <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />. RealSense ties D4xx depth quality to the input images and warns of grainy low-light images, underexposure and overexposure. For its projected-pattern example, reducing background light with the projector on can raise the pattern's signal-to-noise ratio <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />.
- RealSense names fences and wire grids as scenes where left-right correspondence can become ambiguous. Its D4 processor compares the best and second-best match scores, and raising DSSecondPeakThreshold rejects more matches as zero depth <Cite id="realsense-tuning-2026" />.
- Azure Kinect notes that a surface invalidated by multi-path from one camera view can reappear from another <Cite id="azure-kinect-depth-docs-2026" />.

The 2% figure is measured on a texture-less white target with the projector at default power <Cite id="realsense-d400-datasheet-2026" />, and the PhoXi page publishes no target condition for its 0.200 mm figure <Cite id="photoneo-phoxi-l-2026" />. Accuracy for transparent or specular surfaces is not disclosed in either datasheet.

Depth yields a <Term id="point-cloud">point cloud</Term>, an unordered set that a network must process without depending on input order. PointNet uses a shared per-point encoder and a symmetric pooling function <Cite id="pointnet-2017" />. PointNet++ applies PointNet within radius neighbourhoods around sampled centroids, in coordinates relative to each centroid, building local features at growing scales. Density-adaptive variants combine scales where small neighbourhoods hold too few samples <Cite id="pointnet-plus-plus-2017" />.

## Detection: from pixels to candidate objects

Classical detectors return boxes over a class list fixed at training time. Mask R-CNN extends Faster R-CNN with a branch that predicts an object mask in parallel with box recognition <Cite id="mask-rcnn-2017" />.

Open-vocabulary detection removes the fixed list. Grounding DINO adds language-vision fusion to the DINO detector's feature enhancer, query selection and decoder and accepts category names or referring expressions. It performs weakly on referring expressions without REC training data. Its Swin-L model reports 52.5 AP on COCO 2017 validation after pretraining on O365, OpenImage and GoldG without COCO training images <Cite id="grounding-dino-2024" />.

DINOv2 learns image and patch features by self-supervised pretraining on the curated LVD-142M dataset. Its transfer evaluations freeze the encoder and train linear heads for classification and segmentation, and linear or DPT heads for depth <Cite id="dinov2-2023" />.

## Segmentation: the grounding layer

A box around a mug also contains the table behind it, so a grasp planner needs the surface region that <Term id="semantic-segmentation">per-pixel labelling</Term> provides. Segment Anything (SAM, 2023) makes segmentation promptable; its default ViT-H model was trained on SA-1B, reported as 1.1 billion automatically generated masks on 11 million images. Point, box and mask prompts condition the decoder, and an ambiguous point can return several candidate masks for a part or a whole object. Fine structures and boundaries remain failure cases, and the heavy image encoder prevents overall real-time performance <Cite id="segment-anything-2023" />.

SAM 2 extends prompting to video with a streaming memory of earlier predictions and prompted frames. Objects are inferred independently, and failures persist after shot changes or long occlusions, in crowded scenes and on thin, fast-moving details <Cite id="sam2-2024" />.

Grounded SAM feeds Grounding DINO's open-set boxes to SAM <Cite id="grounded-sam-2024" />, and CNOS segments unseen objects by matching DINOv2 descriptors of SAM proposals against references built from CAD models <Cite id="cnos-2023" />.

<Term id="promptable-segmentation">Promptable segmentation</Term> supplies object masks to MOKA and ReKep, two [keypoint interfaces](/manipulation/hierarchical) with different perception stacks. MOKA proposes marked candidate points from GroundedSAM masks, then prompts a pretrained VLM to select grasp, function and target keypoints, waypoint regions and motion attributes. Calibrated depth lifts the points into the robot frame, and a separate grasp sampler and motion pipeline produce actions; the prompted method trains no robot policy <Cite id="moka-2024" />.

ReKep proposes semantic keypoints from DINOv2 features within SAM masks and calibrated RGB-D geometry. A large vision-language model writes Python sub-goal and path constraints from a marked image and an instruction, and a solver repeatedly plans end-effector motion from tracked keypoints. The solver penalizes constraint violations without guaranteeing physical feasibility, and rigidity assumptions and tracking failures remain limitations. Some evaluations use human annotations or disable tracking <Cite id="rekep-2024" />.

RoboPoint instruction-tunes a VLM to predict image-space affordance points from synthetic object and free-space data mixed with real-image VQA and LVIS detection data. It needs no external detector at test time, though its robot pipeline adds depth, an end-effector offset and a motion planner <Cite id="robopoint-2024" />.

Dense Object Nets learns a descriptor for each RGB pixel, self-supervised by matches from static RGB-D reconstructions, and demonstrates correspondence across viewpoints and moderately deformable configurations. Class-consistent training generalizes across sufficiently similar hats, shoes and mugs, while instance-specific training distinguishes objects; for grasping, the gripper's 6-DoF orientation needs further information <Cite id="dense-object-nets-2018" />.

## 6-DoF pose estimation, and how to read a claim about it

<Term id="pose-estimation">Pose estimation</Term> turns a mask into a rigid body with a position and orientation, and methods divide by what they assume about the object. Instance-level methods train on specific objects: PoseCNN localizes each object's centre, predicts its distance and regresses a rotation quaternion, with a loss that handles symmetric objects <Cite id="posecnn-2018" />. DOPE, trained only on synthetic domain-randomized and photorealistic data, performs competitively with a network trained on real and synthetic data <Cite id="dope-2018" />.

Category-level methods such as NOCS estimate the pose and size of unseen instances in known categories with no exact CAD model at training or test time <Cite id="nocs-2019" />. Novel-object methods drop per-object training. MegaPose uses render-and-compare against a CAD model supplied at test time <Cite id="megapose-2022" />, and FoundationPose works from a CAD model or a few reference images and reports results comparable to instance-level methods <Cite id="foundationpose-2024" />.

To read a pose result, check its evaluation protocol. Hinterstoisser and colleagues evaluate a model $\mathcal M$ with ground-truth pose $(R,T)$ and estimate $(\tilde R,\tilde T)$ by averaging distances between corresponding transformed model points <Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />:

$$
m = \operatorname{avg}_{x \in \mathcal M}
\lVert (Rx+T)-(\tilde R x+\tilde T) \rVert
$$

An estimate is correct when $m \leq k_m d$, where $d$ is the model's diameter and $k_m$ a chosen coefficient; their LINEMOD evaluation uses $k_m=0.1$ <Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />. For the <Term id="add-s-metric">symmetry-aware pose error</Term>, each ground-truth-transformed point is matched to its nearest estimated-pose point before averaging <Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />:

$$
m = \operatorname{avg}_{x_1 \in \mathcal M}
\min_{x_2 \in \mathcal M}
\lVert (Rx_1+T)-(\tilde R x_2+\tilde T) \rVert
$$

They apply this rule to objects that are ambiguous, or look ambiguous from some views, naming "cup", "bowl", "box" and "glue" <Cite id="hinterstoisser-2012" />.

The BOP Challenge 2023 report scores 6-DoF localization differently: each dataset score averages the VSD, MSSD and MSPD recalls, each over several thresholds with a strict test $e < \theta_e$, and $AR_C$ averages seven core datasets (LM-O, T-LESS, ITODD, HB, YCB-V, TUD-L, IC-BIN) on a 0 to 100 scale. Seen and unseen tracks share the method, with object identities and instance counts supplied at test time, and the 2D-detection $AP_C$ averages over objects first <Cite id="bop-challenge-2023" />.

From 2017 to 2023, seen-object accuracy rose from 56.9 to 85.6 $AR_C$, a relative gain of over 50 percent. The endpoints are Vidal's point-pair-feature method with ICP and no training images, and GPose2023 with object-specific networks trained on PBR and real images <Cite id="bop-challenge-2023" />.

For unseen objects, GenFlow-MultiHypo16 led 2023's Task 4 with 67.4 $AR_C$, against 69.8 for the best 2020 seen-object entry, CosyPose, and the report calls these comparable across distinct tracks; average image times were 34.58 and 13.74 seconds on unmatched hardware. Task 4 still supplies mesh models for onboarding, capped at 5 minutes per object on one GPU with no real images of the objects, and GenFlow fails under heavy occlusion <Cite id="bop-challenge-2023" />.

In the BOP 2024 report, the best unseen-object method, FreeZeV2.1, is 22% more accurate on BOP-Classic-Core than GenFlow and 4% behind GPose2023, but takes 24.9 against 2.7 seconds per image, and the report names 2D detection as the main bottleneck for unseen objects <Cite id="bop-2024" />.

## Visual servoing: image-based and position-based control

<Term id="visual-servoing">Visual servoing</Term> uses vision inside the control loop, and Hutchinson, Hager and Corke's 1996 tutorial divides such systems into position-based and image-based classes <Cite id="hutchinson-hager-corke-1996" />. Espiau, Chaumette and Rives's 1992 paper applies a task-function framework to image-based control, with a task function designed for regulation that need not be the raw feature difference. Its interaction matrix relates feature rates to the camera's translational and rotational velocity in the camera frame. Convergence depends on task and model conditions, and neglecting target motion can leave a tracking error <Cite id="espiau-1992" />.

For a fixed goal and motionless target, Chaumette and Hutchinson's Part I writes $\dot{s} = L_s v_c$, with $v_c$ the camera's six-component spatial velocity. Image-based visual servoing (IBVS) uses image features, and position-based visual servoing (PBVS) uses estimated pose. The IBVS point-feature interaction matrix still uses depth and camera intrinsics, estimated from current depths or depths at the desired pose <Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2006" />.

Redundant-feature IBVS is locally asymptotically stable when the interaction matrices have full rank and the estimated pseudoinverse times the true matrix meets a positivity condition; coarse estimates can change the camera path, while poor ones can make the system unstable. Large displacements can meet local minima or singularities, PBVS points can leave the field of view, and in PBVS pose-estimation errors affect final accuracy <Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2006" />.

Part II (2007) adds switching and planning methods that address visibility, occlusions and joint limits, for eye-in-hand and eye-to-hand systems <Cite id="chaumette-hutchinson-2007" />. Bateux and colleagues fine-tuned a CNN to estimate the relative pose between current and desired images and kept a pose-based control law <Cite id="bateux-2018" />.

Learned servoing can also aim the camera. EyeRobot 2.0, from Ken Goldberg (UC Berkeley) and colleagues (October 2026), swivels the two eye viewpoints of a single stereo camera to fixate a 3D point, with a gaze-servoing policy and a fixation selector both trained with RL on real data. It beat passive stereo by 40% in real trials and, when a grasped object hid the wrist views, more than doubled the success of policies that had them, 48% against 22% <Cite id="eyerobot-2-2026" />.

## Separate model error from geometric error

Hand-eye accuracy depends on noise and motion range, so Enebuse and colleagues advise accounting for both when benchmarking or calibrating <Cite id="enebuse-2022" />. A detector can keep stable image coordinates while the robot misses because the camera transform drifted, so each stage is checked in its own output first. The [calibration guide](/classical/calibration) defines the checks to rerun after a camera remount, and the [ROS 2 guide](/classical/ros2-for-ml-engineers) shows where images, transforms and timestamps meet at runtime.

## What the end-to-end models replaced

End-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) policies absorb the explicit pipeline into weights. Gemini Robotics is a VLA model that controls robots directly, built on an embodied-reasoning model that can detect objects, point, and predict trajectories, grasps and 3D bounding boxes <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. Such a policy needs no detector class or CAD model for a new object, and it has no hand-eye residual to re-solve when it misses a grasp by 8 mm.

Some frontier systems keep geometric intermediates explicit, as the [keypoint interfaces](/manipulation/hierarchical) did while a learned model produced the geometry: MolmoAct encodes observations into depth-aware perception tokens and generates editable trajectory traces before predicting low-level actions <Cite id="molmoact-2025" />.
