Space robotics is the design and operation of robots beyond Earth, from planetary rovers to station arms and satellite servicers. A signal needs 4 to 20 minutes to reach Mars <Cite id="jpl-ipn-pnt-2024" />, so rovers drive mostly on their own <Cite id="enav-perseverance-2026" />, and commercial servicers have docked with satellites in orbit since 2020 <Cite id="mev1-servicing-2025" />.

## Autonomy paced by the speed of light

NASA and JPL rovers have used onboard autonomy since 2004 <Cite id="mer-mobility-software-2006" />. Sheridan's 1993 review found that space teleoperation had tended to avoid time delay by not teleoperating from the ground <Cite id="sheridan-time-delay-1993" />. The Mars Exploration Rovers layered goal-based driving over reactive and predictive safety checks, and drove over 11,000 m combined by October 2005 <Cite id="mer-mobility-software-2006" />. They were the first robots to use visual odometry on another world, and within two years it grew from an "extra credit" capability into a critical vehicle safety system <Cite id="mer-visual-odometry-2007" />.

In Perseverance's first Mars year, AutoNav evaluated 88 percent of the 17.7 km driven, against Opportunity's previous record of 2.4 km over fourteen years <Cite id="perseverance-autonomy-2023" />. It drove 699.9 m without humans reviewing the imagery and 347.7 m in one sol, both planetary rover records <Cite id="perseverance-autonomy-2023" />. Its Enhanced Autonomous Navigation runs on a single-core CPU and raised average daily driving distance by an order of magnitude <Cite id="enav-perseverance-2026" />. By October 28, 2024, it had evaluated the terrain for about 90 percent of 32.1 km <Cite id="enav-perseverance-2026" />.

Since February 2026 Perseverance can [fix its own position](/classical/state-estimation/) by matching navcam panoramas to orbital imagery, to within 25 cm in about two minutes <Cite id="mars-global-localization-2026" />. In December 2025 JPL used [vision-language models](/manipulation/vla-models/) on the ground to generate Perseverance waypoints from HiRISE orbital imagery and terrain-slope data <Cite id="perseverance-ai-drive-2026" />. After a digital twin verified over 500,000 telemetry variables, the rover drove 210 m on December 8 and 246 m two days later <Cite id="perseverance-ai-drive-2026" />.

Onboard software also picks science targets. On Curiosity since 2016, AEGIS has increased science data return by selecting targets for the ChemCam laser spectrometer <Cite id="aegis-curiosity-2017" />. On Perseverance it picks SuperCam targets from wide-angle images during or right after long drives, without waiting for a ground cycle <Cite id="perseverance-autonomy-2023" />. In 2024 NASA described PIXL's "adaptive sampling" as the first use of AI on Mars to make autonomous decisions from real-time rock composition analysis <Cite id="pixl-adaptive-sampling-2024" />.

On April 19, 2021, Ingenuity made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet, in an atmosphere with about one percent of Earth's surface pressure <Cite id="ingenuity-first-flight-2021" />. Designed for five flights over thirty days, it flew 72 times in almost three years before NASA ended the mission in January 2024 <Cite id="ingenuity-mission-end-2024" />. NASA's accident investigation named the most likely cause: a lack of surface texture gave the navigation system too little information <Cite id="ingenuity-investigation-2024" />.

## Living off the land: ISRU

MOXIE, on Perseverance, was the first demonstration of <Term id="in-situ-resource-utilization">in-situ resource utilization</Term> (ISRU) on another planet, making oxygen by solid-oxide electrolysis of atmospheric carbon dioxide <Cite id="moxie-sciadv-2022" />. A scaled-up version would make the tens of tons of oxygen an ascent rocket needs on Mars, instead of launching hundreds of tons from Earth <Cite id="moxie-sciadv-2022" />. Across sixteen runs MOXIE produced 122 grams of oxygen, peaking at 12 grams per hour at 98 percent purity or better, about twice NASA's original goals <Cite id="moxie-completion-2023" />. It concluded operations in September 2023 <Cite id="moxie-completion-2023" />.

<Image id="moxie-installation-perseverance-2020" />

Scaled ISRU means drilling and moving abrasive regolith in vacuum and cold. NASA's PRIME-1 suite reached the lunar south pole on Intuitive Machines' IM-2 lander in March 2025, where the lander, Athena, came to rest on its side in a crater <Cite id="prime-1-lunar-2025" />.

NASA put it about 400 m from its target and the mission at about ten hours of the ten days planned; the PRIME-1 team's 2026 paper gives about 500 m and 12 hours <Cite id="prime-1-lunar-2025" /><Cite id="hancock-prime1-2026" />. TRIDENT, Honeybee Robotics' one-metre rotary-percussive drill, executed its commanded motions, but with the lander on its side it could not reach the surface to excavate <Cite id="prime-1-lunar-2025" /><Cite id="hancock-prime1-2026" />. Initial data suggested the MSOLO mass spectrometer detected only gases of human origin, such as propellant and traces of Earth water <Cite id="prime-1-lunar-2025" />. In [evaluation](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/) terms PRIME-1 was one trial: the instruments worked while the landing cost the science <Cite id="prime-1-lunar-2025" />.

Firefly's Blue Ghost landed on March 2, 2025, four days before Athena, and all its NASA payloads operated <Cite id="blue-ghost-lister-2025" />. Its LISTER probe dug with gas to 1 m, the deepest robotic planetary subsurface thermal probe yet <Cite id="blue-ghost-lister-2025" />.

Learned locomotion meets the same regolith in analogue tests on the ground. In October 2026, Adrian Fuhrer, Marco Hutter and colleagues at ETH Zurich reported on a 2025 campaign in the ESA/DLR LUNA lunar analogue facility in Cologne, where the quadrupeds ANYmal-D and Magnecko walked loose regolith simulant and crater-like terrain. The robots could cross the simulant, but sinkage and slip, dust kicked up by foot contacts, and perception failures from overexposure, shadows and low-texture ground all degraded performance <Cite id="luna-legged-2026" />.

## Orbital robotics: servicing and removal

<Term id="on-orbit-servicing">On-orbit servicing</Term> covers satellite repair, refuelling, upgrades and debris removal <Cite id="space-manipulator-review-2025" />. It means matching a target's orbit, then docking with it or grasping it. An arm and its base satellite move each other, and in 1989 Umetani and Yoshida derived a generalized Jacobian from momentum conservation to control such arms <Cite id="umetani-yoshida-1989" />. A 2025 review organises capture control around free-floating and free-flying modes, from vision-based estimation to post-capture stabilisation <Cite id="space-manipulator-review-2025" />.

<OrbitalServicingTable className="my-6 wide-measure" />

ROTEX flew on Space Shuttle Columbia in early 1993, worked autonomously, under astronaut teleoperation and under ground control, and grasped free-floating objects <Cite id="rotex-1993" />. Japan's ETS-VII demonstrated autonomous rendezvous and docking between two satellites in 1998, with flight experiments for a 2 m, six-degree-of-freedom arm <Cite id="ets-vii-ard-2001" /><Cite id="ets-vii-robot-2001" />. In DARPA's 2007 Orbital Express, the ASTRO servicer autonomously captured NextSat and transferred propellant and a battery to it over four months <Cite id="orbital-express-2008" /><Cite id="boeing-orbital-express-2007" />.

On the International Space Station, Canadarm2, a 17 m arm, assembled the station and still grapples and berths arriving cargo vehicles <Cite id="canadarm2-csa-2024" />. Dextre, a two-armed robot that rides it, replaces exterior equipment including 100 kg batteries by <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> from the ground <Cite id="dextre-csa-2024" />.

<Image id="dextre-canadarm2-iss-2017" />

In 2020 Northrop Grumman's MEV-1 made the first docking of two commercial spacecraft in orbit, capturing Intelsat 901 in the graveyard orbit above geosynchronous altitude and returning it to service <Cite id="mev1-servicing-2025" />. After five years it undocked, the first such undocking in that orbit, and moved to its next mission <Cite id="mev1-servicing-2025" />. On July 21, 2026, Northrop Grumman's Mission Robotic Vehicle launched with the DARPA-funded RSGS payload, twin robotic arms built by the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory <Cite id="rsgs-mrv-launch-2026" />. It will install small propulsion modules called mission extension pods to extend satellites' lives <Cite id="rsgs-mrv-launch-2026" />.

Debris is the uncooperative case, and ESA's 2026 report states that active debris removal is required to stop collisions multiplying debris <Cite id="esa-space-environment-2026" />. In 2024 Astroscale's ADRAS-J, flying for JAXA, inspected a discarded rocket upper stage in three fly-arounds and closed to about 15 m <Cite id="adras-j-15m-2024" />. No commercial spacecraft had come closer to debris, and the onboard collision-avoidance system aborted autonomously after an unexpected relative attitude anomaly <Cite id="adras-j-15m-2024" />.

By March 25, 2026, ADRAS-J had begun a controlled deorbit after 293 days in orbit <Cite id="adras-j-deorbit-2026" />. ADRAS-J2 targets launch in Japan's fiscal year 2027 to capture and remove an upper stage weighing about 3 t <Cite id="adras-j2-isar-2026" />.

NASA discontinued OSAM-1, a mission to refuel an unprepared satellite, in March 2024 after an independent review <Cite id="osam1-discontinued-2024" />. It cited technical, cost and schedule challenges and what it called a broader community evolution away from refuelling unprepared spacecraft, which left no committed partner <Cite id="osam1-discontinued-2024" />. In August 2026 NASA and Katalyst called off the LINK servicer's planned capture and boost of Swift, launched in 2004, over an attitude-control issue on the commercial spacecraft <Cite id="swift-boost-update-2026" />. NASA expected Swift to re-enter later in 2026 <Cite id="swift-boost-update-2026" />.

## What robot learning takes from it

Perception latency of tens of milliseconds caps a [drone's](/adjacent/drones/) safe speed <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />. Mars delays run to minutes, so spacecraft there must handle off-nominal situations without real-time commanding from Earth <Cite id="jpl-ipn-pnt-2024" />.

Learned control has flown on NASA's Astrobee free-flyers aboard the ISS. In 2025 Banerjee and colleagues reported the first in-space test of learned warm starts for trajectory optimization, while a convex solver still enforced safety <Cite id="banerjee-warm-starts-2025" />. The network cut solver iterations by 60 percent with rotational dynamics and 50 percent with in-distribution obstacles <Cite id="banerjee-warm-starts-2025" />. On May 27, 2025, the Naval Research Laboratory's APIARY team ran what it calls the first reinforcement-learning control of a free-flyer in space, with a policy trained in randomized simulation <Cite id="apiary-astrobee-2025" />.

The Space Robotics Bench authors say costly technology demonstrations and limited data severely hinder robot learning in space <Cite id="space-robotics-bench-2025" />. A September 2026 review adds space-specific dynamics and sensing, limited onboard resources and stringent safety requirements <Cite id="ai-space-robot-ops-review-2026" />. AI4MARS offers 326,000 crowdsourced terrain labels on 35,000 rover images, and a model trained on it exceeded 96 percent accuracy <Cite id="ai4mars-2021" />.

Space shares its [reliability](/frontier/reliability-gap/) bar with [surgical robotics](/adjacent/surgical/). Ingenuity flew more than fourteen times its five planned flights <Cite id="ingenuity-mission-end-2024" />, while OSAM-1 was cancelled before flight <Cite id="osam1-discontinued-2024" />. AEGIS and the digital-twin check on AI-planned drives both add autonomy in bounded, verified steps <Cite id="aegis-curiosity-2017" /><Cite id="perseverance-ai-drive-2026" />.
