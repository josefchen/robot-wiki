Space robotics builds and operates robots beyond Earth. Examples include planetary rovers, robotic arms on space stations, and commercial satellite servicing spacecraft, which have docked with satellites since 2020 <Cite id="mev1-servicing-2025" />. Communication with Mars takes between 4 and 20 minutes each way <Cite id="jpl-ipn-pnt-2024" />, so rovers mostly drive on their own <Cite id="enav-perseverance-2026" />.

## Light delay makes rovers drive themselves

A rover cannot be steered live. In his 1993 review, Sheridan noted that space <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> had coped with time delay by not teleoperating from the ground <Cite id="sheridan-time-delay-1993" />. From 2004 NASA's Mars Exploration Rovers used onboard autonomy to drive themselves, and by October 2005 they had traveled over 11,000 m <Cite id="mer-mobility-software-2006" />. Within two years their visual odometry system had become critical for safe driving <Cite id="mer-visual-odometry-2007" />.

Perseverance's AutoNav judged the terrain for 88 percent of the 17.7 km it drove in its first Mars year; Opportunity's record was 2.4 km over fourteen years <Cite id="perseverance-autonomy-2023" />. Perseverance's ENav software runs on a single-core CPU and raised daily driving by an order of magnitude <Cite id="enav-perseverance-2026" />. Since February 2026 Perseverance can [fix its own position](/classical/state-estimation/) by comparing its panoramic images against orbital images, within 25 cm in around two minutes <Cite id="mars-global-localization-2026" />.

## Rovers that plan, choose and fly

In December 2025, JPL used [vision-language models](/manipulation/vla-models/) to plan driving waypoints for Perseverance based on slope data and imagery from orbit. A digital twin was used to check more than 500,000 variables in the telemetry data, and the rover drove 210 m, then 246 m two days later <Cite id="perseverance-ai-drive-2026" />.

Onboard software chooses science targets: Curiosity's ChemCam targeting software, AEGIS, has selected laser targets since 2016 <Cite id="aegis-curiosity-2017" /> and also selects targets for Perseverance's SuperCam instrument following long driving segments <Cite id="perseverance-autonomy-2023" />. NASA describes PIXL's adaptive sampling as the first AI on Mars to make decisions based on real-time rock chemistry <Cite id="pixl-adaptive-sampling-2024" />.

On April 19, 2021, Ingenuity achieved the first powered, controlled flight on another planet <Cite id="ingenuity-first-flight-2021" />. Designed to last five flights, it made 72 flights over nearly three years <Cite id="ingenuity-mission-end-2024" />. According to NASA, the likeliest cause of its final crash is terrain that did not offer enough features for its navigation <Cite id="ingenuity-investigation-2024" />.

Also: wheel-slip prediction from terrain <Cite id="traversability-slip-2023" />, slip detection from wheel sensors <Cite id="proprioceptive-slip-2022" />.

## Mars oxygen worked; lunar digging is harder

<Term id="in-situ-resource-utilization">In-situ resource utilization</Term>, making supplies on site, was first demonstrated on another planet by MOXIE, which produced oxygen from the carbon dioxide in Mars' atmosphere <Cite id="moxie-sciadv-2022" />. During sixteen production runs, MOXIE produced 122 g of oxygen with a peak production rate of 12 g per hour and an oxygen purity of 98 percent, roughly twice NASA's objectives <Cite id="moxie-completion-2023" />.

<Image id="moxie-installation-perseverance-2020" />

NASA's PRIME-1 instrument suite flew on the Athena lander, which reached the lunar south pole in March 2025 but landed on its side, limiting operations to 10 to 12 hours of the planned ten days. Its TRIDENT drill operated as commanded but did not make contact with the lunar surface <Cite id="prime-1-lunar-2025" /><Cite id="hancock-prime1-2026" />. All of NASA's payloads operated on Firefly's Blue Ghost, including LISTER, which burrowed 1 m into the lunar regolith, the deepest robotic thermal probe measurement on another planetary body to date <Cite id="blue-ghost-lister-2025" />.

## Learned walking is tested on Earth first

In 2025, ETH Zurich's ANYmal-D and Magnecko slowed down when traversing lunar soil simulant in the ESA/DLR LUNA hall, owing to sinkage, slip, dust and harsh lighting <Cite id="luna-legged-2026" />. In simulation, humanoid policies trained for Earth fell under lunar gravity, and Moon-trained ones fell on Earth <Cite id="casadesus-humanoid-moon-2026" />.

MMX, JAXA's Phobos sample return mission, launched on October 20, 2026 <Cite id="jaxa-mmx-launch-2026" /> and carries the Idefix rover built by DLR and CNES <Cite id="dlr-idefix-2026" />. Chang'e-7, China's rover and hopper mission to the Moon's south pole, has been delayed from August 2026 <Cite id="xinhua-change7-delay-2026" /><Cite id="planetary-change7" />.

Also: cave planner PLGRIM <Cite id="plgrim-2021" />, jumping legs <Cite id="mars-jumping-2023" />, ReachBot <Cite id="reachbot-melt-2024" />.

## In orbit, an arm pushes its own satellite

<Term id="on-orbit-servicing">On-orbit servicing</Term> includes satellite refueling, repair and upgrading as well as debris removal <Cite id="space-manipulator-review-2025" />. In free fall, swinging an arm turns its satellite the other way. Umetani and Yoshida derived how to control such arms from conservation of momentum in 1989 <Cite id="umetani-yoshida-1989" />.

## Debris does not cooperate

ESA's 2026 report says active removal is necessary to stop collisions from generating additional space debris <Cite id="esa-space-environment-2026" />. ADRAS-J approached to within 15 m of a discarded upper stage, but autonomously aborted after detecting an attitude anomaly <Cite id="adras-j-15m-2024" />. Its controlled deorbit was initiated by March 2026 <Cite id="adras-j-deorbit-2026" />. Its successor ADRAS-J2 is planned to demonstrate the capture and removal of a 3 t rocket upper stage in fiscal 2027 <Cite id="adras-j2-isar-2026" />.

Not all servicing missions reach their target. NASA canceled OSAM-1, which was to refuel an unprepared satellite, in March 2024 due to technical, cost and schedule issues <Cite id="osam1-discontinued-2024" />. Plans for LINK to boost the Swift observatory were canceled in August 2026 <Cite id="swift-boost-update-2026" />, and Swift reentered the atmosphere on November 4 <Cite id="nasa-swift-reentry-2026" />.

On October 29, 2026 NASA issued a request for information asking industry to propose ways that Hubble could be boosted, serviced or safely deorbited <Cite id="nasa-hubble-rfi-2026" />.

## Space forces robots to act alone

Manipulation can postpone working with no one to reset the robot; space cannot. Latencies on the order of tens of milliseconds limit a [drone's](/adjacent/drones/) safe speed <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />, while communication delays of minutes mean that spacecraft on Mars respond autonomously to unexpected situations <Cite id="jpl-ipn-pnt-2024" />.

## Data is the bottleneck

Costly demonstrations and scarce data hinder robot learning in space <Cite id="space-robotics-bench-2025" />. Learning-based methods must also cope with the unique dynamics of space robots, stringent safety requirements, and limited computing resources available onboard <Cite id="ai-space-robot-ops-review-2026" />. Most data is either rendered or crowdsourced.

| Dataset or tool | What it provides |
| --- | --- |
| SPEED (2019) | Synthetic and real images for estimating a spacecraft's pose from one camera <Cite id="speed-challenge-2019" /> |
| URSO (2019) | Spacecraft images rendered with Unreal Engine 4 <Cite id="urso-2019" /> |
| AI4MARS (2021) | 326,000 crowdsourced terrain labels on 35,000 rover images; a model trained on it exceeded 96 percent accuracy <Cite id="ai4mars-2021" /> |
| SPEED+ (2021) | Lab images with controlled poses and space-like lighting <Cite id="speedplus-2021" /> |
| SPARK (2021) | Simulated color and depth images labeled by spacecraft or debris class <Cite id="spark-2021" /> |
| SurRender (2021) | Ray tracer spanning celestial distances and fine target detail <Cite id="surrender-2021" /> |

Space shares its [reliability](/frontier/reliability-gap/) bar with [surgical robotics](/adjacent/surgical/): add autonomy in small, verified steps, as AEGIS and the digital-twin check did <Cite id="aegis-curiosity-2017" /><Cite id="perseverance-ai-drive-2026" />.
