Space robotics builds and operates robots beyond Earth, from planetary rovers to station arms and satellite servicers. A signal takes 4 to 20 minutes to reach Mars <Cite id="jpl-ipn-pnt-2024" />, so rovers mostly drive themselves <Cite id="enav-perseverance-2026" />, and commercial servicers have docked with satellites since 2020 <Cite id="mev1-servicing-2025" />.

## Light delay makes rovers drive themselves

A rover cannot be steered live. Sheridan's 1993 review found that space <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> avoided delay by not teleoperating from the ground <Cite id="sheridan-time-delay-1993" />. NASA's Mars Exploration Rovers drove with onboard autonomy from 2004, over 11,000 m by October 2005 <Cite id="mer-mobility-software-2006" />, and within two years their visual odometry became a critical safety system <Cite id="mer-visual-odometry-2007" />.

Perseverance's AutoNav judged the terrain for 88 percent of the 17.7 km it drove in its first Mars year; Opportunity's record was 2.4 km over fourteen years <Cite id="perseverance-autonomy-2023" />. Its ENav software runs on a single-core CPU and raised daily driving by an order of magnitude <Cite id="enav-perseverance-2026" />. Since February 2026 the rover can [fix its own position](/classical/state-estimation/) by matching panoramas to orbital imagery, within 25 cm in about two minutes <Cite id="mars-global-localization-2026" />.

## Rovers that plan, choose and fly

In December 2025 JPL had [vision-language models](/manipulation/vla-models/) plan Perseverance's waypoints from orbital imagery and slope data. A digital twin checked over 500,000 telemetry variables, and the rover drove 210 m, then 246 m two days later <Cite id="perseverance-ai-drive-2026" />.

Onboard software picks science targets. AEGIS has aimed Curiosity's ChemCam laser since 2016 <Cite id="aegis-curiosity-2017" /> and picks Perseverance's SuperCam targets right after long drives <Cite id="perseverance-autonomy-2023" />. NASA calls PIXL's adaptive sampling the first AI on Mars to decide from real-time rock chemistry <Cite id="pixl-adaptive-sampling-2024" />.

On April 19, 2021, Ingenuity made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet <Cite id="ingenuity-first-flight-2021" />. Built for five flights, it flew 72 in almost three years <Cite id="ingenuity-mission-end-2024" />. NASA's likeliest cause for its final crash is ground too featureless for its navigation <Cite id="ingenuity-investigation-2024" />.

Also: wheel-slip prediction from terrain <Cite id="traversability-slip-2023" />, slip detection from wheel sensors <Cite id="proprioceptive-slip-2022" />.

## Mars oxygen worked; lunar digging is harder

<Term id="in-situ-resource-utilization">In-situ resource utilization</Term>, making supplies on site, first worked on another planet with MOXIE, which made oxygen from Martian carbon dioxide <Cite id="moxie-sciadv-2022" />. Across sixteen runs it made 122 g, peaking at 12 g per hour at 98 percent purity, about twice NASA's goals <Cite id="moxie-completion-2023" />.

<Image id="moxie-installation-perseverance-2020" />

NASA's PRIME-1 reached the lunar south pole in March 2025 on Athena, which landed on its side and worked 10 to 12 hours of the ten days planned. Its TRIDENT drill moved as commanded but never reached the ground <Cite id="prime-1-lunar-2025" /><Cite id="hancock-prime1-2026" />. Firefly's Blue Ghost ran every NASA payload, and its LISTER probe dug to 1 m, the deepest robotic planetary thermal probe yet <Cite id="blue-ghost-lister-2025" />.

## Learned walking is tested on Earth first

ETH Zurich's ANYmal-D and Magnecko crossed lunar soil simulant in the ESA/DLR LUNA hall in 2025, slowed by sinkage, slip, dust and harsh light <Cite id="luna-legged-2026" />. In simulation, humanoid policies trained for Earth fell under lunar gravity, and Moon-trained ones fell on Earth <Cite id="casadesus-humanoid-moon-2026" />.

JAXA's MMX launched on October 20, 2026 to return a Phobos sample <Cite id="jaxa-mmx-launch-2026" />, carrying the DLR and CNES rover Idefix <Cite id="dlr-idefix-2026" />. China postponed Chang'e-7, a lunar south-pole rover and hopper, from August 2026 <Cite id="xinhua-change7-delay-2026" /><Cite id="planetary-change7" />.

Also: cave planner PLGRIM <Cite id="plgrim-2021" />, jumping legs <Cite id="mars-jumping-2023" />, ReachBot <Cite id="reachbot-melt-2024" />.

## In orbit, an arm pushes its own satellite

<Term id="on-orbit-servicing">On-orbit servicing</Term> means repairing, refueling and upgrading satellites and removing debris <Cite id="space-manipulator-review-2025" />. In free fall, swinging an arm turns its satellite the other way. Umetani and Yoshida derived how to control such arms from conservation of momentum in 1989 <Cite id="umetani-yoshida-1989" />.

## Debris does not cooperate

ESA's 2026 report says active removal is required to stop collisions multiplying debris <Cite id="esa-space-environment-2026" />. ADRAS-J closed to 15 m of a discarded upper stage, then aborted on its own after an attitude anomaly <Cite id="adras-j-15m-2024" />, and began its controlled deorbit by March 2026 <Cite id="adras-j-deorbit-2026" />. ADRAS-J2 targets fiscal 2027 to capture and remove a 3 t upper stage <Cite id="adras-j2-isar-2026" />.

Some servicers never reach their target. NASA dropped OSAM-1, meant to refuel an unprepared satellite, in March 2024 over technical, cost and schedule problems <Cite id="osam1-discontinued-2024" />. LINK's planned boost of the Swift telescope was canceled in August 2026 <Cite id="swift-boost-update-2026" />, and Swift re-entered on November 4 <Cite id="nasa-swift-reentry-2026" />.

On October 29, 2026 NASA asked industry how it might boost, service or safely deorbit Hubble <Cite id="nasa-hubble-rfi-2026" />.

## Space forces robots to act alone

Manipulation can postpone working with no one to reset the robot; space cannot. Tens of milliseconds cap a [drone's](/adjacent/drones/) safe speed <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />, while Mars delays run to minutes, so spacecraft there handle surprises without real-time commands <Cite id="jpl-ipn-pnt-2024" />.

## Data is the bottleneck

Costly demonstrations and scarce data hinder robot learning in space <Cite id="space-robotics-bench-2025" />, on top of odd dynamics, small onboard computers and strict safety rules <Cite id="ai-space-robot-ops-review-2026" />. Most data is rendered or crowdsourced.

| Dataset or tool | What it provides |
| --- | --- |
| SPEED (2019) | Synthetic and real images for estimating a spacecraft's pose from one camera <Cite id="speed-challenge-2019" /> |
| URSO (2019) | Spacecraft images rendered with Unreal Engine 4 <Cite id="urso-2019" /> |
| AI4MARS (2021) | 326,000 crowdsourced terrain labels on 35,000 rover images; a model trained on it exceeded 96 percent accuracy <Cite id="ai4mars-2021" /> |
| SPEED+ (2021) | Lab images with controlled poses and space-like lighting <Cite id="speedplus-2021" /> |
| SPARK (2021) | Simulated color and depth images labeled by spacecraft or debris class <Cite id="spark-2021" /> |
| SurRender (2021) | Ray tracer spanning celestial distances and fine target detail <Cite id="surrender-2021" /> |

Space shares its [reliability](/frontier/reliability-gap/) bar with [surgical robotics](/adjacent/surgical/): add autonomy in small, verified steps, as AEGIS and the digital-twin check did <Cite id="aegis-curiosity-2017" /><Cite id="perseverance-ai-drive-2026" />.
