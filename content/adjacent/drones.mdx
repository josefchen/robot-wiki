Aerial robotics builds and controls flying robots, from delivery drones and racing quadrotors to <Term id="swarm-robotics">swarms</Term>. Flight was an early learning win: a reinforcement-learning drone beat human world champions in its lab in 2023 <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />, and a one-camera drone won an organizer-run race in 2025 <Cite id="monorace-2026" />.

A drone's top safe speed depends on how fast its camera reports an obstacle: about 70 ms for a stereo camera, far less for an event camera <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />.

## Flight simulates well

A quadrotor in open air touches nothing. Its motion is differentially flat, so a smooth path alone fixes the inputs to fly it <Cite id="faessler-flatness-2018" />, and standard models cover its dynamics and control <Cite id="mahony-multirotor-2012" />. The hard part is speed: racing quadrotors pass 100 km/h at several g <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="flying" />

Classical stacks plan a smooth trajectory <Cite id="mellinger-minimum-snap-2011" /> and track it with a geometric controller <Cite id="lee-se3-control-2010" />. In 2021 time-optimal planning beat professional pilots <Cite id="foehn-time-optimal-2021" />. Neural-Fly learns aerodynamic effects offline and adjusts a few coefficients in flight as the wind changes <Cite id="neural-fly-2022" />.

Also: simulators Flightmare <Cite id="flightmare-2020" />, FlightGoggles <Cite id="flightgoggles-2019" />, AirSim <Cite id="airsim-2017" />.

## Learned controllers beat the champions

Zurich's Swift beat three human champions, including two league world champions, in several races each. It learned by model-free reinforcement learning in simulation, then retrained on noise and dynamics models fitted from real flights <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

Reinforcement learning beat optimal control on the same task above 12 g and 108 km/h, because it optimizes the race directly instead of tracking a planned path <Cite id="racing-rl-vs-oc-2023" />. A hybrid with a differentiable model-predictive controller inside the learner matched it at up to 21 m/s <Cite id="ac-mpc-2026" />.

The wins came with conditions. At the 2025 A2RL race the organizers set hardware and track <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" />, and TU Delft's one-camera drone beat every AI team and three human world champions <Cite id="monorace-2026" />. In January 2026 Minchan Kim won a best-of-nine final from 4 to 4 when TII Racing's drone struck a gate <Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />.

Champion policies often crash at once on unseen tracks; a 2026 method generalized 7.4 times better <Cite id="green-generalization-2026" />.

## The autonomy stack on a flying robot

A drone must know where it is without GPS. <Term id="visual-inertial-odometry">Visual-inertial odometry</Term> fuses one camera and a low-cost <Term id="imu">IMU</Term> into the pose and velocity the controller needs <Cite id="vins-mono-2018" /><Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. The inner loop often runs on a PX4 microcontroller, with far lower latency than Linux <Cite id="px4-2015" />.

<Image id="dji-mavic-pro-in-flight" />

### Perception latency bounds the whole system

A drone cannot dodge what its camera has not reported. At speed $v$, an obstacle seen at range $s$ leaves $s/v - \tau$ after latency $\tau$; if that is shorter than the swerve, no controller saves it <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />. An event camera reports per-pixel brightness changes without waiting for a frame.

| Camera | Sideways acceleration | Top safe speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 70 ms stereo | 25 m/s² | About 19 m/s <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" /> |
| 70 ms stereo | 50 m/s² | About 25 m/s |
| Event, 8 m range | 10 m/s² | 7% faster |
| Event, 8 m range | 50 m/s² | 22% faster, 31.03 against 25.40 m/s |
| Event against fastest stereo | 200 m/s² | 7 to 12% faster |

In 2020 an event-camera quadrotor dodged fast-moving objects with only onboard sensing and computing <Cite id="falanga-event-avoidance-2020" />.

Also: perception-aware control PAMPC <Cite id="pampc-2018" />.

Language-driven flight is far slower.

### End-to-end learning skips the interfaces

Each hand-off between sensing, mapping and planning adds delay and passes on errors <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" />. Swift kept a modular front end feeding a small network that outputs thrust and body rates <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. Later controllers remove more.

| System | Approach | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning to Fly by Crashing (2017) | 11,500 crashes plus the good parts of the same flights | A navigation policy <Cite id="learning-crash-2017" /> |
| Deep Drone Acrobatics (2020) | Imitates a privileged optimal controller in simulation | Maneuvers at up to 3 g <Cite id="deep-drone-acrobatics-2020" /> |
| Loquercio et al. (2021) | Same recipe, depth images to paths | Forests, snow and collapsed buildings with no extra training <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" /> |
| Geles et al. (2024) | Reinforcement learning, pixels to pilot commands, no state estimator | Gates at up to 40 km/h <Cite id="geles-pixels-2024" /> |
| Dream to Fly (2025) | A <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> learned from pixels | Racing where PPO and SAC struggled <Cite id="dream-to-fly-2025" /> |
| Zhang et al. (2025) | Differentiable physics, depth to control | Forests at up to 20 m/s on a \$21 computer <Cite id="zhang-diff-physics-2025" /> |
| MonoRace (2025) | Network sends motor commands directly | 500 commands per second <Cite id="monorace-2026" /> |
| Bianco et al. (2026) | Differentiable physics, vision | 99.3 percent through a moving gap in simulation after minutes of training; 8 of 10 real attempts <Cite id="bianco-narrow-gap-2026" /> |
| Crazyflow (2026) | Reinforcement learning during flight | Recovery policy learned in 0.37 seconds as a thrown drone fell <Cite id="crazyflow-2026" /> |

## Swarms coordinate without assigning tasks

Swarms fly many cheap drones at once, for search or light shows. Reynolds' 1987 flocking model treats the group as local attraction and repulsion between neighbors <Cite id="reynolds-boids-1987" />. Vásárhelyi and colleagues tuned such rules to flock 30 drones outdoors <Cite id="vasarhelyi-flocking-2018" />, but reactive rules do not look ahead.

These drones coordinate through shared paths or learned behavior. In Ukraine, jamming is pushing military drones toward terrain navigation, autonomous target lock and, eventually, mesh-networked swarms <Cite id="spectrum-ukraine-autonomy-2026" />.

## Delivery drones fly at commercial scale

Drones have what manipulation lacks: commercial fleets. Zipline reports more than 3 million deliveries and nearly 150 million autonomous miles since 2016 <Cite id="zipline-safety-facts" />, and Wing passed one million deliveries in June 2026 <Cite id="wing-walmart-2026" />. Walmart passed 2 million drone deliveries in November 2026 and agreed to add 300 Zipline sites <Cite id="walmart-zipline-300-2026" />.

Also: Walmart's first million <Cite id="walmart-drone-1m-2026" />, Zipline's \$1.5 billion raise <Cite id="zipline-raise-2026" />.

## Robotics takes latency budgets and sim-to-real from flight

A policy inferring at 20 Hz controls a contact-rich task at about 20 Hz whatever the arm's bandwidth, the budget the latency study set for flight <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" /> ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/)). The sim-to-real recipe transfers too: gap models fitted from real data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" /> and privileged imitation with realistic sensor noise <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" /> ([sim-to-real transfer](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/), [parallel simulation](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl/)).

Champion claims need their conditions attached <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" /><Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />. The distance from peak to reliable performance is the [reliability gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), and [surgical robotics](/adjacent/surgical/) shows a bar cleared under regulators.
