Aerial robotics is the design and control of robots that fly, from delivery drones and racing quadrotors to <Term id="swarm-robotics">swarms</Term>. Flight was an early learning success because its dynamics simulate well: a reinforcement-learning system beat human world champions in its own lab in 2023 <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />, and a one-camera drone won an organiser-run race in 2025 <Cite id="monorace-2026" />.

## Why flight was the first solved testbed

A quadrotor is a benign case for simulation. Its dynamics are differentially flat in position and heading, even with linear rotor drag <Cite id="faessler-flatness-2018" />, and standard models cover its dynamics, estimation and control <Cite id="mahony-multirotor-2012" />. Flight also has no contact: an aerial robot either flies in free space or has already failed. Racing quadrotors still exceed 100 km/h and accelerate at several times gravity <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

Classical stacks plan a trajectory and then track it. Mellinger and Kumar generated minimum-snap trajectories for quadrotors <Cite id="mellinger-minimum-snap-2011" />, and Lee and colleagues built a geometric tracking controller on SE(3) <Cite id="lee-se3-control-2010" />. In 2021 time-optimal trajectory planning outperformed professional human pilots in racing <Cite id="foehn-time-optimal-2021" />.

Swift, from the University of Zurich, raced three human champions, including the world champions of two leagues, and won several races against each <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. It trained its control policy in simulation with model-free reinforcement learning <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. The team then fitted residual models of perception noise and unmodeled dynamics from real flight data and retrained <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

In September 2023, Song and colleagues showed that an RL controller outperformed optimal control on the same racing task <Cite id="racing-rl-vs-oc-2023" />. Optimal control splits the problem into planning and control with a trajectory as the interface, which limits the controller under unmodeled effects. RL optimizes a task-level objective directly and handles model uncertainty through domain randomization; it flew above 12 g and 108 km/h <Cite id="racing-rl-vs-oc-2023" />. A later hybrid embedded a differentiable MPC in an actor-critic framework and matched model-free RL at up to 21 m/s <Cite id="ac-mpc-2026" />.

In April 2025 a TU Delft drone with one rolling-shutter camera and an IMU won the A2RL race in Abu Dhabi, beating all AI teams and three human world champions in a knockout tournament <Cite id="monorace-2026" />. TU Delft notes that Swift raced in a lab where the researchers controlled conditions, hardware and track, while the A2RL organisers designed both hardware and track <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" />.

In January 2026 world FPV champion Minchan Kim beat TII Racing's drone from a 4 to 4 tie when it struck a gate and could not recover <Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />. Champion policies stay narrow: they often crash immediately in unseen configurations, and a 2026 method improved generalization 7.4x <Cite id="green-generalization-2026" />.

## The autonomy stack on a flying robot

One camera and one low-cost IMU are the minimum sensor suite for metric six-DoF state estimation <Cite id="vins-mono-2018" />. <Term id="visual-inertial-odometry">Visual-inertial odometry</Term> fuses image features with IMU measurements into the pose and velocity the controller needs, without GPS or motion capture <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. The fast inner loop often runs on a microcontroller stack such as PX4, which has substantially lower latency than a typical robotics Linux system <Cite id="px4-2015" />.

### Perception latency bounds the whole system

Falanga, Kim and Scaramuzza asked how fast a drone can fly given its perception latency and the acceleration its airframe can produce <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />. A drone at speed $v$ sees an obstacle at sensing range $s$, and its pipeline delays that information by latency $\tau$. Avoiding the obstacle at maximum lateral acceleration $u$ takes time proportional to the square root of obstacle size over $u$. If $s/v - \tau$ is shorter than the maneuver, no controller saves the drone <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />.

A 70 ms stereo pipeline, the upper bound the study took from a commercial camera's datasheet, supports about 19 m/s at 25 m/s² of lateral acceleration and 25 m/s at 50 m/s² <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />. An event camera reports per-pixel brightness changes without waiting for a frame. At 8 m of range its ceiling is 7 percent higher on the least agile airframe and 22 percent higher at 50 m/s², 31.03 against 25.40 m/s. The paper's own summary of 7 to 12 percent is for its most agile airframe, at 200 m/s², against the fastest stereo camera <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />.

In 2020 an event-camera quadrotor dodged fast-moving objects using only onboard sensing and computation <Cite id="falanga-event-avoidance-2020" />. Vision-language-action flight is far slower: RaceVLA averaged 1.04 m/s with a top speed of 2.02 m/s <Cite id="racevla-2025" />. On the CognitiveDrone benchmark RaceVLA succeeded 31.3 percent of the time, and CognitiveDrone-R1, which adds a reasoning module, 77.2 percent <Cite id="cognitivedrone-2025" />.

### End-to-end learning skips the interfaces

Deep Drone Acrobatics trained a policy entirely in simulation by imitating an optimal controller with privileged information, and flew maneuvers at up to 3 g <Cite id="deep-drone-acrobatics-2020" />. In 2021 Loquercio and colleagues used the same recipe to map noisy depth images directly to collision-free trajectories <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" />. With realistic simulated sensor noise, the policy transferred to forests, snow, derailed trains and collapsed buildings with no additional training. The authors argue that separating sensing, mapping and planning "can be problematic for high-speed navigation in cluttered environments" because latency compounds and errors propagate <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" />.

Swift kept a modular front end: a visual-inertial estimator and a gate-detection network feed a Kalman filter, and a small two-layer RL network maps that state to collective thrust and body rates <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. Later systems drop explicit state estimation. Geles and colleagues fly from pixels with the thrust and body-rate commands human pilots use <Cite id="geles-pixels-2024" />, and Dream to Fly learned racing from pixels with a world model where PPO and SAC struggled <Cite id="dream-to-fly-2025" />.

MonoRace keeps a state estimator, but its network skips inner-loop controllers and sends motor commands at 500 Hz <Cite id="monorace-2026" />. Training through differentiable physics produced a depth-to-control policy that flies forests at up to 20 m/s on a \$21 computer <Cite id="zhang-diff-physics-2025" />.

## Swarm robotics

Swarm robotics uses many cheap platforms to cover a search area, map a forest or fly a light show. Its founding models came from biology, Reynolds' 1987 flocking model being the canonical one <Cite id="reynolds-boids-1987" />, and treat the group as a dynamical system of local attraction and repulsion. Vásárhelyi and colleagues optimized those terms and flew 30 drones in coherent outdoor flocking <Cite id="vasarhelyi-flocking-2018" />. Reactive models have no lookahead.

<SwarmControlTable className="my-6" />

Soria, Schiano and Floreano folded the interaction terms into a nonlinear model-predictive controller with obstacle and neighbour terms <Cite id="soria-nmpc-swarm-2021" />. In simulation it flew faster and with fewer collisions than the reactive equivalent, and five quadrotors flew it through a real indoor obstacle field <Cite id="soria-nmpc-swarm-2021" />. EGO-Swarm's agents generate safe, dynamically feasible trajectories in several milliseconds over an unreliable trajectory-sharing network <Cite id="ego-swarm-2021" />.

Zhou and colleagues ran a spatial-temporal trajectory optimizer on palm-sized drones, solving in milliseconds from onboard sensing with no external localization <Cite id="micro-drone-swarm-2022" />. Ten of them flew through a dense bamboo forest <Cite id="micro-drone-swarm-2022" />. The differentiable-physics policy coordinates several drones without communication or centralized planning <Cite id="zhang-diff-physics-2025" />. Multi-agent RL with league self-play beat a champion-level human pilot in multi-player races above 22 m/s and cut collisions by 50 percent against single-agent baselines <Cite id="geles-marl-2026" />.

Everything cited above coordinates implicitly; none of it decides who goes where. In Ukraine, electronic warfare is pushing military drones toward terrain-based navigation, autonomous target lock-on and, eventually, mesh-networked swarms <Cite id="spectrum-ukraine-autonomy-2026" />.

## Delivery and regulation

Zipline reports more than 3 million deliveries and more than 140 million commercial autonomous miles since its first commercial delivery in 2016 <Cite id="zipline-safety-facts" />. In January 2026 it reported US delivery growth of about 15 percent week over week for seven months <Cite id="zipline-2m-2026" />. Wing reports well over one million commercial deliveries and plans more than 270 Walmart locations reaching 40 million Americans by 2027 <Cite id="wing-walmart-2026" />. Walmart reported 1 million drone deliveries in May 2026, from 66 stores in four states <Cite id="walmart-drone-1m-2026" />.

The FAA and TSA proposed Part 108 in August 2025 to enable routine flight beyond visual line of sight <Cite id="faa-part-108-nprm-2025" />. The FAA reopened comments on electronic conspicuity and right-of-way until February 11, 2026 <Cite id="faa-part-108-reopen-2026" />.

## What the rest of robotics takes from it

Latency budgets transfer directly. A policy that infers at 20 Hz controls a contact-rich task at about 20 Hz whatever the arm's bandwidth, the reasoning the perception-latency study formalised for flight <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />. [Real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) covers that budget for manipulators.

The sim-to-real recipe also carries over: residual models fitted from real data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" /> plus privileged imitation with realistic sensor noise <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" />. [Sim-to-real transfer](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/) and [parallel simulation](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl/) cover it for other robots.

Champion claims need context. Swift won in its own lab and MonoRace on organiser hardware <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" />, and the 2026 final turned on one unrecovered gate strike <Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />. The distance between peak and reliable performance is the [reliability gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), and [surgical robotics](/adjacent/surgical/) shows a bar cleared under regulatory oversight.
