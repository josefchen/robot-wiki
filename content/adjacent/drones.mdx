Aerial robotics builds and controls flying robots, ranging from delivery drones and quadrotors used for racing to <Term id="swarm-robotics">swarms</Term> of robots. Flight was an early learning win. In 2023, a drone using reinforcement learning outperformed human world champions in its lab <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. And in 2025, a drone equipped with one camera won a drone race organized by the competition's hosts <Cite id="monorace-2026" />.

## Flight simulates well

A quadrotor in open air touches nothing. The system is differentially flat, meaning that given a smooth trajectory, the inputs required to follow it can be calculated <Cite id="faessler-flatness-2018" />. There are also standard models of quadrotor dynamics and control <Cite id="mahony-multirotor-2012" />. The hard part is speed, as racing drones can reach speeds in excess of 100 km/h at several g <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="flying" />

Classical approaches use a trajectory planner to generate a smooth trajectory <Cite id="mellinger-minimum-snap-2011" /> followed by a geometry-based tracking controller <Cite id="lee-se3-control-2010" />. In 2021 autonomous planners producing time-optimal trajectories beat professional pilots <Cite id="foehn-time-optimal-2021" />. Neural-Fly learns a model of aerodynamic effects offline, and fine-tunes a small number of coefficients online to accommodate changes in wind <Cite id="neural-fly-2022" />.

Also: simulators Flightmare <Cite id="flightmare-2020" />, FlightGoggles <Cite id="flightgoggles-2019" />, AirSim <Cite id="airsim-2017" />.

## Learned controllers beat the champions

Swift, developed in Zurich, defeated three human champions (two of them league world champions), each in multiple races. First trained with model-free reinforcement learning in simulation, Swift was retrained using noise and dynamics models fitted from real flight data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

Reinforcement learning outperformed optimal control on the same task above 12 g and 108 km/h, since it directly optimizes the race instead of attempting to follow a planned path <Cite id="racing-rl-vs-oc-2023" />. A differentiable model-predictive controller embedded into a reinforcement learning algorithm matched it at up to 21 m/s <Cite id="ac-mpc-2026" />.

The wins came with conditions. In the 2025 A2RL competition, drones raced on a track and with hardware specified by the competition <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" />. A drone from TU Delft used one camera and defeated all AI teams as well as three human world champions <Cite id="monorace-2026" />. In January 2026, Minchan Kim was victorious in a best-of-nine drone racing final from 4 to 4 after TII Racing's drone hit a gate <Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />.

However, champion policies often crash immediately when deployed on a new track; a 2026 method showed a 7.4 times improvement in generalization performance <Cite id="green-generalization-2026" />.

## The autonomy stack on a flying robot

Autonomous flight requires a state estimator that does not rely on GPS. <Term id="visual-inertial-odometry">Visual-inertial odometry</Term> fuses one camera and a low-cost <Term id="imu">IMU</Term> into the pose and velocity the controller needs <Cite id="vins-mono-2018" /><Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. Inner-loop control on drones is often performed on PX4 since microcontrollers provide much lower latencies compared to Linux-based solutions <Cite id="px4-2015" />.

### Perception latency bounds the whole system

The reaction time of a flying robot is limited by the latency of its perception. Obstacles detected at a distance $s$ when flying with velocity $v$ can only be avoided if $s/v - \tau$ (where $\tau$ is the latency) is larger than the time required to execute an evasive maneuver <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />. Event cameras only report changes in brightness per pixel and do not suffer from frame latency.

| Camera | Sideways acceleration | Speed in the study’s model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stereo, 70 ms, 8 m range | 25 m/s² | About 19 m/s <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" /> |
| Stereo, 70 ms, 8 m range | 50 m/s² | About 25 m/s |
| Event, 8 m range | 10 m/s² | 7% faster |
| Event, 8 m range | 50 m/s² | 22% faster, 31.03 against 25.40 m/s |
| Event against fastest stereo | 200 m/s² | 7 to 12% faster |

In 2020 an event-camera quadrotor was able to avoid fast-moving obstacles using only onboard computation and perception <Cite id="falanga-event-avoidance-2020" />.

Also: perception-aware control PAMPC <Cite id="pampc-2018" />.

Language-driven flight is far slower.

### End-to-end learning skips the interfaces

Removing interfaces between sensing, mapping and planning reduces latency and avoids compounding module errors <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" />. Swift uses a front end with multiple modular components, followed by a small neural network to predict thrust and body rate control commands <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. Later controllers have removed more components.

| System | Approach | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning to Fly by Crashing (2017) | 11,500 crashes plus the good parts of the same flights | A navigation policy <Cite id="learning-crash-2017" /> |
| Deep Drone Acrobatics (2020) | Imitates a privileged optimal controller in simulation | Maneuvers at up to 3 g <Cite id="deep-drone-acrobatics-2020" /> |
| Loquercio et al. (2021) | Same recipe, depth images to paths | Forests, snow and collapsed buildings with no extra training <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" /> |
| Geles et al. (2024) | Reinforcement learning, pixels to pilot commands, no state estimator | Gates at up to 40 km/h <Cite id="geles-pixels-2024" /> |
| Dream to Fly (2025) | A <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> learned from pixels | Racing where PPO and SAC struggled <Cite id="dream-to-fly-2025" /> |
| Zhang et al. (2025) | Differentiable physics, depth to control | Forests at up to 20 m/s on a \$21 computer <Cite id="zhang-diff-physics-2025" /> |
| MonoRace (2025) | Network sends motor commands directly | 500 commands per second <Cite id="monorace-2026" /> |
| Bianco et al. (2026) | Differentiable physics, vision | 99.3 percent through a moving gap in simulation after minutes of training; 8 of 10 real attempts <Cite id="bianco-narrow-gap-2026" /> |
| Crazyflow (2026) | Reinforcement learning during flight | Recovery policy learned in 0.37 seconds as a thrown drone fell <Cite id="crazyflow-2026" /> |

## Swarms coordinate without assigning tasks

Swarms fly many cheap drones at once, for search or light shows. Reynolds' 1987 work simulated flocking through attraction and repulsion rules between neighbors <Cite id="reynolds-boids-1987" />, an idea which Vásárhelyi and colleagues have since tuned to control 30 drones flying outside <Cite id="vasarhelyi-flocking-2018" />. These rules are reactive and do not look ahead.

These drones coordinate through shared paths or learned behavior. In Ukraine, jamming is driving military drones to navigate using terrain features, lock on to targets autonomously and, in the longer term, operate as mesh-networked swarms <Cite id="spectrum-ukraine-autonomy-2026" />.

## Delivery drones fly at commercial scale

Drones have what manipulation lacks: commercial fleets. Zipline reports that its drones have flown close to 150 million miles autonomously since 2016, making over 3 million deliveries <Cite id="zipline-safety-facts" />. In June 2026, Wing surpassed one million deliveries <Cite id="wing-walmart-2026" />, and in November 2026 Walmart surpassed 2 million deliveries by drone and agreed to add 300 Zipline sites <Cite id="walmart-zipline-300-2026" />.

Also: Walmart's first million <Cite id="walmart-drone-1m-2026" />, Zipline's \$1.5 billion raise <Cite id="zipline-raise-2026" />.

In August 2025, the FAA and TSA put out proposed draft Part 108 regulations to allow routine flight beyond the pilot's sight <Cite id="faa-part-108-nprm-2025" />, with the comment period reopened through February 11, 2026 <Cite id="faa-part-108-reopen-2026" />. As of November 2026, Wing characterized the final rule as forthcoming <Cite id="wing-fort-worth-2026" />.

## Robotics takes latency budgets and sim-to-real from flight

A policy running inference at 20 Hz controls a contact-rich task at about 20 Hz whatever the arm's bandwidth, which is the budget the latency study set for flight <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" /> ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/)). Robotics also benefits from flight research on how to close the sim-to-real gap, including modeling the gap using real data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" /> and privileged imitation with realistic sensor noise <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" /> ([sim-to-real transfer](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/), [parallel simulation](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl/)).

Champion claims should be reported with their conditions stated <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" /><Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />. The gap between peak and reliably achievable performance is the [reliability gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), and [surgical robotics](/adjacent/surgical/) shows a bar cleared under regulators.
