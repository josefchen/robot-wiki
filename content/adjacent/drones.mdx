Aerial robotics builds and controls flying robots, from delivery drones and racing quadrotors to <Term id="swarm-robotics">swarms</Term>. Flight was an early learning success because its dynamics simulate well: a reinforcement-learning system beat human world champions in its own lab in 2023 <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />, and a one-camera drone won an organizer-run race in 2025 <Cite id="monorace-2026" />.

How fast a drone can safely fly depends on how quickly its camera reports an obstacle: about 70 ms for an ordinary stereo camera, much less for an event camera <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />.

## Why flight was an early success

A quadrotor in open air touches nothing, so it is easy to simulate. Its motion is differentially flat in position and heading, even with rotor drag: a smooth path alone fixes the inputs needed to fly it <Cite id="faessler-flatness-2018" />, and standard models cover its dynamics, estimation and control <Cite id="mahony-multirotor-2012" />. The difficulty is speed: racing quadrotors exceed 100 km/h and accelerate at several times gravity <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="flying" />

Drone simulators keep rendering apart from physics <Cite id="flightmare-2020" />, and some put real hardware in the loop: FlightGoggles feeds rendered images to a real vehicle flying in a motion-capture room <Cite id="flightgoggles-2019" />, and AirSim connects flight hardware to Unreal Engine rendering <Cite id="airsim-2017" />.

Classical stacks plan a smooth trajectory <Cite id="mellinger-minimum-snap-2011" /> and track it with a geometric controller <Cite id="lee-se3-control-2010" />. In 2021 time-optimal planning outperformed professional human pilots in racing <Cite id="foehn-time-optimal-2021" />. Learning helps where models fall short: Neural-Fly learns a shared model of aerodynamic effects offline, then adjusts a few coefficients in flight as the wind changes <Cite id="neural-fly-2022" />.

## Learned controllers beat the champions

The University of Zurich's Swift raced three human champions, including the world champions of two leagues, and won several races against each. It was trained in simulation by model-free reinforcement learning; the team then fitted models of perception noise and unmodeled dynamics from real flights and retrained <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />.

In September 2023 Song and colleagues showed a reinforcement-learning controller outperforming optimal control on the same racing task, above 12 g and 108 km/h. Optimal control hands a trajectory from planner to controller, which limits the controller when the model is wrong; reinforcement learning optimizes the race objective directly and copes with model error through randomized training <Cite id="racing-rl-vs-oc-2023" />. A later hybrid with a differentiable model-predictive controller inside the learner matched model-free learning at up to 21 m/s <Cite id="ac-mpc-2026" />.

The wins came with conditions. Swift raced in a lab where the researchers controlled conditions, hardware and track; at the April 2025 A2RL race in Abu Dhabi the organizers designed the hardware and track <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" />, and a TU Delft drone with one rolling-shutter camera and an inertial sensor beat all AI teams and three human world champions in a knockout tournament <Cite id="monorace-2026" />. In January 2026 world FPV champion Minchan Kim beat TII Racing's drone in a best-of-nine tied at 4 to 4, when the drone struck a gate in the deciding race and could not recover <Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />. Champion policies are narrow and often crash immediately on unseen tracks; Green, Scaramuzza and colleagues reported a 7.4-fold gain in generalization in 2026 <Cite id="green-generalization-2026" />.

## The autonomy stack on a flying robot

A drone must know where it is without GPS. One camera and one low-cost <Term id="imu">IMU</Term>, a sensor for acceleration and rotation, are the minimum for estimating position and orientation <Cite id="vins-mono-2018" />, and <Term id="visual-inertial-odometry">visual-inertial odometry</Term> fuses the two into the pose and velocity the controller needs <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. The fast inner loop often runs on a microcontroller autopilot such as PX4, with substantially lower latency than a typical robotics Linux system <Cite id="px4-2015" />.

### Perception latency bounds the whole system

A drone cannot dodge what its camera has not yet reported. Falanga, Kim and Scaramuzza turned that into a speed limit: a drone at speed $v$ that sees an obstacle at range $s$ learns of it only after latency $\tau$, and if $s/v - \tau$ is shorter than the time to dodge at its maximum sideways acceleration, no controller can save it. A 70 ms stereo pipeline, the study's upper bound from a commercial camera's datasheet, supports about 19 m/s at 25 m/s² of lateral acceleration and 25 m/s at 50 m/s². An event camera reports per-pixel brightness changes without waiting for a frame; at 8 m of range it raises the ceiling by 7 percent on the least agile airframe and 22 percent at 50 m/s², 31.03 against 25.40 m/s. Against the fastest stereo camera on the most agile airframe, at 200 m/s², the gain is 7 to 12 percent <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" />. In 2020 an event-camera quadrotor dodged fast-moving objects using only onboard sensing and computing <Cite id="falanga-event-avoidance-2020" />, and PAMPC plans motion that keeps a chosen point in view <Cite id="pampc-2018" />.

Language-driven flight is far slower. The vision-language-action model RaceVLA averaged 1.04 m/s with a top speed of 2.02 m/s <Cite id="racevla-2025" />, and on the CognitiveDrone benchmark it succeeded 31.3 percent of the time against 77.2 percent for CognitiveDrone-R1, which adds a reasoning module <Cite id="cognitivedrone-2025" />. In October 2026 Jaewoo Lee, Guanya Shi and colleagues let a coding agent for an aerial manipulator test its programs in simulation before declaring a task done, raising success on six simulated tasks to 75 percent from 31.7 percent; the programs then completed 4 of 5 real pick-and-place trials <Cite id="fly-by-code-2026" />.

### End-to-end learning skips the interfaces

Each hand-off between sensing, mapping and planning adds delay and passes on errors, which Loquercio and colleagues argue "can be problematic for high-speed navigation in cluttered environments" <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" />. Swift kept a modular front end, an estimator and gate detector feeding a small network that outputs thrust and body rates <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" />. Later controllers remove more of them.

## Swarms

Swarm robotics uses many cheap platforms at once, to search an area or fly a light show. Its founding models, above all Reynolds' 1987 flocking model <Cite id="reynolds-boids-1987" />, came from biology and treat the group as local attraction and repulsion between neighbors. Vásárhelyi and colleagues tuned those rules to fly 30 drones in coherent outdoor flocking <Cite id="vasarhelyi-flocking-2018" />, but reactive rules do not look ahead.

<SwarmControlTable className="my-6" />

Lookahead helped: a model-predictive version flew faster and with fewer collisions than the reactive one in simulation, and five quadrotors flew it through a real indoor obstacle field <Cite id="soria-nmpc-swarm-2021" />. EGO-Swarm's drones plan safe paths in milliseconds while sharing them over an unreliable network <Cite id="ego-swarm-2021" />, and Zhou and colleagues flew ten palm-sized drones through a dense bamboo forest with no external positioning <Cite id="micro-drone-swarm-2022" />. The differentiable-physics policy coordinates several drones with no communication <Cite id="zhang-diff-physics-2025" />, and multi-agent reinforcement learning with league self-play beat a champion-level human in multi-player races above 22 m/s, with 50 percent fewer collisions than single-agent baselines <Cite id="geles-marl-2026" />.

None of these systems assigns tasks among drones; they coordinate through shared paths or learned behavior. In Ukraine, electronic jamming is pushing military drones toward navigating by terrain, locking on to targets alone and, eventually, mesh-networked swarms <Cite id="spectrum-ukraine-autonomy-2026" />.

## Delivery and regulation

Drones have what manipulation lacks, commercial fleets at scale. Zipline reports more than 3 million deliveries and nearly 150 million commercial autonomous miles since its first commercial delivery in 2016 <Cite id="zipline-safety-facts" />. Wing reported well over one million commercial deliveries in June 2026 and plans more than 270 Walmart locations reaching 40 million Americans by 2027 <Cite id="wing-walmart-2026" />. Walmart, which uses both, passed 1 million drone deliveries in May 2026 <Cite id="walmart-drone-1m-2026" /> and 2 million in November, when it agreed to add 300 Zipline sites <Cite id="walmart-zipline-300-2026" />; Zipline raised \$1.5 billion the same day <Cite id="zipline-raise-2026" />.

Routine flight beyond the pilot's line of sight awaits rules: the FAA and TSA proposed Part 108 to enable it in August 2025 <Cite id="faa-part-108-nprm-2025" />, and the FAA reopened comments on electronic conspicuity and right-of-way until February 11, 2026 <Cite id="faa-part-108-reopen-2026" />. In November 2026 Wing still described the final rule as upcoming <Cite id="wing-fort-worth-2026" />.

## What the rest of robotics takes from flight

Latency budgets transfer: a policy inferring at 20 Hz controls a contact-rich task at about 20 Hz whatever the arm's bandwidth, the reasoning the latency study formalized for flight <Cite id="falanga-latency-2019" /> ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/)). So does the sim-to-real recipe: models of the gap fitted from real data <Cite id="swift-drone-racing-2023" /> plus privileged imitation with realistic sensor noise <Cite id="high-speed-flight-2021" /> ([sim-to-real transfer](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/), [parallel simulation](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl/)). Champion claims need their conditions attached, a lab or an organizer's track and a final decided by one gate strike <Cite id="tudelft-a2rl-2025" /><Cite id="a2rl-2026-final" />. The distance between peak and reliable performance is the [reliability gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), and [surgical robotics](/adjacent/surgical/) shows a bar cleared under regulatory oversight.
