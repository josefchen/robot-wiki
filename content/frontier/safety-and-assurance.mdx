Safety assurance is the work of showing that a robot system is safe enough to run. No industrial standard certifies a learned policy, so what ships is a verifiable safety layer around the policy <Cite id="iso-iec-tr-5469-2024" />.

<Image id="robotic-welding-cell-guarding-2014" />

<span id="four-ways-to-share-a-workspace" />
## Fenceless robots use four collaborative modes, two of them quantitative

<CollaborativeOperationModes className="my-6" />

ISO/TS 15066 defines four ways to share space, NIST's Marvel and Norcross summarize. A monitored stop forbids motion while a person is present, and hand guiding gives the operator control. <Term id="speed-and-separation-monitoring">Speed and separation monitoring</Term> keeps a computed distance, and <Term id="power-and-force-limiting">power and force limiting</Term> allows contact but bounds it <Cite id="marvel-norcross-2017" />.

Separation monitoring assumes a person heads straight for the robot at up to 2,000 mm/s unless measured, in an equation Marvel and Norcross call a rough approximation <Cite id="marvel-norcross-2017" />.

Force limits come from measured pain. Haddadin and colleagues measured it with crash tests in 2009 <Cite id="haddadin-2009" />, and Han and colleagues' 37-subject impactor tests give limits by body region ([contact forces](/classical/control/)) <Cite id="han-force-pain-2024" />.

Also: ISO/TS 15066 <Cite id="iso-ts-15066" />, folded into ISO 10218 in 2025 <Cite id="hartmann-iso-10218-2026" />.

<span id="the-stack-the-standards-actually-form" />
## Risk assessment comes first, and a compliant robot does not make a compliant cell

Every robot standard starts from a <Term id="risk-assessment">risk assessment</Term> of the installation. ISO 12100 sets the steps: identify hazards, estimate and evaluate risks, then reduce them <Cite id="iso-12100" />, in the order design, guard, inform <Cite id="osha-otm-robots" />.

ISO 10218-1 makes the robot the manufacturer's obligation, and ISO 10218-2 makes the cell the integrator's <Cite id="iso-10218-1-2025" /> <Cite id="iso-10218-2-2025" />. Both were revised in February 2025, and the US adoption adds a part for users <Cite id="a3-r1506-2025-store" />.

## Safety controls carry rated integrity levels, and an emergency stop cannot detect a hazard

<Term id="functional-safety">Functional safety</Term> grades the control system that enforces these requirements: IEC 61508 by <Term id="safety-integrity-level">safety integrity level</Term> <Cite id="iec-61508-1-2010" />, and ISO 13849-1, for machinery, by <Term id="performance-level">performance level</Term> <Cite id="iso-13849-1-2023" />.

An <Term id="emergency-stop">emergency stop</Term> cannot detect a hazard; someone must see one and press. ISO 13850 specifies it, with IEC 60204-1 for the electrical side <Cite id="iso-13850-2015" /> <Cite id="iec-60204-1-2016" />.

<span id="the-humanoid-gap-and-the-standard-that-is-not-finished" />
### The humanoid standard is still a committee draft at stage 30.60

Humanoids fall, and industrial standards never planned for it. ISO/CD 25785-1, for actively balancing robots, closed comments on 8 July 2026 and still sat at stage 30.60 in November <Cite id="iso-cd-25785-1" />. ISO 13482's second edition, for service robots, passed its final-draft ballot on 11 November 2026 <Cite id="iso-13482-revision-2026" />.

Each humanoid runs under its own risk assessment: South Korea exempted 20 Robros humanoids from fence rules on that condition <Cite id="korea-humanoid-sandbox-2026" />. Their safety is disputed:

| Who | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| Agility | Digit 5 avoids people, stops or sits down under an independent safety controller <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" /> |
| Rodney Brooks | Walking humanoids "will remain too unsafe to be in close proximity to real humans" <Cite id="brooks-scorecard-2026" /> |
| Figure's former safety head | Alleged its robots "were powerful enough to fracture a human skull"; Figure calls this "falsehoods" <Cite id="cnbc-figure-whistleblower-2025" /> |

Past harm came from fixed robots. Of 41 US robot-related workplace deaths from 1992 to 2017, 83% involved stationary robots, often during maintenance <Cite id="layne-robot-fatalities-2023" />.

<span id="why-a-learned-policy-cannot-be-certified" />
## Certification needs a specification beyond the policy weights

Integrity levels attach to safety functions with a verifiable specification and a process that prevents design faults <Cite id="iso-13849-1-2023" />. A network's weights state neither.

Testing cannot fill the gap: it is insufficient evidence to certify autonomous robots <Cite id="luckcuck-formal-2019" />, and proving autonomous-vehicle reliability would take fleets tens to hundreds of years of driving <Cite id="kalra-paddock-2016" /> ([deployment numbers](/frontier/reliability-gap/)). Proof works only in narrow settings, such as Reluplex on ACAS Xu collision-avoidance networks <Cite id="reluplex-2017" />.

<span id="the-pattern-a-verifiable-layer-wrapping-an-unverifiable-one" />
### A verifiable safety filter can wrap an unverifiable policy

Shipped systems put a simple, verifiable mechanism with override authority between policy and actuators <Cite id="iso-iec-tr-5469-2024" />. The formal version is the safety filter: a <Term id="control-barrier-function">control barrier function</Term> defines states the system must stay inside and turns staying there into a constraint on each command <Cite id="ames-cbf-2019" />. Filters change any proposed input as little as necessary <Cite id="wabersich-safety-filters-2023" />.

| Variant | How it intervenes |
| --- | --- |
| CBF quadratic program | Balances barrier constraints against performance <Cite id="cbf-qp-2016" /> |
| Predictive safety filter | Checks proposed inputs ahead <Cite id="predictive-safety-filter-2018" /> |
| Shielding | Applies a temporal-logic shield <Cite id="shielding-2017" /> |
| Neural Simplex | Switches to a baseline controller <Cite id="neural-simplex-2019" /> |
| Learned barriers | Learns them from safe expert trajectories <Cite id="learn-cbf-2020" /> |
| Constrained policy optimization | Adds constraints during training <Cite id="constrained-policy-2017" /> |

<SafetyFilterFence className="my-6" />

The pattern now wraps robot foundation models. VLPSA puts a barrier-function filter around π0.5 without retraining and raised SafeLIBERO collision avoidance from 23.1% to 91.2% <Cite id="vlpsa-2026" />. CarryOn screens a frozen VLA's actions and cut a safety-tuned VLA's violation rate from 52.6% to 6.1% <Cite id="carryon-harness-2026" />.

### Barrier functions now reach slender robots, and industrial limiters remain the norm

Weighted along a slender robot, barrier functions cut tracking error by up to 87.7% in physical tests <Cite id="wcbf-hyper-redundant-2026" />.

<Image id="wcbf-hyper-redundant-robot-2026" />

Industry deploys a cruder version: actuators limit force, drives limit velocity and safety controllers limit workspace. Guardrails inside a model are no substitute: RoboPAIR jailbroke language-model robots, a deployed Unitree Go2 among them, often with 100% success <Cite id="robopair-2024" />.

<span id="the-assurance-case-for-what-testing-cannot-reach" />
### A safety case argues for what testing cannot show

What the wrapper cannot cover goes into a <Term id="safety-case">safety case</Term>, an evidenced argument that a system is safe enough in a stated environment. UL 4600 applies it to autonomous products <Cite id="ul-4600-2023" />, written in Goal Structuring Notation <Cite id="gsn-standard-v3" />. AMLAS adds patterns for machine-learning components <Cite id="amlas-2021" />.

An argument built to conclude safety usually does. Koopman argues from [autonomous vehicles](/adjacent/autonomous-vehicles/) that "safe enough" means more than crashes per mile <Cite id="koopman-safe-enough-2026" />, and responsibility-sensitive safety offers a checkable formal rule set <Cite id="rss-2017" />.

<span id="the-machine-learning-half-of-the-wrapper" />
## Conformal prediction and monitors tell a learned system when to stop or ask

<Term id="conformal-prediction">Conformal prediction</Term> turns a model's output into a set of answers that contains the right one at a guaranteed rate, if new data resemble the calibration data <Cite id="vovk-conformal-2022" /> <Cite id="angelopoulos-conformal-2021" />. KnowNo uses it so a robot planner asks for help when unsure <Cite id="knowno-2023" />. The guarantees are aggregate, never per decision.

Also: risk control <Cite id="conformal-risk-2022" />, risk-controlling sets <Cite id="risk-prediction-sets-2021" />, coverage under drift <Cite id="adaptive-conformal-2021" />.

<Image id="knowno-ask-for-help-2023" />

<Term id="out-of-distribution-detection">Out-of-distribution detection</Term> asks whether an observation resembles the training data. A fast anomaly detector can trigger a fallback while slower reasoning catches up <Cite id="sinha-anomaly-2024" />, and predictor failures can be caught with guaranteed error rates <Cite id="farid-failure-2022" />.

Also: learned rejection <Cite id="selectivenet-2019" />, energy scores <Cite id="energy-ood-2020" />.

DeepMind's ASIMOV benchmarks semantic safety, such as judging whether a person is close enough to stop <Cite id="asimov-agentic-2026" />. Gemini Robotics ER 2 follows safety instructions 97.9% of the time <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er2-2026" />, and Gemini 4 Argon resists 99.5% of disguised unsafe requests <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />.

<span id="regulation-catches-up" />
## EU rules for learned safety components apply from January 2027

From 20 January 2027, the EU Machinery Regulation lists "safety components with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour" and calls for third-party assessment <Cite id="eu-machinery-regulation-2023" />. The AI Act's rules for high-risk AI in regulated products apply from 2 August 2028 <Cite id="eu-ai-act-framework-2026" />. The Digital Omnibus excludes AI used only for convenience or performance <Cite id="eu-ai-omnibus-2026" />.

<span id="what-would-actually-close-this" />
## Three missing pieces block certification of learned systems

Certification needs a language that states a manipulation task's safety requirement precisely enough to check <Cite id="luckcuck-formal-2019" />. It needs a humanoid standard past committee draft <Cite id="iso-cd-25785-1" />, and a published assurance case for a deployed learned system <Cite id="amlas-2021" />.

Also: evidence-based trust criteria <Cite id="trustworthy-physical-ai-2026" />.
