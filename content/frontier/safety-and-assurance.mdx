Safety assurance is the procedure for showing that a robot system is safe enough to run. Industrial robots have a settled, numerical standards stack, but none certifies a learned policy, so what ships is a verifiable safety layer around it <Cite id="iso-iec-tr-5469-2024" />.

## Fenceless robots use four collaborative modes, two of them quantitative

Marvel and Norcross summarise the four scenarios from ISO/TS 15066 (2016). Two are procedural: a safety-rated monitored stop permits no motion while a person is present, and hand guiding gives the operator the control channel. Two are quantitative: <Term id="speed-and-separation-monitoring">speed and separation monitoring</Term> keeps a computed distance between robot and person, while <Term id="power-and-force-limiting">power and force limiting</Term> gives up distance and bounds the contact <Cite id="marvel-norcross-2017" />.

ISO/TS 15066, published in 2016, supplemented the 2011 ISO 10218 for collaborative operation and is now marked for revision <Cite id="iso-ts-15066" />. The 2025 revision of ISO 10218 integrated it normatively and introduced new classifications for collaborative applications, Hartmann and colleagues report <Cite id="hartmann-iso-10218-2026" />.

## Separation criteria assume a person approaching at 1600 to 2000 mm/s

Marvel and Norcross reproduce the ISO 13855:2010 decision criteria: an intrusion margin of at least 850 mm for a normal approach protected by multiple separate beams, against 1200 mm for a single-height beam. A two-handed control device may reduce the margin to 250 mm, but they exclude typical two-handed teach pendants from that exception and doubt that stationary-machinery criteria transfer to robots <Cite id="marvel-norcross-2017" />.

For operator approach they call 1600 mm/s a worst-case assumption and report a 2000 mm/s stationary-machinery maximum, with the 1600 mm/s option only when separation is greater than 500 mm. Approach speed may be measured directly; otherwise it is typically assumed constant and directed toward the robot. They add that 2000 mm/s may be more prudent for rapid motions and detection uncertainty, and a risk assessment may call for a faster approach <Cite id="marvel-norcross-2017" />.

The instrument is an authored teaching model whose defaults are not certified limits: C = 0.85 m, 10 m/s² deceleration, a 0.1 s reaction delay, 0.1 m position uncertainty and a 1.6 m workcell. A 100 Hz update period is 0.01 s, which says nothing about detect-to-command latency. The NIST paper calls its early-draft equation a rough approximation and requires traceable stopping-time and braking-distance measurements for a real robot <Cite id="marvel-norcross-2017" />.

## Force limits rest on measured pain thresholds by body region

Haddadin, Albu-Schäffer and Hirzinger made the force question empirical with crash-test impacts <Cite id="haddadin-2009" />. Later pain-threshold studies, such as Han and colleagues' 37-subject impactor tests, report limits by body region and contact geometry <Cite id="han-force-pain-2024" />. A power-and-force-limited application therefore attaches a measured number to a named body part, the value the [contact-force lab in the control module](/classical/control/) also reads <Cite id="han-force-pain-2024" />.

<span id="the-stack-the-standards-actually-form" />
## Risk assessment comes first, and a compliant robot does not make a compliant cell

<Term id="risk-assessment">Risk assessment</Term> begins with ISO 12100, the general-principles standard for machinery safety, at edition 1 since 2010 and due to be replaced by a revision now in draft <Cite id="iso-12100" />. Its abstract describes the procedure: identify the hazards, estimate and evaluate the risks, then reduce them, documenting and verifying each step <Cite id="iso-12100" />. The reduction measures form a hierarchy: design the hazard out, guard against what remains, then inform the operator, the "3-step approach" OSHA's manual reproduces from the robot industry association <Cite id="osha-otm-robots" />.

The robot-specific layer splits liability in two. ISO 10218-1 covers the robot as partly completed machinery, the manufacturer's obligation; ISO 10218-2 covers the application and its cell, the integrator's <Cite id="iso-10218-1-2025" /> <Cite id="iso-10218-2-2025" />. Both were republished in February 2025 at editions 3 and 2, the first major revision since 2011. The US adoption, ANSI/A3 R15.06-2025, added a Part 3, approved on October 7, 2025, that gives guidance to users of industrial robot cells <Cite id="a3-r1506-2025-store" /> <Cite id="rr-ansi-r1506-2026" />.

## Safety controls carry rated integrity levels, and an emergency stop cannot detect a hazard

<Term id="functional-safety">Functional safety</Term> grades the control system that enforces these requirements. IEC 61508, whose Part 1 is at edition 2 from 2010, is the generic standard for electrical, electronic and programmable systems that carry out safety functions, rated by <Term id="safety-integrity-level">safety integrity level</Term> <Cite id="iec-61508-1-2010" />. ISO 13849-1, at edition 4 from 2023, is the machinery counterpart for safety-related control parts, including software, rated by <Term id="performance-level">performance level</Term>; it defers low-demand operation to IEC 61508 <Cite id="iso-13849-1-2023" />.

ISO 13850, at edition 3 from 2015, specifies the <Term id="emergency-stop">emergency stop</Term> independently of the energy source and names IEC 60204-1, at its 2016 edition, for the electrical realisation <Cite id="iso-13850-2015" /> <Cite id="iec-60204-1-2016" />. The button cannot detect a hazard; it waits for a person to see one and press. In stop category 0 the actuators lose power and the brakes engage, while in category 1 power is maintained through a controlled stop <Cite id="marvel-norcross-2017" />.

<Image id="emergency-stop-siemens-2006" />

For mobile robots, ISO 3691-4, at edition 2 from 2023, covers driverless industrial trucks, naming automated guided vehicles and autonomous mobile robots among its examples <Cite id="iso-3691-4-2023" />. In the United States the R15.08 series covers industrial mobile robots, with Part 1 published in 2020 and Part 2 in 2023 <Cite id="a3-robot-safety-standards" />.

<span id="the-humanoid-gap-and-the-standard-that-is-not-finished" />
### The humanoid standard is still a committee draft at stage 30.60

A humanoid falls, a hazard class the industrial documents were not written for. ISO/CD 25785-1 covers industrial mobile robots that need active control to remain balanced, including bipedal machines <Cite id="iso-cd-25785-1" />. It is a committee draft: the comment period closed on 8 July 2026, and in late October 2026 the catalogue still showed stage 30.60, with edition 1 unpublished and no date for the next stage <Cite id="iso-cd-25785-1" />. A parallel technical report for the United States and Canada, ANSI/A3 TR R15.108, is under development for dynamically stable industrial mobile robots including humanoids <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />.

Until those documents are agreed, a humanoid deployed beside people runs under a risk assessment that invents its own treatment of fall risk. Agility reports that Digit 5 avoids people, stops or sits down under an independent safety controller, and that Digit passed an independent field evaluation on a customer production line against safety standards administered by OSHA <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />. Brooks predicts that without new types of mechanical systems walking humanoids "will remain too unsafe to be in close proximity to real humans" <Cite id="brooks-scorecard-2026" />.

OSHA's technical manual chapter on industrial robots covers safeguarding devices, the teach-pendant hazard and lockout and tagout, with accident descriptions <Cite id="osha-otm-robots" />. NIOSH counted 41 robot-related workplace fatalities in the United States from 1992 to 2017. Most involved stationary robots (83%) striking the worker under their own power (78%), often during maintenance <Cite id="layne-robot-fatalities-2023" />.

<span id="why-a-learned-policy-cannot-be-certified" />
## A learned policy cannot be certified, because it has no written specification

A safety integrity level and a performance level are both assigned to a safety function <Cite id="iso-13849-1-2023" />. Both presume a verifiable specification of what the function must do, and a development process that avoids systematic faults, the design errors that redundant hardware cannot remove.

A policy trained from demonstrations has neither: its weights encode behaviour with no separable statement of intent, and no process avoids systematic faults in a function nobody wrote down. Luckcuck and colleagues find testing and simulation insufficient evidence for certifying autonomous robots <Cite id="luckcuck-formal-2019" />. Kalra and Paddock calculate that existing fleets would need tens and sometimes hundreds of years of driving to demonstrate autonomous-vehicle reliability <Cite id="kalra-paddock-2016" />, and [deployment numbers](/frontier/reliability-gap/) show the same distance.

<span id="the-pattern-a-verifiable-layer-wrapping-an-unverifiable-one" />
### A verifiable safety filter can wrap an unverifiable policy

What ships instead puts something simple enough to verify between the black-box policy and the actuators, with authority to override. ISO/IEC TR 5469 (2024) names this use: non-AI safety related functions that ensure safety for AI-controlled equipment <Cite id="iso-iec-tr-5469-2024" />.

The formal instance is the safety filter. A <Term id="control-barrier-function">control barrier function</Term> defines a set the system must stay inside and turns forward invariance of that set into a constraint on the commanded input, enforced each step <Cite id="ames-cbf-2019" />. Wabersich and colleagues survey the family, including reachability and predictive variants, and describe a filter that takes a proposed input from any source and modifies it as little as necessary <Cite id="wabersich-safety-filters-2023" />.

<SafetyFilterFence className="my-6" />

In 2026 the pattern reached vision-language-action policies. VLPSA wraps π0.5 in a full-body CBF-QP safety filter without retraining, and on SafeLIBERO it raised collision avoidance from 23.1% to 91.2% while surpassing the base policy's task success <Cite id="vlpsa-2026" />.

### Barrier functions now reach slender robots, and industrial limiters remain the norm

Barrier functions are also being adapted to bodies that rigid-arm controllers handle poorly. In September 2026, Zijian Cai, Daniela Rus, Cecilia Laschi and colleagues weighted the control barrier functions along a slender hyper-redundant robot to enforce obstacle constraints while correcting tracking errors from uneven loading. On a circular path-following task with obstacles, fixed weights cut the root-mean-square tracking error of the unweighted method by up to 59.6% in simulation and 87.7% in physical experiments <Cite id="wcbf-hyper-redundant-2026" />.

<Image id="wcbf-hyper-redundant-robot-2026" />

The industrial instance is cruder and far more widely deployed: force limiting in the actuator, velocity limiting in the drive, and workspace limiting in the safety controller. The standards above know how to rate those mechanisms, whatever generated the trajectory. Guardrails inside a model are no substitute: RoboPAIR jailbroke LLM-controlled robots, often with 100% attack success, including a deployed Unitree Go2 <Cite id="robopair-2024" />.

<span id="the-assurance-case-for-what-testing-cannot-reach" />
### A safety case argues for what testing cannot show

What the wrapper leaves over gets a <Term id="safety-case">safety case</Term>: a structured, documented argument that a system is acceptably safe in a stated environment, with evidence attached to each step. UL 4600, at edition 3 from March 2023, applies the approach to autonomous products <Cite id="ul-4600-2023" />. The Goal Structuring Notation community standard, version 3, supplies the usual notation, decomposing a top-level goal into subgoals that each rest on cited evidence <Cite id="gsn-standard-v3" />. For ML components, AMLAS supplies safety case patterns and a process for generating the evidence base <Cite id="amlas-2021" />.

An argument built to conclude that a system is safe will usually reach that conclusion, and Koopman argues that "safe enough" means more than crashes divided by miles <Cite id="koopman-safe-enough-2026" />. The alternative is a rule set formal enough to check, which Shalev-Shwartz, Shammah and Shashua proposed as responsibility-sensitive safety <Cite id="rss-2017" />. Robotics is inheriting that decade-long [autonomous vehicles](/adjacent/autonomous-vehicles/) argument late.

<span id="the-machine-learning-half-of-the-wrapper" />
## Conformal prediction and monitors tell a learned system when to stop or ask

<Term id="conformal-prediction">Conformal prediction</Term> turns any model's raw output into a prediction set with a guaranteed coverage rate under exchangeability. Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer built the framework <Cite id="vovk-conformal-2022" />, and Angelopoulos and Bates's tutorial carried it into general machine-learning practice <Cite id="angelopoulos-conformal-2021" />. In KnowNo, Ren and colleagues calibrate a language-model planner conformally so that the robot asks a human for help when its prediction set is ambiguous, with a statistical guarantee on task completion <Cite id="knowno-2023" />.

<Image id="knowno-ask-for-help-2023" />

<Term id="out-of-distribution-detection">Out-of-distribution detection</Term> asks whether the observation resembles anything the policy was trained on. Sinha and colleagues run a fast anomaly detector on the live observation stream to trigger a reactive fallback while slower reasoning catches up <Cite id="sinha-anomaly-2024" />. Runtime monitoring generalises both: Farid and colleagues detect trajectory-predictor failures by their effect on the planner, with guarantees on false-positive and false-negative rates <Cite id="farid-failure-2022" />.

The ASIMOV benchmark tests semantic safety in robotics agents, from payload and reach limits to judging from stereo images whether a person is close enough to require a stop <Cite id="asimov-agentic-2026" />. Gemini Robotics ER 2, in public preview, reports 97.9% safety-instruction-following accuracy and 93.0% on 1 m human proximity <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er2-2026" />.

<span id="regulation-catches-up" />
## EU rules for learned safety components apply from January 2027

From 20 January 2027, the EU Machinery Regulation lists "safety components with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour using machine learning approaches ensuring safety functions" in Annex I Part A. Its recitals call for third-party conformity assessment of such components <Cite id="eu-machinery-regulation-2023" />. The AI Act's rules for high-risk AI systems embedded in the regulated products of its Annex I apply from 2 August 2028, after the AI Omnibus extended the transition <Cite id="eu-ai-act-framework-2026" />.

<span id="what-would-actually-close-this" />
## Three missing pieces block certification of learned systems

The first missing piece is a specification language in which a manipulation task's safety requirement can be written precisely enough to check <Cite id="luckcuck-formal-2019" />. The second is a humanoid standard past committee draft, giving fall risk an agreed treatment <Cite id="iso-cd-25785-1" />. The third is a published assurance case for a deployed learned system, complete enough that an outsider could dispute a specific step <Cite id="amlas-2021" />.

A position paper from October 2026, by researchers at Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna, MIT CSAIL and four other universities, asks for the same kind of evidence on the human side of the interaction. Niccolò Pagliarani, Maximilian Stölzle, Daniela Rus, Matteo Cianchetti and colleagues argue that a claim that a robot is trustworthy around people should rest on evidence for three properties in the intended setting: bounded physical risk, behavior that people can anticipate, and expectations that match what the robot has shown it can and cannot do. In their view these should guide design and evaluation from the outset, and the paper offers candidate evaluation criteria as a research agenda, with no new measurements <Cite id="trustworthy-physical-ai-2026" />.
