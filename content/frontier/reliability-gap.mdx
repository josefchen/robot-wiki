The reliability gap is the distance between how often a robot succeeds in a lab or demo and how often it must succeed to be useful. Bessemer Venture Partners (2026) calls 80% <Term id="success-rate">success</Term> a demo and 99.9% a product, and closing the gap "not a linear problem" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

<span id="the-arithmetic-of-compounding" />
## At 99.9% per step, a 30-step task still fails about once in 34 runs

A task is a chain of steps that succeeds only if every link holds. If each of $N$ steps succeeds independently with probability $p$, the task succeeds with probability $p^N$ <Cite id="maker-2025" />: at 99% per step, a 30-step task finishes about three runs in four. A success rate is therefore meaningless without the task's length, and papers often omit runs, starting conditions and success criteria <Cite id="kress-gazit-evaluation-2024" />.

## Each mistake makes the next one likelier

Real errors are worse, because a slip puts the robot somewhere its training data never showed, where the next slip is likelier: <Term id="compounding-error">compounding error</Term>. An imitator with per-step error $\varepsilon$ can make up to $T^2\varepsilon$ mistakes over $T$ steps <Cite id="dagger-2011" />, and smooth deterministic imitators of continuous actions can suffer error "exponentially larger, as a function of problem horizon" <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" />.

In NVIDIA's GR00T 2 report (November 2026), an arm taught a toy dump-truck assembly from about 20 handheld demonstrations finished 1 of 10 trials, yet its average progress score was 81 out of 100 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />: most steps went right, most runs failed. Holding 99% end-to-end over 50 steps needs about 99.98% per step, which no generalist policy publishes.

<span id="why-the-last-stretch-is-different" />
## The last stretch to 99.9% needs other tools

Formal proof scales badly: Hamilton-Jacobi reachability, a standard method, faces "exponential computational complexity with respect to the number of state variables" <Cite id="hj-reachability-2017" />, and no deployed <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> publishes formal safety guarantees. Assurance rests on testing and monitoring ([evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)). Bessemer argues that the last 20% of the climb from 80% requires "fundamentally different approaches", such as tactile sensing and force feedback, and Lisa Yan of Argus Systems calls closing the gap between 99% and 99.9% "a steep hill climb that takes longer than most people realize" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

<span id="what-is-closing-the-gap" />
## Practice on the job lifts single tasks above 90%

The most direct fix is <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term> on the robot's own attempts. Physical Intelligence reports that π*0.6 with Recap at least halves failure rates on hard tasks <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />, with every task except diverse laundry in the 90%+ range <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. HIL-SERL (2024) reached near-perfect success within 1 to 2.5 hours of training <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />, RL-100 succeeded in 1,000 of 1,000 episodes across 8 tasks <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />, and Real-Time EXPO-FT lifted four dynamic tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />. In an October 2026 Tsinghua and AgiBot preprint, 96 robots pooling their executions raised phone packaging from 79.2% to 98.3% over 120 trials, though operators labeled outcomes and reset scenes <Cite id="collective-experience-learning-2026" />.

Most are single-task specialists, though RL-100 keeps about 90% success when the environment and dynamics shift <Cite id="rl-100-2025" /> and RAPolicy raised five jointly trained tasks from 52% to 88% <Cite id="rapolicy-2026" />. Generalist AI reports that GEN-1 averages 99% on fine-tuned tasks where GEN-0 reached 64% <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

<span id="runtime-monitors-make-failures-visible-and-human-help-rescues-episodes" />
## Monitors flag failures, and help fixes them

A runtime monitor flags a failure as it starts. FAIL-Detect learns this "from successful training data alone" <Cite id="fail-detect-2025" />, and Sentinel's two detectors together catch 18% more failures than either alone <Cite id="sentinel-2024" />. Google DeepMind's ASIMOV-Agentic benchmark checks payload, reach and safety limits <Cite id="asimov-agentic-2026" />, and DeepMind calls Gemini Robotics ER 2 its safest robotics model on such benchmarks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Fixing the failure is a separate job: a language model can explain it and replan (REFLECT) <Cite id="reflect-robot-2023" />, a vision-language model can check plan constraints during execution (DoReMi) <Cite id="doremi-robot-2023" />, a second policy can steer back to safety (Recovery RL) <Cite id="recovery-rl-2020" />, or the arm can retrace and retry, which cut failures on simulated LIBERO-Long tasks by 19 to 48% (Backtrack) <Cite id="backtrack-vla-2026" />.

The best long-task results lean on help. From cluttered starts, GR00T 2 assembled three toys in 97 or more of 100 trials each only with corrective data and a repairing agent, against 71 to 84 without it, and in trash sorting the robot waited for its agent a median 38% of each episode <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />. People are the last backstop: π0.7 takes step-by-step language coaching <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />, HG-DAgger lets an expert take over <Cite id="hg-dagger-2019" />, and 1X offers NEO owners a remote expert for unfamiliar chores <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />.

<span id="what-is-actually-deployed" />
## Deployment records count hours and parts

Companies publish hours and parts, rarely failures. Agility's November 2026 Nasdaq listing cites more than 65,000 hours of Digit operation <Cite id="agility-nasdaq-listing-2026" /> ([industrial deployment](/data-hardware/industrial-deployment/)) and about 98% on-task accuracy across 100,000 totes at one GXO site <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />. Figure says its BMW Spartanburg deployment logged 1,250+ runtime hours and loaded 90,000+ parts in eleven months, with its 84-second cycle and over 99% placement accuracy stated as targets <Cite id="figure-bmw-production-2025" />. BMW told Fortune in 2025 and Forbes in October 2026 that one Figure robot worked at a time <Cite id="fortune-figure-bmw-2025" /> <Cite id="forbes-adcock-figure-2026" />. Tesla says Optimus production began on Fremont's high-volume lines, with no rate disclosed <Cite id="tesla-q3-2026-update" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## Showing 99.9% takes about 3,000 straight successes

With zero failures in $n$ trials, the 95% upper bound on the failure rate is still about ${3/n}$ <Cite id="hanley-zero-numerators-1983" />, so a 99.9% claim needs about 3,000 successes in a row. Real-robot comparisons often stop at 10 or 50 trials <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />, and none of the vendor records above discloses a <Term id="mean-time-between-failures">mean time between failures</Term> or an intervention count. Toyota Research Institute's blind, randomized trials show the discipline needed <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />, and Waymo's crash-rate comparison rested on 56.7 million rider-only miles <Cite id="waymo-crash-rates-2025" /> ([autonomous vehicles](/adjacent/autonomous-vehicles/)). [Safety and assurance](/frontier/safety-and-assurance/) covers the standards a learned policy cannot be rated under.
