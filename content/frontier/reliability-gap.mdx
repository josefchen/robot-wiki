The reliability gap is the distance between how often a robot succeeds in a demo and how often it must succeed to be useful. Bessemer Venture Partners calls 80% <Term id="success-rate">success</Term> a demo and 99.9% a product <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

<span id="the-arithmetic-of-compounding" />
## At 99.9% per step, a 30-step task still fails about once in 34 runs

A task succeeds only if every step does. With $N$ independent steps at probability $p$, it succeeds with probability $p^N$ <Cite id="maker-2025" />, so at 99% per step a 30-step task finishes three runs in four. A success rate means nothing without the task's length, and papers often omit runs, starting conditions and success criteria <Cite id="kress-gazit-evaluation-2024" />.

## Each mistake makes the next one likelier

A slip puts the robot where its training data never went, so the next slip is likelier: <Term id="compounding-error">compounding error</Term>. An imitator with per-step error $\varepsilon$ can make up to $T^2\varepsilon$ mistakes over $T$ steps <Cite id="dagger-2011" />, and smooth imitators of continuous actions can suffer error "exponentially larger" with horizon <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" />.

In GR00T 2's report, an arm taught a toy truck assembly from about 20 demonstrations finished 1 of 10 trials while scoring 81 of 100 on progress <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />. Holding 99% end-to-end over 50 steps needs about 99.98% per step, which no generalist policy publishes.

<span id="why-the-last-stretch-is-different" />
## The last stretch to 99.9% needs other tools

Formal proof scales badly: Hamilton-Jacobi reachability faces "exponential computational complexity with respect to the number of state variables" <Cite id="hj-reachability-2017" />. No deployed <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> publishes formal guarantees, so assurance rests on testing and monitoring ([evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)).

<span id="what-is-closing-the-gap" />
## Practice on the job lifts single tasks above 90%

<Term id="reinforcement-learning">Reinforcement learning</Term> on the robot's own attempts is the most direct fix, mostly one task at a time:

| System | Result |
| --- | --- |
| π*0.6 with Recap (2025) | At least halves failures on hard tasks <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />; 90%+ on all but diverse laundry <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> |
| HIL-SERL (2024) | Near-perfect after 1 to 2.5 hours of training <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" /> |
| RL-100 (2025) | 1,000 of 1,000 episodes across 8 tasks <Cite id="rl-100-2025" /> |
| Real-Time EXPO-FT (2026) | Four dynamic tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" /> |
| 96 pooled robots (2026) | Phone packaging from 79.2% to 98.3%, operators resetting scenes <Cite id="collective-experience-learning-2026" /> |
| RAPolicy (2026) | Five jointly trained tasks from 52% to 88% <Cite id="rapolicy-2026" /> |
| GEN-1 (2026) | 99% on fine-tuned tasks, against 64% for GEN-0 <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" /> |

<span id="runtime-monitors-make-failures-visible-and-human-help-rescues-episodes" />
## Monitors flag failures, and help fixes them

A runtime monitor flags a failure as it starts. FAIL-Detect learns this "from successful training data alone" <Cite id="fail-detect-2025" />, and Sentinel's two detectors together catch 18% more failures than either alone <Cite id="sentinel-2024" />. DeepMind's ASIMOV-Agentic checks payload, reach and safety limits <Cite id="asimov-agentic-2026" />, and Gemini Robotics ER 2 is its safest model on such tests <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

| Fix | Method |
| --- | --- |
| A language model explains the failure and replans | REFLECT <Cite id="reflect-robot-2023" /> |
| A vision-language model checks plan constraints | DoReMi <Cite id="doremi-robot-2023" /> |
| A second policy steers back to safety | Recovery RL <Cite id="recovery-rl-2020" /> |
| The arm retraces and retries, 19 to 48% fewer failures | Backtrack <Cite id="backtrack-vla-2026" /> |

The best long-task results lean on help. GR00T 2 assembled three toys in 97 or more of 100 trials each only with corrective data and a repairing agent, against 71 to 84 without <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Also: language coaching <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />, expert takeover <Cite id="hg-dagger-2019" />, remote experts for NEO owners <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## Showing 99.9% takes about 3,000 straight successes

With zero failures in $n$ trials, the 95% upper bound on the failure rate is still about ${3/n}$ <Cite id="hanley-zero-numerators-1983" />, so a 99.9% claim needs about 3,000 successes in a row. Real-robot comparisons often stop at 10 or 50 trials <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />, and no vendor above discloses a <Term id="mean-time-between-failures">mean time between failures</Term>.

Toyota Research Institute's blind, randomized trials show the discipline needed <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />, and Waymo's crash-rate comparison rested on 56.7 million rider-only miles <Cite id="waymo-crash-rates-2025" /> ([autonomous vehicles](/adjacent/autonomous-vehicles/)). [Safety and assurance](/frontier/safety-and-assurance/) covers the standards a learned policy cannot be rated under.
