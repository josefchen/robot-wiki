The reliability gap is the difference between the <Term id="success-rate">success rates</Term> a robot shows in controlled settings and the rates deployment requires. A robot that finishes a task 80% of the time is a demo, one that finishes it 99.9% of the time is a product, and Bessemer calls closing the gap "not a linear problem" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

<span id="the-arithmetic-of-compounding" />
## At 99.9% per step, a 30-step task still fails about once in 34 runs

A task succeeds only when every step does: if each of $N$ steps succeeds independently with probability $p$, the task completes with probability $p^N$ <Cite id="maker-2025" />. A result without its horizon cannot be compared, and papers often omit runs, initial conditions and success criteria <Cite id="kress-gazit-evaluation-2024" />. Try the presets, then drag the per-step slider under "Adjust more" toward 100.

Real errors are not independent: a mistake carries the robot outside its training distribution, where the next is likelier, so <Term id="compounding-error">compounding error</Term> makes the true curve worse. An imitator with per-step error $\varepsilon$ can make up to $T^2\varepsilon$ mistakes over $T$ steps <Cite id="dagger-2011" />, and smooth deterministic imitators of continuous actions suffer error "exponentially larger, as a function of problem horizon" <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" />. Holding 99% end-to-end on a 50-step task needs about 99.98% per step, which no generalist policy publishes.

<span id="why-the-last-stretch-is-different" />
## Formal methods do not close the last stretch from 99% to 99.9%

Bessemer argues the last stretch needs "fundamentally different approaches", naming tactile sensing, force feedback and better sim-to-real transfer. Lisa Yan of Argus Systems calls closing the gap between 99% and 99.9% "a steep hill climb that takes longer than most people realize" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

Hamilton-Jacobi reachability faces "exponential computational complexity with respect to the number of state variables" <Cite id="hj-reachability-2017" />, and no deployed <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> publishes formal safety guarantees. Assurance therefore rests on empirical evaluation and runtime monitoring, which makes the reliability gap and [the evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/) one problem.

<span id="what-is-closing-the-gap" />
## RL from experience lifts single tasks into the 90% range and above

Physical Intelligence reports that π*0.6 with Recap, learning from the robot's own experience, cuts failure rates by 2x or more on hard tasks, with more than 2x throughput on some of the hardest tasks <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />. Its paper puts every task except diverse laundry in the 90%+ range <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. RL-100 succeeded in 1,000 of 1,000 episodes across 8 tasks, including 250 consecutive successes on one task, and a juicing robot served mall customers for about seven hours without failure <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />. Real-Time EXPO-FT lifts four dynamic tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />.

These are mostly per-task specialists, though RL-100 keeps about 90% zero-shot success under environmental and dynamics shifts <Cite id="rl-100-2025" /> and RAPolicy raised five jointly trained tasks from 52% to 88% <Cite id="rapolicy-2026" />. Generalist reports a pretraining route: GEN-1 averages 99% on fine-tuned tasks where GEN-0 reached 64% <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

## Runtime monitors make failures visible, and human help rescues episodes

Google's ASIMOV-Agentic benchmark tests whether an embodied-reasoning agent respects payload, reach and safety limits and stops near people <Cite id="asimov-agentic-2026" />. DeepMind calls Gemini Robotics ER 2 its safest robotics model on such benchmarks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. FAIL-Detect learns to flag failures "from successful training data alone" <Cite id="fail-detect-2025" />, and Sentinel's two detectors together catch 18% more failures than either alone <Cite id="sentinel-2024" />. A monitor catches failures instead of preventing them, so it raises effective reliability without changing the policy's own success rate.

π0.7 accepts step-by-step language coaching that walks it through tasks it has not seen <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. HG-DAgger lets a human expert take control to correct compounding error <Cite id="hg-dagger-2019" />, and 1X says NEO owners can schedule a remote expert for chores it does not know <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />.

<span id="what-is-actually-deployed" />
## Dated company records report hours and a single site's accuracy

Agility said in September 2026 that Digit 4 has logged more than 65,000 hours of operational time ([industrial deployment](/data-hardware/industrial-deployment/)), with about 98% on-task accuracy across 100,000 totes at GXO's Flowery Branch site <Cite id="agility-digit-5-2026" />. Figure's November 2025 report says its BMW Spartanburg deployment logged 1,250+ runtime hours and loaded 90,000+ parts in eleven months, while its 84-second cycle and >99% placement accuracy were targets it did not report as achieved <Cite id="figure-bmw-production-2025" />. Tesla's Q1 and Q2 2026 updates list both Optimus lines as under construction, with first builds going to an "Optimus Academy for training data collection" <Cite id="tesla-q1-2026-update" /> <Cite id="tesla-q2-2026-update" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## Showing 99.9% takes about 3,000 straight successes

A testable yardstick would document mean time between failures on real tasks, the human-robot safety assurance method and a protocol for remaining failures. None of the vendor hours above discloses a mean time between failures, an intervention count or general-purpose deployment at scale. With zero failures in $n$ trials, the 95% upper bound on the failure rate is about ${3/n}$ <Cite id="hanley-zero-numerators-1983" />, so showing 99.9% takes about 3,000 straight successes.

Real-robot comparisons are often limited to 10 or 50 trials <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />, and TRI's blind, randomized trials show the needed discipline <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Waymo's crash-rate claim rested on 56.7 million rider-only miles <Cite id="waymo-crash-rates-2025" /> ([autonomous vehicles](/adjacent/autonomous-vehicles/)). [Safety and assurance](/frontier/safety-and-assurance/) covers the standards a learned policy cannot be rated under.
