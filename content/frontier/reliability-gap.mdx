The reliability gap is the difference between the level of reliability needed to showcase a demo and to offer a useful product: Bessemer Venture Partners pegs the first at 80% <Term id="success-rate">success</Term> and the second at 99.9% <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

<span id="the-arithmetic-of-compounding" />
## At 99.9% per step, a 30-step task still fails about once in 34 runs

Success of a task requires success in each step: if each step is independently successful with probability $p$, the probability of successfully completing a task with $N$ steps is $p^N$ <Cite id="maker-2025" />. Without the task's length, a success rate means nothing, and papers often do not specify the number of trials, initial conditions and success criteria <Cite id="kress-gazit-evaluation-2024" />.

## Each mistake makes the next one likelier

When the robot slips, it is in a state that was not covered by its training data, so it is more likely to slip again, leading to <Term id="compounding-error">compounding error</Term>. An imitator can make as many as $T^2\varepsilon$ errors over $T$ steps, where $\varepsilon$ is its error in each step <Cite id="dagger-2011" />, and for smooth imitation policies of continuous actions, error can grow "exponentially larger" as the horizon increases <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" />.

GR00T 2's report says that a robot arm trained on about 20 demonstrations to assemble a toy truck fully completed the task in 1 out of 10 trials, despite receiving a progress score of 81 out of 100 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

<span id="why-the-last-stretch-is-different" />
## The last stretch to 99.9% needs other tools

Formal proof is computationally challenging, as current methods scale poorly: Hamilton-Jacobi reachability has "exponential computational complexity with respect to the number of state variables" <Cite id="hj-reachability-2017" />. Since no deployed <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> publishes formal guarantees, assurance rests on monitoring and testing ([evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)).

<span id="what-is-closing-the-gap" />
## Practice on the job lifts single tasks above 90%

<Term id="reinforcement-learning">Reinforcement learning</Term> on the robot's attempts fixes tasks mostly one at a time:

| System | Result |
| --- | --- |
| π*0.6 with Recap (2025) | At least halves failures on hard tasks <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />; 90%+ on all but diverse laundry <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> |
| HIL-SERL (2024) | Near-perfect after 1 to 2.5 hours of training <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" /> |
| RL-100 (2025) | 1,000 of 1,000 episodes across 8 tasks <Cite id="rl-100-2025" /> |
| Real-Time EXPO-FT (2026) | Four dynamic tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" /> |
| 96 pooled robots (2026) | Phone packaging from 79.2% to 98.3%, operators resetting scenes <Cite id="collective-experience-learning-2026" /> |
| RAPolicy (2026) | Five jointly trained tasks from 52% to 88% <Cite id="rapolicy-2026" /> |
| GEN-1 (2026) | 99% on fine-tuned tasks, against 64% for GEN-0 <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" /> |

On SenseNova's eight real robots, with no robot-specific training, GPT-6 Astra had 17 out of 18 successes on pick-and-place tasks involving rigid objects, 2 out of 4 on cloth folding with one arm, 9 out of 23 on navigation-and-search tasks, and 0 out of 11 on plug insertions; hardware faults ended some trials <Cite id="sensenova-astra-2026" />.

<span id="runtime-monitors-make-failures-visible-and-human-help-rescues-episodes" />
## Monitors flag failures, and help fixes them

A runtime monitor flags failures as they start. FAIL-Detect learns to predict when a behavior is likely to fail "from successful training data alone" <Cite id="fail-detect-2025" />, and Sentinel's two detectors complement each other to detect 18% more failures than either detector <Cite id="sentinel-2024" />. ASIMOV-Agentic, proposed by DeepMind, verifies if the actions satisfy requirements around payload, reach and safety <Cite id="asimov-agentic-2026" />, and Gemini Robotics ER 2 is DeepMind's safest model in such tests <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

| Fix | Method |
| --- | --- |
| A language model explains the failure and replans | REFLECT <Cite id="reflect-robot-2023" /> |
| A vision-language model checks plan constraints | DoReMi <Cite id="doremi-robot-2023" /> |
| A second policy steers back to safety | Recovery RL <Cite id="recovery-rl-2020" /> |
| The arm retraces and retries, 19 to 48% fewer failures | Backtrack <Cite id="backtrack-vla-2026" /> |

Using corrective data and a repairing agent, GR00T 2 assembled three toys in at least 97 out of 100 trials each, as opposed to between 71 and 84 without them <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Also: language coaching <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />, expert takeover <Cite id="hg-dagger-2019" />, remote experts for NEO owners <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## Showing 99.9% takes about 3,000 straight successes

Even with zero failures observed in $n$ trials, the 95% upper confidence bound for the true failure rate is still roughly ${3/n}$ <Cite id="hanley-zero-numerators-1983" />, so substantiating a claim of 99.9% would require observing about 3,000 successful trials in a row. Real-robot comparisons often stop at 10 or 50 trials <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />, and none of the above vendors report a <Term id="mean-time-between-failures">mean time between failures</Term>.

Toyota Research Institute's blind, randomized trials demonstrate the level of rigor required <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Waymo's comparison of crash rates was based on 56.7 million miles driven in rider-only mode <Cite id="waymo-crash-rates-2025" /> ([autonomous vehicles](/adjacent/autonomous-vehicles/)). [Safety and assurance](/frontier/safety-and-assurance/) covers the standards that learned policies cannot be assessed against.
