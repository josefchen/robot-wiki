Generalization is a robot policy's ability to work in settings it was not trained in: new rooms, objects and clutter, a <Term id="distribution-shift">distribution shift</Term> it must handle without data collected at each site <Cite id="droid-2024" />. It decides whether one trained model can serve every home and factory or must be retrained for each.

<Image id="droid-cook-lentils-rollouts-2024" />

The difficulty is that robot data comes from few places. DROID's authors wrote in 2024 that even the most general policies are "mostly trained on data collected in a small number of environments" <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and in a new home a one-centimeter grasp error drops the object.

<span id="what-pi05-demonstrated" />
## π0.5 cleaned three unseen homes, trained mostly on other data

Physical Intelligence's π0.5 (April 2025), one end-to-end <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term>, cleaned kitchens and bedrooms in homes absent from its training data <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. Only about 400 hours of its mixture is mobile-manipulator data from real homes; 97.6% of the examples in the first training phase come from elsewhere, including other robots, laboratory tasks and web data. Removing the other robots' data degrades performance sharply, while removing web data mainly hurts unseen objects. Breadth stood in for local data: a model trained on the test homes themselves scored about the same as the 104-location model that never saw them <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

## Knowing what is easier than knowing how

Pretraining on web images and text supplies semantic generalization, knowing what "the spatula" is in a new kitchen, as RT-2 showed for novel objects <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Motor generalization, grasping that spatula with this gripper, needs more, and the semantic half survives only if action training preserves it. Physical Intelligence found that naively adding an action expert "significantly harms both training speed and knowledge transfer" <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />; others report that action fine-tuning "can erode the original VLM's generalist reasoning abilities" <Cite id="int-act-2025" /> and that open VLAs "often struggle with semantic generalization" <Cite id="star-gen-2025" />.

Earlier designs kept recognition and spatial control in separate parts <Cite id="clipport-2021" /> <Cite id="roboflamingo-2023" /> <Cite id="vima-2022" />, learned visual features for robots <Cite id="voltron-2023" /> <Cite id="liv-2023" />, or gave the policy a 3D picture of the scene, trading precision against computation <Cite id="peract-2022" /> <Cite id="rvt-2023" /> <Cite id="act3d-2023" /> <Cite id="gnfactor-2023" />. None won outright; in CortexBench no visual representation won every task <Cite id="vc1-2023" />.

<span id="what-pi07-adds" />
## π0.7 recombines trained skills and transfers them to new robots

π0.7 (April 2026, [The Pi Line](/manipulation/pi-line/)) adds compositional generalization: recombining trained skills into tasks it was never taught. Asked to load a sweet potato into an air fryer without robot demonstrations, the model "makes a reasonable attempt, performing part of the task after a few false starts, but not finishing it fully" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. With step-by-step language coaching the robot completes it, and the coaching transcripts train a high-level policy that runs the task with no additional <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Generalist reports a competing route: GEN-1.5 learns a new task in context from 3 to 12 seconds of one demonstration, with no training <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />.

Open X-Embodiment pooled episodes from 22 kinds of robot to test <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> transfer, learning on one robot and working on another <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. Physical Intelligence reports that π0.7 folds laundry on a two-armed UR5e that never had laundry data at 80% success, against 80.6% for expert teleoperators on their first try with that robot <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Google DeepMind reports Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 adapts to new two-armed robots in a few hours, "typically with less than 200 examples" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<span id="what-these-results-do-not-demonstrate" />
## None of these results shows reliable, unaided success in unseen homes

The new tasks still need help: the air fryer attempt does not finish without coaching, and the authors claim only "early signs of compositional task generalization" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Reliability is unmeasured. π0.5's headline evaluation is three homes and ten trials per task <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> ([The Reliability Gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), [The Evaluation Crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)). No published result shows a generalist policy sustaining better than 95% <Term id="success-rate">success</Term> across a broad range of tasks in unseen environments, and Generalist's reported 99% average for GEN-1 covers only tasks it was fine-tuned on <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Benchmark scores hide brittleness: modest perturbations cut LIBERO-Plus success "from 95% to below 30%" <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" /> and Colosseum's by 30 to 50% <Cite id="colosseum-2024" />, and on LIBERO-PRO over-90% performance "collapses to 0.0%" <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" />. Harder simulated suites are improving: in the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge, 100 long household tasks across seven scenes, the organizers report full-task success rising from 12% to 48% in a year <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2026" /> <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2026-results" />.

Physics transfers worse than meaning: evaluations lean on recognition, while new objects bring new contact ([Dexterity](/frontier/dexterity/)). DreamZero's authors write that VLAs "struggle to generalize to unseen physical motions in novel environments", and report over 2x better generalization from a video world action model <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. NVIDIA's November 2026 GR00T 2 succeeded zero-shot in 40% of trials on 50 tasks absent from a two-handed robot's own data, and five demonstrations per task raised that to 95%; the 50 tasks were chosen among those the model learns from five, so that rate "is high partly by construction" <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Transfer works best when source and target share structure. In π0.7's ablations, performance falls as the gap between robot bodies widens, until the latest model recovers it <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Across body shapes, the closest evidence is EgoScale's transfer from a hand with 22 <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term> to simpler hands <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

<span id="the-scaling-evidence-and-what-it-measures" />
## EgoScale's law holds from 1k to 20k hours of human video

NVIDIA's EgoScale trained a VLA on 20,854 hours of action-labeled <Term id="egocentric-video">egocentric human video</Term>, more than twenty times prior efforts. Its validation loss, the error on held-out data, follows $L = 0.024 - 0.003 \ln D$ with $D$ in thousands of hours, so each doubling of data lowers the loss by the same step. The fit spans five scales from 1k to 20k hours at $R^2 = 0.9983$. After post-training, average task completion rises from 0.30 at 1k hours to 0.71 at 20k, and the recipe lifts success by 54% over no pretraining on the 22-degree-of-freedom hand <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

NVIDIA's GR00T models train on such data: N1 mixed real-robot trajectories, human videos and synthetic data <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, and N1.7 adds 20K hours of EgoScale video <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> ([Cross-Embodiment Transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/)). In GR00T 2's ablation, with robot data held fixed, adding all of its human video raised real-robot success on new tasks from 18% to 41%, with no leveling off at the largest scale tested <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Extended past 20k hours, the completion fit crosses an impossible 100% near 250k hours, and the authors "do not extrapolate beyond the measured range" <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. And validation loss is a proxy that correlates with real-robot performance without being a success rate in unseen environments. Lin et al. come closest to a law for that metric: single-task performance in unseen environments "follows a roughly power-law relationship with the number of environments and objects" <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />.

## No published law yet predicts real-world success in unseen settings

Generalist fits a power law to GEN-0's 270,000 hours of real-world manipulation data, with "next-action prediction error" as its target <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />, and Figure's human-to-humanoid transfer law is fit to robot-action prediction <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />. TRI's blind, randomized trials found performance "predictably increases as pretraining scale and diversity grows", without fitting a law <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

The scaling camp cites EgoScale's "no signs of saturation in the explored regime" <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />, AgiBot World's "predictable performance scaling with increased data volume" <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" /> and π0.7's compositional results <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Bessemer, which sees scaling laws emerging, says of robotics' ChatGPT moment: "We don't think that moment is years away. But it's not here yet." <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />. The data keeps growing. Generalist reports over half a million hours for GEN-1 <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 (November 10, 2026) adds practice in simulated digital twins of real scenes, and reports 30 to 100% higher success across 200 tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />. Xiaomi trained on over 100k hours from UMI handheld grippers <Cite id="umi-2024" />, with stronger pretraining giving better out-of-the-box results in unseen environments <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />.

Ken Goldberg frames the shortfall as a 100,000-year data gap; he expects end-to-end learning to win eventually, but argues that pairing good old-fashioned engineering with learning gets robots deployed and collecting data sooner <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />. Rodney Brooks argues the missing ingredients are touch and engineered priors <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Karcini and co-authors argue that policy scaling lacks mechanisms that turn unstructured behavior into grounded robot supervision <Cite id="karcini-position-2026" />. The settling test is whether a 10x or 100x data increase, with no architectural change, yields proportional real-world gains in unseen environments; Shi et al. find task diversity "more critical than per-task demonstration quantity" <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## No system yet meets the solved bar of 90% across dozens of unseen homes

Generalization would be solved when a single policy succeeds more than 90% of the time across dozens of unseen homes, with no data collection or fine-tuning at each site, in a published, reproducible evaluation spanning many tasks and objects. No system has been evaluated this way. Physical Intelligence's October 2026 API beta still has customers upload their own data and fine-tune <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />. Figure reports Helix 2.5 reached 56% zero-shot success, against 9% without Index pretraining, on three whole-body tasks in 30 unseen Bay Area homes, graded blind with no published trial counts <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

On EgoScale's completion measure the best point is 0.71 against a 0.90 bar, on a friendlier metric, after post-training, on tasks the evaluators chose <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. The straight-line fit reaches 0.90 only near 111k hours of human video, five times past the measured range.
