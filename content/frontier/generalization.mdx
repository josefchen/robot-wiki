Robot policies that generalize perform well in new environments (unseen rooms, clutter, and objects) despite the <Term id="distribution-shift">distribution shift</Term>, without additional data from these new domains <Cite id="droid-2024" />. Generalization determines whether a learned policy can be deployed in every home or has to be retrained for each new one.

<Image id="droid-cook-lentils-rollouts-2024" />

Robot data comes from few places. According to DROID's authors, even the most general policies are "mostly trained on data collected in a small number of environments" <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

<span id="what-pi05-demonstrated" />
## π0.5 cleaned three unseen homes, trained mostly on other data

Physical Intelligence's <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> π0.5 was able to clean kitchens and bedrooms in homes it had never seen before. Only about 400 hours of its data came from mobile robots in real homes. A model trained on the test homes themselves scored about the same as the 104-location model that never saw them <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

## Knowing what is easier than knowing how

RT-2 showed that models pretrained on the web learn concepts that transfer to new situations ("the spatula" in a novel kitchen) <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. However, grasping "the spatula" with a specific robot gripper requires additional robot learning. Robot action training can overwrite knowledge from the web. Physical Intelligence found that naively adding an action expert "significantly harms both training speed and knowledge transfer" <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

Earlier designs split the problem differently, and none won outright:

| Approach | Works |
| --- | --- |
| Separate recognition from spatial control | CLIPort, RoboFlamingo, VIMA <Cite id="clipport-2021" /> <Cite id="roboflamingo-2023" /> <Cite id="vima-2022" /> |
| Visual features learned for robots | Voltron, LIV <Cite id="voltron-2023" /> <Cite id="liv-2023" /> |
| 3D scene input, precision against compute | PerAct, RVT, Act3D, GNFactor <Cite id="peract-2022" /> <Cite id="rvt-2023" /> <Cite id="act3d-2023" /> <Cite id="gnfactor-2023" /> |
| No representation wins every task | CortexBench <Cite id="vc1-2023" /> |

Also: action tuning erodes reasoning <Cite id="int-act-2025" />, open VLAs miss semantics <Cite id="star-gen-2025" />.

<span id="what-pi07-adds" />
## π0.7 recombines trained skills and transfers them to new robots

π0.7 ([The Pi Line](/manipulation/pi-line/)) recombines previously learned skills to perform new tasks. Asked to load an air fryer, it "makes a reasonable attempt" without "finishing it fully" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. Language coaching gets it there, and these language coaching interactions train a new policy that can perform the task without any additional <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> demonstrations <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

<Term id="cross-embodiment">Cross-embodiment</Term> transfer refers to learning on one robot and working on another. In the Open X-Embodiment project, data from 22 robot types was pooled together to see if cross-embodiment transfer would help <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. π0.7 folds laundry on a two-armed UR5e embodiment (with no laundry data), achieving 80%, compared to 80.6% for expert teleoperators on their first attempt <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Also: GEN-1.5 learns from one short demonstration <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />, Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 adapts from under 200 examples <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<span id="what-these-results-do-not-demonstrate" />
## None of these results shows reliable, unaided success in unseen homes

π0.5's headline results are reported in three held-out homes, with ten trials per task in each <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> ([The Reliability Gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), [The Evaluation Crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)). GEN-1 reported a 99% <Term id="success-rate">success</Term> rate on fine-tuned tasks <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. In neither case did the authors show 95% success on a wide range of tasks in novel environments.

Benchmark scores hide brittleness:

| Benchmark | Under modest perturbation |
| --- | --- |
| LIBERO-Plus | 95% falls "below 30%" <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" /> |
| Colosseum | Down 30 to 50% <Cite id="colosseum-2024" /> |
| LIBERO-PRO | Over 90% "collapses to 0.0%" <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" /> |

Harder suites are improving: BEHAVIOR Challenge full-task success rose from 12% to 48% in a year <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2026" /> <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2026-results" />.

DreamZero's authors found that VLAs "struggle to generalize to unseen physical motions in novel environments", while a video world action model generalizes over 2x better <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. Physics transfers worse than meaning, because new objects bring new contact ([Dexterity](/frontier/dexterity/)).

GR00T 2 succeeded zero-shot in 40% of trials on 50 tasks new to its robot. Performance increases to 95% success when provided with five demonstrations for each task. The 50 were picked from tasks it learns from five, so 95% "is high partly by construction" <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Transfer effectiveness depends on similarity between source and target embodiments. Ablations on π0.7 illustrate how performance in cross-embodiment settings decreases with increasing differences between embodiments <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. EgoScale shows cross-embodiment transfer from a 22-<Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degree-of-freedom</Term> source hand to simpler target hands <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

<span id="the-scaling-evidence-and-what-it-measures" />
## EgoScale's law holds from 1k to 20k hours of human video

NVIDIA's EgoScale trained a VLA on 20,854 hours of action-labeled <Term id="egocentric-video">egocentric human video</Term>, over twenty times prior efforts. Validation loss decreases logarithmically with training data (fit to $L = 0.024 - 0.003 \ln D$, where $D$ is training data size in thousands of hours; $R^2 = 0.9983$ across five scales). After post-training, task completion increases from 0.30 (with 1k hours of training data) to 0.71 (with 20k hours) <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

<EgoScaleScaling defaultHorizonHours={250_000} className="my-6" />

Extended, the completion fit crosses an impossible 100% near 250k hours; the authors "do not extrapolate beyond the measured range" <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. The closest work to finding a scaling law for unseen settings is that of Lin et al., who find that performance "follows a roughly power-law relationship with the number of environments and objects" <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />.

In GR00T 2, including all its human video data (keeping the amount of robot data fixed) increased the success rate on unseen tasks from 18% to 41% on the real robot without showing signs of saturation <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Also: GR00T N1's data mix <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, N1.7's 20K EgoScale hours <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> ([Cross-Embodiment Transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/)).

## No published law yet predicts real-world success in unseen settings

The laws that exist predict proxies:

| Work | What it predicts |
| --- | --- |
| GEN-0, 270,000 hours | "Next-action prediction error" <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" /> |
| Figure, human to humanoid | Robot-action prediction <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" /> |
| TRI, blind randomized trials | Gains with scale and diversity, no law fitted <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" /> |

The data keeps growing:

| Source | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| AgiBot World | "Predictable performance scaling with increased data volume" <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" /> |
| Gemini Robotics 2.5 | Practice in simulated twins; 30 to 100% higher success on 200 tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" /> |
| Xiaomi Robotics 1 | Over 100k hours from UMI grippers <Cite id="umi-2024" />; better results in unseen settings <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" /> |

Bessemer says robotics' ChatGPT moment is "not here yet" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />. Ken Goldberg calls the shortfall a 100,000-year data gap and argues that a combination of learning and engineering accelerates the deployment of robots <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />.

Also: Brooks on touch and priors <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />, Karcini on grounded supervision <Cite id="karcini-position-2026" />.

The settling test is whether 10x or 100x more data, with no architecture change, yields proportional gains in unseen settings. Shi et al. find task diversity "more critical than per-task demonstration quantity" <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## No system yet meets the solved bar of 90% across dozens of unseen homes

Generalization would be solved if someone showed a published, reproducible evaluation of one policy that could perform tasks in dozens of different, previously unseen homes at a success rate of over 90%, trained on no data collected in those homes. Customers of Physical Intelligence's API are still expected to do fine-tuning <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />. Figure's Helix 2.5 model achieved 56% zero-shot on three whole-body tasks in 30 previously unseen homes (compared to 9% when the Index pretraining stage was removed), but Figure did not report how many trials were used to compute these numbers <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

<HelixZeroShotSuccess className="my-6" />

The best result for EgoScale is 0.71, falling short of the target of 0.90, even on a friendlier metric after post-training <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. The straight-line fit would reach the target value of 0.90 only with about 111k hours of data, which is five times beyond the measured range.
