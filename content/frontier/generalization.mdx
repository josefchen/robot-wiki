Generalization is a robot policy's ability to work in settings it was not trained in, closing the <Term id="distribution-shift">distribution shift</Term> of new geometry, objects and clutter without per-site data. Even the most general policies are "mostly trained on data collected in a small number of environments" <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and in a new home a one-centimeter grasp error drops the object.

<span id="what-pi05-demonstrated" />
## π0.5 cleaned three unseen homes, trained mostly on other data

Physical Intelligence's π0.5 (April 2025) is one end-to-end <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term>, tested cleaning kitchens and bedrooms in homes absent from its training data <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

Only about 400 hours of the mixture is mobile-manipulator data from real homes; 97.6% of the training examples in the first training phase come from somewhere else, including other robots, laboratory tasks and web data. Removing the other robots' data degrades performance sharply, while removing web data mainly hurts unseen objects. A model trained on data from the test homes scored about the same as the 104-location model that never saw them <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

Web-scale vision-language pretraining supplies semantic generalization, knowing what "the spatula" is in a new kitchen, as RT-2 showed for novel objects <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Motor generalization, grasping that spatula with this gripper, needs more, and the semantic half survives only if action training preserves it. Naively adding an action expert "significantly harms both training speed and knowledge transfer" <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. Action finetuning "can erode the original VLM's generalist reasoning abilities" <Cite id="int-act-2025" />, and open VLAs "often struggle with semantic generalization" <Cite id="star-gen-2025" />.

<span id="what-pi07-adds" />
## π0.7 recombines trained skills and transfers them to new robots

π0.7 (April 2026; see [The Pi Line](/manipulation/pi-line/)) adds compositional generalization, recombining trained skills into untrained tasks. Asked to load a sweet potato into an air fryer without robot demonstrations, the model "makes a reasonable attempt, performing part of the task after a few false starts, but not finishing it fully" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. With step-by-step language coaching the robot completes it, and the coaching transcripts train a high-level policy that runs the task with no additional <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Generalist reports a competing route: GEN-1.5 learns a new task in context from 3 to 12 seconds of one demonstration, with no training <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />.

Physical Intelligence also reports <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> transfer: π0.7 folds laundry on a bimanual UR5e that never had laundry data at 85.6% task progress and 80% success. Expert teleoperators averaging 375 hours reached 90.9% and 80.6% on their first try with that robot <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Google DeepMind reports that Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 adapts to new bi-arm embodiments in a few hours, "typically with less than 200 examples" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<span id="what-these-results-do-not-demonstrate" />
## None of these results shows reliable, unaided success in unseen homes

First, the air fryer attempt does not finish the task without coaching, and the authors claim only "early signs of compositional task generalization" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Second, reliability is unshown: π0.5's headline evaluation is three homes and ten trials per task <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> ([The Reliability Gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), [The Evaluation Crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)). No published result shows a generalist policy sustaining better than 95% <Term id="success-rate">success</Term> across a broad task distribution in unseen environments. Generalist's reported 99% average for GEN-1 covers only tasks it was fine-tuned on <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

Benchmark scores hide brittleness: modest perturbations cut LIBERO-Plus success "from 95% to below 30%" <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" /> and Colosseum's by 30 to 50% <Cite id="colosseum-2024" />. On LIBERO-PRO, over-90% performance "collapses to 0.0%" <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" />.

Third, the evaluations lean semantic, while new object physics bring in touch and contact ([Dexterity](/frontier/dexterity/)). DreamZero's authors write that VLAs "struggle to generalize to unseen physical motions in novel environments", and report over 2x better generalization from a video world action model <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. In March 2026 NVIDIA previewed GR00T N2, built on DreamZero research, and says it succeeds at new tasks more than twice as often as leading VLAs <Cite id="nvidia-gtc-gr00t-n2-2026" />.

Fourth, transfer works best when source and target share task structure. In π0.7's ablations, performance degrades as the embodiment gap widens before the latest model recovers it <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Across morphologies, the closest evidence is EgoScale's transfer from a 22-DoF hand to lower-DoF hands <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

<span id="the-scaling-evidence-and-what-it-measures" />
## EgoScale's law holds from 1k to 20k hours of human video

NVIDIA's EgoScale trained a VLA on 20,854 hours of action-labeled egocentric human video, more than twenty times prior efforts. Held-out validation loss follows $L = 0.024 - 0.003 \ln D$ with $D$ in thousands of hours, fit over five scales from 1k to 20k hours at $R^2 = 0.9983$. After post-training, average task completion rises from 0.30 at 1k hours to 0.71 at 20k, and the recipe lifts success by 54% over no pretraining on a 22-DoF hand <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

NVIDIA's GR00T N1 mixed "real-robot trajectories, human videos, and synthetically generated datasets" <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. N1.7 adds 20K hours of EgoScale video through an end-effector space shared by robots and humans ([Cross-Embodiment Transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/)) <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

The chart extends the fit past 20k hours, although the authors "do not extrapolate beyond the measured range", and its completion fit crosses an impossible 100% near 250k hours <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. The law is fit to validation loss, a proxy that correlates with real-robot performance without being a <Term id="success-rate">success rate</Term> across unseen environments. Lin et al. come closest to a law for that metric: single-task performance in unseen environments "follows a roughly power-law relationship with the number of environments and objects" <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />.

## No published law yet predicts real-world success in unseen settings

Generalist fits a power law to GEN-0's 270,000 hours of real-world manipulation data, with "next-action prediction error" as its target <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />. Figure's human-to-humanoid transfer law is also fit to robot-action prediction <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />. TRI's blind, randomized trials found that performance "predictably increases as pretraining scale and diversity grows", without fitting a law <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

The scaling camp cites EgoScale's "no signs of saturation in the explored regime" <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />, AgiBot World's "predictable performance scaling with increased data volume" <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" /> and π0.7's compositional results <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Investor analysis from Bessemer, arguing that scaling laws are beginning to show up in robotics data, says of robotics' ChatGPT moment: "We don't think that moment is years away. But it's not here yet." <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

Generalist reports over half a million hours for GEN-1 <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Xiaomi trained on over 100k hours from UMI handheld grippers <Cite id="umi-2024" />, with stronger pretraining giving better out-of-the-box results in unseen environments <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />.

Ken Goldberg frames the shortfall as a 100,000-year data gap. He expects end-to-end learning to win eventually, but argues that pairing good old-fashioned engineering with learning gets robots deployed and collecting data sooner <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />. Rodney Brooks argues the missing ingredients are touch and engineered priors <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Karcini and co-authors argue that policy scaling lacks mechanisms that turn unstructured behavior into grounded robot supervision <Cite id="karcini-position-2026" />.

The settling test is whether a 10x or 100x data increase, with no architectural change, yields proportional real-world gains in unseen environments. Shi et al. find task diversity "more critical than per-task demonstration quantity" <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## No system yet meets the solved bar of 90% across dozens of unseen homes

Generalization is solved when a single policy achieves better than 90% success across many unseen homes, dozens of them, with no per-site data collection or fine-tuning, in a published, reproducible evaluation spanning many tasks and objects. No current system has been evaluated this way. Figure reports that Helix 2.5 reached 56% zero-shot success, against 9% without Index pretraining, on three whole-body tasks in 30 unseen Bay Area homes, graded blind with no published trial counts <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

The solved bar sits at 0.90 on the chart's completion axis, and the best measured point is 0.71, on a friendlier metric, after post-training, on tasks the evaluators chose <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. The extrapolation crosses the bar only near 111k hours of human video, five times past the measured range.
