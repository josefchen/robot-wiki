Generalization is a robot policy's ability to work in new rooms, among new objects and clutter, a <Term id="distribution-shift">distribution shift</Term> it must handle without data from each site <Cite id="droid-2024" />. It decides whether one model can serve every home or must be retrained for each.

<Image id="droid-cook-lentils-rollouts-2024" />

Robot data comes from few places. Even the most general policies are "mostly trained on data collected in a small number of environments", DROID's authors wrote <Cite id="droid-2024" />.

<span id="what-pi05-demonstrated" />
## π0.5 cleaned three unseen homes, trained mostly on other data

Physical Intelligence's π0.5, a <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term>, cleaned kitchens and bedrooms in homes it never trained in. Only about 400 hours of its data came from mobile robots in real homes. A model trained on the test homes themselves scored about the same as the 104-location model that never saw them <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

## Knowing what is easier than knowing how

Web pretraining teaches what "the spatula" is in a new kitchen, as RT-2 showed <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Grasping it with this gripper needs more, and action training can erase the first half. Physical Intelligence found that naively adding an action expert "significantly harms both training speed and knowledge transfer" <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

Earlier designs split the problem differently, and none won outright:

| Approach | Works |
| --- | --- |
| Separate recognition from spatial control | CLIPort, RoboFlamingo, VIMA <Cite id="clipport-2021" /> <Cite id="roboflamingo-2023" /> <Cite id="vima-2022" /> |
| Visual features learned for robots | Voltron, LIV <Cite id="voltron-2023" /> <Cite id="liv-2023" /> |
| 3D scene input, precision against compute | PerAct, RVT, Act3D, GNFactor <Cite id="peract-2022" /> <Cite id="rvt-2023" /> <Cite id="act3d-2023" /> <Cite id="gnfactor-2023" /> |
| No representation wins every task | CortexBench <Cite id="vc1-2023" /> |

Also: action tuning erodes reasoning <Cite id="int-act-2025" />, open VLAs miss semantics <Cite id="star-gen-2025" />.

<span id="what-pi07-adds" />
## π0.7 recombines trained skills and transfers them to new robots

π0.7 ([The Pi Line](/manipulation/pi-line/)) recombines trained skills into new tasks. Asked to load an air fryer, it "makes a reasonable attempt" without "finishing it fully" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. Language coaching gets it there, and the coaching trains a policy that needs no new <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

<Term id="cross-embodiment">Cross-embodiment</Term> transfer means learning on one robot and working on another; Open X-Embodiment pooled 22 robot types to test it <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. π0.7 folds laundry at 80% on a two-armed UR5e that never had laundry data, against 80.6% for expert teleoperators on their first try <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Also: GEN-1.5 learns from one short demonstration <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />, Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 adapts from under 200 examples <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<span id="what-these-results-do-not-demonstrate" />
## None of these results shows reliable, unaided success in unseen homes

Reliability is unmeasured: π0.5's headline evaluation is three homes and ten trials per task <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> ([The Reliability Gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/), [The Evaluation Crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)). No generalist publishes 95% <Term id="success-rate">success</Term> across broad tasks in unseen settings; GEN-1's 99% covers only fine-tuned tasks <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

Benchmark scores hide brittleness:

| Benchmark | Under modest perturbation |
| --- | --- |
| LIBERO-Plus | 95% falls "below 30%" <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" /> |
| Colosseum | Down 30 to 50% <Cite id="colosseum-2024" /> |
| LIBERO-PRO | Over 90% "collapses to 0.0%" <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" /> |

Harder suites are improving: BEHAVIOR Challenge full-task success rose from 12% to 48% in a year <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2026" /> <Cite id="behavior-challenge-2026-results" />.

Physics transfers worse than meaning, because new objects bring new contact ([Dexterity](/frontier/dexterity/)). DreamZero's authors find VLAs "struggle to generalize to unseen physical motions in novel environments", and a video world action model generalizes over 2x better <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />.

GR00T 2 succeeded zero-shot in 40% of trials on 50 tasks new to its robot, and five demonstrations each raised that to 95%. The 50 were picked from tasks it learns from five, so 95% "is high partly by construction" <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Transfer works best when bodies share structure. In π0.7's ablations, performance falls as robot bodies differ more <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, and EgoScale transfers from a 22-<Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degree-of-freedom</Term> hand to simpler hands <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

<span id="the-scaling-evidence-and-what-it-measures" />
## EgoScale's law holds from 1k to 20k hours of human video

NVIDIA's EgoScale trained a VLA on 20,854 hours of action-labeled <Term id="egocentric-video">egocentric human video</Term>, over twenty times prior efforts. Validation loss follows $L = 0.024 - 0.003 \ln D$, with $D$ in thousands of hours, at $R^2 = 0.9983$ across five scales. After post-training, task completion rises from 0.30 at 1k hours to 0.71 at 20k <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

<EgoScaleScaling defaultHorizonHours={250_000} className="my-6" />

Extended, the completion fit crosses an impossible 100% near 250k hours; the authors "do not extrapolate beyond the measured range" <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. Lin and colleagues come closest to a law for unseen settings: performance "follows a roughly power-law relationship with the number of environments and objects" <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />.

In GR00T 2, adding all its human video at fixed robot data raised real-robot success on new tasks from 18% to 41%, with no leveling off <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Also: GR00T N1's data mix <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, N1.7's 20K EgoScale hours <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> ([Cross-Embodiment Transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/)).

## No published law yet predicts real-world success in unseen settings

The laws that exist predict proxies:

| Work | What it predicts |
| --- | --- |
| GEN-0, 270,000 hours | "Next-action prediction error" <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" /> |
| Figure, human to humanoid | Robot-action prediction <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" /> |
| TRI, blind randomized trials | Gains with scale and diversity, no law fitted <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" /> |

The data keeps growing:

| Source | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| AgiBot World | "Predictable performance scaling with increased data volume" <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" /> |
| Gemini Robotics 2.5 | Practice in simulated twins; 30 to 100% higher success on 200 tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" /> |
| Xiaomi Robotics 1 | Over 100k hours from UMI grippers <Cite id="umi-2024" />; better results in unseen settings <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" /> |

Bessemer says robotics' ChatGPT moment is "not here yet" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />. Ken Goldberg calls the shortfall a 100,000-year data gap and argues that engineering plus learning gets robots deployed sooner <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />.

Also: Brooks on touch and priors <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />, Karcini on grounded supervision <Cite id="karcini-position-2026" />.

The settling test is whether 10x or 100x more data, with no architecture change, yields proportional gains in unseen settings. Shi et al. find task diversity "more critical than per-task demonstration quantity" <Cite id="diversity-scaling-2025" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## No system yet meets the solved bar of 90% across dozens of unseen homes

Generalization is solved when one policy succeeds over 90% of the time across dozens of unseen homes, with no per-site data, in a published, reproducible evaluation. Physical Intelligence's API still has customers fine-tune <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />. Figure's Helix 2.5 reached 56% zero-shot on three whole-body tasks in 30 unseen homes, against 9% without Index pretraining, with no trial counts <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

<HelixZeroShotSuccess className="my-6" />

EgoScale's best is 0.71 against a 0.90 bar, on a friendlier metric after post-training <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. The straight-line fit reaches 0.90 only near 111k hours, five times past the measured range.
