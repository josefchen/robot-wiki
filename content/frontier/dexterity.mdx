Dexterity is skilled <Term id="contact-rich-manipulation">contact-rich manipulation</Term>, such as reorienting an object in the hand or folding cloth. It remains unsolved: a model Physical Intelligence fine-tuned on Benjie Holson's Humanoid Olympics tasks averaged 52% success on them <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

<Image id="uskin-tactile-allegro-hand-2021" />

Rodney Brooks opens his dexterity essay with Heinrich Ernst's PhD work: by 1961, he writes, Ernst had connected a computer-controlled arm and hand to MIT's TX-0 and had it picking up and stacking blocks <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Hands are hard because of contact: grip force, slip and contact geometry decide success, and cameras lose most of that state once the fingers close. [Impedance control](/classical/control) governs a contact where position or force commands alone cannot, and touch lets a controller observe the contact it shapes.

The five 2026 hands below rank differently by touch and by price.

## The tactile gap

<TactileSlip className="my-6" />

A review of Roland Johansson's work reports about 17,000 low-threshold <Term id="mechanoreceptor">mechanoreceptors</Term> in the hand's glabrous skin, roughly 1,000 of them at each fingertip <Cite id="macefield-touch-2022" />. Brooks cites two videos from Johansson's lab. In his account, lighting a match took seven seconds, then four times as long once the same person's fingertips were anesthetized, with difficulty picking up and orienting the match. He says she could still sense other things in the rest of her fingers and hand and all the forces she ordinarily felt with her skeletal muscle system <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

In his September 8, 2025 post, Benjie Holson described limitations of the learning-from-demonstration setups he was seeing, explicitly calling them a general trend with exceptions. He pointed to the lack of good standard ways to relay wrist-force information to the human teleoperator, difficulty controlling fingers beyond open and close, and the difficulty of making human-like touch sensing available to the puppeteer. His estimate of about 1 to 3 cm of task precision was a guess from videos. He said it was likely more a <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> limitation than a model limitation and pointed to a video he described as showing sub-centimeter tasks <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />.

Luo and colleagues define tactile robotics as developing and integrating tactile-sensing technologies into robotic systems. Their 2025 outlook discusses challenges across sensor materials, networks, simulation, benchmarking, data interpretation, multimodal learning, and active touch <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />. When he wrote the essay, Brooks concluded: "It looks like humanoid robots will need a sense of touch, and a level of touch sensing that no one has yet built in the lab" for tasks such as the match demonstration <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

## The bet against touch

Brooks frames the scaling counterargument as an imagined inner dialogue: end-to-end learning succeeded in speech-to-text, image labeling, and language models, so collecting examples of human hand use might yield dexterous control. He replies that those successes depended on engineered inputs and that dexterity learning will likely require the right sensory data and the right thing to learn <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

Brooks reproduces an eWeek report that describes Tesla as moving Optimus training toward a "vision-only approach" instead of motion capture suits and teleoperation. The quoted report says workers wear helmet-and-backpack rigs with five in-house cameras, record tasks such as folding a t-shirt or picking up an object, and use the videos to train Optimus to mimic those actions <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

Figure's September 2025 Project Go-Big announcement describes a pretraining data-collection initiative; it reports no general-dexterity result. Its initial human-video result is navigation. Helix, trained on 100% egocentric human video with no robot demonstrations for that approach, maps images and language to low-level SE(2) velocity commands, a transfer Figure calls zero-shot and, "to our knowledge", a first. Collection in Brookfield's residential environments had begun and would expand; the announcement's more than 100,000 residential units describe Brookfield's portfolio, without stating that those units were homes or trajectories collected <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.

In October 2026, Haonan Chen, Edward Adelson, Jiajun Wu, Robert Howe, Yilun Du and colleagues at Harvard, MIT, the University of Pennsylvania and Stanford trained a camera-based estimator that reads grip force from how a compliant Fin Ray gripper bends. On three held-out episodes the mean error was 0.91 N, against 4.20 N from motor current. Their Diffusion Policy also outputs force, samples 32 actions per step and keeps the one closest to a target force from the demonstrations. On an ARX arm with no force or tactile sensor, over 15 trials per task, this raised success over a single sample from 46.7% to 86.7% for grasping an empty soda can, 0% to 73.3% for berry picking, 6.7% to 46.7% for in-hand reorientation and 26.7% to 73.3% for plug insertion <Cite id="visual-predicted-force-2026" />.

## The bet on touch

Jeremy Fishel, principal researcher at Sanctuary AI, counters that with video alone you do not know you have touched something until well after the collision has physically moved the object <Cite id="sanctuary-tactile-2025" />. Sanctuary's CEO James Wells calls touch a key enabler for creating human-level dexterity in robots <Cite id="sanctuary-tactile-2025" />. The company's hydraulic Phoenix hands carry fingerpad arrays of micro-barometer cells sensitive to about five millinewtons, against roughly three for a human fingertip <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />.

Figure's October 2025 announcement describes a palm camera in each Figure 03 hand. It says each fingertip sensor can detect "three grams of pressure", comparing the sensitivity to a paperclip's weight <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />. Its January 2026 Helix 02 announcement lists head cameras, palm cameras, fingertip tactile sensors, and full-body proprioception as System 1 inputs. Figure describes this as "the first time we've demonstrated neural network policies that depend on these modalities" <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Touch now ships on flagship hands, Figure's included, and tactile foundation models exist, but no tactile training pipeline yet runs at vision scale. Sparsh-X pretrained on about a million contact-rich interactions from Meta's Digit 360 sensor and lifted policy success rates by 63% over an end-to-end model using tactile images <Cite id="sparsh-x-2025" />. TouchWorld couples a tactile world model with a fast tactile refinement policy to reach 65% success on six long-horizon contact-rich tasks, 15.7 points over the strongest baseline <Cite id="touchworld-2026" />.

In June 2026, Dantong Niu, Yuke Zhu, Trevor Darrell and colleagues described T-Rex. It pairs a 100-hour tactile-rich dataset with a vision-language-action model that reads high-frequency touch through a variable-rate mixture-of-transformers and a temporal tactile encoder. On 12 tasks requiring delicate force control and deformable-object manipulation, its average success rate was more than 30% higher than the strongest baseline's <Cite id="t-rex-2026" />.

<Image id="t-rex-bimanual-tactile-setup-2026" />

In September 2026, Enyi Wang and Yan Wang of Tsinghua University's Institute for AI Industry Research and colleagues reported TacDyn-WAM. This world action model predicts learned tactile representations instead of reconstructing tactile images. On five real-robot tasks with 60 demonstrations each, it averaged 71.0% success, 16 points above the tactile policy FTP-1. Pretraining on about 6,000 real visuo-tactile trajectories raised that to 85.0% <Cite id="tacdyn-wam-2026" />.

Google's Gemini Robotics 2 drives the 22-DoF SharpaWave hand, itself a tactile design <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" /><Cite id="robozaps-unitree-h2-2026" />. It reports 92% success unscrewing a light bulb but 32 to 44% on its other multi-finger tasks. DeepMind itself flags this spread as the hard remainder <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## Five hands, five bets

Of the five hands compared above, Tesla's 22-<Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degree-of-freedom</Term> tendon design has the most articulation and an admitted redesign behind it. Days after the V3 hand patents surfaced, Musk said of the design, "this one didn't actually work" <Cite id="teslarati-optimus-hand-2026" /> <Cite id="droids-optimus-v3-hand-2026" />. Sanctuary's hydraulic hand pairs the most sensitive disclosed touch with a working in-hand demo, and its maker has since pivoted to selling software <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />.

Shadow's Dexterous Hand, the two-decade research benchmark, runs more than 100 sensors at 1 kHz <Cite id="shadow-dexterous-hand-2026" />. Each finger has independent side-to-side motion for in-hand work <Cite id="shadow-dexterous-hand-2026" />, and the lab price is €110,000 including support <Cite id="shadow-hand-cost-2022" />.

Unitree lists the entire H2 robot at \$29,900, but the base model ships with non-functional placeholder hands. Its tactile option, the H2 Plus with Sharpa Wave hands, lists at \$100,000 <Cite id="robozaps-unitree-h2-2026" />. Figure's own release for Figure 02, dated August 6, 2024 and hosted by PRNewswire, describes the second-generation robot's "4th generation hands" as "equipped with 16 degrees of freedom". It does not say whether that count is per hand or combined, or give an actuator count <Cite id="figure-02-2024" />.

Boston Dynamics announced a new Atlas hand on October 1, 2026. It has four fingers and 13 directly actuated degrees of freedom, up from seven, and pressure sensors across the fingertips and palm <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />. It has no little finger, which would have needed three more actuators. It is built to be simulated accurately enough for training with reinforcement learning <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />.

In September 2026, Amirhossein Kazemipour, Hehui Zheng and Robert Katzschmann of ETH Zurich's Soft Robotics Lab made a hand the whole robot. Their reinforcement-learning policies, trained in a simulator calibrated from hardware measurements, let an off-the-shelf 738 g WUJI hand with its own power and computer crawl on its fingers and work with them. On hardware it got back up from a fall in 21 of 25 trials and pressed the commanded arrow key for 29 of 32 consecutive commands while supporting its own weight. Guided by an overhead camera, it completed 15 cube deliveries to targets 10 to 40 cm away with a mean final error of 17 mm <Cite id="fingers-as-legs-2026" />.

## In-hand manipulation

<Term id="in-hand-manipulation">In-hand manipulation</Term>, reorienting a held object without setting it down, separates a hand from a gripper. In the gold-medal "Use a key" event of Holson's Humanoid Olympics, a keyring with at least two keys and a keychain is dropped into the robot's waiting palm or gripper. Without putting the keys down, the robot must align, insert, and turn the correct key in a lock <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. The challenge rules do not report a completed robot run.

Sanctuary announced in-hand manipulation with its 21-DoF hydraulic hands in December 2024 <Cite id="sanctuary-inhand-2024" /> <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />. It then showed a sim-trained reinforcement-learning policy reorienting objects against gravity with a 500 g weight added, a vendor-run result <Cite id="sanctuary-hydraulic-rl-2025" />.

Figure says Helix 02, using Figure 03's fingertip sensors and palm cameras, performs four tasks it describes as manipulation previously out of reach for its stack, in videos it calls fully autonomous and without teleoperation: unscrewing a bottle cap, extracting a single pill from an organizer, a task titled "Push exactly 5 ml from a syringe", and picking small metal parts from clutter. The announcement publishes no task-level success rates, volume calibration, or sensor-ablation results <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. None of these is the general skill. Each is a task or a task family with its own training run.

In June 2026, Xinghao Zhu, Yuke Zhu and colleagues reported CHORD, a reinforcement-learning framework for long-horizon dexterous manipulation. It compares human and robot motions by the forces and torques they can induce on the object. On 1,831 tasks from its simulated benchmark of 4,739 bimanual tasks, it averaged 82.12% success. Its learned policies transferred to the real world <Cite id="chord-2026" />.

In September 2026, Yutong Liang, Quanquan Peng, Matthew Kim and Xiaolong Wang reported FINGR, a flow policy that turns Rubik's Cube layers with a single real dexterous hand. It succeeded on 99.0% of 300 turn attempts, against 79.7% for the base flow policy. With grasping and table-assisted regrasping added, it solved all ten scrambled 2×2×2 cubes in about 137 seconds on average <Cite id="fingr-2026" />.

<Image id="fingr-cube-layer-turn-2026" />

In October 2026, Harsh Gupta, Tyler Ga Wei Lum, Jeannette Bohg, Shuran Song and colleagues at Stanford reported that human hand-motion priors help most when they structure exploration and leave the action space unchanged. Their EigenDEXplore adds noise along eigenvectors of human hand motion to ordinary joint-space noise. Across several dexterous hands, it outperformed joint-space and learned action-space baselines in grasping, in-hand reorientation and contact-rich manipulation, with the largest gains where rewards and curricula were least engineered <Cite id="eigendexplore-2026" />.

## Deformables and the long tail

A wooden block rests on one of six sides. A towel can be bunched up in more ways than any state estimator will ever enumerate, and some tasks add water, grease, or peanut butter <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. Cloth, liquids, and compliant packaging fill Holson's task list: hang a dress shirt that starts with one sleeve inside out, clean peanut butter off your own manipulator.

RL-100 folds cloth and ran a juicing robot in a shopping mall for about seven hours without failure, both per-task results earned through on-robot reinforcement learning <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />. Physical Intelligence's π0.7 does laundry and espresso tasks with language steering <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Physical Intelligence fine-tuned its π0.6-based model on Holson's tasks and took gold in three of five categories with under nine hours of data for most tasks. The model averaged 52% success and 72% task progress, against 9% progress for a baseline VLM without robot pretraining. Two gold tasks were physically impossible for its gripper, and peeling an orange needed a tool and did not count <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

In September 2026, Nidhya Shivakumar, Ken Goldberg and colleagues reported TRACE, which traces monochrome cables in both directions. It uses interactive perception primitives to resolve ambiguous crossings. In 110 physical experiments with up to 4 cables and 40 crossings, it traced about 90% of cable length correctly. The strongest prior method, HANDLOOM 2.0, managed about 60% <Cite id="trace-cables-2026" />.

## What solved would look like

Dexterity is solved when one policy does Holson's fifteen tasks in unseen homes at human-competent speed, with no per-task data and with published success rates. Nobody is close: the best public scoreboard is about half success with per-task training. The open question is which data closes the gap. If Tesla or Figure reach household dexterity from video at scale, the tactile gap was a transitional hardware shortage.
