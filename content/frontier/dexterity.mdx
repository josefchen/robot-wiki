Dexterity is skilled <Term id="contact-rich-manipulation">contact-rich manipulation</Term>, such as reorienting an object in the hand or folding cloth. It remains unsolved: a model Physical Intelligence fine-tuned on Benjie Holson's Humanoid Olympics tasks averaged 52% success on them <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

<Image id="uskin-tactile-allegro-hand-2021" />

The problem is old. Rodney Brooks opens his dexterity essay with Heinrich Ernst's PhD work: by 1961, he writes, Ernst had a computer-controlled arm and hand on MIT's TX-0 picking up and stacking blocks <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Hands are hard because of contact. Grip force, slip and the shape of the contact decide success, and cameras lose most of that once the fingers close. [Impedance control](/classical/control) handles contact that position or force commands alone cannot, and touch lets a controller observe the contact it shapes.

Current hands rank differently by touch and by price. Figure says each Figure 03 fingertip can detect three grams of pressure <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />, and Shadow priced its research hand at €110,000 including support in 2022 <Cite id="shadow-hand-cost-2022" />.

<span id="the-tactile-gap" />
## Without touch, even people fumble

<TactileSlip className="my-6" />

Human hands are dense with touch sensors. A review of Roland Johansson's work reports about 17,000 low-threshold <Term id="mechanoreceptor">mechanoreceptors</Term> in the hand's hairless skin, roughly 1,000 at each fingertip <Cite id="macefield-touch-2022" />. Brooks cites two videos from Johansson's lab. In his account, lighting a match took seven seconds, then four times as long once the same person's fingertips were anesthetized, although she could still feel forces through her muscles. He concluded: "It looks like humanoid robots will need a sense of touch, and a level of touch sensing that no one has yet built in the lab" <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

The people who teach robots lack touch too. In September 2025 Holson described the learning-from-demonstration setups he was seeing, as a general trend with exceptions: no standard way to relay wrist force to the human operator, finger control limited to open and close, and no human-like touch for the operator. He guessed from videos that task precision was about 1 to 3 cm, more likely a <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> limit than a model limit <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />.

Robot touch has open problems from sensor materials and simulation to benchmarking and learning across senses, in Luo and colleagues' 2025 outlook <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />. Durability is one: ReSkin separates its magnetic-sensing electronics from a soft, replaceable skin, and a learned model absorbs the variation between skins <Cite id="reskin-2021" />.

<span id="the-bet-against-touch" />
## The bet on video: learn hands by watching people

The scaling bet holds that enough examples of human hand use can teach dexterity without new sensors. Brooks frames it as an imagined inner dialogue: end-to-end learning succeeded in speech-to-text, image labeling and language models, so hand data might do the same. He replies that those successes depended on engineered inputs, and that dexterity will need the right sensory data and the right thing to learn <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

Two companies are making the bet. Brooks reproduces an eWeek report that Tesla moved Optimus training toward a "vision-only approach": workers wear helmet-and-backpack rigs with five cameras, record tasks such as folding a t-shirt, and the videos train Optimus to mimic them <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Figure's September 2025 Project Go-Big collects human video for pretraining. Its first reported result was navigation: Helix, trained only on egocentric human video, turned images and language into walking commands, a transfer Figure calls zero-shot. The 100,000 residential units it cites describe Brookfield's portfolio; Figure does not say how many homes it recorded <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.

NVIDIA tested the bet on dexterous hands in November 2026. Shown a task only by humans, GR00T 2, pretrained partly on human video, had a robot with two five-fingered hands line a lab funnel with filter paper in 65% of trials. After post-training it averaged 68.6% on seven dexterous-hand tasks, such as swapping a memory module, against at most 0.7% for GR00T 1.7 and π0.5 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Vision can also stand in for a force sensor. In October 2026 Haonan Chen and colleagues trained a camera-based estimator that reads grip force from how a soft Fin Ray gripper bends, with a mean error of 0.91 N against 4.20 N from motor current, and steered a Diffusion Policy toward a target force. On an arm with no force or touch sensor, over 15 trials per task, this raised berry picking from 0% to 73.3% and plug insertion from 26.7% to 73.3% <Cite id="visual-predicted-force-2026" />.

<span id="the-bet-on-touch" />
## The bet on touch: feel contact before it moves the object

The tactile bet holds that vision reports contact too late. Jeremy Fishel of Sanctuary AI says that with video alone you do not know you have touched something until the collision has already moved the object, and CEO James Wells calls touch a key enabler of human-level dexterity <Cite id="sanctuary-tactile-2025" />. Sanctuary's hydraulic Phoenix hands carry fingerpad arrays sensitive to about five millinewtons, against roughly three for a human fingertip <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />. Figure has added touch as well. Each Figure 03 hand has a palm camera and fingertip sensors that detect "three grams of pressure" <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />, and Figure's January 2026 Helix 02 announcement calls policies using these inputs "the first time we've demonstrated neural network policies that depend on these modalities" <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

The evidence for touch is growing. In controlled real-robot comparisons, touch added more than a closer camera, and models built around touch, pretrained on large tactile datasets, beat their strongest baselines on contact-rich tasks:

Touch does not yet finish the job. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 drives the 22-degree-of-freedom SharpaWave hand, itself a tactile design <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" /><Cite id="robozaps-unitree-h2-2026" />, and reports 92% success unscrewing a light bulb but 32 to 44% on its other multi-finger tasks, a spread DeepMind flags as the hard remainder <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<span id="five-hands-five-bets" />
## Five hands, five bets

Each hand trades articulation, touch, price and durability differently. Tesla's 22-<Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degree-of-freedom</Term> tendon design has the most articulation and an admitted redesign behind it: days after the V3 hand patents surfaced, Musk said of the design, "this one didn't actually work" <Cite id="teslarati-optimus-hand-2026" /> <Cite id="droids-optimus-v3-hand-2026" />. On Tesla's October 2026 earnings call, engineer Konstantinos Laskaris called the hand "one of the most challenging hardware areas", and Musk tied outside Optimus deliveries around the end of 2027 to solving it <Cite id="tesla-q3-2026-call" />. Sanctuary's hydraulic hand pairs the most sensitive disclosed touch with a working in-hand demo, and its maker has since pivoted to selling software <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />.

Shadow's Dexterous Hand, the research benchmark for two decades, runs more than 100 sensors at 1 kHz and gives each finger independent side-to-side motion for in-hand work <Cite id="shadow-dexterous-hand-2026" />. Unitree lists its whole H2 robot at \$29,900, but the base model ships with non-functional placeholder hands; the H2 Plus with tactile Sharpa Wave hands lists at \$100,000 <Cite id="robozaps-unitree-h2-2026" />. Figure's August 2024 release gives Figure 02's "4th generation hands" "16 degrees of freedom" without saying whether the count is per hand <Cite id="figure-02-2024" />.

Boston Dynamics announced a new Atlas hand on October 1, 2026, with four fingers, 13 directly actuated degrees of freedom (up from seven) and pressure sensors across the fingertips and palm. It has no little finger, which would have needed three more actuators, and is built to be simulated accurately enough for reinforcement-learning training <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />. At ETH Zurich in September 2026, a hand became the whole robot: policies trained in a calibrated simulator let an off-the-shelf 738 g WUJI hand, with its own power and computer, crawl on its fingers, get back up from a fall in 21 of 25 trials and complete 15 camera-guided cube deliveries with a mean final error of 17 mm <Cite id="fingers-as-legs-2026" />.

<span id="in-hand-manipulation" />
## In-hand manipulation separates a hand from a gripper

<Term id="in-hand-manipulation">In-hand manipulation</Term> means reorienting a held object without setting it down. In Holson's gold-medal "Use a key" event, a keyring is dropped into the robot's palm or gripper, and without putting the keys down it must align, insert and turn the correct key in a lock <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. In January 2026 Holson gave Physical Intelligence the gold, at about five times human time. Its robot passed the key between two grippers instead of turning it in one hand, so he offered another gold for a one-handed run <Cite id="holson-pi-deep-dive-2026" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="hand" />

Most progress is still one task at a time. Sanctuary announced in-hand manipulation with its 21-degree-of-freedom hydraulic hands in December 2024 <Cite id="sanctuary-inhand-2024" /> <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />, then showed a policy trained in simulation reorienting objects against gravity with a 500 g weight added, a vendor-run result <Cite id="sanctuary-hydraulic-rl-2025" />. Figure says Helix 02, using Figure 03's fingertip sensors and palm cameras, performs four tasks previously out of reach for its system, in videos it calls fully autonomous: unscrewing a bottle cap, extracting a single pill from an organizer, pushing exactly 5 ml from a syringe and picking small metal parts from clutter. It publishes no task-level success rates or sensor-ablation results, and each is a task or task family with its own training run <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Research systems push precision and breadth. FINGR (September 2026) turns Rubik's Cube layers with a single real hand, succeeding on 99.0% of 300 turns against 79.7% for its base policy, and with regrasping added it solved all ten scrambled 2×2×2 cubes in about 137 seconds on average <Cite id="fingr-2026" />. CHORD (June 2026) compares human and robot motions by the forces and torques they can apply to the object, averaged 82.12% on 1,831 of its 4,739 simulated two-handed tasks, and transferred to the real world <Cite id="chord-2026" />. Stanford's EigenDEXplore (October 2026) found that human hand-motion patterns help most when they guide exploration and leave the action space unchanged; it beat joint-space and learned action-space baselines across several hands, with the largest gains where rewards and curricula were least engineered <Cite id="eigendexplore-2026" />.

<Image id="fingr-cube-layer-turn-2026" />

Where to put the fingers is its own problem. Dexonomy starts from one human-annotated template per hand and grasp type, then refines the hand around the object in simulation <Cite id="dexonomy-2025" />, and Chen and colleagues synthesize grasps for task-specific forces and torques, such as turning knobs and pressing buttons <Cite id="task-wrench-boundary-2023" />.

<span id="deformables-and-the-long-tail" />
## Soft objects have endless states

Deformable objects have far more possible states than rigid ones. A wooden block rests on one of six sides, while a towel can be bunched in more ways than any state estimator will enumerate, and some tasks add water, grease or peanut butter. Holson's list includes hanging a dress shirt that starts with one sleeve inside out and cleaning peanut butter off the robot's own hand <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />.

Cloth methods first reduce the disorder. FlingBot learns to pick, stretch and fling a cloth flat <Cite id="flingbot-2021" />; SpeedFolding smooths a garment and then folds it along user-specified lines with two grippers <Cite id="speedfolding-2022" />; Cloth Funnels chooses between flings and pick-and-place actions to bring garments into a standard shape for later skills <Cite id="cloth-funnels-2022" />. A different tool changes the forces available: DextAIRity adds directed airflow to grasping <Cite id="dextairity-2022" />, and RoboCook models tool interactions with soft objects using graph neural networks <Cite id="robocook-2023" />.

Real-world results remain per task. RL-100 folds cloth and ran a juicing robot in a shopping mall for about seven hours without failure, both earned through reinforcement learning on the robot <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />, and π0.7 does laundry and espresso tasks with language steering <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. TRACE (September 2026) traces tangled single-color cables by moving them to resolve ambiguous crossings, following about 90% of cable length correctly in 110 physical experiments with up to 4 cables and 40 crossings, against about 60% for the strongest prior method <Cite id="trace-cables-2026" />.

Holson's Olympics is the nearest thing to a scoreboard. Physical Intelligence fine-tuned its π0.6-based model on his tasks and took gold in three of five categories with under nine hours of data for most tasks. The model averaged 52% success and 72% task progress, against 9% progress for a baseline vision-language model without robot pretraining. Two gold tasks were physically impossible for its gripper, and peeling an orange needed a tool and did not count <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## What solved would look like

Dexterity is solved when one policy does Holson's fifteen tasks in unseen homes within ten times Holson's own time, with no per-task data and with published success rates <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. Per-task medals came fast. Holson wrote in January 2026 that nearly all his first challenges fell within three months, about 15 months sooner than he expected, and set harder ones such as separating an egg yolk and fitting a duvet cover <Cite id="holson-olympics-part2-2026" />. The single-policy test is still open: the best public scoreboard is about half success with per-task training <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />. If Tesla or Figure reach household dexterity from video at scale, the tactile gap will have been a temporary hardware shortage <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" /> <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.
