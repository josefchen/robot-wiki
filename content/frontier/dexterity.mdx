Dexterity is skilled <Term id="contact-rich-manipulation">contact-rich manipulation</Term>, such as reorienting an object in the hand or folding cloth. It is unsolved: the Physical Intelligence model fine-tuned on 11 manipulation tasks from Benjie Holson's Humanoid Olympics achieved an average success rate of 52% <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

<Image id="uskin-tactile-allegro-hand-2021" />

The problem is old: by 1961 Heinrich Ernst had demonstrated stacking of blocks using a computer-controlled hand at MIT <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Grasping and manipulation are challenging as success depends on contact, which is difficult to sense. Grip force and slip decide success, but once the fingers are closed, cameras cannot tell what is going on inside the grasp, and [impedance control](/classical/control) shapes contact that only touch observes.

<span id="the-tactile-gap" />
## Without touch, even people fumble

<TactileSlip className="my-6" />

The human hand contains some 17,000 low-threshold <Term id="mechanoreceptor">mechanoreceptors</Term>, about 1,000 of them in each fingertip <Cite id="macefield-touch-2022" />. With anesthetized fingertips, a person in Roland Johansson's lab took four times as long to light a match. Robots, Brooks notes, "will need a sense of touch, and a level of touch sensing that no one has yet built in the lab" <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

The people who teach robots lack touch too. Holson also found that, in the rigs he saw, the teleoperators' hands could only open and close and provided no force or touch feedback to the operator. As such, he pegged the limits of <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> at a task precision of 1 to 3 cm <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />.

Also: open problems in robot touch <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />, ReSkin's replaceable magnetic skin <Cite id="reskin-2021" />.

<span id="the-bet-against-touch" />
## The bet on video: learn hands by watching people

The "scaling bet" is the claim that with enough video of human hands performing tasks, robots can learn dexterity without new sensors. Brooks argues that speech and image learning succeeded with carefully designed input modalities, and learning dexterity will require appropriate modalities <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

Figure's Project Go-Big pretrains on human video, and its first result was Helix walking from egocentric human video alone <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />. Tesla moved Optimus to a "vision-only approach", in which workers are tasked with performing activities such as folding a t-shirt while wearing rigs outfitted with five cameras <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

GR00T 2 tested the bet on hands. It was shown how to perform a task (lining a lab funnel with filter paper) only by watching humans perform it, and when asked to reproduce the task on a robot with two five-fingered hands, it was successful 65% of the time <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

A camera can stand in for a force sensor. Haonan Chen and coworkers were able to infer the gripping force of a soft gripper by watching it deform on camera, and by using this estimated force to condition a Diffusion Policy, improved the success rate of a berry-picking task from 0% to 73.3% <Cite id="visual-predicted-force-2026" />.

<span id="the-bet-on-touch" />
## The bet on touch: feel contact before it moves the object

Sanctuary AI's Jeremy Fishel points out that, using video alone, the robot doesn't learn it's touched anything until after that contact has moved the object <Cite id="sanctuary-tactile-2025" />. The Phoenix fingerpads developed by Sanctuary AI reportedly sense touch down to about 5 mN, compared to roughly 3 mN for a human finger <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />. Figure 03 fingertips can sense objects as light as three grams <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />, and the company says Helix 02 marks the first set of Figure policies to make use of touch <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Touch beats a closer camera and lifts models above their baselines:

| Work | Test | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tactile vs. wrist camera (2026) | Same data and architecture, three contact-rich tasks | Finger arrays +31.7 to 60.0 points; wrist cameras +18.3 to 28.3 <Cite id="tactile-vs-wrist-camera-2026" /> |
| Tactile cues study (2026) | 720 real trials | 57.8% without touch, 78.9% with it <Cite id="tactile-cues-study-2026" /> |
| Sparsh-X (2025) | About a million Digit 360 contact interactions | +63% over tactile images end to end, on plug insertion <Cite id="sparsh-x-2025" /> |
| LingBot-Touch (2026) | Robbyant, open source, three sensor types <Cite id="lingbot-touch-2026" /> | Four policies up 14 to 26 points, company-reported <Cite id="lingbot-touch-release-2026" /> |
| Spark-0 (2026) | Human touch supervises pretraining; simulated, no touch at test | 71.7%, against 52.5% with touch discarded <Cite id="spark-0-2026" /> |
| TouchScale (2026) | Human vision-and-touch mid-training, 500 hours; four real tasks | 22.5% to 57.5% <Cite id="touchscale-2026" /> |
| TouchWorld (2026) | Tactile world model, six long-horizon tasks | 65%, 15.7 points over the best baseline <Cite id="touchworld-2026" /> |
| T-Rex (2026) | Touch-reading VLA, 12 delicate-force tasks | Over 30% above the best baseline <Cite id="t-rex-2026" /> |
| TacDyn-WAM (2026) | Predicts tactile features, five real tasks | 71.0%, 16 points above FTP-1 <Cite id="tacdyn-wam-2026" /> |
| GPT-6 Astra (2026) | Tactile images as visual input, 19 simulated tasks | UniVTAC 93.8%, OpenViTac 81.8%; touch adds 17.5 and 1.2 points <Cite id="astra-visuotactile-2026" /> |

<Image id="t-rex-bimanual-tactile-setup-2026" />

Touch does not finish the job. Gemini Robotics 2 drives a tactile 22-degree-of-freedom SharpaWave hand, unscrewing a light bulb with 92% success but achieving 32 to 44% success rates on other multi-finger tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<span id="five-hands-five-bets" />
## Every hand trades articulation, touch and price differently

Tesla's tendon-driven 22-<Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degree-of-freedom</Term> hand is the most articulated. Musk said of the V3 design "this one didn't actually work" <Cite id="teslarati-optimus-hand-2026" /> <Cite id="droids-optimus-v3-hand-2026" />, and he tied outside Optimus deliveries around the end of 2027 to solving it <Cite id="tesla-q3-2026-call" />. Sanctuary's hand reportedly comes close to human-level tactile sensation and has been demonstrated manipulating an object in-hand, and the company has since shifted to selling software <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />.

Shadow's Dexterous Hand packs more than 100 sensors sampling at up to 1 kHz and has been the research benchmark for two decades <Cite id="shadow-dexterous-hand-2026" />. It cost €110,000 with support in 2022 <Cite id="shadow-hand-cost-2022" />. Unitree's H2 Plus has five-fingered Sharpa hands <Cite id="unitree-h2-plus-announcement" /> (\$29,900 for the base H2 model <Cite id="unitree-h2-2026" />). Pricing for the H2 Plus is available upon contacting Unitree's sales team <Cite id="unitree-h2-plus-store" />.

Boston Dynamics equipped Atlas with a hand with touch sensing in the palm and fingertips. This hand, with 13 actuated degrees of freedom, is designed to be accurately simulatable for reinforcement learning <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />. At ETH Zurich, researchers showed how a 738 g robot consisting of nothing but a WUJI hand can crawl using its fingers <Cite id="fingers-as-legs-2026" />.

Also: Figure 02's 16-degree-of-freedom hands <Cite id="figure-02-2024" />.

<span id="in-hand-manipulation" />
## In-hand manipulation separates a hand from a gripper

<Term id="in-hand-manipulation">In-hand manipulation</Term> refers to the task of reorienting an object while holding it (without placing it on a surface). In Holson's "Use a key" event, a keyring is placed in the robot's hand and the robot is tasked with inserting and turning the correct key <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. The winning solution from Physical Intelligence passed the keys between two grippers and completed the task in about five times human time <Cite id="holson-pi-deep-dive-2026" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="hand" />

Most progress happens one task at a time. Sanctuary's 21-degree-of-freedom hydraulic hands can manipulate objects by reorienting them <Cite id="sanctuary-inhand-2024" />, even when an additional 500 g mass is attached <Cite id="sanctuary-hydraulic-rl-2025" />. Figure's Helix 02 is capable of performing tasks such as unscrewing bottle caps and pushing out a precise volume (5 ml) from a syringe (although success rates are not reported) <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Research systems push precision and breadth:

| System | Task | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FINGR (2026) | Rubik's Cube layer turns, one real hand | 99.0% of 300 turns, against 79.7% for its base policy <Cite id="fingr-2026" /> |
| CHORD (2026) | Two-handed tasks matched by object forces | 82.12% on 1,831 simulated tasks, then real <Cite id="chord-2026" /> |
| EigenDEXplore (2026) | Human hand patterns guide exploration | Beat joint-space and learned baselines across hands <Cite id="eigendexplore-2026" /> |
| Blind Baoding (2026) | Shadow Hand Lite, joint angles and binary touch | 225 consecutive half-turns; mean 34.75 turns, 5 without touch <Cite id="blind-baoding-2026" /> |

<Image id="fingr-cube-layer-turn-2026" />

Other works focus on generating finger poses for grasps. Dexonomy refines one human template per grasp type in simulation <Cite id="dexonomy-2025" />, and Chen and colleagues synthesize grasps for task forces such as turning knobs <Cite id="task-wrench-boundary-2023" />.

<span id="deformables-and-the-long-tail" />
## Soft objects have endless states

While a block can only rest on one of its six sides, a towel can be crumpled into far more positions than any estimator can list. Holson's tasks include hanging a shirt with one sleeve inside out and wiping peanut butter off the robot's own hand <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />.

Cloth methods either start by removing some of the mess, or by modifying the forces at play:

| Method | Move |
| --- | --- |
| FlingBot (2021) | Flings cloth flat <Cite id="flingbot-2021" /> |
| SpeedFolding (2022) | Smooths, then folds on chosen lines with two arms <Cite id="speedfolding-2022" /> |
| Cloth Funnels (2022) | Flings or places garments into a standard shape <Cite id="cloth-funnels-2022" /> |
| DextAIRity (2022) | Adds directed airflow to grasping <Cite id="dextairity-2022" /> |
| RoboCook (2023) | Graph networks model tools on soft objects <Cite id="robocook-2023" /> |

RL-100 ran a juicing robot in a mall for about seven hours without failure <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />, and TRACE traces about 90% of tangled cable length correctly, against 60% before <Cite id="trace-cables-2026" />.

Also: π0.7 on laundry and espresso <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

In Holson's Olympics, a model based on Physical Intelligence's π0.6 won gold medals in three of five categories, achieving 72% task progress (compared to 9% for a VLM baseline) <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## What solved would look like

Dexterity is solved when one policy does Holson's fifteen tasks in unseen homes within ten times his own time, with no per-task data and published success rates <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. Per-task medals came fast: nearly all his first challenges fell within three months, prompting him to post harder challenges such as separating an egg yolk <Cite id="holson-olympics-part2-2026" />. If Figure or Tesla demonstrate household dexterity learned from videos, lack of tactile sensing was a temporary hardware limitation <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" /> <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.
