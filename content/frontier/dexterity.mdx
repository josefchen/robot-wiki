Dexterity is skilled <Term id="contact-rich-manipulation">contact-rich manipulation</Term>, such as reorienting an object in the hand or folding cloth. It is unsolved: a model Physical Intelligence fine-tuned on 11 of Benjie Holson's Humanoid Olympics tasks averaged 52% success on them <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

<Image id="uskin-tactile-allegro-hand-2021" />

The problem is old: by 1961 Heinrich Ernst had a computer-controlled hand stacking blocks at MIT <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Contact makes it hard. Grip force and slip decide success, cameras lose them once the fingers close, and [impedance control](/classical/control) shapes contact that only touch observes.

<span id="the-tactile-gap" />
## Without touch, even people fumble

<TactileSlip className="my-6" />

The human hand carries about 17,000 low-threshold <Term id="mechanoreceptor">mechanoreceptors</Term>, roughly 1,000 per fingertip <Cite id="macefield-touch-2022" />. With anesthetized fingertips, a person in Roland Johansson's lab took four times as long to light a match. Brooks concludes that robots "will need a sense of touch, and a level of touch sensing that no one has yet built in the lab" <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

The people who teach robots lack touch too. Holson saw demonstration rigs with open-close fingers and no force or touch for the operator, and put task precision at 1 to 3 cm, a <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperation</Term> limit <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />.

Also: open problems in robot touch <Cite id="tactile-outlook-2025" />, ReSkin's replaceable magnetic skin <Cite id="reskin-2021" />.

<span id="the-bet-against-touch" />
## The bet on video: learn hands by watching people

The scaling bet holds that enough video of human hands teaches dexterity without new sensors. Brooks answers that speech and image learning succeeded on engineered inputs, and that dexterity needs the right sensory data <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

Tesla moved Optimus to a "vision-only approach", with workers in five-camera rigs recording tasks such as folding a t-shirt <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Figure's Project Go-Big pretrains on human video, and its first result was Helix walking from egocentric human video alone <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.

GR00T 2 tested the bet on hands. Shown a task only by humans, it lined a lab funnel with filter paper in 65% of trials on a robot with two five-fingered hands <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

A camera can stand in for a force sensor. Haonan Chen and colleagues read grip force from how a soft gripper bends, and steering a Diffusion Policy with that estimate raised berry picking from 0% to 73.3% <Cite id="visual-predicted-force-2026" />.

<span id="the-bet-on-touch" />
## The bet on touch: feel contact before it moves the object

With video alone, says Jeremy Fishel of Sanctuary AI, a robot learns it touched something only after the collision has moved it <Cite id="sanctuary-tactile-2025" />. Sanctuary's Phoenix fingerpads reportedly sense about 5 mN, against roughly 3 mN for a human finger <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />. Figure 03 fingertips detect three grams <Cite id="figure-03-2025" />, and Figure calls Helix 02 its first policies that depend on that touch <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Touch beats a closer camera, and tactile-pretrained models beat their strongest baselines:

| Work | Test | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tactile vs. wrist camera (2026) | Same data and architecture, three contact-rich tasks | Finger arrays +31.7 to 60.0 points; wrist cameras +18.3 to 28.3 <Cite id="tactile-vs-wrist-camera-2026" /> |
| Tactile cues study (2026) | 720 real trials | 57.8% without touch, 78.9% with it <Cite id="tactile-cues-study-2026" /> |
| Sparsh-X (2025) | About a million Digit 360 contact interactions | +63% over tactile images end to end, on plug insertion <Cite id="sparsh-x-2025" /> |
| LingBot-Touch (2026) | Robbyant, open source, three sensor types <Cite id="lingbot-touch-2026" /> | Four policies up 14 to 26 points, company-reported <Cite id="lingbot-touch-release-2026" /> |
| TouchWorld (2026) | Tactile world model, six long-horizon tasks | 65%, 15.7 points over the best baseline <Cite id="touchworld-2026" /> |
| T-Rex (2026) | Touch-reading VLA, 12 delicate-force tasks | Over 30% above the best baseline <Cite id="t-rex-2026" /> |
| TacDyn-WAM (2026) | Predicts tactile features, five real tasks | 71.0%, 16 points above FTP-1 <Cite id="tacdyn-wam-2026" /> |

<Image id="t-rex-bimanual-tactile-setup-2026" />

Touch does not finish the job. Gemini Robotics 2 drives the tactile 22-degree-of-freedom SharpaWave hand <Cite id="robozaps-unitree-h2-2026" />, unscrewing a light bulb 92% of the time but scoring 32 to 44% on its other multi-finger tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<span id="five-hands-five-bets" />
## Every hand trades articulation, touch and price differently

Tesla's 22-<Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degree-of-freedom</Term> tendon hand has the most articulation. Of the V3 design, Musk said "this one didn't actually work" <Cite id="teslarati-optimus-hand-2026" /> <Cite id="droids-optimus-v3-hand-2026" />, and he tied outside Optimus deliveries around the end of 2027 to solving it <Cite id="tesla-q3-2026-call" />. Sanctuary has the most sensitive disclosed touch and a working in-hand demo, and now sells software instead <Cite id="robozaps-phoenix-2026" />.

Shadow's Dexterous Hand, the research benchmark for two decades, runs more than 100 sensors at up to 1 kHz <Cite id="shadow-dexterous-hand-2026" /> and cost €110,000 with support in 2022 <Cite id="shadow-hand-cost-2022" />. Unitree's H2 lists at \$29,900 with placeholder hands, and at \$100,000 with tactile Sharpa Wave hands <Cite id="robozaps-unitree-h2-2026" />.

Boston Dynamics built its new Atlas hand, with 13 actuated degrees of freedom and touch in fingertips and palm, to simulate accurately for reinforcement learning <Cite id="bd-atlas-hand-2026" />. At ETH Zurich a 738 g WUJI hand became the whole robot, crawling on its fingers <Cite id="fingers-as-legs-2026" />.

Also: Figure 02's 16-degree-of-freedom hands <Cite id="figure-02-2024" />.

<span id="in-hand-manipulation" />
## In-hand manipulation separates a hand from a gripper

<Term id="in-hand-manipulation">In-hand manipulation</Term> means reorienting a held object without setting it down. Holson's "Use a key" event drops a keyring into the robot's hand, which must insert and turn the right key <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. Physical Intelligence won at about five times human time by passing the keys between two grippers <Cite id="holson-pi-deep-dive-2026" />.

<ExplainerFigure id="hand" />

Most progress comes one task at a time. Sanctuary's 21-degree-of-freedom hydraulic hands reorient objects <Cite id="sanctuary-inhand-2024" />, even with a 500 g weight added <Cite id="sanctuary-hydraulic-rl-2025" />. Figure's Helix 02 unscrews bottle caps and pushes exactly 5 ml from a syringe, with no published success rates <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Research systems push precision and breadth:

| System | Task | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FINGR (2026) | Rubik's Cube layer turns, one real hand | 99.0% of 300 turns, against 79.7% for its base policy <Cite id="fingr-2026" /> |
| CHORD (2026) | Two-handed tasks matched by object forces | 82.12% on 1,831 simulated tasks, then real <Cite id="chord-2026" /> |
| EigenDEXplore (2026) | Human hand patterns guide exploration | Beat joint-space and learned baselines across hands <Cite id="eigendexplore-2026" /> |

<Image id="fingr-cube-layer-turn-2026" />

Where to put the fingers is its own problem. Dexonomy refines one human template per grasp type in simulation <Cite id="dexonomy-2025" />, and Chen and colleagues synthesize grasps for task forces such as turning knobs <Cite id="task-wrench-boundary-2023" />.

<span id="deformables-and-the-long-tail" />
## Soft objects have endless states

A block rests on one of six sides; a towel bunches in more ways than any estimator can list. Holson's tasks include hanging a shirt with one sleeve inside out and wiping peanut butter off the robot's own hand <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />.

Cloth methods first cut the disorder or change the forces available:

| Method | Move |
| --- | --- |
| FlingBot (2021) | Flings cloth flat <Cite id="flingbot-2021" /> |
| SpeedFolding (2022) | Smooths, then folds on chosen lines with two arms <Cite id="speedfolding-2022" /> |
| Cloth Funnels (2022) | Flings or places garments into a standard shape <Cite id="cloth-funnels-2022" /> |
| DextAIRity (2022) | Adds directed airflow to grasping <Cite id="dextairity-2022" /> |
| RoboCook (2023) | Graph networks model tools on soft objects <Cite id="robocook-2023" /> |

RL-100 ran a juicing robot in a mall for about seven hours without failure <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />, and TRACE traces about 90% of tangled cable length correctly, against 60% before <Cite id="trace-cables-2026" />.

Also: π0.7 on laundry and espresso <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

On Holson's Olympics, Physical Intelligence's π0.6-based model took gold in three of five categories, at 72% task progress against 9% for a vision-language baseline <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

<span id="what-solved-would-look-like" />
## What solved would look like

Dexterity is solved when one policy does Holson's fifteen tasks in unseen homes within ten times his own time, with no per-task data and published success rates <Cite id="holson-olympics-2025" />. Per-task medals came fast: nearly all his first challenges fell within three months, so he set harder ones such as separating an egg yolk <Cite id="holson-olympics-part2-2026" />. If Tesla or Figure reach household dexterity from video, the tactile gap was a passing hardware shortage <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" /> <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.
