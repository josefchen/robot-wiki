The bear case argues robotics repeats earlier AI winters: capital arrives on demonstrations, deployment evidence lags, and funding runs out before the reliability gap closes. Robotics startups raised \$18.8B by late June 2026 per Crunchbase, past 2025's record \$15B <Cite id="crunchbase-robotics-funding-2026" />.

<Image id="asimo-miraikan-2016" />

Eight milestones would settle the argument. Switch "Show" to "Done" to empty the board, or to "Partly" to leave five tiles. In one, RL-100 and π*0.6 report some of the highest published per-task reliabilities <Cite id="rl-100-2025" /> <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

<span id="the-skeptic-record" />
## Brooks calls the dexterity forecasts fantasy, and Morgan Stanley asks for ROI

Evidence: Rodney Brooks, co-founder of iRobot and Rethink Robotics, calls expecting practical humanoid dexterity from current methods within decades "pure fantasy thinking", since vision-only training gives no force channel ([dexterity](/frontier/dexterity/)) <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. His January 2026 scorecard predicts deployable dexterity "will remain pathetic compared to human hands" beyond 2036, and walking humanoids too unsafe near people without new mechanical systems <Cite id="brooks-scorecard-2026" />. Rethink raised \$150 million and closed in 2018 when sales fell short <Cite id="rethink-shutdown-2018" />.

<Image id="roomba-870-2014" />

Evidence: Morgan Stanley's July 2026 note says humanoids have a "PR problem": they are framed as worker substitutes instead of tools for hazardous or labor-constrained tasks. It adds "the industry's social license to deploy may matter just as much as technical performance". Investors "are increasingly looking for tangible evidence of real-world return on investment". It says the US ban on Chinese humanoid imports risks raising R&D costs, retaining a 50,000-unit China shipment estimate for 2026 <Cite id="morgan-stanley-pr-problem-2026" />.

<span id="unit-economics" />
## Unitree's Q1 2026 adjusted profit fell 52.55% year over year

Evidence: Unitree's Q1 2026 adjusted net profit fell 52.55% year over year, per its STAR Market prospectus filed in May 2026 <Cite id="unitree-prospectus-2026" />.

Evidence: Bessemer expects industry data costs above \$3B over the next two years and calls capital the moat <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" /> ([industrial deployment](/data-hardware/industrial-deployment/)).

Evidence: at Computex 2026, a Qualcomm-powered humanoid collapsed face-first during the live keynote, attributed to a communication glitch <Cite id="computex-collapse-2026" />.

<span id="the-demo-to-deployment-gap" />
## Vendor hours and shipment counts do not document productive work

The bear case: closing the demo-to-product [reliability gap](/frontier/reliability-gap/) outlasts funding. Lisa Yan, quoted in Bessemer's analysis, describes the climb from 99% to 99.9% as steep <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

Agility reports 65,000 hours of Digit production experience on an undated homepage <Cite id="agility-digit-production" />; Figure reports 1,250+ hours at BMW Spartanburg by November 2025 <Cite id="figure-bmw-production-2025" />; neither counts autonomous units or supervised time ([evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)). Omdia counted about 13,000 humanoids shipped in 2025 <Cite id="agibot-omdia-ranking-2026" /> <Cite id="agibot-omdia-2026" /> and SAG 19.1K in 2026's first half <Cite id="sag-humanoid-shipments-2026" />. The IFR counted about 7,000 full-size humanoids above 140 cm sold in 2025 <Cite id="ifr-service-robots-2026" />. Tesla's Q2 2026 update reports ongoing installation of its first Optimus lines, whose initial builds serve an "Optimus Academy for training data collection" <Cite id="tesla-q2-2026-update" />.

<span id="the-capital-cycle" />
## Robotics funding passed 23 billion dollars by June 2026, and China repriced first

Evidence: beyond Crunchbase's narrower venture count <Cite id="crunchbase-robotics-funding-2026" />, a newsletter put funding above \$23B by early June 2026 by PitchBook's tally <Cite id="robotics-funding-23b-2026" />. Bessemer Venture Partners argues there is "no robotics bubble" and the sector is "structurally underinvested", but concedes "some valuations are stretched" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />. Skepticism rests on Brooks's timeline predictions and Morgan Stanley's ROI evidence demand: Brooks calls some humanoid hype "downright delusional" <Cite id="brooks-scorecard-2026" />, while Morgan Stanley makes no bubble call <Cite id="morgan-stanley-pr-problem-2026" /> ([competing theses](/frontier/competing-theses/)).

Evidence: on September 21, 2026, Reuters reported regulators were slowing humanoid listings after Unitree's shares rose more than fivefold on debut, then fell 55% from their peak, and quoted a venture capitalist on some private-market projects' 30% to 50% valuation cuts <Cite id="reuters-china-humanoid-ipo-2026" />.

<span id="how-a-winter-would-happen" />
## Three hypothetical failures could start a winter

The three mechanisms are hypothetical scenarios. Their precedent, the 1973 Lighthill report, found robot-building progress "even more disappointing" than in the rest of AI <Cite id="lighthill-1973" />.

**Speculation:** the reliability gap outlasts investor patience. If 99.9% takes five more years of per-task engineering, the 2026 fund vintage marks to market against pilots. Bessemer says the last stretch "takes longer than most timelines assume" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />.

**Speculation:** the data thesis plateaus. If 10x more data stops buying <Term id="success-rate">success-rate</Term> gains, new science is needed, like tactile integration, contact-faithful simulation or world models ([data bottleneck](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck/)). Ken Goldberg's 100,000-year data gap is the timing version: he accepts data-driven methods "will eventually produce general purpose robots", asking when <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />.

**Speculation:** the form factor is wrong. If task-specific systems keep winning cost per task, the humanoid thesis underlying most capital collapses, as Brooks's "pure fantasy thinking" line predicts <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

<span id="the-watchlist" />
## As of September 2026, none of the eight milestones is met

The board reads three not met, five partial, zero met. In unseen homes, π0.5 cleaned kitchens and bedrooms <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, and in September 2026 Figure reported 56% zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes, against 9% without Index pretraining <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />. Generalist reports 99% average success versus earlier models' 64%, though "not all tasks" reach those rates <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. RoboArena, RoboChallenge and ManipulationNet build shared evaluation infrastructure <Cite id="roboarena-2025" /> <Cite id="robochallenge-2025" /> <Cite id="manipulationnet-2026" />.

Figure's Helix 02 takes fingertip tactile input <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />; T-Rex beats its strongest baseline by over 30%, though most <Term id="vision-language-action-model">VLA</Term> models still "overlook the tactile modality" <Cite id="t-rex-2026" />. Sparsh-X and TouchWorld are tactile foundation models <Cite id="sparsh-x-2025" /> <Cite id="touchworld-2026" />. Surveys name [contact modeling](/rl-sim2real/sim2real-transfer/) a key contributor to the <Term id="sim-to-real-gap">sim-to-real</Term> gap, "particularly problematic in contact-rich tasks like robotic manipulation" <Cite id="reality-gap-survey-2026" />.

<span id="what-would-prove-the-bears-wrong" />
## Four measurable results would falsify the bear case

<BearCaseLock className="my-6" />

A verified deployment of 10,000 humanoids doing documented work breaks the commercial-viability premise, currently measured in vendor-recorded hours <Cite id="agility-digit-production" /> <Cite id="figure-bmw-production-2025" />, and one policy above 90% across more than ten unseen homes breaks the generalization premise <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" /> ([generalization](/frontier/generalization/)). Cost-per-task parity breaks the form-factor premise <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />.

A real-world success-rate scaling law across tasks breaks the plateau premise: EgoScale's <Term id="scaling-law">scaling law</Term> is fit to validation loss <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />; Lin et al. find a "roughly power-law relationship" for single-task generalization <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />. Until then, over \$23B was raised by early June against that scoreboard.
