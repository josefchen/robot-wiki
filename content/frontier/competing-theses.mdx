Competing theses are the rival bets on how to build general robot intelligence, from scaling one model to building humanoids. The bets are expensive: Figure committed \$3.5B of compute to training Helix <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

<span id="the-six-theses" />
## The theses can coexist, and π0.7 already combines several

π0.7 is an end-to-end <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action model</Term> that reasons in steps, can take subgoal images from a lightweight <Term id="world-model">world model</Term>, and absorbs experience from π*0.6's RL-trained specialists <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

<ThesisExplorer className="my-6 wide-measure" />

<span id="end-to-end-vla-scaling" />
## Scaling has early laws, mostly on stand-in measures

The scaling thesis: grow data and model size, and general intelligence follows, as it did for language. Rich Sutton's The Bitter Lesson frames it: general methods that use computation ultimately win <Cite id="sutton-bitter-lesson-2019" />. Physical Intelligence staked π0 through π0.7 on that bet <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, and Bessemer says "scaling laws are emerging" <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" /> ([VLA models](/manipulation/vla-models/)).

| Result | Scaling evidence |
| --- | --- |
| EgoScale | Validation loss falls on a <Term id="scaling-law">log-linear line</Term> (R² = 0.9983) with human-video hours; the full recipe lifts success 54% on a 22-degree-of-freedom hand <Cite id="egoscale-2026" /> |
| GR00T 2 | With robot data fixed, its full human-video set lifts real success from 18% to 41%, with no leveling off <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| Figure Index pretraining | Zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56% <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" /> |
| Lin et al. (2024) | Single-task success follows "a roughly power-law relationship with the number of environments and objects" <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" /> |

## Brooks and Goldberg cite engineered priors and a 100,000-year data gap

Rodney Brooks replied six days later that learning leans on human-designed structure, such as convolution, which finds an object wherever it sits <Cite id="brooks-better-lesson-2019" />. Video gives robots no sense of force ([dexterity](/frontier/dexterity/)) <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />, and current machine learning "may run out of steam" <Cite id="brooks-does-until-2026" />.

Ken Goldberg would bootstrap data with old-fashioned engineering <Cite id="goldberg-data-gap-2025" />. Karcini and co-authors say scaling misses the interfaces that turn physical data into supervision <Cite id="karcini-position-2026" />.

The thesis fails if real-world success plateaus despite ten times more data and no change of architecture. So far the laws govern a proxy loss, a single task or a fixed task set ([generalization](/frontier/generalization/)).

<span id="a-hierarchical-planner-over-skills" />
## Hierarchical systems run long tasks, while single networks blur the line

The hierarchy thesis splits thinking from moving: a vision-language model plans the steps and faster learned skills execute them. SayCan picked from a fixed skill library <Cite id="saycan-2022" />; today the split sits inside one system ([hierarchical approaches](/manipulation/hierarchical/)). Gemini Robotics ER 2 plans for a lower-level VLA <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er2-2026" />; Helix 02 stacks three layers down to a <Term id="whole-body-control">whole-body controller</Term> <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

<HelixClockwork className="my-6" />

Helix 02 ran a dishwasher for four minutes without resets or human help <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />, and Gemini Robotics 2.5 kitted parts on Apptronik humanoids at 96% success in a Mercedes-Benz proof of concept <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />. Against it, π0.7 combines skills in new ways from one network <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, and Gemini Robotics 1.5 "interleaves actions with a multi-level internal reasoning process" inside one VLA <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />.

The thesis fails if a flat model with no planning pass beats the best hierarchical system on long tasks.

<span id="world-model-based-training" />
## World models plan from under 62 hours of robot video but still miss contact

The world-model thesis: learn [how the world changes](/world-models/taxonomy/), then plan or train the policy inside it. NVIDIA's Jim Fan argues [world action models](/world-models/generative-video/), which predict video and actions together, will replace VLAs <Cite id="fan-robotics-end-game-2026" />.

| Model | Result |
| --- | --- |
| GR00T 2 | NVIDIA's world action model, released November 2026 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| DreamZero | Over 2x better generalization to new tasks and environments than leading VLAs <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" /> |
| V-JEPA 2 | Zero-shot pick-and-place in unseen labs after under 62 hours of unlabeled robot video <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" /> |
| Hierarchical world model | Real pick-and-place from a goal image 70% of the time, against 0% single-level <Cite id="hwm-2026" /> |
| RoboJEPA | Prediction error follows a power law in compute, across 12 embodiments <Cite id="robojepa-2026" /> |

The gap is contact. Cosmos 3 trained on up to 31 trillion tokens <span className="block" data-cosmos-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />,</span> yet no world model predicts contact well enough for manipulation, where a millimeter turns a grip into a slip. It fails if world-model training cannot beat VLA scaling alone on real contact-rich tasks.

<span id="rl-fine-tuning-on-imitation" />
## RL fine-tuning reaches 90% to 100% per task, but each result is a specialist

The RL thesis: pretrain on demonstrations, then close the reliability gap with reinforcement learning on the real robot ([RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/)).

| Method | Result |
| --- | --- |
| HIL-SERL | Near-perfect success after 1 to 2.5 hours of training <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" /> |
| π*0.6 with Recap | Throughput more than doubled; 90%+ on every task but diverse laundry <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> |
| RL-100 | 100% across 1,000 evaluation episodes on eight tasks <Cite id="rl-100-2025" /> |
| ENPIRE | Coding agents run the improvement loop; 99% on dexterous tasks <Cite id="enpire-2026" /> |
| 96 robots pooling executions | Phone packaging from 79.2% to 98.3% <Cite id="collective-experience-learning-2026" /> |

Neither π*0.6 nor RL-100 claims transfer to unseen tasks <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />. One π0.7 model that absorbed Recap experience matches the specialists on laundry, espresso and box building <Cite id="pi07-2026" /> <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. RL becomes a niche tool if specialists keep needing per-task retraining.

<span id="teleoperation-as-a-bridge" />
## Teleoperation feeds deployment data, but its share of vendor hours is unreported

The bridge thesis: put <Term id="teleoperation">teleoperated</Term> or supervised robots to work now, harvest the data, and fade people out.

| Company | How people stay in the loop |
| --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Humanoids deployed under human supervision <Cite id="nucleus-supervised-2026" /> |
| 1X | NEO owners book an expert for chores the robot does not know, "helping NEO learn" <Cite id="1x-neo-2026" /> |
| Physical Intelligence | API beta built on customers' own demonstrations <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />; Recap learns from teleoperated interventions <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> |

Neither Agility's 65,000+ Digit hours <Cite id="agility-nasdaq-listing-2026" /> nor Figure's 1,250+ hours at BMW <Cite id="figure-bmw-production-2025" /> is broken down by teleoperated share ([teleoperation rigs](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/)).

Opponents say teleoperation cannot scale: "you'll never get the scale or diversity you need from teleop alone," Zeromatter's Ian Glow tells Bessemer <Cite id="bessemer-robotics-2026" />. Jim Fan expects <Term id="egocentric-video">egocentric human video</Term>, which already has a scaling law, to replace it <Cite id="fan-robotics-end-game-2026" /> <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

Also: Sergey Levine on surrogate data as "a lousy spoon with holes" <Cite id="levine-sporks-2025" />.

Generalist reports base models "trained without any robot data" that reach 99% average success with "only 1 hour of robot data" per task <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. The bridge thesis fails if autonomous policies pass 99% success trained without any teleoperation data.

<span id="humanoid-versus-task-specific" />
## Cost per task decides between humanoid and task-specific robots

Figure, Tesla, 1X, Apptronik and Unitree bet a human-shaped body fits a world built for humans. Rodney Brooks bets purpose-built machines win on cost and reliability for any prespecified task <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />, and Amazon has deployed its millionth robot <Cite id="amazon-million-robots-2025" />. Agility sits between: Digit does narrow logistics work <Cite id="agility-digit-production" />, backed by over \$300 million in committed Digit 5 orders <Cite id="agility-nasdaq-listing-2026" />.

<Image id="amazon-drive-unit-2023" />

Gemini Robotics 2 controls a full humanoid from feet to fingertips with one model <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Musk expects outside Optimus deliveries around the end of 2027 <Cite id="tesla-q3-2026-call" />, and after a small test BMW told Forbes no Figure robots were working at its plant <Cite id="forbes-adcock-figure-2026" />.

Brooks argues articulated fingers lack the robustness, force and lifetime for industry, and forecasts task-specialized robots still called humanoids for fifteen years <Cite id="brooks-dexterity-2025" />. Both kill conditions measure cost per task. If task-specific robots keep winning the same applications, the humanoid thesis dies; if a humanoid reaches parity across many tasks, the task-specific thesis does.

<span id="what-would-settle-it" />
## Six observations would settle the theses, and GEN-1 nearly meets one

The observations run from [real-world success](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/) flat after a tenfold data increase to humanoid cost-per-task parity. GEN-1 nearly meets the teleoperation test but still uses 1 hour of robot data per task <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />; [the bear case](/frontier/bear-case/) tracks all six.
