<Term id="cross-embodiment">Cross-embodiment transfer</Term> is training one policy on data from bodies with different joint counts, action dimensions and sensors, or moving a policy to an unseen body. Open X-Embodiment pools data from 22 robot embodiments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />, and human video adds a body whose actions are estimated from images <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. ## Counting robot and human data

π0's 10,000 hours are its own data from 7 robot configurations and 68 tasks, added to OXE, DROID and Bridge. By timesteps, open-source data is 9.1% of the mixture, against 903 million steps of π0's own <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. RT-X experiments used 9 of OXE's 22 embodiments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. GR00T N1.7's rounded 20K pretraining hours are EgoScale human video <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

Moritz Reuss wrote in October 2025 that "OXE is mostly low-quality data" and that imitation learning lacks good ways to measure [data quality](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck/) <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />. Re-Mix's learned mixture weights beat uniform weights by 38% on average and human-selected ones by 32% <Cite id="remix-2024" />. Toyota Research Institute's controlled study of 89 policies found that co-training with cross-embodiment robot data improves generalization to distribution shifts and unseen tasks <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" />.

## Robot action interfaces

ACT's ALOHA action is a 14-dimensional absolute-joint vector for two 6-DoF ViperX arms with grippers, which the paper's observation description counts as "7+7=14 DoF" <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. π0 zero-pads every action vector to the largest robot's 18 dimensions <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

Octo curates 25 <Term id="open-x-embodiment">OXE</Term> datasets with delta <Term id="end-effector">end-effector</Term> control, zero-pads missing camera channels and aligns grippers to +1 open, 0 closed; new spaces get lightweight encoders or heads on the kept transformer <Cite id="octo-2024" />. CrossFormer trains one transformer on 900K trajectories from 20 embodiments without manually aligning observation or action spaces <Cite id="crossformer-2024" />.

HPT maps each embodiment's proprioception and vision to a few tokens for a shared trunk, scaling to 52 datasets <Cite id="hpt-2024" />, and X-VLA learns one soft-prompt embedding set per data source as an embodiment prompt <Cite id="x-vla-2025" />. LAP writes low-level actions as natural language; LAP-3B reports over 50% average zero-shot success on unseen robots, about twice the strongest prior VLAs <Cite id="lap-2026" />.

## Motion Transfer across robot embodiments

Gemini Robotics 1.5 trains on ALOHA, bi-arm Franka and Apptronik Apollo data plus Internet text, images and video. Its report presents Motion Transfer as an architecture and training recipe that aligns embodiments and extracts shared knowledge. Ablations against variants without it, trained on one or all embodiments, run interleaved on one work cell and report mean progress with standard error <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />.

On tasks demonstrated only on another robot, Motion Transfer helps, less so for Apollo. One checkpoint drives all three robots, though the long-horizon agent adds bi-arm Franka post-training. The report specifies continuous actions and an orchestrator over the <Term id="vision-language-action-model">VLA</Term>, but not the alignment representation. It says the architecture can already learn from unlabeled human and synthetic video and leaves that to future work <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. DeepMind says tasks shown only to ALOHA 2 "just work" on Apollo and bi-arm Franka, and vice versa <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-blog-2025" />.

The June 2025 On-Device model, trained only on ALOHA, was adapted to a bi-arm Franka FR3 and Apollo <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />. DeepMind says On-Device 2, natively multi-embodiment with Motion Transfer, adapts to new bi-arm robots in a few hours, typically from fewer than 200 examples, shown on Dexmate, SO101 and Trossen <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

## Relative end-effector actions for robots and humans

GR00T N1 embedded state and action with embodiment-aware encoders <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. In December 2025, N1.6 moved most embodiments to state-relative action chunks, which NVIDIA found smoother and more accurate than absolute actions <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />. N1.7 shares relative end-effector deltas between human and robot embodiments, calls this a key factor in cross-embodiment performance and widens state and action dimensions from 29 to 132 <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

EgoScale uses relative wrist motion and <Term id="retargeting">retargets</Term> 21 human hand keypoints to the 22-DoF Sharpa hand; arms take relative end-effector actions and hands target joint angles. Embodiment-conditioned MLP adapters encode <Term id="proprioception">proprioception</Term> and decode hand actions, while wrist prediction, backbone and action expert are shared <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

Morphometric Imitation (Tara Sadjadpour, Jitendra Malik and colleagues, September 2026) retargets reconstructed hand-object interactions across [hand morphologies](/frontier/dexterity/), keeping demonstrated contacts, then refines them with residual RL and distills visuomotor policies. On a Sharpa hand these reached 89.3% zero-shot success in 300 real trials on 30 objects in 10 categories <Cite id="morphometric-imitation-2026" />.

## Closing the visual embodiment gap

Mirage masks out an unseen target robot and inpaints the source robot in real time <Cite id="mirage-2024" />. RoVi-Aug synthesizes training images with other robots and viewpoints and deploys zero-shot on an unseen robot; co-training with it raises success by up to 30% <Cite id="rovi-aug-2024" />. Shadow replaces the robot with a source-and-target mask, reporting over twice the strongest baseline's success <Cite id="shadow-2025" />.

## Latent actions as a shared interface

LAPA learns discrete latent actions between video frames with a VQ-VAE objective, pretrains a VLA on them and fine-tunes on robot data; training only on human video still transfers positively <Cite id="lapa-2025" />.

## Human-to-robot transfer recipes

Simar Kareer's EgoMimic treated human and robot data equally and found an extra hour of hand data worth more than one of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />. On December 16, 2025, Kareer and Physical Intelligence colleagues co-fine-tuned pretrained π0.5 on human data and the most relevant robot data. Humans were one more embodiment, with 3D hand positions as actions and no special transfer method. PI reports about 2× performance on four scenarios present only in human data, bussing, spice, dresser and eggs, without trial counts <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

A t-SNE of mean-pooled final-layer VLM tokens shows human and robot features overlapping more as robot pretraining diversifies, which correlates with generalization; the human fine-tuning data stays fixed <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

EgoScale's Ruijie Zheng and coauthors instead pretrain on human video, mid-train on aligned human-robot data and post-train on task robot data. They write that transfer needs scale, explicit motion supervision and a small amount of precise human-robot alignment. Their unaligned human pretraining beats a mid-training-only baseline on most tasks, and adding aligned mid-training is best overall <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

EgoBridge aligns human and robot policy latents with optimal transport and reports a 44% absolute success gain over human-augmented cross-embodiment baselines <Cite id="egobridge-2025" />. H-RDT pretrains on egocentric human data and fine-tunes with per-robot action encoders and decoders, gaining 40.5% over training from scratch on real robots <Cite id="h-rdt-2025" />.

Phantom needs no robot data: it inpaints the human arm, overlays a rendered robot and deploys zero-shot, with success up to 92% <Cite id="phantom-2025" />. Masquerade robotizes in-the-wild human video, fine-tunes on 50 [robot demonstrations](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/) per task and beats baselines by 5 to 6 times <Cite id="masquerade-2026" />. Figure reports that Index human-video pretraining, all else fixed, raised Helix 2.5's zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56% <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />. Generalist's GEN-1 base model (April 2026) uses no robot data: it pretrains on over half a million hours from wearable devices on humans, then adapts with about an hour of robot data per task <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

## Scaling the number of embodiments

Across about 1,000 procedurally generated locomotion bodies, adding embodiments generalizes more broadly than adding data on fixed ones <Cite id="embodiment-scaling-2025" />. Skild reports training one model on 100,000 simulated robots and testing zero-shot on robots excluded from training <Cite id="skild-omni-bodied-2025" />. DreamZero adapted from AgiBot G1 to a YAM bimanual robot with 55 trajectories, about 30 minutes of play data; its authors describe the result qualitatively <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />.

GOTT, posted on October 2, 2026 by Pieter Abbeel, Haozhi Qi and colleagues at Amazon FAR, UC Berkeley, UC San Diego and the University of Chicago, trains one closed-loop contact primitive with RL across XHand, Sharpa and Allegro hands. On four Franka-Sharpa tasks it averaged 80.0% task success against 35.0% for open-loop grasp baselines. On unseen simulated Allegro variants it matched per-variant oracle policies, 72.1% against 72.2% <Cite id="gott-2026" />.
