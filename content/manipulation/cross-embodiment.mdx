<Term id="cross-embodiment">Cross-embodiment transfer</Term> trains one policy on data from bodies with different joints, controls and cameras, or moves it to an unseen body. No robot has enough data alone: Open X-Embodiment pools 22 robot types <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />, and human video adds a body whose actions must be estimated <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

## Human video now outweighs robot data

GR00T 2's pretraining mix <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />:

| Source | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| First-person human video | 402,730, of which 100,000 are open <Cite id="human-1m-100k-2026" /> |
| Robot teleoperation | 23,500 |
| Handheld UMI gripper <Cite id="umi-2024" /> | 10,184 |

Open data, including DROID <Cite id="droid-2024" /> and Bridge <Cite id="bridgedata-v2-2023" />, adds 9.1% by timesteps to π0's 10,000 robot hours <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

[Data quality](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck/) varies: Moritz Reuss judged in October 2025 that "OXE is mostly low-quality data" <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />, and Re-Mix's learned mixture weights beat uniform ones by 38% on average <Cite id="remix-2024" />. In TRI's study of 89 policies, co-training across robots improved generalization to new conditions and tasks <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" />.

## One action format for many bodies

A gripper and a humanoid hand need different commands in one model. π0 pads every action to the largest robot's 18 dimensions <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

| Model | How it shares actions |
| --- | --- |
| Octo <Cite id="octo-2024" /> | One hand-motion format across 25 <Term id="open-x-embodiment">OXE</Term> datasets, small heads for new robots |
| CrossFormer <Cite id="crossformer-2024" /> | Trains on 900K unaligned trajectories from 20 embodiments |
| HPT <Cite id="hpt-2024" /> | Maps each robot's sensors to a few shared tokens, across 52 datasets |
| X-VLA <Cite id="x-vla-2025" /> | A learned prompt per data source tells the model which robot it is reading |
| LAP <Cite id="lap-2026" /> | Low-level actions as words: over 50% average zero-shot success on unseen robots, about twice the strongest prior <Term id="vision-language-action-model">VLAs</Term> |
| CIALLO <Cite id="ciallo-2026" /> | Adapts from one recording of 10 seconds or less; beat fully fine-tuned π0 on two real arms, 76.7% to 70.0%, with 20 demonstrations per task |

## Motion Transfer: one checkpoint, three robots

Gemini Robotics 1.5 trains one checkpoint on Internet data, ALOHA <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />, a two-armed Franka and Apptronik's Apollo humanoid. Its Motion Transfer recipe helps on tasks shown only on another robot, less so for Apollo; the report omits how bodies are aligned <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. DeepMind says tasks shown only to ALOHA 2 "just work" on the other two, and vice versa <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-blog-2025" />.

On-Device, trained only on ALOHA, was adapted to Franka and Apollo <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />; DeepMind says On-Device 2 adapts to new two-armed robots in hours from under 200 examples <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Gemini Robotics 2.5 reports 96% for Apollo 2 kitting parts at Mercedes-Benz <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />.

## Relative motion links robot and human hands

GR00T N1 used body-specific encoders <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />; N1.6 moved most robots to state-relative actions, smoother and more accurate per NVIDIA <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />. N1.7 shares relative <Term id="end-effector">end-effector</Term> motion between humans and robots and calls it key to cross-embodiment performance <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

EgoScale <Term id="retargeting">retargets</Term> 21 human hand keypoints to the 22-joint Sharpa hand <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. Morphometric Imitation retargets hand-object interactions across [hand shapes](/frontier/dexterity/), refines them with residual RL and reached 89.3% zero-shot success on a Sharpa hand over 300 real trials <Cite id="morphometric-imitation-2026" />.

## Image edits make robots look alike

Mirage masks the new robot and paints in the familiar one in real time <Cite id="mirage-2024" />. RoVi-Aug synthesizes other robots and viewpoints and adds up to 30% success when co-trained <Cite id="rovi-aug-2024" />; Shadow swaps the robot for a composite segmentation mask <Cite id="shadow-2025" />.

## Latent actions need no robot labels

A latent action is a learned code for the change between two video frames. LAPA pretrains a VLA on such codes before robot fine-tuning, and even human video alone transfers positively <Cite id="lapa-2025" />.

| Model | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Multi-view latent action model <Cite id="multiview-lam-2026" /> | A second camera was worth more than four extra embodiments; 20% gain on hardware from human-hand demonstrations, over 850 rollouts |
| Latent Action Diffusion <Cite id="latent-action-diffusion-2025" /> | Aligns human-hand, robot-hand and gripper actions |
| LAC-WM <Cite id="lac-wm-2026" /> | A world model on shared latent actions picks a VLA's chunks: 82.4% to 92.0% on five unseen LIBERO tasks <Cite id="libero-2023" /> |

## Recipes for learning from people

EgoMimic treated human and robot data alike and found an extra hour of hand data worth more than one of robot data <Cite id="egomimic-2024" />. Physical Intelligence fine-tuned π0.5 <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> on human data, with 3D hand positions as actions, plus relevant robot data. It reports about twice the performance on four human-only scenarios, without trial counts <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

<Image id="phantom-human-video-robot-2025" />

EgoScale finds that a middle stage on aligned human-robot data, between human-video pretraining and robot post-training, helps most <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

| Recipe | Result |
| --- | --- |
| H-RDT <Cite id="h-rdt-2025" /> | Adapts a human-video model through modular action layers |
| EgoBridge <Cite id="egobridge-2025" /> | Aligns human and robot representations: 44% absolute gain |
| Open-HumanWAM <Cite id="open-humanwam-2026" /> | 50,000 hours of first-person video; simulated success rises log-linearly with hours |
| EgoLAP <Cite id="egolap-2026" /> | Shared language-action chain for human and robot trajectories: 80.1% mean task progress on two-armed YAM robots |

## Human video alone can teach robots

| System | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Phantom <Cite id="phantom-2025" /> | No robot data: paints out the human arm, overlays a rendered robot, up to 92% zero-shot success |
| Masquerade <Cite id="masquerade-2026" /> | Everyday human video turned into robot video, plus 50 [robot demonstrations](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/) per task: 5 to 6 times the baselines |
| Helix 2.5 <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" /> | Index human-video pretraining lifted zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56%, per Figure |
| GEN-1 <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" /> | Pretrains on over half a million hours from wearables alone |

In GR00T 2 tests, a two-handed robot taught only by gloved humans lined a funnel with filter paper in 65% of trials. A YAM arm taught only with UMI or DexUMI <Cite id="dexumi-2025" /> devices sorted 86% of conveyor items, against 28% for GR00T 1.7 and 0% for π0.5 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" />.

## More bodies beat more data per body

Across about 1,000 generated walking bodies, adding embodiments generalized more broadly than adding data on fixed ones <Cite id="embodiment-scaling-2025" />. Skild reports a model trained on 100,000 simulated robots and tested zero-shot on new ones <Cite id="skild-omni-bodied-2025" />. Its Steel solver simulates mixed batches of robots and tasks at 13.0 times PhysX's peak throughput <Cite id="skild-steel-2026" />.
