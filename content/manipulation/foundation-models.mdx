A foundation model is a network trained on broad data at scale and then adapted to many tasks <Cite id="bommasani-foundation-models-2021" />. In robotics the label covers <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action models</Term>, which turn web knowledge into movement, and world models, which learn how scenes change. The test is whether one checkpoint carries over to new tasks, places or robots with little new data.

## Four things a claim must name

The promise is that learning once, from plentiful data, makes every later robot task cheaper. A claim should say what the model learned from, what it learned to predict, what it outputs and how much new data a new task needs. The data runs from web images and text to human video and robot recordings: Open X-Embodiment pools 22 kinds of robot from 21 institutions <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />, and NVIDIA's GR00T 2 adds 402,730 hours of first-person human video to 23,500 hours of robot teleoperation <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

## How much a new task costs

Models differ most here. RoboCat adapted to new tasks and robots from 100 to 1,000 examples <Cite id="robocat-2023" />, Gemini Robotics learned new short tasks from 100 demonstrations <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, and Octo fine-tunes to new sensors in hours on consumer GPUs <Cite id="octo-2024" />. GR00T 2 succeeds on 40% of trials on 50 tasks with no demonstrations on the target robot, and 95% with five <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

Some learn from one example at run time, without changing their weights. Skild AI's S1, shown one video of an unseen long task, succeeded 66% of the time, about as well as fine-tuning on 380 demonstrations <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />. GEN-1.5 learns from one 3 to 12 second demonstration <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />, and a retrieved video raised a frozen DreamZero policy from 47.0% to 56.3% on three simulated DROID <Cite id="droid-2024" /> tasks <Cite id="robota-embed-2026" />. These figures are mostly the developers' own. Practice helps too: π*0.6's reinforcement learning from experience more than doubles throughput on some of the hardest tasks <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />, and Physical Intelligence's API beta offers hosted fine-tuning <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />.

## Vision-language-action models start from web knowledge

RT-1 argued for one large model trained on diverse robot data <Cite id="rt1-2022" />; RT-2 trained a vision-language model on robot and web data together, writing actions as reserved words <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. OpenVLA opened a comparable recipe <Cite id="openvla-2024" />, and Gemini Robotics builds on Gemini 2.0 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. π0 adds an action expert that outputs continuous motion <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, π0.5 credits its generalization to training on many kinds of task <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, and π0.7 is billed as steerable <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Gemini Robotics 2.5 reports 30 to 100% higher success than earlier baselines across 200 tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />, and Kyutai's open REKI uses only public data <Cite id="reki-2026" />.

New setups still need tuning: OpenVLA-OFT's recipe lifted OpenVLA's average on four simulated task suites from 76.5% to 97.1% <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

## Memory lets a policy use the past

A policy that sees only the current view fails tasks that depend on something seen earlier, so several 2026 models add memory. T²Mem stores the past in weights updated while the policy runs, learned from actions alone <Cite id="t2mem-2026" />. RoboTTT stretches this to 8K timesteps and scored 79% task completion on long real tasks, against 42% for GR00T N1.7 <Cite id="robottt-2026" />. Stanford's ECoMEM turns a written record of task facts into memory, reaching 86.1% on two new real tasks against 8.6% without it <Cite id="ecomem-2026" />. IMPRINT (Amazon FAR and Carnegie Mellon) learns a summary of past frames and actions; on nine simulated memory tasks, 100 trials each, it raised π0.5 from 25.4% to 72.8%, against 67.8% for the best of six rivals <Cite id="imprint-2026" />.

## Humanoids: the body limits what transfers

A humanoid's shape, balance and action format limit what it can reuse from other robots. GR00T N1 pairs a vision-language module with a diffusion model that generates motion <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. WholeBodyVLA learns from first-person video without action labels <Cite id="wholebodyvla-2025" />, and LeVERB's policy sends learned commands to a separate whole-body controller <Cite id="leverb-2025" />.

Human data can replace robot pretraining. Figure trained [Helix 2.5](/manipulation/generalist-policies/) from random weights on Index, its dataset of human behavior; with identical task data it succeeded on 56% of zero-shot trials in 30 unseen homes, against 9% from scratch <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />. DeepMind reports Gemini Robotics 2.5 driving Apptronik's Apollo 2 to 96% success at kitting parts in a Mercedes-Benz pilot <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />.

## World models learn how scenes change

World models learn to predict what happens next. V-JEPA 2 learns from over 1M hours of internet video, then the effect of actions from under 62 hours of DROID robot video <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. R3M's image features, pretrained on Ego4D <Cite id="ego4d-2022" /> human video, beat training from scratch by over 20% <Cite id="r3m-2022" />. Dreamer 4, trained inside such a model, was the first agent to obtain diamonds in Minecraft purely from recorded data <Cite id="dreamer4-2025" />.

<Image id="cosmos-policy-aloha-2026" />

NVIDIA's Cosmos is a general world model that users fine-tune <Cite id="cosmos-predict-2025" />; Cosmos 3 also outputs actions <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />, and Cosmos Policy reaches 98.5% on LIBERO <Cite id="cosmos-policy-2026" />. Ctrl-World uses one to rank policies without real rollouts <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />.

## Predicting video and actions together

A [world action model](/world-models/taxonomy/) predicts future video and the actions that produce it in one network. DreamZero (February 2026) more than doubled average task progress over leading VLAs on new tasks and environments in real-robot tests, running at 7 Hz with 14 billion parameters <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. NVIDIA based GR00T N2 on that research <Cite id="nvidia-gtc-gr00t-n2-2026" />, and GR00T 2 generates video and actions in one transformer <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" />. Dyna-2, pretrained only on human video, reports a scaling law that carries to unseen robots <Cite id="dyna-2-2026" />. Runway's open Praxis-1 reports 46.2% on RoboLab-120 <Cite id="robolab-2026" /> against 28.0% for π0.5 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" />.

## Pretraining saves data but does not remove the need

In Toyota Research Institute's blind, randomized trials, pretrained policies matched from-scratch ones with 3 to 5 times less data <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Generalist reports that GEN-1, pretrained on over half a million hours of human data from wearables, averaged 99% on three tasks with about an hour of robot data each, against 19% from scratch <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

The gains are uneven. VC-1 found no visual representation best everywhere <Cite id="vc1-2023" />. Robot-only training degrades the backbone's visual-language understanding <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" />, and a naively trained new action expert slows training and weakens transfer <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" /> ([knowledge insulation](/manipulation/knowledge-insulation/)). Safety stays with the builder: RoboPAIR jailbroke language-model-controlled robots, including a deployed Unitree Go2, often at 100% attack success <Cite id="robopair-2024" />.

## Testing a foundation claim

A convincing claim varies tasks, places, robots and adaptation data, against a from-scratch baseline with the same budget. Single scores hide brittleness: VLAs near 95% on LIBERO drop below 30% under modest changes of camera or starting positions <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />, and models above 90% fall to 0.0% under LIBERO-PRO's generalized setting <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" />.
