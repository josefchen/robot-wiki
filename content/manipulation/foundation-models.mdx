A foundation model is a network trained on broad data at scale and adaptable to many downstream tasks <Cite id="bommasani-foundation-models-2021" />. In robotics, the test is whether one checkpoint transfers to new tasks, environments or embodiments after bounded adaptation. Three families carry the label: <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action models</Term>, predictive world models, and multimodal backbones adapted into either. ## What must be specified

A claim names its pretraining data, objective, interface and adaptation budget. Data spans web text and images, human video, robot trajectories and simulation; Open X-Embodiment pools 22 robots from 21 institutions <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. Objectives include next-token prediction, contrastive learning, video prediction and action denoising; interfaces include text, action chunks, predicted futures, values and embeddings.

Budgets run from zero-shot prompting through linear probes, parameter-efficient and full fine-tuning to new robot data at scale. RoboCat adapts to new tasks and robots from 100 to 1,000 examples <Cite id="robocat-2023" />, Gemini Robotics learns short-horizon tasks from as few as 100 demonstrations <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, and Octo fine-tunes to new sensors and action spaces in hours on consumer GPUs <Cite id="octo-2024" />. π*0.6's RL from experience more than doubles throughput on some of the hardest tasks <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The newest rung is in-context learning: Generalist reports GEN-1.5 learning a task from one 3 to 12 second demonstration with no training <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />, and Skild reports S1 executing a task shown in one video <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />.

## VLA foundations

RT-1 argued for task-agnostic training of high-capacity models on diverse robot data <Cite id="rt1-2022" />. RT-2 co-fine-tunes a vision-language model on robot trajectories and internet-scale vision-language tasks, writing actions as reserved tokens <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. OpenVLA made a comparable recipe public: DINOv2 and SigLIP features, a Llama-family backbone and fine-tuning on Open X-Embodiment <Cite id="openvla-2024" />.

Calibration, gripper dynamics, control rate and success labels enter during adaptation: VLAs struggle with novel robot setups until fine-tuned, and OpenVLA-OFT's recipe lifted OpenVLA's average success across four task suites from 76.5% to 97.1% <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

π0 pairs a pretrained VLM with a separate flow-matching expert for continuous action chunks <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Knowledge insulation trains the backbone on discretized actions alongside the continuous expert <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. π0.5 credits its generalization to co-training on heterogeneous tasks <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, and π0.7's authors call their 2026 model a steerable generalist robotic foundation model <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Test-time training stores past observations in fast weights that gradient descent updates while the policy runs. In July 2026, Yunfan Jiang, Li Fei-Fei, Yuke Zhu, Linxi Fan and colleagues reported RoboTTT, which extends visuomotor context to 8K timesteps without growing inference latency. On long-horizon real-robot tasks it averaged a 79% task completion score, against 42% for the single-step-context GR00T N1.7 baseline, and it fully completed a five-minute, ten-stage assembly task that no baseline completed <Cite id="robottt-2026" />. In September 2026, Yize Liu, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu and colleagues applied the mechanism to memory with T²Mem, which raised average success on 16 RoboMME tasks from 17.93% to 56.83% over its memory-free base policy <Cite id="t2mem-2026" />. ECoMEM, posted on September 30, 2026 by Stanford's Yize Liu, Mac Schwager, Jiajun Wu and colleagues, keeps an explicit record of task facts that a learned reader turns into memory tokens for the VLA. It led the evaluated policies on 15 of 16 RoboMME tasks, and on two new real-robot tasks it reached 86.1% success, against 8.6% for a VLA without memory <Cite id="ecomem-2026" />.

## Humanoid foundations

GR00T N1 combines a vision-language module with a diffusion transformer, trained on real-robot trajectories, human videos and synthetic datasets <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. For a humanoid, morphology, whole-body balance and shared action coordinates set the reuse boundary. In September 2026, Figure reported pretraining [Helix 2.5](/manipulation/generalist-policies/) from random initialization entirely on Index, its dataset of human behavior. With identical task data, it succeeded on 56% of zero-shot trials in 30 unseen homes, against 9% for a policy trained from scratch <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

## Predictive foundations

V-JEPA 2 learns representations from over 1M hours of internet video, then an action-conditioned stage on fewer than 62 hours of unlabeled DROID video <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. Its asset is a representation for comparing predicted futures. R3M's embedding, pretrained on Ego4D human video, beats from-scratch training by over 20% <Cite id="r3m-2022" />, and Dreamer 4 draws most of its knowledge from unlabeled video <Cite id="dreamer4-2025" />.

Cosmos frames a world foundation model as a general-purpose model fine-tuned into custom ones <Cite id="cosmos-predict-2025" />. Cosmos 3 unifies understanding, generation, forward and inverse dynamics and policy outputs in one omnimodal model <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />, and Cosmos Policy turns Cosmos-Predict2 into a policy in one post-training stage, reaching 98.5% on LIBERO <Cite id="cosmos-policy-2026" />. Each output needs its own test: Ctrl-World ranks policies without real rollouts <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />.

DreamZero (Ye, Zhu and colleagues, February 2026) is a [world action model](/world-models/taxonomy/) on a pretrained video diffusion backbone, predicting video and actions together. In real-robot tests it more than doubled average task progress on new tasks and environments over state-of-the-art VLAs, and its 14-billion-parameter model ran closed-loop at 7 Hz <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. On 16 March 2026 NVIDIA previewed GR00T N2, based on DreamZero research and slated for release by year end <Cite id="nvidia-gtc-gr00t-n2-2026" />.

## What pretraining does not erase

Pretraining can cut semantic supervision, representation learning from small robot datasets, initialization across related tasks and per-policy visual backbones. In TRI's blind, randomized trials, multitask pretraining matched from-scratch policies with 3 to 5 times less data <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. VC-1 found no pretrained visual representation universally dominant, and more pretraining data helped only on average <Cite id="vc1-2023" />. In April 2026, Generalist reported that GEN-1's base model trains on over half a million hours of wearable human data and no robot data. With about one hour of robot data per task, it averaged 99% on three tasks, against 19% from scratch <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Toyota Research Institute's 89-policy study found that vision-language and cross-embodiment co-training improve generalization, and that robot-only training degrades the backbone's visiolinguistic understanding <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" />.

It leaves synchronized, correctly transformed actions, calibration and control integration, closed-loop evaluation under perturbation, latency and memory limits, and safety cases. RoboPAIR jailbroke LLM-controlled robots, including a deployed Unitree Go2, often at 100% attack success <Cite id="robopair-2024" />. A downloadable checkpoint can hide its training mixture, adaptation data, controller assumptions or benchmark protocol.

## Evaluating a foundation claim

Look for a matrix: rows vary tasks and environments, columns vary embodiment, adaptation data and evaluation shift. Report the checkpoint, every tuned component, the new robot data and a same-budget from-scratch baseline. VLAs near 95% on LIBERO drop below 30% under modest camera or initial-state perturbations <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />, and models above 90% fall to 0.0% under LIBERO-PRO's generalized setting <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" />. Full retraining for a new arm marks a narrow embodiment boundary; a small adapter working across new tasks earns the label.
