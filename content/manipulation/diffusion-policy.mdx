<Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion Policy</Term> (Chi et al., 2023) makes robot motion the way image generators make pictures: it starts from random numbers and removes noise step by step, guided by camera images. On 15 tasks it averaged a 46.9% relative gain in success over the best earlier methods in simulation, excluding multi-operator data <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

<Image id="aloha-unleashed-shirt-rollout-2024" />

## Averaging demonstrations fails

In Push-T a robot pushes a T-shaped block onto a target, and the gripper can go around either side. A policy that minimizes average error blends the two routes and drives into the block. Diffusion Policy learned both and committed to one per attempt <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

Capturing several modes may not be why it works: a two-step regression policy essentially matched flow policies <Cite id="much-ado-noising-2025" />. OpenVLA-OFT, trained by plain regression, beat from-scratch Diffusion Policy and ACT by up to 15 points <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

Also: implicit policies <Cite id="implicit-bc-2021" />, earlier diffusion policies <Cite id="pearce-diffusion-bc-2023" />, a Gaussian readout at up to twice the speed <Cite id="gaussian-action-readout-2026" />.

## Each decision cleans up random actions

<DiffusionDenoising className="my-6" />

Starting from random actions, a network removes some noise given the latest camera frames. Repeated, this leaves the next $T_p$ actions, as DDPM does with pixels <Cite id="ddpm-2020" />:

$$
A_t^{k-1} = \alpha \left( A_t^k - \gamma \, \epsilon_\theta(O_t, A_t^k, k) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I) \right)
$$

Training uses improved DDPM's noise schedule <Cite id="iddpm-2021" />. On real robots the DDIM sampler <Cite id="ddim-2021" /> ran 10 steps in 0.1 seconds on an Nvidia 3080 <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

## Plan sixteen moves, carry out eight

The policy predicts $T_p$ actions, carries out the first $T_a$ and replans; a common setting is 16 and 8. At 10 commands per second, that commits the robot for 0.8 seconds <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

<RecedingHorizon className="my-6" />

Long commitments move smoothly but react slowly; short ones can switch strategy mid-motion, as with ACT's chunks <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## The convolutional network is the safe default

| Network | Reads observations through | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1D U-Net, adapted from Diffuser <Cite id="diffuser-2022" /> | FiLM <Cite id="film-2018" /> | Default; needs little tuning |
| minGPT-style transformer | Cross-attention | Best with robot state as input; needs more tuning <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" /> |

Both see through a ResNet-18 trained with the policy, with GroupNorm <Cite id="groupnorm-2018" />. ScaleDP grew the transformer from 10 million to 1 billion parameters <Cite id="scaledp-2024" />.

## The recipe spread to 3D, symmetry and scale

| Work | Change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DP3 <Cite id="dp3-2024" /> | 3D point clouds | Most of 72 simulated tasks from 10 demonstrations |
| Equivariant Diffusion Policy <Cite id="equivariant-diffusion-policy-2024" /> | Built-in rotational symmetry | 21.9% higher average success |
| ALOHA Unleashed <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" /> | Large-scale [ALOHA 2](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/) data | Two-armed work with cloth |
| TRI Large Behavior Models <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" /> <Cite id="tri-lbm-scirobotics-2026" /> | About 1,700 hours of data | Matched from-scratch policies with 3 to 5 times less data |

NVIDIA's GR00T 2 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> and Runway's open 27-billion-parameter Praxis-1 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" /> denoise video and actions together.

## One step can be enough

Ten network passes per decision are slow; consistency models need one <Cite id="consistency-models-2023" />.

| Method | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Consistency Policy <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" />, distilled | Matched its teacher in 1 to 3 steps on simulated nut-on-peg, 0.88 to 0.92 success against 0.84 to 0.92 |
| One-Step Diffusion Policy <Cite id="one-step-diffusion-2024" /> | 1.5 to 62 predictions a second on real Franka arms |
| MP1 <Cite id="mp1-2026" />, no teacher | 6.8 milliseconds |
| Drifting Models <Cite id="drifting-models-2026" />, no teacher | Matched or beat 100-step Diffusion Policy on 10 of 14 simulated settings |

Also: One-Step Flow Policy, teacher-free <Cite id="one-step-flow-policy-2026" />.

## Flow matching takes a straight path

π0 replaced DDPM with <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>, which moves noise toward data along fixed paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />. π0's paths are straight, as in rectified flow <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />, and it follows them in 10 steps <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Across six flow-matching VLAs, one step largely matched many once models were large and well trained <Cite id="one-step-euler-flow-2026" />.

Also: coarse steps over long horizons <Cite id="horizon-depth-coupling-2026" />, Bilinear Flow Policy for unfamiliar goals <Cite id="bilinear-flow-policy-2026" />.

## It reacts late and memorizes small datasets

A committed chunk ignores events until the next prediction <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. <Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> plans the next chunk during the current one and holds throughput under 200 milliseconds of injected delay <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />; [training-time RTC](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) removes its overhead <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

With few demonstrations the policy acts as a lookup table, replaying the nearest training example even for pictures of cats and dogs <Cite id="demystifying-diffusion-policies-2025" />.

Also: Octo, with language and goal-image instructions <Cite id="octo-2024" />.
