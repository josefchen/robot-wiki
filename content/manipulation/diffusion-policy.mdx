<Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion Policy</Term> is a visuomotor robot policy that generates an action sequence by conditional denoising, from Chi et al. (2023). Its extended journal version evaluates 15 tasks and reports a 46.9% average relative improvement in simulation, using the best baseline and backbone per column, multi-human data excluded <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

<Image id="aloha-unleashed-shirt-rollout-2024" />

## Receding-horizon control

Diffusion Policy executes $T_a$ actions of a $T_p$-action prediction, then observes and replans. The CNN configuration executes 8 of 16 on many tasks, and the next inference can warm-start from the previous prediction <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

$T_a$ acts like ACT's chunk size: large values give smooth motion that reacts slowly, and small values react fast but resample often, so each sample can switch mode <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. At the policy's 10 Hz command rate, $T_a = 8$ commits the robot for 0.8 seconds <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Try “Never rethink”: the dashed tails vanish.

## Multimodal demonstrations

When one operator passes an obstacle on the left and another on the right, mean-squared-error regression averages the demonstrations into a collision. Energy-based implicit policies often beat such explicit policies <Cite id="implicit-bc-2021" />, and diffusion models learn an expressive distribution over the joint action space <Cite id="pearce-diffusion-bc-2023" />. Pan et al. (2025) contest this motivation: generative policies owe their gains to supervised iterative computation, and a two-step regression policy essentially matches flow policies <Cite id="much-ado-noising-2025" />. Fine-tuned OpenVLA-OFT, an L1-regression VLA, beat from-scratch Diffusion Policy and ACT by up to 15 points of average success <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

## Denoising in action space

<DiffusionDenoising className="my-6" />

The policy is a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) <Cite id="ddpm-2020" /> conditioned on recent observations. Where a regression head computes $a_t = f_\theta(o_t)$, Diffusion Policy models $p(A_t \mid O_t)$ over the next $T_p$ actions. Inference starts from Gaussian noise and repeatedly subtracts a fraction of the noise that the denoiser $\epsilon_\theta$ predicts,

$$
A_t^{k-1} = \alpha \left( A_t^k - \gamma \, \epsilon_\theta(O_t, A_t^k, k) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I) \right)
$$

until the sample is a valid action chunk. Training uses noise-prediction MSE with improved DDPM's square-cosine schedule <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" /> <Cite id="iddpm-2021" />. Real-world runs use DDIM, which keeps DDPM training but samples more efficiently <Cite id="ddim-2021" />, with 10 inference iterations and 0.1-second latency on an Nvidia 3080 in the main text and 16 in the appendix <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

## CNN and transformer variants

The CNN backbone adapts Diffuser's one-dimensional temporal U-Net over state-action trajectories <Cite id="diffuser-2022" /> to model only $p(A_t \mid O_t)$ and predict only actions <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, injecting observations through FiLM's feature-wise affine transformation <Cite id="film-2018" />:

The CNN is the recommended default and works on most tasks without much tuning. The transformer gives most of the best state-based results, especially with fast action changes, but costs more tuning, and adding layers sometimes hurts <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. ScaleDP confirms the layer problem and scales the transformer from 10 million to 1 billion parameters <Cite id="scaledp-2024" />.

## Variants and scale

DP3 conditions on sparse point clouds and handles most of 72 simulation tasks with 10 demonstrations <Cite id="dp3-2024" />, and Equivariant Diffusion Policy builds in SO(2) symmetry for 21.9% higher average success <Cite id="equivariant-diffusion-policy-2024" />. ALOHA Unleashed pairs Diffusion Policies with large-scale [ALOHA 2](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/) data for deformable, contact-rich bimanual tasks <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />, and TRI's Large Behavior Models scale the recipe to approximately 1,700 hours and match from-scratch policies with 3 to 5 times less data <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

## Distillation

Iterative denoising is slow for mobile manipulators and quadrotors without high-end GPUs. Consistency models generate in one step by design <Cite id="consistency-models-2023" />, and Consistency Policy distills a Diffusion Policy teacher into one- or three-step generation; on Robomimic Square, three teachers at 0.84 to 0.92 success yielded students at 0.88 to 0.92 <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" />.

One-Step Diffusion Policy lifts action prediction from 1.5 Hz to 62 Hz on real Franka tasks <Cite id="one-step-diffusion-2024" />. Drifting Models (Kaiming He and colleagues, February 2026) train a one-step generator without distillation; swapped into Diffusion Policy, one step matched or beat the 100-step original on 10 of 14 simulated task settings <Cite id="drifting-models-2026" />.

## Flow matching

π0 replaced the DDPM with conditional <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>, which regresses vector fields of fixed conditional probability paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, on a straight path as in rectified flow <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />. It samples noise $\varepsilon$ and forms the interpolant

$$
A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\varepsilon, \qquad u(A_t^\tau \mid A_t) = A_t - \varepsilon
$$

then trains the network to predict $u$, drawing $\tau$ from a beta distribution weighted toward the noisy end. Sampling integrates from $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 1$ with 10 forward Euler steps <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

Bilinear Flow Policy (Georgia Tech, University of Washington and Toyota Research Institute, October 5, 2026) targets goals outside the training distribution: across five simulated tasks it reached 2.63 times the out-of-distribution success of a goal-conditioned flow policy, and on two real tasks 50% against 18% <Cite id="bilinear-flow-policy-2026" />.

## Real-time chunking

<Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> generates the next chunk while the current one executes, holding average throughput at +100 and +200 ms of injected delay on six real π0.5 tasks <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />, and [training-time RTC](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) removes its inference overhead <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

## Limitations

The original paper conditions on observations alone, on single- and multi-task benchmarks and separate UR5 and Franka setups <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023-v1" />; Octo adds language or goal-image conditioning <Cite id="octo-2024" />, and π0 a pretrained vision-language backbone <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

Reactivity is bounded by $T_a$: events inside a committed segment wait for the next inference <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. Sampling cost remains, though one-step successors such as MP1 cut it to 6.8 ms <Cite id="mp1-2026" />. With sparse data it acts like an action lookup table, returning training actions even for images of cats and dogs <Cite id="demystifying-diffusion-policies-2025" />.
