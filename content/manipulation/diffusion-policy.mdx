<Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion Policy</Term> (Chi et al., 2023) generates a robot's next moves the way image generators make pictures: guided by camera images, it starts from random numbers and removes noise step by step. On 15 tasks it scored on average 46.9% higher than the best earlier methods in simulation, excluding multi-operator data <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

<Image id="aloha-unleashed-shirt-rollout-2024" />

## Why averaging demonstrations fails

People demonstrate the same task differently. In the paper's Push-T task, where a robot pushes a T-shaped block onto a target, the gripper can go around the block on either side. A policy trained to minimize average error blends the two, and halfway between the routes is the block itself. Diffusion Policy learned both routes and committed to one per attempt <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, as earlier implicit <Cite id="implicit-bc-2021" /> and diffusion <Cite id="pearce-diffusion-bc-2023" /> policies aimed to.

Why it works is disputed. Pan et al. (2025) found that generative policies gain from computing in supervised steps, and that capturing several modes is not the source; a two-step regression policy essentially matched flow policies <Cite id="much-ado-noising-2025" />. A Gaussian readout swapped into flow policies kept near-teacher success at up to twice the speed <Cite id="gaussian-action-readout-2026" />, and OpenVLA-OFT, trained by plain regression, beat from-scratch Diffusion Policy and ACT by up to 15 points <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

## How noise becomes motion

<DiffusionDenoising className="my-6" />

At each decision the policy starts from random actions, asks a network which part is noise given the latest camera frames, removes part of it and repeats until a clean sequence of the next $T_p$ actions is left. The DDPM image generator works the same way on pixels <Cite id="ddpm-2020" />:

$$
A_t^{k-1} = \alpha \left( A_t^k - \gamma \, \epsilon_\theta(O_t, A_t^k, k) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I) \right)
$$

Training teaches the network $\epsilon_\theta$ to predict noise added to demonstrations, on improved DDPM's schedule <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" /> <Cite id="iddpm-2021" />. On real robots the faster DDIM sampler <Cite id="ddim-2021" /> took 0.1 seconds for 10 steps on an Nvidia 3080 graphics card <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

## Plan sixteen moves, carry out eight

The policy predicts $T_p$ actions, carries out the first $T_a$ and replans. A common setting is 16 and 8, and each prediction can start from the unused part of the last <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. At 10 commands per second, 8 actions commit the robot for 0.8 seconds <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

<RecedingHorizon className="my-6" />

As with ACT's chunk size, long commitments give smooth motion that reacts slowly, and short ones react fast but resample often and can switch strategy mid-motion <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## Two network designs

The paper offers two denoising networks.

The default convolutional network adapts Diffuser's trajectory U-Net <Cite id="diffuser-2022" /> to predict only actions <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />, with FiLM scaling its features by the observations <Cite id="film-2018" />, and needs little tuning; GroupNorm frees its image encoder from batch size <Cite id="groupnorm-2018" />. The transformer gave most of the best results when given the robot's state directly, but needs more tuning, and extra layers sometimes hurt <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. ScaleDP addressed that and scaled it from 10 million to 1 billion parameters <Cite id="scaledp-2024" />.

## The recipe spreads

Later work changed the inputs and the scale. DP3 reads 3D point clouds and handled most of 72 simulated tasks from 10 demonstrations <Cite id="dp3-2024" />, Equivariant Diffusion Policy builds in rotational symmetry for 21.9% higher average success <Cite id="equivariant-diffusion-policy-2024" />, and ALOHA Unleashed used large-scale [ALOHA 2](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/) data for two-armed work with cloth and other deformable objects <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />.

<Image id="dp3-real-robot-setup-2024" />

Toyota Research Institute's Large Behavior Models trained on about 1,700 hours and matched from-scratch policies with 3 to 5 times less data (Science Robotics, April 2026) <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" /> <Cite id="tri-lbm-scirobotics-2026" />; noise shaped like real actions did not improve their fine-tuning <Cite id="gaussian-prior-lbm-2026" />. NVIDIA's GR00T 2 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> and Runway's open-weight, 27-billion-parameter Praxis-1 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" /> denoise video and actions together.

## Fewer steps

Ten network passes per decision are slow. Consistency models generate in one step by design <Cite id="consistency-models-2023" />, and Consistency Policy distilled Diffusion Policy into one- and three-step students that matched their teachers on a simulated nut-on-peg task, 0.88 to 0.92 success against 0.84 to 0.92 <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" />. One-Step Diffusion Policy raised predictions on real Franka arms from 1.5 to 62 per second <Cite id="one-step-diffusion-2024" />. One-Step Flow Policy <Cite id="one-step-flow-policy-2026" />, MP1 (6.8 milliseconds) <Cite id="mp1-2026" /> and Drifting Models need no teacher; Drifting Models (Kaiming He and colleagues, February 2026) matched or beat the 100-step Diffusion Policy on 10 of 14 simulated settings <Cite id="drifting-models-2026" />.

## Flow matching takes a straight path

π0 swapped the DDPM for <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>, which learns to move noise toward data along fixed paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, here straight lines as in rectified flow <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />. The network learns the direction from a noise sample to a real action chunk, and π0 follows it in 10 steps <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Across six flow-matching VLAs, one step largely matched many once models were large and well trained <Cite id="one-step-euler-flow-2026" />, and coarse steps helped long stretches without replanning <Cite id="horizon-depth-coupling-2026" />. Bilinear Flow Policy (October 2026) reached 2.63 times a goal-conditioned flow policy's success on unfamiliar goals in five simulated tasks, and 50% against 18% on two real ones <Cite id="bilinear-flow-policy-2026" />.

## What is still hard

The original policy takes observations but no instructions <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />; Octo added language or goal images <Cite id="octo-2024" />, and π0 a vision-language backbone <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Events inside a committed chunk wait for the next prediction <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. <Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> plans the next chunk during the current one, holding throughput under 200 milliseconds of injected delay <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />, and [training-time RTC](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) removes its overhead <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />. With few demonstrations the policy acts as a lookup table, replaying the nearest training example even for pictures of cats and dogs <Cite id="demystifying-diffusion-policies-2025" />.
