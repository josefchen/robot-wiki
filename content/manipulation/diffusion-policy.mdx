<Term id="diffusion-policy">Diffusion Policy</Term> is a visuomotor robot policy that generates an action sequence by conditional denoising, from Chi et al. (2023). Its extended journal version evaluates 15 tasks and reports a 46.9% average relative improvement in simulation, using the best baseline and backbone per column, multi-human data excluded <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

## Receding-horizon control

Diffusion Policy executes $T_a$ actions of a $T_p$-action prediction, then observes and replans. The CNN configuration executes 8 of 16 on many tasks, and the next inference can warm-start from the previous prediction <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

$T_a$ acts like ACT's chunk size: large values give smooth motion that reacts slowly; small values react fast but resample often, and each sample can switch mode <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. Chunking captures temporal dependencies at the cost of reactivity to unexpected states <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. At the policy's 10 Hz command rate, $T_a = 8$ commits the robot for 0.8 seconds <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Try “Never rethink”: the dashed tails vanish.

## Multimodal demonstrations

Demonstrations are multimodal: one operator passes an obstacle on the left, another on the right, and mean-squared-error regression averages them into a collision. Energy-based implicit policies often beat such explicit policies <Cite id="implicit-bc-2021" />, and diffusion models learn an expressive distribution over the joint action space <Cite id="pearce-diffusion-bc-2023" />. Pan et al. (2025) contest this motivation: generative policies owe their gains to supervised iterative computation, and a two-step regression policy essentially matches flow policies <Cite id="much-ado-noising-2025" />. Fine-tuned OpenVLA-OFT, an L1-regression VLA, beat from-scratch Diffusion Policy and ACT by up to 15 points of average success <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

## Denoising in action space

<DiffusionDenoising className="my-6" />

The policy is a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) <Cite id="ddpm-2020" /> conditioned on recent observations. Where a regression head computes $a_t = f_\theta(o_t)$, Diffusion Policy models $p(A_t \mid O_t)$ over the next $T_p$ actions. Inference starts from Gaussian noise and repeatedly subtracts a fraction of the noise that the denoiser $\epsilon_\theta$ predicts,

$$
A_t^{k-1} = \alpha \left( A_t^k - \gamma \, \epsilon_\theta(O_t, A_t^k, k) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I) \right)
$$

until the sample is a valid action chunk. Training uses noise-prediction MSE with improved DDPM's square-cosine schedule <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" /> <Cite id="iddpm-2021" />. Real-world runs use DDIM, which keeps DDPM training but samples more efficiently <Cite id="ddim-2021" />. The main text gives 100 training timesteps, 10 inference iterations and 0.1-second latency on an Nvidia 3080; the appendix and real-world table rows of both releases give 16 iterations <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

## CNN and transformer variants

The CNN backbone adopts Diffuser's temporal CNN with modifications: it models only $p(A_t \mid O_t)$ and predicts only actions <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Diffuser itself is a one-dimensional temporal U-Net over state-action trajectories <Cite id="diffuser-2022" />. The CNN injects observations through FiLM's feature-wise affine transformation <Cite id="film-2018" />:

The CNN is the recommended default and works on most tasks without much tuning. The transformer gives most of the best state-based results, especially with fast action changes, which the authors suspect reflects the CNN's low-frequency bias; it costs more tuning, and adding layers sometimes hurts <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. ScaleDP confirms the layer problem and scales the transformer from 10 million to 1 billion parameters <Cite id="scaledp-2024" />.

The visual encoder swaps BatchNorm for GroupNorm, whose computation is independent of batch size <Cite id="groupnorm-2018" />, to train stably with DDPMs' exponential moving average of weights <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

## Variants and scale

DP3 conditions on sparse point clouds and handles most of 72 simulation tasks with 10 demonstrations <Cite id="dp3-2024" />. Equivariant Diffusion Policy builds in SO(2) symmetry and averages 21.9% higher success than Diffusion Policy <Cite id="equivariant-diffusion-policy-2024" />. ALOHA Unleashed pairs Diffusion Policies with large-scale [ALOHA 2](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/) data for deformable, contact-rich bimanual tasks <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />. TRI's Large Behavior Models scale the recipe to approximately 1,700 hours and match from-scratch policies with 3 to 5 times less data <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Xiaomi-Robotics-1 pairs a VLM with a diffusion-transformer action module and pretrains on over 100,000 hours of UMI handheld-gripper and egocentric data <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />.

## Distillation

Iterative denoising is slow for mobile manipulators and quadrotors without high-end GPUs. Consistency models generate in one step by design <Cite id="consistency-models-2023" />, and Consistency Policy distills an EDM-based Diffusion Policy teacher into one- or three-step generation. On Robomimic Square, teachers at 0.92, 0.88 and 0.84 success yielded students at 0.92, 0.92 and 0.88, robust over that range <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" />. One-Step Diffusion Policy lifts action prediction from 1.5 Hz to 62 Hz on real Franka tasks <Cite id="one-step-diffusion-2024" />, and MP1 averages 6.8 ms per one-step inference <Cite id="mp1-2026" />. The teacher-free One-Step Flow Policy outperforms 100-step diffusion and flow policies across 56 simulated tasks <Cite id="one-step-flow-policy-2026" />. Drifting Models (Mingyang Deng, Kaiming He and colleagues, February 2026) train a one-step generator without distillation. Swapped into Diffusion Policy, one step matched or beat the 100-step original on 10 of 14 simulated task settings <Cite id="drifting-models-2026" />.

## Flow matching

π0 replaced the DDPM with conditional <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>, which regresses vector fields of fixed conditional probability paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, on a straight path as in rectified flow <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />. It samples noise $\varepsilon$ and forms the interpolant

$$
A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\varepsilon, \qquad u(A_t^\tau \mid A_t) = A_t - \varepsilon
$$

then trains the network to predict $u$, drawing $\tau$ from a beta distribution weighted toward the noisy end. Sampling integrates from $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 1$ with 10 forward Euler steps <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

On October 5, 2026, Wonsuhk Jung (Georgia Tech), Abhishek Gupta (University of Washington), Haruki Nishimura (Toyota Research Institute) and colleagues posted Bilinear Flow Policy for goals outside the training distribution. Across five simulated tasks it reached 2.63 times the out-of-distribution success of a goal-conditioned flow policy; on two real tasks it reached 50% against 18% <Cite id="bilinear-flow-policy-2026" />.

## Real-time chunking

<Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> generates the next chunk while executing the current one, freezing actions that will execute and inpainting the rest, with no retraining. On six real tasks with π0.5 and five denoising steps, average throughput held at injected delays of +100 and +200 ms on top of model and LAN latency <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />. [Training-time RTC](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) conditions on action prefixes instead, removing inference overhead and beating inference-time RTC at higher delays in simulation <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

## Limitations

The original paper conditions on observations alone, on single- and multi-task benchmarks and separate UR5 and Franka setups. Its reference encoder trains end-to-end without pretraining, though ImageNet- and R3M-pretrained encoders were tested <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023-v1" />. Octo adds language or goal-image conditioning, a DDPM action head and multi-task, multi-embodiment pretraining <Cite id="octo-2024" />, and π0 a pretrained vision-language backbone, a flow-matching expert and cross-embodiment training <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Reactivity is bounded by $T_a$: events inside a committed segment wait for the next inference. Sampling cost remains, though one-step successors cut it to milliseconds <Cite id="mp1-2026" />. With sparse data it acts like an action lookup table, returning training actions even for images of cats and dogs <Cite id="demystifying-diffusion-policies-2025" />.
