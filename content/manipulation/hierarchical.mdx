Hierarchical approaches are robot architectures in which one level decides *what* to do next and another decides *how* to move. In 2022 the high level was a separate planner choosing from a skill library <Cite id="saycan-2022" />. By 2026 it can run inside the policy network, as in π0.5 <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, or as a separate orchestrator such as Gemini Robotics ER 2 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Drag the playhead.

## SayCan: scoring usefulness and affordance

LLMs can decompose high-level tasks into mid-level plans zero-shot, but their steps often fail to map to admissible actions <Cite id="huang-zero-shot-planners-2022" />. SayCan (2022) scores each skill in a supplied library twice. The LLM rates its description as the next step, and an <Term id="affordance">affordance</Term> value estimates whether it can succeed in the current state. The product picks the skill to execute, its description is appended to the prompt, and the loop repeats until a termination option wins <Cite id="saycan-2022" />.

On the mobile manipulator, picking uses a multitask language-conditioned behavior-cloning policy. RL-trained value functions, calibrated before use as probabilities, score skills without a separate model per skill. Navigation and placing use hand-designed policies or affordance rules. The skill library bounds the system, feedback reaches the planner only through the values at the current step, and the paper reports difficulty reacting when a skill fails despite a high value <Cite id="saycan-2022" />.

Inner Monologue closed the loop with language feedback such as success detection and scene descriptions, which significantly improved instruction completion <Cite id="inner-monologue-2022" />. Text2Motion checks geometric feasibility across whole skill sequences and solves 82% of long-horizon problems, against 13% for earlier language planners <Cite id="text2motion-2023" />.

## Code as Policies: the planner writes programs

Code as Policies (2022) prompts a code-writing LLM with example instruction-program pairs to generate Python that calls supplied perception and control APIs. Missing functions are generated recursively. Programs can use loops, conditionals, spatial arithmetic and feedback over perception outputs. It assumes feasible instructions and cannot check correctness in advance. Its real robots lift ViLD or MDETR detections to 3D with depth and a pre-registered camera-to-robot transform, and many failures trace to inaccurate detections. Quantitative evaluation covers only a tabletop simulation <Cite id="code-as-policies-2022" />.

VoxPoser's LLM-written code queries a <Term id="vision-language-model">VLM</Term> to compose 3D value maps, which a model-based planner turns into closed-loop trajectories <Cite id="voxposer-2023" />.

In September 2026, several groups returned to code-writing planners with coding agents that test and repair their own programs. SimEX, from Jiaheng Hu, Guanya Shi and colleagues, has the agent build a robot toolbox in simulation and then correct the simulator from a few physical trials; the authors report towel folding, barcode scanning and plate manipulation learned with no demonstrations and 10 minutes of real-robot interaction <Cite id="simex-2026" />. EmbodiedSWE, from Zeyu Shen, Dhruv Shah and colleagues, expands one verified coding-agent solution into many simulated demonstrations, and a VLA fine-tuned only on those demonstrations completed a long-horizon task on a real robot <Cite id="embodiedswe-2026" />. RoboHarn-Evo, from Shifeng Bao, Cheng Chi and colleagues, keeps the model weights fixed and stores task-level and action-level knowledge that physical feedback revises; after 80 interaction rollouts, held-out success rose from 48.3% to 75.0% with GPT-5.5 and from 70.0% to 88.3% with GPT-6 <Cite id="roboharn-evo-2026" />. ASENA, from An-Chieh Cheng, Linxi Fan, Yuke Zhu and colleagues, gives a coding agent navigation tools; with simulator feedback, ten passes over recurring 100-task subsets raised success from 72% to 98% on R2R and from 65% to 89% on RxR <Cite id="asena-2026" />. RPG, posted on October 1, 2026 by Yen-Jen Wang, Pieter Abbeel, Haozhi Qi and colleagues, also keeps the weights frozen: it practices in simulation and revises a symbolic skill library and its system prompt. On held-out starts of 22 manipulation tasks, success rose from 28.6% after the first practice round to 95.0% after 15, and the frozen system succeeded in all 30 physical trials, ten on each of three tasks <Cite id="rpg-2026" />.

## The keypoint turn: VLMs emit geometry instead of skill names

MOKA marks candidate points with GroundedSAM masks and prompts a pretrained VLM to select grasp, function and target keypoints, waypoint regions and motion attributes. Calibrated depth from the [perception pipeline](/classical/perception/) lifts the points into the robot frame, and a separate grasp sampler and motion pipeline execute them. No full 6-DoF object pose is needed. It also studies in-context examples and distillation into a policy <Cite id="moka-2024" />.

ReKep proposes keypoints from DINOv2 features within SAM masks and calibrated RGB-D. GPT-4o writes stage-wise Python sub-goal and path constraints over them, and a hierarchical solver replans end-effector motion while penalizing violations. Results rest on tracking and rigidity assumptions, and some evaluations use human annotations or disable tracking <Cite id="rekep-2024" />.

RoboPoint instruction-tunes a VLM to predict image-space affordance points, co-training synthetic object and free-space data with real-image VQA and LVIS detection data. On Where2Place's 100 real images it reaches 46.77% point-in-mask accuracy against 29.06% for GPT-4o, means of three runs. Its robot experiments add depth, an end-effector offset and a [motion planner](/classical/motion-planning/) <Cite id="robopoint-2024" />.

RT-Trajectory conditions a policy on rough 2D trajectory sketches <Cite id="rt-trajectory-2023" />, and HAMSTER fine-tunes a high-level VLM to draw a coarse 2D end-effector path, averaging a 20-point success gain over OpenVLA, a 50% relative gain, across seven generalization axes <Cite id="hamster-2025" />. Gemini Robotics-ER predicts points, trajectories and grasps <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, and RoboBrain 2.5 outputs 3D manipulation traces as ordered keypoint sequences <Cite id="robobrain-25-2026" />.

## Reasoning before acting

ECoT trains a <Term id="vision-language-action-model">VLA</Term> to write a task plan, subtask reasoning, movement descriptions, object bounding boxes and the gripper's pixel position before its action tokens. Its synthetic annotations reuse existing Bridge V2 data. Over 314 trials per approach, it beats OpenVLA trained on the same data by 28 percentage points: 66% versus 44% in the in-distribution camera view and 64% versus 30% out of distribution. The suite spans familiar tasks, spatial relations, unseen objects and instructions, with means and one standard error <Cite id="ecot-2024" />.

ECoT's main evaluation holds high-level reasoning fixed for five steps, and a separate variant predicts four future gripper positions, which the authors call an imperfect proxy for how the policy would behave. They name reasoning-token generation as a speed limitation <Cite id="ecot-2024" />.

RT-H predicts a fine-grained language motion and then actions conditioned on it <Cite id="rt-h-2024" />. CoT-VLA predicts future image frames as visual goals before a short action sequence and beats the best prior VLA by 17% on real tasks <Cite id="cot-vla-2025" />. MolmoAct emits depth-aware perception tokens and an editable trajectory trace before actions, reaching 86.6% average success on LIBERO <Cite id="molmoact-2025" />.

## Learned and orchestrated hierarchies, 2025 to 2026

Figure's Helix pairs a 7B VLM at 7 to 9 Hz with an 80M-parameter visuomotor policy at 200 Hz, trained end-to-end <Cite id="helix-2025" />. GR00T N1 runs its VLM at 10 Hz on an L40 GPU and its action module at 120 Hz <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, and Fast-in-Slow partially shares parameters between the two, reaching 117.7 Hz with eight-action chunks <Cite id="fast-in-slow-2025" />.

π0.5 co-trains on robot data, semantic subtask labels and multimodal web tasks, pretraining with discrete tokens and adding a continuous action expert in post-training. At inference the same model predicts a language subtask and then actions conditioned on it, running the high level less often; its mobile manipulator takes 50 Hz targets in 50-action chunks. Training also predicts relevant bounding boxes before subtask labels and adds box-annotated indoor and household data to the web mixture <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

Hi Robot trains two policies from PaliGemma: a high-level VLM that turns images and open-ended instructions into simpler language commands, and a low-level π0 VLA that also reads robot state. The high level reruns after one second or on new human feedback, and users can intervene by text or speech or return to the prior task. Training mixes human-labelled demonstrations with synthetic interactions, with one high-level policy per domain, across table bussing, sandwich making and grocery shopping. The authors report missing high-level memory, low-level instruction violations and trouble recovering dropped objects <Cite id="hi-robot-2025" />.

π0.7 can condition actions on subgoal images from a BAGEL-initialized world model; subtask and image generation run in separate threads, and the VLA uses the latest outputs <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. In π0.6-MEM, a high-level policy predicts the next language subtask and an updated memory summary, and the low-level policy acts on the subtask and a shorter observation history <Cite id="mem-2026" />. VISTA's high-level world-model planner raises the same VLA from 14% to 69% in novel scenarios <Cite id="vista-2026" />.

AgiBot describes GO-2 as a unified system with an asynchronous two-module hierarchy: a lower-frequency System 2 generates action intents through Progressive Refinement, and a higher-frequency System 1 combines them with observations through Residual Refinement. It gives no numerical rates or shared-weights claim, and teacher forcing is a training mechanism <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />. Figure's Helix 02 adds S0 beneath S2's behavior sequencing and S1's 200 Hz full-body joint targets from vision, touch and proprioception. S0 is a 10M-parameter network that outputs actuator commands at 1 kHz; Helix 02 does not restate the S2 rate <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

With thinking on, Gemini Robotics 1.5 appends natural-language reasoning before acting, and its agentic system adds a separate GR-ER 1.5 orchestrator whose useful thinking budget depends on the task. ER 1.5 also produces point sequences that trace trajectories <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. Google made ER 1.5 broadly available as a high-level model that can call a VLA or user-defined functions <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-15-dev-2025" />. In [Gemini Robotics 2](/manipulation/generalist-policies/), ER 2 serves as the robot's high-level brain, coordinates with the VLA and tracks multi-minute tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Google says ER 2 hands off motor execution to any lower-level VLA and reports 57.4% accuracy on progress classification <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-2-2026" />.

## The verdict

[Both designs persist](/frontier/competing-theses/). In a controlled 2026 study on a real ALOHA, the best hierarchy placed 12 of 15 fruits, against 9 for a naive hierarchy and 3 for a flat VLA. The authors expect orchestration to stay important as VLAs improve <Cite id="orchestrating-policies-2026" />. In-network hierarchy can fail too: in a waypoint pipeline adapted from π0.5, erasing waypoint endpoints barely changed success. Token-by-token planning took 57 VLM forward passes, and the authors' fixes cut planning latency 8.7-fold <Cite id="fast-plans-2026" />.
