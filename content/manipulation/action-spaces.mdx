An action space defines the numeric outputs from a policy as well as the controller's understanding of those outputs. The choice of action space decides what the neural network will learn as well as whether or not different robots can learn from each other's data.

## The contract has four parts

| Part | What it fixes |
| --- | --- |
| Coordinates | Joints, tool frame or object frame |
| Meaning | Target, change, velocity, force or controller setting |
| Timing | Rate, horizon, stale predictions |
| Execution | Smoothing, clipping, conversion to joints, safety limits |

A label alone does not fix it. In Open X-Embodiment, the same seven action values could correspond to absolute positions, relative positions, or velocities <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. Just changing the scale of the action space (joint positions) changes median task success by 0.645 to 1.000 across three Factory assembly tasks <Cite id="factory-2022" /> <Cite id="same-label-interface-2026" />.

Also: DROID's recorded action spaces <Cite id="droid-2024" />, robosuite <Cite id="robosuite-2020" />.

## Joint actions are simple but tied to one robot

Joint actions tell each of the robot's motors where to go. The same angle at the shoulder joint would result in different positions for different robot arms. ACT predicts sequences ("chunks") of absolute joint position and gripper actions for bimanual manipulation with two ViperX arms <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. In RL-based locomotion, specifying actions as joint angles (with local feedback) instead of raw torques improved sample efficiency and locomotion ability <Cite id="peng-action-space-2016" />.

Also: [joint-to-tool kinematics](/classical/kinematics/) <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />.

## Tool motion can be shared across arms

Actions specified in the Cartesian frame refer to movements of the robot end-effector, for example "move 2 cm to the left". A change with respect to the current end-effector pose is meaningful independently of the particular arm being controlled, as long as its controller can translate it into motor commands, as in Khatib's operational space formulation <Cite id="khatib-1987" />. Orientations should be represented with 6D vectors instead of quaternions or Euler angles to avoid discontinuities in network outputs <Cite id="rotation-continuity-2018" />.

| System | Relative tool motion |
| --- | --- |
| UMI <Cite id="umi-2024" /> | Handheld grippers record motion relative to the current pose, usable on any robot |
| GR00T N1.6 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" /> | Smoother and more accurate than absolute actions, but errors build up on small datasets |
| GR00T N1.7 <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> | Shared between humans and robots |
| Matched VLA pretraining <Cite id="vla-scaling-alignment-2026" /> | One tool-relative format was critical for cross-embodiment transfer |

## Force and stiffness handle contact

Controlling torques requires rapid control, as well as additional safety measures, since it directly deals with forces. For example, the control interface of the robot arm of Franka allows setting torques with a control frequency of 1 kHz <Cite id="franka-fci-docs" />. Predicting motor torques from raw images was demonstrated by Levine et al. <Cite id="levine-visuomotor-2015" />. FACTR 2 estimates external joint torques without force sensors and outperforms previous force-aware policies by over 17% in terms of task progress <Cite id="factr-2-2026" />.

<Image id="adaptive-compliance-tasks-2024" />

Impedance actions are a middle ground between position and force control: the policy outputs a desired end-effector pose and a stiffness, and the controller treats the robot as a spring trying to reach that pose. Hogan argues that neither position nor force control is sufficient on its own <Cite id="hogan-1985" />.

| Study | Impedance result |
| --- | --- |
| Varin et al. <Cite id="varin-action-spaces-2019" /> | Task-space impedance needed the fewest samples against torque, joint PD and inverse dynamics |
| Martín-Martín et al. <Cite id="martin-martin-2019" /> | Variable tool-space impedance improved RL sample efficiency, energy use and safety |
| Adaptive Compliance Policy <Cite id="acp-2024" /> | Beat position-only policies by over 50% |

## Chunks change the time axis

An <Term id="action-chunking">action chunk</Term> predicts several steps per inference. ACT and two other baselines benefit from predicting chunks of actions instead of single steps <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> ([action chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking/)). Diffusion Policy only executes the first part of its predicted horizon of actions and then performs replanning <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Chunks go stale when contact changes the scene, and [real-time chunk replacement](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) fixes that <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

| System | Timing |
| --- | --- |
| GEN-1.5 <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" /> | 100 Hz trajectories |
| Helix 02 <Cite id="helix-02-2026" /> | 1 kHz joint commands beneath 200 Hz targets |
| T-Rex <Cite id="t-rex-2026" /> | Touch drives a variable-rate policy; over 30% above the strongest baseline on 12 delicate tasks |
| ODE Policy <Cite id="ode-policy-2026" /> | Integrates each command from the last, so the trajectory is continuous |

## Actions are tokens or continuous numbers

RT-2 <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> and OpenVLA discretize actions into language tokens from a predefined vocabulary; OpenVLA rounds each of seven numbers to one of 256 values <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Rounding introduces limitations in terms of action resolution. Such methods need to generate actions in an autoregressive manner, which is slow.

| Tokenizer | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| BEAST <Cite id="beast-2025" /> | Fixed-length B-spline tokens that decode in parallel |
| FAST <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" /> | Compresses a chunk's frequency components; far beats per-number rounding, with a universal tokenizer from 1M real trajectories |
| ReAT <Cite id="reat-2026" /> | Builds each token from the error earlier ones leave; 4 tokens beat 8-token baselines on LIBERO <Cite id="libero-2023" /> |
| LAP <Cite id="lap-2026" /> | Writes tool motions as short phrases |

OpenVLA-OFT demonstrated that continuous actions along with parallel decoding of actions increase performance (on LIBERO, from 76.5% to 97.1% success) <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Figure says Helix outputs continuous control <Cite id="helix-2025" />. Naive implementations that simply add continuous action experts to a VLA slow down training and negatively impact knowledge transfer, which [knowledge insulation](/manipulation/knowledge-insulation/) fixes <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

One study evaluated swapping in different action heads on a PaliGemma-based <Cite id="paligemma-2024" /> VLA, reporting results over five random seeds in the simulated RoboCasa environment <Cite id="robocasa-2024" /> <Cite id="discrete-actions-2026" />.

| Action head | Success |
| --- | --- |
| Binned flow | 53.9% |
| Continuous flow | 52.7% |
| One-pass binned, with guidance | 49.2% |
| One-pass continuous regression | 38.8% |
| One-pass binned | 27.1% |

## Choose the lowest level the task needs

A study of 500+ models and 13,000+ real robot rollouts found that predicting the change from the current pose consistently helped, with joint space favoring stable control and tool space generalization <Cite id="action-space-design-2026" />. For picking and pushing, predicting joint velocities beat predicting poses or joint increments when transferring policies from simulation to reality <Cite id="rl-action-spaces-2026" />.
