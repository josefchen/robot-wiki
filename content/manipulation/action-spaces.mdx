An action space is the format of the numbers a policy outputs, plus the controller's agreement on what they mean. It decides what the network learns and whether robots can share data.

<Image id="action-space-study-platforms-2026" />

## The contract has four parts

| Part | What it fixes |
| --- | --- |
| Coordinates | Joints, tool frame or object frame |
| Meaning | Target, change, velocity, force or controller setting |
| Timing | Rate, horizon, stale predictions |
| Execution | Smoothing, clipping, conversion to joints, safety limits |

A label alone does not fix it. In Open X-Embodiment, the same seven numbers can mean absolute or relative positions or velocities <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. On three Factory assembly tasks <Cite id="factory-2022" />, changing only the scale of joint-position actions moved median success by 0.645 to 1.000 <Cite id="same-label-interface-2026" />.

Also: DROID's recorded action spaces <Cite id="droid-2024" />, robosuite <Cite id="robosuite-2020" />.

## Joint actions are simple but tied to one robot

Joint actions say where each motor goes, and the same shoulder angle moves different arms differently. ACT predicts chunks of absolute joint and gripper targets for two ViperX arms <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. In locomotion RL, joint-angle targets with local feedback learned faster and moved better than raw torques <Cite id="peng-action-space-2016" />.

Also: [joint-to-tool kinematics](/classical/kinematics/) <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />.

## Tool motion can be shared across arms

Cartesian actions say what the tool does: move 2 cm left. A change from the current pose means the same on any arm whose controller converts it, as in Khatib's operational space formulation <Cite id="khatib-1987" />. Use a 6D rotation format: Euler angles and quaternions jump discontinuously as network outputs <Cite id="rotation-continuity-2018" />.

| System | Relative tool motion |
| --- | --- |
| UMI <Cite id="umi-2024" /> | Handheld grippers record motion relative to the current pose, usable on any robot |
| GR00T N1.6 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" /> | Smoother and more accurate than absolute actions, but errors build up on small datasets |
| GR00T N1.7 <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> | Shared between humans and robots |
| Matched VLA pretraining <Cite id="vla-scaling-alignment-2026" /> | One tool-relative format was critical for cross-embodiment transfer |

## Force and stiffness handle contact

Torque actions command forces directly and need fast control plus a safety layer; Franka's interface takes torques at 1 kHz <Cite id="franka-fci-docs" />. Levine et al. mapped raw images to motor torques <Cite id="levine-visuomotor-2015" />. FACTR 2 estimates external joint torques without force sensors and beat prior force-aware policies by over 17% in task progress <Cite id="factr-2-2026" />.

<Image id="adaptive-compliance-tasks-2024" />

Impedance actions sit between: the policy sets a target pose and a stiffness, and the controller pulls toward it like a spring. Hogan judged position or force control alone inadequate <Cite id="hogan-1985" />.

| Study | Impedance result |
| --- | --- |
| Varin et al. <Cite id="varin-action-spaces-2019" /> | Task-space impedance needed the fewest samples against torque, joint PD and inverse dynamics |
| Martín-Martín et al. <Cite id="martin-martin-2019" /> | Variable tool-space impedance improved RL sample efficiency, energy use and safety |
| Adaptive Compliance Policy <Cite id="acp-2024" /> | Beat position-only policies by over 50% |

## Chunks change the time axis

An <Term id="action-chunking">action chunk</Term> predicts several steps per inference, which helped ACT and two other baselines <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> ([action chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking/)). Diffusion Policy runs the start of each predicted horizon, then replans <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Chunks go stale when contact changes the scene, and [real-time chunk replacement](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) fixes that <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

| System | Timing |
| --- | --- |
| GEN-1.5 <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" /> | 100 Hz trajectories |
| Helix 02 <Cite id="helix-02-2026" /> | 1 kHz joint commands beneath 200 Hz targets |
| T-Rex <Cite id="t-rex-2026" /> | Touch drives a variable-rate policy; over 30% above the strongest baseline on 12 delicate tasks |
| ODE Policy <Cite id="ode-policy-2026" /> | Integrates each command from the last, so the trajectory is continuous |

## Actions are tokens or continuous numbers

RT-2 <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> and OpenVLA write actions as vocabulary tokens; OpenVLA rounds each of seven numbers to one of 256 values <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Rounding limits resolution, and token-by-token decoding is slow.

| Tokenizer | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| BEAST <Cite id="beast-2025" /> | Fixed-length B-spline tokens that decode in parallel |
| FAST <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" /> | Compresses a chunk's frequency components; far beats per-number rounding, with a universal tokenizer from 1M real trajectories |
| ReAT <Cite id="reat-2026" /> | Builds each token from the error earlier ones leave; 4 tokens beat 8-token baselines on LIBERO <Cite id="libero-2023" /> |
| LAP <Cite id="lap-2026" /> | Writes tool motions as short phrases |

OpenVLA-OFT's continuous actions and parallel decoding lifted LIBERO success from 76.5% to 97.1% <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Figure says Helix outputs continuous control <Cite id="helix-2025" />. Naively added continuous experts slow training and hurt knowledge transfer, which [knowledge insulation](/manipulation/knowledge-insulation/) fixes <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

One study compared heads on a PaliGemma <Cite id="paligemma-2024" /> VLA in simulated RoboCasa <Cite id="robocasa-2024" />, over five seeds <Cite id="discrete-actions-2026" />.

| Action head | Success |
| --- | --- |
| Binned flow | 53.9% |
| Continuous flow | 52.7% |
| One-pass binned, with guidance | 49.2% |
| One-pass continuous regression | 38.8% |
| One-pass binned | 27.1% |

## Choose the lowest level the task needs

Pick the lowest-level action that the platform runs safely. Across 13,000+ real rollouts and 500+ models, predicting changes from the current pose consistently helped; joint space favored stable control and tool space generalization <Cite id="action-space-design-2026" />. In sim-to-real picking and pushing, joint velocity beat pose and joint-increment actions <Cite id="rl-action-spaces-2026" />.
