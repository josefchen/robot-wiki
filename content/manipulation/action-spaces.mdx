An action space is the interface between a policy and the rest of the robot: what the network predicts, which errors the controller absorbs, how often inference runs and whether embodiments share labels. In Open X-Embodiment, the same seven numbers can mean absolute or relative positions or velocities <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />. Try the check below.

## The contract has four parts

A contract names coordinates (joint, Cartesian, object-relative or another frame), semantics (absolute target, delta, velocity, force, torque or controller parameter), timing (rate, horizon and stale-prediction handling) and execution (interpolation, clipping, inverse kinematics, feedback and safety limits). DROID records equivalent commands in several action spaces <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and robosuite's controllers span joint and Cartesian spaces <Cite id="robosuite-2020" />.

## Joint-space actions

**Joint position targets** are a common imitation baseline: on matched leader-follower rigs, demonstrated angles become labels after calibration checks. ACT uses the leader arms' joint positions as actions, predicting chunks of absolute joint-and-gripper targets for two ViperX arms <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. The controller interpolates, enforces limits and closes a fast loop, but one shoulder angle moves different arms differently.

**Joint velocity targets** encode increments and can drift when integrated. The [kinematics module](/classical/kinematics/) derives the forward map and Jacobian that both need <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />. In locomotion RL, target joint angles with local feedback beat raw torques for learning speed, robustness and motion quality <Cite id="peng-action-space-2016" />.

## Cartesian actions

Cartesian actions say what the tool should do: translate, rotate, open or close. Relative deltas from the current pose can label arms with different joint counts and link lengths alike once range and frame are fixed. Each robot then needs inverse kinematics or an operational-space controller, singularity handling and joint-limit avoidance. Khatib's 1987 IEEE Journal on Robotics and Automation paper on the operational space formulation unifies motion and force control <Cite id="khatib-1987" />.

Euler angles and quaternions are discontinuous as network outputs, while 6D representations are continuous <Cite id="rotation-continuity-2018" />. Axis-angle increments must also name the tool or world frame.

NVIDIA says GR00T N1.7 shares relative end-effector deltas across robot and human embodiments, deciding what non-robot data can become a target <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />. Its N1.6 notes find relative actions smoother and more accurate than absolute ones but prone to error accumulation on small datasets <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />. UMI pairs relative-trajectory actions with latency matching to deploy one policy on several arms <Cite id="umi-2024" />, and a unified end-effector-relative representation was critical for cross-embodiment transfer in matched VLA pretraining ablations <Cite id="vla-scaling-alignment-2026" />. X-VLA instead adds a separate set of learnable soft-prompt embeddings for each data source, which act as embodiment-specific prompts <Cite id="x-vla-2025" />.

## Torque and impedance actions

**Torque actions** expose dynamics and contact; Levine et al. mapped raw images to motor torques <Cite id="levine-visuomotor-2015" />. They need a high rate, accurate timing, actuator-specific scaling and a safety layer that holds when the output is wrong. libfranka sends 1 kHz commands in five modes, including gravity- and friction-compensated joint torque <Cite id="franka-fci-docs" />. Rudin et al.'s locomotion pipeline sends joint-position targets to a PD controller instead <Cite id="rudin-2021" />.

**Impedance actions** sit between: the policy picks a pose target plus stiffness and damping, and a controller turns error into force. Hogan judged position or force control alone inadequate for manipulation <Cite id="hogan-1985" />. Variable end-effector impedance has been studied as an RL action space for contact-rich tasks <Cite id="martin-martin-2019" />. Task-space impedance references needed the fewest samples in a comparison with torque, joint PD and inverse dynamics <Cite id="varin-action-spaces-2019" />, and Adaptive Compliance Policy's predicted compliance beats position-only visuomotor policies by over 50% <Cite id="acp-2024" />.

FACTR 2 (Carnegie Mellon and Waseda University, June 2026) estimates external joint torque from motor current, with no force sensor. Across five long-horizon tasks, its force-aware policy beat prior force-aware policies by over 17% in task progress <Cite id="factr-2-2026" />. T-Rex feeds high-frequency touch to a VLA through a variable-rate mixture of transformers and beat its strongest baseline by over 30% average success on 12 tasks <Cite id="t-rex-2026" />.

## Chunks change the time axis

An <Term id="action-chunking">action chunk</Term> predicts several steps per inference; chunking helped ACT and two retrofitted baselines ([action chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking/)) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. Diffusion Policy executes the first part of a predicted horizon, then replans <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Open-loop chunks go stale when contact changes the scene; receding horizons, temporal ensembling and real-time chunk replacement answer this differently <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" /> ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/)). Rates differ by layer: GEN-1.5 produces 100 Hz action trajectories <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />, and Helix 02's S0 sends joint-level actuator commands at 1 kHz beneath S1's 200 Hz joint targets <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Report predicted and executed horizons separately.

## Tokens change the output geometry

RT-2 <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> and OpenVLA map continuous actions to vocabulary tokens to reuse language-model training, OpenVLA binning seven dimensions into 256 values <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Binning allows cross-entropy, limits resolution and adds autoregressive decoding to latency. FAST quantizes a chunk's frequency coefficients and applies byte-pair encoding, compressing far better than per-dimension binning; its universal FAST+ tokenizer was trained on 1M real robot action trajectories <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. BEAST's B-spline tokens need no tokenizer training and decode in parallel <Cite id="beast-2025" />, and VQ-BeT's vector-quantized actions infer 5 times faster than Diffusion Policy <Cite id="vq-bet-2024" />. With the ActionCodec tokenizer (February 2026), SmolVLM2-2.2B reached 95.5% average LIBERO success without robotics pretraining <Cite id="actioncodec-2026" />. LAP writes end-effector motions as short natural-language phrases with no learned tokenizer, and LAP-3B averaged over 50% zero-shot success on three unseen robots <Cite id="lap-2026" />.

Continuous heads skip tokens: OpenVLA-OFT's continuous actions, parallel decoding and L1 objective lifted LIBERO success from 76.5% to 97.1% <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />, and Figure says Helix outputs continuous control to avoid tokenization <Cite id="helix-2025" />. Naively adding a continuous expert to a VLM harms training speed and knowledge transfer, which [knowledge insulation](/manipulation/knowledge-insulation/) addresses <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. Tokens mean something only under the continuous convention they encode.

## A selection rule

Choose the lowest-level action the task requires and the platform supports safely. Across 13,000+ real rollouts and 500+ models, predicting delta actions consistently helped, with joint space favoring control stability and task space favoring generalization <Cite id="action-space-design-2026" />. Hold controller, rate, horizon and observation history fixed when comparing architectures, or the comparison measures interface changes.
