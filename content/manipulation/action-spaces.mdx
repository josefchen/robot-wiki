## The contract has four parts

Contracts name coordinates (joint, Cartesian, object-relative or another frame), semantics (absolute target, delta, velocity, force, torque or controller parameter), timing (rate, horizon and stale-prediction handling) and execution (interpolation, clipping, inverse kinematics, feedback and safety limits). DROID records equivalent commands across action spaces <Cite id="droid-2024" />; robosuite's controllers span joint and Cartesian spaces <Cite id="robosuite-2020" />.

## Joint-space actions

Common imitation baselines target joint positions; ACT predicts two ViperX arms' absolute joint-and-gripper target chunks <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. The controller interpolates and enforces limits; one shoulder angle moves arms differently.

Integrated joint velocity targets drift; the [kinematics module](/classical/kinematics/) derives the forward map and Jacobian both need <Cite id="modern-robotics-2017" />. In locomotion RL, joint-angle targets with local feedback beat raw torques for learning speed, robustness and motion quality <Cite id="peng-action-space-2016" />.

## Cartesian actions

Cartesian actions specify tool behavior. With range and frame fixed, deltas from current poses label arms with different joint counts and link lengths alike, given each robot's inverse kinematics or operational-space controller; Khatib's 1987 operational space formulation unifies motion and force control <Cite id="khatib-1987" />. Euler angles and quaternions are discontinuous network outputs; 6D representations are continuous <Cite id="rotation-continuity-2018" />.

GR00T N1.6's notes find relative actions smoother and more accurate than absolute ones but prone to error accumulation on small datasets <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />; matched VLA pretraining ablations found unified end-effector-relative representation critical for cross-embodiment transfer <Cite id="vla-scaling-alignment-2026" />. X-VLA instead learns separate embodiment-specific soft-prompt embeddings per data source <Cite id="x-vla-2025" />.

## Torque and impedance actions

Torque actions expose dynamics and contact; Levine et al. mapped raw images to motor torques <Cite id="levine-visuomotor-2015" />. They require high-rate control and a safety layer withstanding wrong outputs: libfranka's five modes send 1 kHz commands, including gravity- and friction-compensated joint torque <Cite id="franka-fci-docs" />; Rudin et al.'s locomotion pipeline sends a PD controller joint-position targets <Cite id="rudin-2021" />.

<Image id="adaptive-compliance-tasks-2024" />

Impedance actions sit between: policies target pose, stiffness and damping; controllers turn error into force. Hogan judged position or force control alone inadequate for manipulation <Cite id="hogan-1985" />. Task-space impedance references needed fewest samples against torque, joint PD and inverse dynamics <Cite id="varin-action-spaces-2019" />; Adaptive Compliance Policy's predicted compliance beats position-only visuomotor policies by over 50% <Cite id="acp-2024" />.

## Chunks change the time axis

An <Term id="action-chunking">action chunk</Term> predicts several steps per inference, helping ACT and two retrofitted baselines ([action chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking/)) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />; Diffusion Policy executes a predicted horizon's first part, then replans <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Open-loop chunks go stale when contact changes the scene, which receding horizons, temporal ensembling and real-time chunk replacement each address <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" /> ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/)).

Rates differ: GEN-1.5 produces 100 Hz action trajectories <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />; Helix 02's S0 sends joint-level actuator commands at 1 kHz beneath S1's 200 Hz joint targets <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Report predicted and executed horizons separately.

## Tokens change the output geometry

RT-2 <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> and OpenVLA map continuous actions to vocabulary tokens, reusing language-model training; OpenVLA bins seven dimensions into 256 values <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Binning enables cross-entropy, limits resolution and adds autoregressive latency. BEAST's fixed-length B-spline tokens encode sequences, allowing parallel decoding without separate tokenizer training <Cite id="beast-2025" />.

FAST byte-pair-encodes chunk frequency coefficients, far outperforming per-dimension binning; training its universal FAST+ tokenizer used 1M real robot action trajectories <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. ActionCodec tests tokenizers' optimization properties, including temporal overlap, vocabulary redundancy and token independence <Cite id="actioncodec-2026" />. VQ-BeT's vector-quantized actions infer 5 times faster than Diffusion Policy <Cite id="vq-bet-2024" />; LAP's short natural-language phrases encode end-effector motions; LAP-3B averages over 50% zero-shot success on three unseen robots <Cite id="lap-2026" />.

Continuous heads skip tokens: OpenVLA-OFT's continuous actions, parallel decoding and L1 objective lifted LIBERO success from 76.5% to 97.1% <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />; Figure says Helix outputs untokenized continuous control <Cite id="helix-2025" />. Naively adding continuous experts to VLMs harms training speed and knowledge transfer, which [knowledge insulation](/manipulation/knowledge-insulation/) addresses <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

Zeji Yi, Max Simchowitz, Guanya Shi and colleagues (Carnegie Mellon and Amazon FAR, October 2026) gave one PaliGemma VLA four action heads: continuous or binned into 512 values, each using one pass or iterative flow. On simulated RoboCasa, over five seeds of 500 episodes, binned flow reached 53.9% success, continuous flow 52.7% and one-pass continuous regression 38.8%. They trace one-pass binned prediction's 27.1% to independently chosen tokens across time steps and action dimensions; guidance raised it to 49.2% <Cite id="discrete-actions-2026" />.

## A selection rule

Choose the lowest-level action the task requires and the platform supports safely. Across 13,000+ real rollouts and 500+ models, delta-action prediction consistently helped; joint space favored control stability; task space favored generalization <Cite id="action-space-design-2026" />. Fix controller, rate, horizon and observation history across architectures, or comparisons measure interface changes. In one vision-based sim-to-real picking-and-pushing study, joint velocity beat tested pose and joint-increment alternatives <Cite id="rl-action-spaces-2026" />.
