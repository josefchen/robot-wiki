A generalist robot policy is one learned model trained for many tasks, often on several robot bodies <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Beyond the [VLA line](/manipulation/vla-models/) and [Physical Intelligence's π models](/manipulation/pi-line/) <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, the main 2026 efforts come from Google DeepMind, NVIDIA, Figure, AgiBot, Skild AI and Generalist AI.

<Image id="gemini-robotics-apollo-franka-2025" />

## A slow thinker and a fast mover

Most of these systems split control in two: a large web-trained model works out what to do a few times a second, and a smaller model turns that into motor commands many times a second. Figure's Helix, for example, runs its reasoning model at 7 to 9 Hz and its motion model at 200 Hz <Cite id="helix-2025" />; GR00T <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, Gemini Robotics <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, GO-2 <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" /> and Skild <Cite id="skild-brain-2025" /> use variants. Disclosure varies more: GR00T publishes weights, most others only videos and success rates without trial counts or release terms.

## Gemini Robotics

DeepMind's March 2025 report introduced two models built on Gemini 2.0: Gemini Robotics-ER for spatial and temporal understanding, and Gemini Robotics, which builds on ER to produce robot actions <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. The launch post said it more than doubled other leading VLAs on a generalization benchmark <Cite id="gemini-robotics-blog-2025" />.

<Image id="gemini-robotics-aloha-dexterous-2025" />

Version 1.5 (October 2025) thinks in words between actions, transfers motions across robot bodies and uses ER 1.5 as an orchestrator <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. In September 2025 ER 1.5 opened to developers and Gemini Robotics 1.5 to select partners <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-blog-2025" />; On-Device (June 2025) had been DeepMind's first VLA offered for fine-tuning, to trusted testers <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />. ER 1.6 reads gauges, zooming in and using code to estimate between marks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-1-6-2026" />.

Gemini Robotics 2 (July 30, 2026) is a VLA, the reasoning model ER 2 and On-Device 2 for local use. One checkpoint runs the Apollo 2 humanoid and Franka's two-arm setup; On-Device 2 adapts to new two-arm robots in a few hours, typically from fewer than 200 examples <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. ER 2 adds success detection on raw video <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-2-2026" />. DeepMind reports whole-body picking on Apollo at 76.3% from a shelf and 45.7% from the floor, and hand tasks from 92% for unscrewing a bulb to 32% for using a dustpan, without trial counts, and concludes "the multi-finger dexterous manipulation remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

Gemini Robotics 2.5 (November 10, 2026), for partners, reports 30 to 100% higher success than earlier baselines across 200 tasks and 96% success kitting parts on Mercedes-Benz lines. It was developed with Gemini 4 Argon <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />, a general model that scores 87% on the ERQA embodied-reasoning benchmark and can control an arm zero-shot <Cite id="gemini-4-argon-robotics-2026" />. DeepMind discloses continuous actions but no action head, chunk length, command rate, size or weight terms <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" /> <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. Its Veo-based [world-model](/world-models/) policy simulator is checked against more than 1,600 real trials of eight checkpoints <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />.

## GR00T: the open humanoid line

NVIDIA's GR00T N1 (March 2025) couples a <Term id="vision-language-model">VLM</Term> to a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action head and published a 2-billion-parameter checkpoint <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. N1.5 keeps the VLM frozen in training <Cite id="gr00t-n1-5-2025" />, and N1.6 beat N1.5 in simulation and on three real robots, NVIDIA reports <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />. N1.7 (April 18, 2026), a 3-billion-parameter checkpoint, describes robot and human motion alike as relative hand positions, and its pretraining adds 20,000 hours of EgoScale human video <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. Its code is Apache 2.0 and its weights carry the NVIDIA Open Model License <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

GR00T 2 (November 9, 2026) is a world action model: one diffusion transformer predicts future video and actions together. It pretrained on 402,730 hours of egocentric human video, 10,184 hours from handheld grippers and 23,500 hours of robot demonstrations. On 50 tasks absent from the target robot's data it succeeded 40% of the time zero-shot, and 95% after five demonstrations each, on tasks chosen because five sufficed. NVIDIA says code arrives in December <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

## Figure Helix

Helix (February 2025) has S2, an internet-pretrained VLM that writes a compact task representation, and S1, a visuomotor transformer that turns it, with images and body state, into wrist, finger, torso and head targets <Cite id="helix-2025" />. Helix 02 (January 2026) adds S0, a small whole-body controller issuing joint commands at 1 kHz. Figure says S0 trained entirely in simulation on over 1,000 hours of retargeted human motion and replaced 109,504 lines of hand-written C++; a four-minute dishwasher demonstration runs 61 actions without resets or help <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Neither post gives repeated-trial success rates or release terms <Cite id="helix-2025" /> <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Figure bets on human video. In September 2025 it reported robots trained only on egocentric human video navigating from commands like "go to the fridge" <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />. Its Index program (August 2026) counts over 16 million uploaded videos and commits over \$1 billion over 12 months to data and compute <Cite id="figure-index-2026" />, and Figure reports that Index pretraining, all else fixed, lifted Helix 2.5's zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56% <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

## AgiBot: GO-1 to GO-3

AgiBot's GO-1 (March 2025) learns a latent action model from consecutive video frames, so training can include unlabeled human video alongside data from several robots <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. It was open-sourced in September 2025, after the 1,003,672-trajectory AgiBot World Beta dataset <Cite id="agibot-world-repo" />. GO-2 targets what AgiBot calls a "Semantic-Actuation Gap": a slower System 2 writes structured action sequences, a faster System 1 combines them with live observations. AgiBot reports 98.5% average success on LIBERO, 86.6% zero-shot on the disturbed LIBERO-Plus and 82.9% on real robots for a model trained only in simulation, and claims to beat π0.5 and GR00T, without trial counts or versions <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />. Typical VLAs fall from 95% to below 30% on LIBERO-Plus <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />. GO-3 Preview (November 2026) adds future-state prediction; AgiBot says it ranks first on three benchmarks and will be open-sourced by the end of 2026 <Cite id="agibot-go3-preview-2026" />.

## Skild AI

Skild AI's January 14, 2026 [Series C](/market-map/) raised \$1.4 billion at a valuation above \$14 billion. Skild calls its model "omni-bodied", pretrained on simulation and internet video and able to control robots without prior knowledge of their body; it gives no architecture, benchmark protocol or weight terms <Cite id="skild-series-c-2026" />. A July 2025 note describes a slower manipulation and navigation policy feeding a faster one that outputs joint angles and torques <Cite id="skild-brain-2025" />, and a September 2025 post describes training on 100,000 simulated robots <Cite id="skild-omni-bodied-2025" />. S1 (August 18, 2026) learns in context: shown a video of a task, it performs it, and on unseen tasks of 4 to 8 minutes Skild reports 66% cumulative per-step success against 9% for language prompts <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />. S1 then learned soccer through 140 years of simulated self-play before moving to a robot <Cite id="skild-self-play-2026" />, and reports passing \$100 million in annual recurring revenue <Cite id="skild-arr-2026" />.

## Generalist AI

Generalist AI says GEN-0 (November 2025) is pretrained on over 270,000 hours of [real-world manipulation data](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck/), growing by 10,000 hours a week <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />. It reports that GEN-1 (April 2026), trained from scratch on half a million hours, reaches 99% average success on tasks where earlier models achieve 64%, with 1 hour of robot data per task <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />, and that GEN-1.5 (August 2026) learns a task from one demonstration of 3 to 12 seconds, with 59% average one-shot success across 10 tasks <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />.

## Reading vendor claims

Most results above are self-reported. RoboArena ranks policies through double-blind pairwise trials at seven institutions, with more than 600 real-robot episodes across seven policies <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />, and TRI tests its Large Behavior Models in blind, randomized trials with statistical confidence <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Shared benchmarks now compare vendors: on NVIDIA's RoboLab-120, Runway reports 46.2% for its open-weight Praxis-1 against 28.0% for π0.5 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" />, and Apple reports that AMP1, a general-purpose AI model commanding the arm directly, reached 85.6% zero-shot against 49.2% for the leading VLA <Cite id="apple-amp1-2026" />.

## Learning from human video

Physical Intelligence and Georgia Tech co-fine-tune π0.5 on human data, with 3D hand positions as actions, plus related robot data; transfer grows with the amount and variety of robot pretraining <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />. EgoScale adds aligned human-robot mid-training and finds scale and alignment complementary <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. [Cross-embodiment transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) compares them.

NYU's Steertuning (Lerrel Pinto and colleagues, October 2026) pretrains a diffusion policy on egocentric human video alone, then adapts it to a new task from 5 human demonstrations, 2 minutes of fine-tuning and steering toward the closest demonstration. On a mobile manipulator the authors report 71% average success over 12 new tasks; on three lab tasks it succeeded in 25 of 30 trials, against 7 with steering alone and none with the short fine-tune alone <Cite id="steertuning-2026" />.
