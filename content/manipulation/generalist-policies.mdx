A generalist robot policy is one learned model trained for many tasks, often on several robot bodies <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. Beyond the [VLA line](/manipulation/vla-models/) and [Physical Intelligence's π models](/manipulation/pi-line/) <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, the main efforts come from Google DeepMind, NVIDIA, Figure, AgiBot, Skild AI and Generalist AI.

<Image id="gemini-robotics-apollo-franka-2025" />

## A slow thinker and a fast mover

Most of these systems split control in two: a large web-trained model decides a few times a second, and a smaller one sends motor commands many times a second. Figure's Helix runs its reasoning model at 7 to 9 Hz and its motion model at 200 Hz <Cite id="helix-2025" />. GR00T publishes weights; most others publish videos and success rates without trial counts or release terms.

Also: the same split in GR00T <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, Gemini Robotics <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, GO-2 <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />, Skild <Cite id="skild-brain-2025" />.

## Gemini Robotics discloses results, not architecture

DeepMind discloses continuous actions but no action head, chunk length, command rate, size or weight terms <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" /> <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

<Image id="gemini-robotics-aloha-dexterous-2025" />

| Release | What it added |
| --- | --- |
| Gemini Robotics and ER, March 2025 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" /> | Actions and spatial reasoning on Gemini 2.0; more than doubled other leading VLAs on a generalization benchmark <Cite id="gemini-robotics-blog-2025" /> |
| On-Device, June 2025 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" /> | DeepMind's first VLA offered for fine-tuning, to trusted testers |
| 1.5, October 2025 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" /> | Thinks in words between actions and transfers motions across bodies; ER 1.5 opened to developers <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-blog-2025" /> |
| ER 1.6 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-1-6-2026" /> | Reads gauges by zooming in and using code |
| 2, July 2026 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" /> | One checkpoint for Apollo 2 and Franka's two arms; On-Device 2 adapts in hours from under 200 examples; ER 2 detects success on raw video <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-2-2026" /> |
| 2.5, November 2026 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" /> | 30 to 100% higher success across 200 tasks; 96% kitting parts on Mercedes-Benz lines |

On Apollo, Gemini Robotics 2 picked from a shelf 76.3% of the time and from the floor 45.7%, without trial counts <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

Also: Gemini 4 Argon, 87% on ERQA <Cite id="gemini-4-argon-robotics-2026" />; a Veo-based [world-model](/world-models/) simulator checked against 1,600 real trials <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />.

## GR00T is the open humanoid line

| Release | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| N1, March 2025 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" /> | A <Term id="vision-language-model">VLM</Term> plus a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action head; open 2-billion-parameter checkpoint |
| N1.5 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-5-2025" /> | VLM frozen in training |
| N1.6 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" /> | Beat N1.5 in simulation and on three real robots, NVIDIA reports |
| N1.7, April 2026 <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" /> | 3 billion parameters; human and robot motion as relative hand positions; 20,000 hours of EgoScale video <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />; Apache 2.0 code, NVIDIA Open Model License weights |
| GR00T 2, November 2026 <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> | A world action model: one diffusion transformer predicts video and actions |

GR00T 2 pretrained on 402,730 hours of egocentric human video, 10,184 hours from handheld grippers and 23,500 hours of robot demonstrations. On 50 tasks new to the target robot it succeeded 40% of the time zero-shot and 95% after five demonstrations, on tasks chosen because five sufficed <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.

## Figure Helix bets on human video

Helix (February 2025) pairs S2, an internet-pretrained VLM that writes a compact task representation, with S1, a transformer that turns it into wrist, finger, torso and head targets <Cite id="helix-2025" />. Helix 02 (January 2026) adds S0, a 1 kHz whole-body controller that Figure says replaced 109,504 lines of hand-written C++. Neither post gives repeated-trial success rates or release terms <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Figure's Index program counts over 16 million uploaded videos and commits over \$1 billion over 12 months to data and compute <Cite id="figure-index-2026" />. Index pretraining lifted Helix 2.5's zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56% <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

Also: navigation learned from human video alone <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />.

## AgiBot reports high scores without trial counts

GO-1 (March 2025) learns a latent action model from consecutive video frames, so it can train on unlabeled human video <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. It was open-sourced in September 2025, after the 1,003,672-trajectory AgiBot World Beta dataset <Cite id="agibot-world-repo" />.

GO-2's slower System 2 writes structured action sequences for a faster System 1. AgiBot reports 98.5% on LIBERO and 86.6% zero-shot on the disturbed LIBERO-Plus, without trial counts or versions <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />; typical VLAs fall there from 95% to below 30% <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />. GO-3 Preview (November 2026) adds future-state prediction and is promised as open source by the end of 2026 <Cite id="agibot-go3-preview-2026" />.

## Skild AI raised the most and shows the least

Skild AI's January 2026 [Series C](/market-map/) raised \$1.4 billion at a valuation above \$14 billion. It calls its model "omni-bodied" and publishes no architecture, benchmark protocol or weight terms <Cite id="skild-series-c-2026" />. Shown a video of an unseen 4 to 8 minute task, S1 (August 2026) reached 66% cumulative per-step success, against 9% from language prompts <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />.

Also: 100,000 simulated robots <Cite id="skild-omni-bodied-2025" />, soccer from 140 years of simulated self-play <Cite id="skild-self-play-2026" />, over \$100 million in annual recurring revenue <Cite id="skild-arr-2026" />.

| Model | Data | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GEN-0, November 2025 <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" /> | Over 270,000 hours of [real-world manipulation data](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck/) | Data growing by 10,000 hours a week |

## Blind trials and shared benchmarks check the vendors

Most results above are self-reported. RoboArena ranks policies through double-blind pairwise trials at seven institutions, over more than 600 real-robot episodes <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />. TRI tests its Large Behavior Models in blind, randomized trials with statistical confidence <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

<Image id="roboarena-evaluation-2025" />

On NVIDIA's RoboLab-120, Runway reports 46.2% for its open Praxis-1 against 28.0% for π0.5 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" />. Gemini 4 Argon, commanding the arm through Apple's AMP1 harness, reached 85.6% zero-shot against 49.2% for BiMind v0.5, the top VLA entry <Cite id="apple-amp1-2026" />.

## Human video transfers best after broad robot pretraining

Physical Intelligence and Georgia Tech co-fine-tune π0.5 on human data, with 3D hand positions as actions; transfer grows with the amount and variety of robot pretraining <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />. EgoScale finds scale and aligned human-robot mid-training complementary <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. [Cross-embodiment transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) compares them.

NYU's Steertuning pretrains a diffusion policy on human video alone, then adapts it from 5 human demonstrations, 2 minutes of fine-tuning and steering toward the closest demonstration. On three lab tasks it succeeded in 25 of 30 trials, against 7 with steering alone and none with the short fine-tune alone <Cite id="steertuning-2026" />.
