A generalist robot policy is one learned model trained to perform many tasks, often on several robot bodies <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

<Image id="gemini-robotics-apollo-franka-2025" />

Outside the [VLA line](/manipulation/vla-models/) and [Physical Intelligence's π models](/manipulation/pi-line/), the main 2026 lines are Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />, NVIDIA's GR00T, Figure's Helix, AgiBot's GO series, Skild AI and Generalist AI. ## Gemini Robotics

DeepMind's March 2025 report introduces a Gemini 2.0-based family: Gemini Robotics-ER for spatial and temporal understanding, and Gemini Robotics, which builds on ER to produce robot actions <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. The launch post says Gemini Robotics more than doubles performance on a comprehensive generalization benchmark against other state-of-the-art VLAs <Cite id="gemini-robotics-blog-2025" />.

<Image id="gemini-robotics-aloha-dexterous-2025" />

The 1.5 report, first submitted to arXiv on October 2, 2025, interleaves natural-language thinking with VLA actions, introduces Motion Transfer across embodiments and uses the separate GR-ER 1.5 model as an orchestrator <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. The launch post, dated September 25, 2025, made ER 1.5 available through the Gemini API and Gemini Robotics 1.5 to select partners <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-blog-2025" />, after On-Device (June 2025) became the first DeepMind VLA offered for fine-tuning, to trusted testers <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />.

On July 30, 2026, DeepMind announced Gemini Robotics 2 as three models: a VLA, the embodied-reasoning VLM ER 2, and On-Device 2 for local operation. Apollo 2 walks, crouches and stretches to manipulate objects, and one checkpoint runs Apollo 2 with 22-<Term id="degrees-of-freedom">DoF</Term> SharpaWave hands or Inspire hands and Franka Duo with the Robotiq gripper; DeepMind says On-Device 2 adapts to new bi-arm embodiments in a few hours, typically with fewer than 200 examples <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. The ER 2 post adds success detection on raw video feeds <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-2-2026" />.

DeepMind's image alt text reports whole-body averages on Apollo with Inspire hands of 76.3% for picking from a shelf, 68.4% from a table and 45.7% from the floor. Single-task results on Apollo with Sharpa hands are 92% for unscrewing a bulb, 36% for screwing one in, 44% for tying a trash bag, 40% for Ziplock and 32% for Dustpan. Trial counts and error bars are unexplained, and DeepMind concludes that "the multi-finger dexterous manipulation remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

The 1.5 model card specifies continuous numerical actions but no action-head family, chunk lengths, command rate or parameter count <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. The Gemini Robotics 2 announcement also omits them, the VLA went only to early-access partners, and weight and licence terms are undisclosed <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. DeepMind validates its Veo-based [world-model](/world-models/) policy simulator against more than 1,600 real trials of eight Gemini Robotics checkpoints <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />.

## GR00T: the open humanoid line

GR00T N1 (March 2025) couples a <Term id="vision-language-model">VLM</Term> backbone to a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> diffusion-transformer action head and links the public GR00T-N1-2B checkpoint <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. N1.5 freezes the VLM in pretraining and fine-tuning <Cite id="gr00t-n1-5-2025" />, and N1.6, which doubles the DiT to 32 layers, outperforms N1.5 in simulation and on real YAM, AgiBot Genie-1 and Unitree G1 robots, NVIDIA reports <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />.

The Isaac-GR00T repository lists N1.7 on April 18, 2026, with a Cosmos-Reason2-2B backbone, a 3B-parameter base checkpoint and relative end-effector actions shared by robot and human embodiments; pretraining adds 20K hours of EgoScale human video <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />, which the EgoScale paper puts at over 20,854 hours <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. The README's licence section assigns Apache 2.0 to code and the NVIDIA Open Model License to weights, while its introduction calls N1.7 "fully commercially licensable under Apache 2.0" <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

## Figure Helix

Figure's February 20, 2025 Helix post pairs S2, an internet-pretrained VLM producing a latent task representation at 7 to 9 Hz, with S1, a visuomotor transformer that reads it with images and robot state at 200 Hz and outputs wrist, finger, torso and head targets <Cite id="helix-2025" />.

Figure's January 27, 2026 Helix 02 post adds S0, a 10M-parameter whole-body controller that outputs joint-level actuator commands at 1 kHz beneath S1's 200 Hz full-body joint targets. Figure says S0 trains entirely in simulation on over 1,000 hours of retargeted human motion and replaces 109,504 lines of hand-written C++, and describes its dishwasher demonstration as one continuous four-minute task with 61 loco-manipulation actions, no resets and no human intervention <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Neither Helix post gives repeated-trial success tables or release terms for the trained policies <Cite id="helix-2025" /> <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

Figure's August 25, 2026 Index announcement counts over 16M uploaded videos and commits over \$1B over 12 months to data and compute <Cite id="figure-index-2026" />, and Figure reports that Index pretraining, all else fixed, lifted Helix 2.5's zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56% <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

## AgiBot: GO-1 and GO-2

The AgiBot World report (March 2025) introduces GO-1, whose latent action model learns from consecutive video frames, so training can include unlabeled human video alongside cross-embodiment robot data <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. AgiBot presents GO-2 as closing a "Semantic-Actuation Gap": a lower-frequency System 2 generates structured action sequences, a higher-frequency System 1 combines them with live observations, and teacher forcing trains the executor to follow imperfect reasoning. The announcement gives only relative rates for the two modules and says the work was accepted at CVPR 2026 and ACL 2026 <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />.

AgiBot reports 98.5% average success across the four LIBERO suites, 86.6% zero-shot in LIBERO-Plus environments with disturbances, and 82.9% real-world success for a model trained only on simulation. It claims superiority over π0.5 and GR00T without trial counts, uncertainty, disturbance protocol or comparator versions <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />, and on LIBERO-Plus typical VLAs drop from 95% to below 30% under modest perturbations <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />.

## Skild AI

Skild AI's January 14, 2026 [Series C](/market-map/) raised \$1.4 billion at a valuation of over \$14 billion. Skild calls its model "omni-bodied", names simulation and Internet video for pretraining and teleoperation and deployments for post-training, and says the model can control robots without prior knowledge of their body form. Architecture, parameter count, control rate, chunk horizon, benchmark protocol and weight terms are not given <Cite id="skild-series-c-2026" />.

A September 2025 post describes training on 100,000 simulated robots <Cite id="skild-omni-bodied-2025" />. Skild presents S1 (August 18, 2026) as an in-context learner: shown a video of a task, it executes it, and on unseen long-horizon tasks of 4 to 8 minutes Skild reports 66% cumulative per-step success, against 9% for language prompting <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />. The company also reports passing \$100M in annual recurring revenue <Cite id="skild-arr-2026" />.

## Generalist AI

Generalist AI says GEN-0 (November 2025) is pretrained on over 270,000 hours of [real-world manipulation data](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck/), growing by 10,000 hours a week <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />. It reports that GEN-1 (April 2026), trained from scratch on half a million hours, reaches 99% average success on tasks where earlier models achieve 64%, with 1 hour of robot data per task <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />, and that GEN-1.5 (August 2026) learns a task in context from one 3 to 12 second demonstration, with 59% average one-shot success across 10 tasks <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />.

## Independent evaluation

RoboArena ranks generalist policies through double-blind pairwise trials at seven institutions, with more than 600 real-robot episodes across seven policies <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />, and TRI evaluates its Large Behavior Models in blind, randomized trials with statistical confidence <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

## Human-video transfer

Physical Intelligence and Georgia Tech co-fine-tune π0.5 on human data, with 3D hand positions as actions, and relevant robot data, and transfer improves with the quantity and diversity of robot pretraining <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />. EgoScale adds aligned human-robot mid-training and finds scale and alignment complementary <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. [Cross-embodiment transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) compares them.
