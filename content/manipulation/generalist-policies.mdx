A generalist robot policy is one learned model trained to perform many tasks, often on several robot bodies <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

Outside the [VLA line](/manipulation/vla-models/) and [Physical Intelligence's π models](/manipulation/pi-line/), the main 2026 lines are Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />, NVIDIA's GR00T, Figure's Helix, AgiBot's GO series, Skild AI and Generalist AI. ## Gemini Robotics

DeepMind's March 2025 report introduces a Gemini 2.0-based family. Gemini Robotics-ER extends spatial and temporal understanding for pointing, trajectory prediction and grasp prediction, and Gemini Robotics builds on ER to produce robot actions <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. The March 12, 2025 launch post says Gemini Robotics more than doubles performance on a comprehensive generalization benchmark against other state-of-the-art VLAs, announces an Apptronik humanoid partnership and gives ER to trusted testers <Cite id="gemini-robotics-blog-2025" />.

The 1.5 report, first submitted to arXiv on October 2, 2025, interleaves natural-language thinking with VLA actions, introduces Motion Transfer across embodiments and uses the separate GR-ER 1.5 model as an orchestrator. Varying ER's thinking budget shows that the useful amount of thinking depends on the task <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. The launch post, dated September 25, 2025, made ER 1.5 available through the Gemini API in Google AI Studio and Gemini Robotics 1.5 to select partners <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-blog-2025" />.

Gemini Robotics On-Device (June 2025) was the first DeepMind VLA offered for fine-tuning, through an SDK for trusted testers <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />. ER 1.6, released on April 14, 2026 through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio, added reading of gauges and sight glasses, a use case found with Boston Dynamics <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-1-6-2026" />.

On July 30, 2026, DeepMind announced Gemini Robotics 2 as three models: a VLA that converts vision and language into motor control, the embodied-reasoning VLM ER 2, and On-Device 2 for local operation. Apollo 2 walks, crouches and stretches to manipulate objects, where earlier humanoid models did upper-body tabletop tasks. The SharpaWave hand has 22 <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">DoF</Term>, and the Franka Duo uses two-fingered parallel grippers. One checkpoint runs Apollo 2 with SharpaWave hands, Apollo 2 with Inspire hands and Franka Duo with the Robotiq gripper <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

ER 2 plans multi-step tasks lasting several minutes, tracks progress, coordinates with the VLA and supports collaboration between robots. DeepMind says On-Device 2 adapts to new bi-arm embodiments in a few hours, typically with fewer than 200 examples, shown on Dexmate, SO101 and Trossen <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. The ER 2 post adds success detection on raw video feeds and instrument reading tested on 10 instrument types <Cite id="gemini-robotics-er-2-2026" />.

DeepMind's image alt text reports whole-body averages on Apollo with Inspire hands of 76.3% for picking from a shelf, 68.4% from a table and 45.7% from the floor. Single-task results on Apollo with Sharpa hands are 92% for unscrewing a bulb, 36% for screwing one in, 44% for tying a trash bag, 40% for Ziplock and 32% for Dustpan. Trial counts and error bars are unexplained, and DeepMind concludes that "the multi-finger dexterous manipulation remains challenging" <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

The 1.5 model card specifies continuous numerical actions, plus text when thinking is enabled, but no action-head family, chunk lengths, command rate or parameter count <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />. The Gemini Robotics 2 announcement also omits them and Motion Transfer internals. ER 2 launched on Google AI Studio and in private preview on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, the VLA and On-Device 2 went to early-access partners, and weight and licence terms are undisclosed <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />. DeepMind validates its Veo-based [world-model](/world-models/) policy simulator against more than 1,600 real trials of eight Gemini Robotics checkpoints <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />.

## GR00T: the open humanoid line

GR00T N1, first submitted in March 2025, couples a <Term id="vision-language-model">VLM</Term> backbone to a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> diffusion-transformer action head that reads vision-language tokens through cross-attention, and links the public GR00T-N1-2B checkpoint <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. N1.5, dated June 11, 2025, freezes the VLM in pretraining and fine-tuning and was trained for 250K steps on 1K H100 GPUs <Cite id="gr00t-n1-5-2025" />. N1.6, dated December 15, 2025, doubles the DiT to 32 layers, and NVIDIA reports it outperforms N1.5 in simulation and on real YAM, AgiBot Genie-1 and Unitree G1 robots <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />.

The Isaac-GR00T repository lists N1.7 on April 18, 2026, with a Cosmos-Reason2-2B backbone on the Qwen3-VL architecture and a 3B-parameter base checkpoint. The DiT returns from 32 to 16 layers, the state and action interface grows to 132 dimensions, and the prediction horizon to 40. Actions are relative end-effector deltas shared by robot and human embodiments, and pretraining adds 20K hours of EgoScale human video. Inference needs one GPU with 16 GB or more of VRAM <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />. The EgoScale paper itself reports over 20,854 hours <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />.

The README's licence section assigns Apache 2.0 to code and the NVIDIA Open Model License to weights, while its introduction calls N1.7 "fully commercially licensable under Apache 2.0". NVIDIA describes N1.7 as comparable to N1.6, with better generalization and language following <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />. On March 16, 2026, NVIDIA put N1.7 into early access with commercial licensing and previewed GR00T N2, a DreamZero-based model slated for year-end that it says succeeds at new tasks more than twice as often as leading VLAs <Cite id="nvidia-gtc-gr00t-n2-2026" />.

## Figure Helix

Figure's February 20, 2025 Helix post pairs two systems. S2, an internet-pretrained VLM, produces a continuous latent task representation at 7 to 9 Hz, and S1, a latent-conditioned visuomotor transformer, reads it with images and robot state at 200 Hz. S1 outputs wrist poses, finger flexion and abduction, torso and head orientation targets, and a synthetic task-completion signal. The post calls the S2 backbone open-source and open-weight <Cite id="helix-2025" />.

Figure's January 27, 2026 Helix 02 post adds a third layer. S2 interprets scenes and language and sequences latent goals. S1 maps head and palm cameras, fingertip touch and full-body proprioception to full-body joint targets at 200 Hz. S0, a 10M-parameter whole-body controller, outputs joint-level actuator commands at 1 kHz. Figure says S0 trains entirely in simulation on over 1,000 hours of joint-level retargeted human motion, using sim-to-real reinforcement learning across more than 200,000 domain-randomized parallel environments. It replaces 109,504 lines of hand-written C++. Its dishwasher demonstration is described as one continuous four-minute task with 61 loco-manipulation actions, implicit error recovery, no resets and no human intervention <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Neither Helix post gives repeated-trial success tables or release terms for the trained policies <Cite id="helix-2025" /> <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />.

In September 2025 Figure reported that robots trained only on egocentric human video navigate from commands like "go to the fridge" <Cite id="figure-go-big-2025" />. Its August 25, 2026 Index announcement counts over 16M uploaded videos and commits over \$1B over 12 months to data and compute <Cite id="figure-index-2026" />. Figure reports that Index pretraining, all else fixed, lifted Helix 2.5's zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56% <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

## AgiBot: GO-1 and GO-2

The AgiBot World report, first submitted in March 2025, introduces GO-1 and its vision-language-latent-action (ViLLA) framework. A latent action model learns from consecutive video frames, a VLM-conditioned planner predicts latent action tokens, and an action expert generates low-level actions. Training includes unlabeled human video and cross-embodiment robot data, and the report presents Colosseo as an open platform <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. The repository dates AgiBot World Beta, with 1,003,672 trajectories, to March 1, 2025 and the open-sourcing of GO-1 to September 19, 2025 <Cite id="agibot-world-repo" />.

AgiBot presents GO-2 as closing a "Semantic-Actuation Gap" between reasoning and motor commands. Its Action Chain-of-Thought first generates a macro-plan of action intents. A lower-frequency System 2, the "General Commander", generates structured action sequences through Progressive Refinement, and a higher-frequency System 1, the "Agile Executor", combines them with live observations and corrects noise with Residual Refinement. Teacher forcing trains the executor to follow imperfect reasoning. The announcement gives only relative rates for the two modules <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />. AgiBot says its action-reasoning work was accepted at CVPR 2026 and its asynchronous architecture at ACL 2026 <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />. The ACoT-VLA paper's arXiv record confirms the CVPR acceptance <Cite id="acot-vla-2026" />.

AgiBot reports 98.5% average success across LIBERO Spatial, Object, Goal and Long; 86.6% zero-shot in LIBERO-Plus environments with disturbances; and 82.9% real-world success for a model trained only on simulation, under a Genie Sim 3.0 heading. It claims superiority over π0.5 and GR00T without trial counts, uncertainty, disturbance protocol or comparator versions <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />. On LIBERO-Plus, typical VLAs drop from 95% to below 30% under modest perturbations <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />.

## Skild AI

Skild AI's January 14, 2026 [Series C](/market-map/) raised \$1.4 billion at a valuation of over \$14 billion. Skild calls its model "omni-bodied" and says it can control robots without prior knowledge of their body form. It names simulation and Internet video for pretraining and teleoperation and deployments for post-training, with teleoperation mapping images and proprioception to joint-torque sequences. Architecture, parameter count, control rate, chunk horizon, benchmark protocol and weight terms are not given <Cite id="skild-series-c-2026" />.

A July 2025 Skild post describes a low-frequency manipulation and navigation policy that feeds a high-frequency low-level policy <Cite id="skild-brain-2025" />, and a September 2025 post describes training on 100,000 simulated robots <Cite id="skild-omni-bodied-2025" />. Skild presents S1 (August 18, 2026) as an in-context learner: shown a video of a task, it executes it. On unseen long-horizon tasks of 4 to 8 minutes, Skild reports 66% cumulative per-step success at 100k hours of pretraining, against 9% for language prompting <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />. Skild says S1 then learned soccer through 140 years of simulated self-play before transfer to a robot <Cite id="skild-self-play-2026" />. The company also reports passing \$100M in annual recurring revenue with 60+ paying customers <Cite id="skild-arr-2026" />.

## Generalist AI

Generalist AI says GEN-0 (November 2025) is pretrained on over 270,000 hours of [real-world manipulation data](/data-hardware/data-bottleneck/), growing by 10,000 hours a week, and tested on 6-DoF, 7-DoF and 16+-DoF semi-humanoid robots <Cite id="generalist-gen0-2025" />. It reports that GEN-1 (April 2026), trained from scratch on half a million hours, reaches 99% average success on tasks where earlier models achieve 64%. It also reports GEN-1 works about 3 times faster than the state of the art, with 1 hour of robot data per task <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. Generalist says GEN-1.5 (August 2026) outputs 100 Hz actions and learns a task from 3 to 12 seconds of one demonstration in context, with 59% average one-shot success across 10 tasks <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />.

## Independent evaluation

RoboArena ranks generalist policies through double-blind pairwise trials at seven institutions, with more than 600 real-robot episodes across seven policies <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />, and TRI evaluates its Large Behavior Models in blind, randomized trials with statistical confidence <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

## Human-video transfer

Simar Kareer and colleagues at Physical Intelligence and Georgia Tech co-fine-tune π0.5 on human data, with 3D hand positions as actions, and relevant robot data, with no special transfer mechanism. Transfer improves with the quantity and diversity of robot pretraining <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />. Ruijie Zheng and colleagues' EgoScale adds aligned human-robot mid-training to relative wrist motion and retargeted hand actions, and finds scale and alignment complementary <Cite id="egoscale-2026" />. [Cross-embodiment transfer](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) compares them.
