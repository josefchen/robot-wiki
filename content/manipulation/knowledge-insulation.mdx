<Term id="knowledge-insulation">Knowledge insulation</Term> (KI) trains a vision-language-action model so that learning to move does not erase what its pretrained backbone knew. KI trains the backbone to predict discretized actions, while a separate <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action expert generates continuous actions; gradients from the action expert are not back-propagated to the backbone <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

## KI uses action tokens and an action expert at once

The first VLAs wrote actions as words: RT-2 rounds each action number to one of 256 levels, as RT-1 did <Cite id="rt1-2022" /> <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Physical Intelligence argued that such action tokenization is not amenable to expressing fast and precise motor commands <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />, and π0 therefore augments a pretrained PaliGemma VLM <Cite id="paligemma-2024" /> <Cite id="pi0-2024" /> with a separate action expert that predicts actions in continuous action space. π0.5 used tokens first and a new flow-matching expert second <Cite id="pi05-2025" />; KI does both in one stage <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

Also: OpenVLA's 256 levels <Cite id="openvla-2024" />.

## A new expert damages the backbone

Gradients from a fresh action expert are detrimental to the pretrained backbone, resulting in slower convergence and worse language following: π0 picks up trash when asked to place a spoon in the trash bin <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. More broadly, fine-tuning with a new randomly initialized output head distorts pretrained features <Cite id="kumar-finetuning-distorts-2022" />; in the context of VLAs, ChatVLA has referred to this effect as spurious forgetting <Cite id="chatvla-2025" />.

Also: pretrained VLAs resist forgetting between robot tasks <Cite id="vla-continual-forgetting-2026" />.

## The recipe: two outputs and a stop-gradient

1. The action expert trains with flow matching <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" /> to predict continuous action chunks.
2. The backbone is trained with vision-language data and FAST discrete <Term id="action-tokenization">action tokens</Term>. Similar to JPEG for images, FAST represents the motion of each joint as tokens that can be decoded into smooth waves <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />.
3. A stop-gradient is applied when feeding backbone features into the action expert, to avoid back-propagating gradients to the backbone.

The compute cost of producing both outputs is about 20% higher <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

## Insulation trains faster

Compared to π0.5 + KI, a many-robot π0 generalist required 7.5 times as many training steps to attain comparable performance on the table-bussing task, and π0-FAST specialists required twice as much wall-clock time (autoregressive, token-by-token generation is slow). On a mobile manipulation task requiring the robot to place unseen objects into a drawer, co-training with web data was found to confer the greatest improvement in generalization <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" /> <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

The stop-gradient alone does not adapt the backbone: frozen, the paper's shirt-folding policy scores 0%. FAST supervision provides the learning signal for the backbone <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

## Outside tests are mixed

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| APT <Cite id="apt-action-expert-pretraining-2026" /> | π0.5 with KI stays near zero on out-of-distribution task changes |
| Representation anchoring <Cite id="anchor-align-2026" /> | A reimplemented co-training plus KI baseline trailed behavior cloning on LIBERO-PRO <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" /> and LIBERO-Plus <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" /> |
| Self-demonstrated control <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" /> | FAST plus flow forgets less than flow alone |
| Knowledge retention across seven VLAs <Cite id="act2answer-2026" /> | Co-training on visual question answering goes with better retention |
| TRI, 89 policies <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" /> | Robot-only training cost the backbone its ability to generate language |

Hancock et al. at NVIDIA argue insulation should not be a default. Trained jointly from the start, letting expert gradients reach the backbone raised closed-loop driving progress by 17 to 40% <Cite id="gradient-routing-mot-vla-2026" />.

## Others protect the backbone differently

| Model | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| GR00T N1 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" /> | Freezes the language part of its backbone |
| GR00T N1.5 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-5-2025" /> | Freezes all of it; real GR-1 language following rose from 46.6% to 93.3% |
| GR00T N1.6 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" /> | Unfreezes the top 4 layers |
| VLM2VLA <Cite id="vlm2vla-2025" /> | Writes actions as language and trains only small adapters |
| UAM <Cite id="uam-forgetting-2026" /> | Keeps over 95% of multimodal ability with no freezing, gradient stopping or co-training |
| Helix <Cite id="helix-2025" /> | Lets its fast policy's gradients into the backbone |
| HybridVLA <Cite id="hybridvla-2025" /> | Trains discrete and diffusion action prediction together |
| InstructVLA <Cite id="instructvla-2025" /> | A mixture of experts on vision-language and robot data |

## Physical Intelligence still uses it

openpi releases π0.5 checkpoints trained with KI but only provides the flow-matching head <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. LAP-3B blocks expert gradients and runs at 25 Hz on a gaming GPU <Cite id="lap-2026" />.

Also: LabVLA <Cite id="labvla-2026" />, Hugging Face's FineART-VLA <Cite id="fineart-2026" />, DM0's hybrid <Cite id="dm0-2026" />, a 2026 review of implementations <Cite id="stulp-full-stack-transfer-2026" />.
