<Term id="knowledge-insulation">Knowledge insulation</Term> (KI) is a training recipe that keeps action learning from damaging a vision-language-action model's pretrained backbone. Discrete FAST action tokens train the backbone, while a stop-gradient keeps a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action expert's gradients out of it <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. Turn off "Protect language skills" to remove the stop-gradient.

## Origins

RT-2 tokenizes each continuous action dimension into 256 uniform bins, as RT-1 did <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Physical Intelligence's 2025 KI note judges such fixed-bin tokens unsuitable for high-frequency, precise or fluent motions <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />, and the 7B token-based OpenVLA decodes at about 6 Hz on an RTX 4090 <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. π0 adds a continuous action expert to a pretrained <Term id="vision-language-model">VLM</Term> <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. π0.5 trained 280k steps on discrete tokens, then post-trained a randomly initialized flow-matching expert <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. KI makes this a single stage <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />, called π0.5 + KI <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

## Naive joint training

Fine-tuning a pretrained network through a random head distorts its features and raises out-of-distribution error <Cite id="kumar-finetuning-distorts-2022" />. The KI paper finds the same in VLAs: gradients from a new action expert degrade the backbone, slowing training and weakening language following. Told to bus a spoon into the bin, π0 grasps trash <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. The note hypothesizes that disrupted language processing lets the model learn other correlations first <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

ChatVLA calls this spurious forgetting, robot training overwriting visual-text alignments <Cite id="chatvla-2025" />, and InstructVLA reports catastrophic forgetting of vision-language skills in existing VLAs <Cite id="instructvla-2025" />. Between robot tasks, pretrained VLAs resist forgetting; simple experience replay sometimes removes it <Cite id="vla-continual-forgetting-2026" />.

## Recipe

1. The action expert trains with flow matching, regressing vector fields of fixed conditional probability paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, and generates continuous action chunks at inference <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.
2. The backbone learns from discrete FAST <Term id="action-tokenization">action tokens</Term> plus vision-language and robot-planning data. FAST normalizes actions, applies a DCT to each action dimension over time, and scales and rounds the coefficients, the lossy step. Byte-pair encoding then compresses the flattened sequence losslessly <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />.
3. Stop-gradient blocks the expert's updates to the backbone. The expert attends to the image, language and state prefix and to itself, never to FAST tokens; the backbone never attends to it. Stop-gradient on the backbone keys and values the expert reads keeps the forward path and cuts the backward one <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

Training both outputs costs about 20% more compute. The paper's backbone is a 2B language model with a 300M expert <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />; the note labels the complete VLM 3B <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />, the size of PaliGemma, which pairs a SigLIP-So400m encoder with Gemma-2B <Cite id="paligemma-2024" />.

FAST tokens are π0.5 + KI's training representation and continuous flow actions its runtime output <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />, so a [comparison matrix](/manipulation/comparison-matrix/) needs both columns.

## Limits and alternatives

Stopping expert gradients does not adapt the backbone: with the backbone frozen, the paper's shirt-folding policy scores 0%. FAST supervision supplies that signal, and vision-language co-training can give plain joint training good language following without stop-gradient <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

GR00T N1 freezes the language part of its VLM <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. NVIDIA reports that N1.5, which froze the whole VLM among other changes, raised real GR-1 language following from 46.6% to 93.3% <Cite id="gr00t-n1-5-2025" />; N1.6 unfreezes the top 4 layers <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />. BLIP-2 bridges frozen image encoders and language models <Cite id="blip2-2023" />. VLM2VLA writes actions as language and trains only LoRA adapters <Cite id="vlm2vla-2025" />. UAM keeps over 95% of its VLM's multimodal capability with no freezing, gradient stopping or co-training <Cite id="uam-forgetting-2026" />.

Figure's Helix instead backpropagates from its fast policy into the VLM through their shared latent vector <Cite id="helix-2025" />. HybridVLA trains discrete and diffusion action prediction together, using a collaborative recipe against interference <Cite id="hybridvla-2025" />.

## Results

A many-embodiment π0 generalist needs 7.5 times the training steps of π0.5 + KI to reach similar table-bussing performance. Single-embodiment π0-FAST specialists need twice the wall-clock time from slow inference <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" /> <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />. Table bussing starts with 12 objects for dish and trash receptacles, at ten episodes per task and policy. When a mobile robot moves unseen objects into an open drawer, web-data co-training boosts generalization most, and language following improves but stays imperfect <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" /> <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

In third-party fine-tuning, π0.5 with KI stays near zero on out-of-distribution task perturbations; KI alone does not fix out-of-distribution language generalization <Cite id="apt-action-expert-pretraining-2026" />. A reimplemented co-training plus KI baseline trailed standard behavior cloning on mean LIBERO-PRO and LIBERO-Plus success <Cite id="anchor-align-2026" />, though the dual FAST and flow objective mitigates forgetting more than flow alone <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" />. Across seven VLAs, VQA co-training goes with better knowledge retention <Cite id="act2answer-2026" />. In Toyota Research Institute's 89-policy co-training study, training only on robot data cost the VLM backbone its ability to generate language, and co-training with effective modalities restored it <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" />.

## Later models

openpi's public π0.5 checkpoints were trained with KI, but the repository serves only the flow-matching head <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. Outside groups therefore reimplemented the FAST loss and stop-gradient <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" />. LAP-3B blocks expert gradients and runs at 25 Hz on an RTX 4090 <Cite id="lap-2026" />, and LabVLA applies KI to a FAST-pretrained Qwen3-VL-4B <Cite id="labvla-2026" />. Dexmal and StepFun's 2B DM0 (February 2026) adopts a hybrid gradient strategy "inspired by Knowledge Insulation": on embodied data, action-expert gradients stay out of the VLM <Cite id="dm0-2026" />. A 2026 review sorts implementations into gradient stopping, as in π0.5-KI and π0.6, and inpainting <Cite id="stulp-full-stack-transfer-2026" />.

The π0.6 card lists a Gemma3 4B backbone predicting FAST tokens, a SigLIP 400M encoder and an approximately 860M equal-depth expert whose gradients stay out of the backbone. A chunk takes 63 ms on one H100 with five denoising steps and three cameras <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. π0.7 keeps a Gemma3-based VLM, a MEM-style video-history encoder and a flow-matching expert under FAST supervision with expert gradients stopped <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.
