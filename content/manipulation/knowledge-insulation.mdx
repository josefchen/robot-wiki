<Term id="knowledge-insulation">Knowledge insulation</Term> (KI) trains a vision-language-action model so that learning to move does not erase what its pretrained backbone knew. The backbone learns from discrete action tokens, while a separate <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action expert makes smooth motion and cannot send its training signal back <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

## KI uses action tokens and an action expert at once

The first VLAs wrote actions as words: RT-2 rounds each action number to one of 256 levels, as RT-1 did <Cite id="rt1-2022" /> <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Physical Intelligence judges such tokens unsuited to fast, precise motion <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />, so π0 adds a continuous action expert to a pretrained PaliGemma <Cite id="paligemma-2024" /> <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. π0.5 used tokens first and a new flow-matching expert second <Cite id="pi05-2025" />; KI does both in one stage <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

Also: OpenVLA's 256 levels <Cite id="openvla-2024" />.

## A new expert damages the backbone

Gradients from a fresh action expert degrade the backbone, slowing training and weakening language following: told to put a spoon in the bin, π0 grasps trash <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. Fine-tuning through a fresh output layer distorts pretrained features in general <Cite id="kumar-finetuning-distorts-2022" />, and ChatVLA calls the VLA case spurious forgetting <Cite id="chatvla-2025" />.

Also: pretrained VLAs resist forgetting between robot tasks <Cite id="vla-continual-forgetting-2026" />.

## The recipe: two outputs and a stop-gradient

1. The action expert trains with flow matching <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" /> and outputs continuous action chunks.
2. The backbone learns from vision-language data and discrete FAST <Term id="action-tokenization">action tokens</Term>, which write each joint's motion as smooth waves, as JPEG does for images <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />.
3. A stop-gradient lets the expert read the backbone's features but blocks its training signal from flowing back.

Both outputs together cost about 20% more compute <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

## Insulation trains faster

A many-robot π0 generalist needed 7.5 times π0.5 + KI's training steps for similar table-bussing performance, and π0-FAST specialists twice the wall-clock time, because token-by-token output is slow. On a mobile robot putting unseen objects in a drawer, co-training on web data helped generalization most <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" /> <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

The stop-gradient alone does not adapt the backbone: frozen, the paper's shirt-folding policy scores 0%. FAST supervision supplies that signal <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

## Outside tests are mixed

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| APT <Cite id="apt-action-expert-pretraining-2026" /> | π0.5 with KI stays near zero on out-of-distribution task changes |
| Representation anchoring <Cite id="anchor-align-2026" /> | A reimplemented co-training plus KI baseline trailed behavior cloning on LIBERO-PRO <Cite id="libero-pro-2025" /> and LIBERO-Plus <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" /> |
| Self-demonstrated control <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" /> | FAST plus flow forgets less than flow alone |
| Knowledge retention across seven VLAs <Cite id="act2answer-2026" /> | Co-training on visual question answering goes with better retention |
| TRI, 89 policies <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" /> | Robot-only training cost the backbone its ability to generate language |

Hancock et al. at NVIDIA argue insulation should not be a default. Trained jointly from the start, letting expert gradients reach the backbone raised closed-loop driving progress by 17 to 40% <Cite id="gradient-routing-mot-vla-2026" />.

## Others protect the backbone differently

| Model | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| GR00T N1 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" /> | Freezes the language part of its backbone |
| GR00T N1.5 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-5-2025" /> | Freezes all of it; real GR-1 language following rose from 46.6% to 93.3% |
| GR00T N1.6 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" /> | Unfreezes the top 4 layers |
| VLM2VLA <Cite id="vlm2vla-2025" /> | Writes actions as language and trains only small adapters |
| UAM <Cite id="uam-forgetting-2026" /> | Keeps over 95% of multimodal ability with no freezing, gradient stopping or co-training |
| Helix <Cite id="helix-2025" /> | Lets its fast policy's gradients into the backbone |
| HybridVLA <Cite id="hybridvla-2025" /> | Trains discrete and diffusion action prediction together |
| InstructVLA <Cite id="instructvla-2025" /> | A mixture of experts on vision-language and robot data |

## Physical Intelligence still uses it

openpi's public π0.5 checkpoints were trained with KI but serve only the flow-matching head <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. LAP-3B blocks expert gradients and runs at 25 Hz on a gaming GPU <Cite id="lap-2026" />.

Also: LabVLA <Cite id="labvla-2026" />, Hugging Face's FineART-VLA <Cite id="fineart-2026" />, DM0's hybrid <Cite id="dm0-2026" />, a 2026 review of implementations <Cite id="stulp-full-stack-transfer-2026" />.

<Image id="pi06-box-building-2025" />

The π0.6 model card lists a Gemma 3 4B backbone <Cite id="gemma3-2025" /> predicting FAST tokens and an expert of about 860M parameters whose gradients are kept out <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. π0.7 keeps the recipe <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. G0.5 instead emits reasoning and action tokens from one decoder <Cite id="g0-5-2026" />, and GR00T 2 generates video and actions in one diffusion transformer <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.
