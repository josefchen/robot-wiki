## Large policies need more than one tick

A large policy needs more than one 20 ms tick to think, so it outputs an <Term id="action-chunking">action chunk</Term> the robot plays while the next is computed <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />. Gemini Robotics takes about 250 ms through the cloud yet controls at 50 Hz from a local decoder <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. GR00T N1 splits language at 10 Hz from actions at 120 Hz <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />.

Old plans cost most when the world moves: in VLALagBench, 100 ms of latency nearly halved success on moving targets <Cite id="vla-lagbench-2026" />. In the <Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> (RTC) team's mobile π0.5 <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, the model took 96.89 ms, the network 21.20 ms and image resizing 11.22 ms <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## Onboard computers are the bottleneck

Jetson Thor <Cite id="jetson-thor-2026" /> has up to 7.5 times the AI compute of the Orin before it <Cite id="nvidia-jetson-thor-available-2025" />. VLA-Perf still predicts π0 on it at only 19 Hz, and that is an upper bound <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />.

<Image id="jetson-agx-orin-auledas-2024" />

| Model | Hardware | Speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| π0, predicted | H100; Jetson Thor | 162.5 and 19.0 per second, without network or sensors <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" /> |
| π0.5 | Jetson AGX Thor | About 132 ms, or 49 ms at reduced precision <Cite id="jetson-ai-lab-openpi-thor" /> |
| GR00T 2 Turbo | Unitree G1's onboard Thor | 124 ms per 1.6-second chunk, played at 50 Hz by the SONIC controller <Cite id="sonic-2025" /> <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| RT-2, 55B parameters | Cloud | 1 to 3 Hz <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> |
| OpenVLA, 7B parameters | RTX 4090 | About 6 Hz <Cite id="openvla-2024" />; 26 times the throughput with OFT's parallel decoding <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" /> |
| Fast-in-Slow | Eight-action chunks | 117.7 Hz control <Cite id="fast-in-slow-2025" /> |

How a model generates actions matters more than its size. Across 18 open VLAs on Thor, size explained at most 15% of latency differences; few-step flow models kept up with control, and token-by-token decoders did not <Cite id="vla-hwbench-2026" />. A 4-bit π0.5 kept 94.04% success on LIBERO-10 on Thor <Cite id="flashrt-pai-2026" />.

Offloading helps only on a fast network. π0 on a remote RTX 4090 takes 38.7 ms over WiFi 6 but 55.7 ms over 5G, against Thor's 52.6 ms <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />. Physical Intelligence's cloud API saw connectivity problems with early partners <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />.

## Inpainting switches chunks without pauses or jerks

Chunks keep motion close to the demonstrations but react late <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. Synchronous execution pauses for inference between them, a stop-start motion unlike the training data <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />. π0 plays 16 or 25 of each 50 actions <Cite id="pi0-2024" />; PI ran π0, π0-FAST <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" /> and π0.5 this way before RTC <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />.

Naive switching removes the pause but jerks, because the new chunk was planned from an older view <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />. <Term id="temporal-ensembling">Temporal ensembling</Term>, from ACT, averages the overlapping predictions <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />; at 100 and 200 ms of added latency, both its variants tripped the robot's protective stop <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

RTC computes the next chunk while one plays, fixing the actions that run during inference and inpainting the rest, with no retraining <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" /> <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" /> <Cite id="lerobot-rtc-docs" />. On six π0.5 tasks its throughput held with up to 200 ms of added delay, and it lit matches at total delays above 300 ms <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## Later methods correct for the stale view

| Method | Fix | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VLASH | Rolls the robot's state forward to execution time | Up to 11.8 times lower reaction latency than synchronous <Cite id="vlash-2025" /> |
| A2C2 | Adds a small correction at every step | 23 points over RTC <Cite id="a2c2-2025" /> |
| FutureRTC | Predicts the camera view at execution time | <Cite id="futurertc-2026" /> |
| Event-triggered inference | Replans when the scene changes | <Cite id="event-triggered-vla-2026" /> |
| SmolVLA <Cite id="smolvla-2025" /> | Asynchronous inference | About twice as fast on a local network <Cite id="lerobot-async-inference-2025" /> |
| ChunkTrust | Chooses how many actions to play before replanning | 6.80 points on 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks <Cite id="robotwin-2-2025" />; 50.4% to 57.5% on four real tasks <Cite id="chunktrust-2026" /> |

Asynchronous replanning helps only while the queue of pending actions never runs empty <Cite id="libero-dynamic-2026" />.

## Policies can learn to expect delay

Simulating delay in training avoids inpainting's extra compute and works better at long delays <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />. π0.7 trains with up to 240 ms of delay <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, and Soft RTC cuts high-delay jerk by 9.6% against the hard version <Cite id="soft-rtc-2026" />.

Chronos Policy timestamps each observation and action and randomizes both in training. On simulated conveyor pick-and-place with up to 200 ms of delay, it kept 76.4% success where Diffusion Policy <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" /> fell to 1.8% <Cite id="chronos-policy-2026" />. [Real-Time EXPO-FT](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) adds a quick edit policy on the newest view, lifting four dynamic real tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />.

Also: GR00T N1.6 uses test-time and train-time RTC <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />; Helix offsets training inputs by its latency gap <Cite id="helix-2025" />.

## Fewer denoising steps make faster chunks

π0 used 10 denoising steps per chunk <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, π0.6 uses 5 <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />, and π0.7's minimal variant takes 38 ms on one H100 <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Distillation and parallel decoding cut further.

| Method | Gain |
| --- | --- |
| One-Step Diffusion Policy | 1.5 Hz to 62 Hz action prediction <Cite id="one-step-diffusion-2024" /> |
| Consistency Policy | An order of magnitude faster than the fastest alternative, at competitive success <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" /> |
| FASTER | One denoising step for the immediate reaction <Cite id="faster-flow-vla-2026" /> |
| PD-VLA, for token VLAs | 2.52 times the execution frequency <Cite id="pd-vla-2025" /> |
