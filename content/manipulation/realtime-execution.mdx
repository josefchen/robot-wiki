Real-time execution means delivering a policy's actions on the controller's clock while the next inference runs. At 50 Hz the control period is 20 ms, shared by cameras, network, preprocessing and model, and the hand-off between action chunks changes what the robot does <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## Where the milliseconds go

A 60 ms inference cannot fit in a 20 ms tick, so the current chunk supplies the 50 Hz commands <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />. Gemini Robotics pairs a cloud backbone with a local decoder: about 250 ms end to end, yet 50 Hz effective control through multi-action chunks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />.

The <Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> (RTC) paper measures π0.5 on an RTX 4090 in bfloat16, with five denoising steps and a wired LAN. Over 50 inference calls in a real episode, the mobile setup spent 96.89 ms in the model, 21.20 ms on the network, 11.22 ms resizing images and 9.67 ms on other work <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## The hardware wall

VLA-Perf (Jiang et al., 2026) is an analytical roofline model assuming optimistic software, adequate model accuracy and attainable peak compute and memory bandwidth. An optimized π0 Triton implementation on an RTX 4090 reaches 73.3 to 82.6% of its predicted throughput with one to three cameras <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />.

<Image id="jetson-agx-orin-auledas-2024" />

Its baseline π0 has 2.7B parameters and models three 224×224 cameras, 32 language tokens, 14 action dimensions, chunk size 50, 10 denoising steps, batch size one, and BF16 or FP16. Excluding network, sensor and execution latency, it predicts 162.5 inferences per second on H100 and 19.0 on Jetson Thor. A hypothetical 9.1B π0-L reaches 3.9 on Thor, all three π0 components are memory-bound there, and on B100 raising denoising steps from 10 to 50 multiplies total latency by 2.15 <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />.

VLA-Perf calls 10 Hz inference "acceptable" and 100 Hz "high-performance" relative to common camera rates. Thor's predicted 19 Hz clears the first; the authors propose smaller models, fewer denoising steps or lower-precision quantization for the second <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />. NVIDIA's Jetson AI Lab measured a different setup, π0.5 with action horizon 10 on Jetson AGX Thor, at about 132 ms in PyTorch BF16 and 49 ms with FP8 plus NVFP4 <Cite id="jetson-ai-lab-openpi-thor" />.

RT-2's 55B model ran at 1 to 3 Hz <Cite id="rt2-2023" />, and the 7B OpenVLA runs at about 6 Hz on an RTX 4090 but 1.2 Hz on its evaluation A5000 <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. OpenVLA-OFT's parallel decoding of chunked continuous actions raised throughput 26x and average success from 76.5% to 97.1% <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Dexmal and StepFun ran π0 with two camera views in 27.3 ms on one RTX 4090 <Cite id="realtime-vla-2025" />, and Fast-in-Slow, which embeds a fast action module in its VLM, reaches 117.7 Hz control with eight-action chunks <Cite id="fast-in-slow-2025" />.

In VLA-Perf, server placement depends on the network: synchronous π0 on an RTX 4090 takes 38.7 ms over WiFi 6 against Thor's 52.6 ms, but 55.7 ms over 5G, and B100 over 4G with a slow cloud link takes 273.4 ms <Cite id="vla-perf-2026" />.

## Three ways to hand off a chunk

<Term id="action-chunking">Chunking</Term> captures temporal dependencies in demonstrations at the cost of reactivity to unexpected states <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. Let inference take $d$ milliseconds during the current chunk.

Synchronous execution runs part of a chunk, pauses for inference, then resumes, the default in many prior works <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />. π0 executes 16 of its 50 actions on its 20 Hz UR5e and Franka setups and 25 on the other 50 Hz robots <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, and Physical Intelligence ran π0, π0-FAST and π0.5 synchronously before RTC <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />. The RTC paper links stop-start motion to distribution shift <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

Naive switching jumps to the new chunk on arrival. That removes dead time but adds a discontinuity: the new chunk comes from an observation $d$ milliseconds old, and the command steps by the two chunks' disagreement in one tick <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

<Term id="temporal-ensembling">Temporal ensembling</Term>, ACT's 2023 method, averages each timestep's predictions with weights that favour older ones <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />, but averaging predictions from different modes need not give a valid action. In RTC's π0.5 real-robot comparison, both ensembling variants triggered the robot's protective stop at +100 and +200 ms of injected latency <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

Real-Time Chunking inpaints the next chunk while the current one executes, fully weighting the first $d$ actions, which execute during inference, and works on diffusion and flow policies without retraining <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" /> <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />. On six π0.5 real-robot tasks, its aggregate throughput did not degrade at +0, +100 or +200 ms of injected delay, and it lit matches precisely at total delays above 300 ms <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

VLASH rolls the robot state forward with the previous chunk and cuts reaction latency up to 11.8x against synchronous inference <Cite id="vlash-2025" />, and A2C2's small per-step correction head gains 23 percentage points of success over RTC <Cite id="a2c2-2025" />. Event-triggered inference sets the inference gap by scene change, averaging 95% real-world success <Cite id="event-triggered-vla-2026" />. SmolVLA's asynchronous stack decouples prediction from execution <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />, and on a local network its round trips stay under 100 ms while tasks finish about 2x faster <Cite id="lerobot-async-inference-2025" />.

## Training the tolerance in

Black et al. (2025) simulate the delay in training, which avoids inference-time inpainting's added latency and works better at higher delays <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />. π0.7 trains with simulated delays of 0 to 12 timesteps, at most 240 ms on a 50 Hz robot <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, and Soft RTC's medium overlap window cuts high-delay action jerk by 9.6% against hard training-time RTC <Cite id="soft-rtc-2026" />.

[Real-Time EXPO-FT](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) learns a lightweight edit policy that reacts to the latest observation while a slow VLA proposes chunks, raising average performance on four dynamic real tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />. NVIDIA uses test-time and train-time RTC for GR00T N1.6 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />, and Figure's Helix offsets its two systems' training inputs to match their deployed latency gap <Cite id="helix-2025" />.

ChunkTrust (September 2026) infers how many actions to execute before replanning from the policy's own outputs while it runs. Over all 50 RoboTwin2.0 tasks it raised π0.5's task-averaged success by 6.80 percentage points, and on four real household tasks the mean normalized process score from 50.4% to 57.5% <Cite id="chunktrust-2026" />.

## Spending the step budget

π0 integrated its flow with 10 Euler steps per chunk <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, π0.6 cut that to 5 <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />, and π0.7's minimal variant takes 38 ms on one H100 with three cameras <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Distillation removes most steps: One-Step Diffusion Policy raises action prediction from 1.5 Hz to 62 Hz <Cite id="one-step-diffusion-2024" />, and Consistency Policy is an order of magnitude faster than the fastest alternative at competitive success <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" />. FASTER finds reaction time set by the time to first action and the execution horizon, and compresses the immediate reaction to a single denoising step <Cite id="faster-flow-vla-2026" />. For token-based VLAs, PD-VLA's parallel decoding recovers 2.52 times the execution frequency <Cite id="pd-vla-2025" />.
