A <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action (VLA)</Term> model is a robot controller made by teaching a vision-language model, an AI system trained on web images and text, to output movements. The hope is that its knowledge of objects, words and space carries over to robots, which have far less data of their own <Cite id="rt2-2023" />.

<Image id="gemini-robotics-aloha-dexterous-2025" />

## What a VLA does when it runs

A running VLA repeats one loop: look at the camera images, read the instruction ("pick up the apple"), write the next move, let the robot carry it out, look again. A move is a short row of numbers saying how far to shift and turn the gripper, how wide to open it and whether the task is done. In the classic design each number is rounded to one of 256 levels with its own token, so the model writes a move as it writes a sentence <Cite id="rt2-2023" />.

<ActionTokenization className="my-6" />

[Behavior cloning](/manipulation/bc-foundations/) and [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) learn the same loop from robot demonstrations alone. A VLA starts out knowing what an apple is.

## RT-1: actions become tokens

RT-1 (2022) showed that one <Term id="transformer">transformer</Term> could learn hundreds of manipulation tasks from real robot data. Three times a second it reads recent camera images and an instruction and outputs 11 action tokens for the arm, gripper, mobile base and a mode switch. Trained on about 130,000 demonstrations collected by 13 robots over 17 months, it performed more than 700 training instructions with 97% success, and 76% of instructions it had never seen <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

## RT-2: web knowledge reaches the robot

RT-2 (2023) took the defining step. It <Term id="fine-tuning">fine-tuned</Term> web-trained <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language models</Term> of up to 55 billion parameters on RT-1's robot data to write actions as number text, keeping web data in the training mix, which generalized better than robot data alone <Cite id="rt2-2023" />.

In about 6,000 real trials RT-2 matched RT-1 on familiar tasks and roughly doubled its success with unseen objects, backgrounds and instructions. It obeyed commands absent from the robot data, such as picking up the smallest object, and with step-by-step reasoning chose a rock as an improvised hammer <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Gemini Robotics 1.5 writes out its reasoning between actions <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />, and VLA-0, writing actions as ordinary text with no new vocabulary, beat models trained on the same robot data on the LIBERO simulation benchmark <Cite id="vla-0-2025" />.

## Open X-Embodiment and RT-X: one dataset, many robots

Each lab records data in its own format for its own robot. <Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> (OXE, 2023) converted 60 existing datasets from 34 labs at 21 institutions into one format: over a million real robot episodes from 22 kinds of robot. RT-X is RT-1 and RT-2 retrained on data from the nine robots pooled by then <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

<Image id="open-x-embodiment-overview-2023" />

The test was <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> transfer: does data from other robots help each robot? RT-1-X beat each lab's own model on four of five small datasets, with 50% higher mean success. On the two large datasets RT-1-X fell behind RT-1 trained on that data alone, while the far larger RT-2-X beat both. On Google's robot, for skills seen only in another arm's data (the WidowX in the Bridge dataset), RT-2-X succeeded 75.8% of the time against 27.3% for RT-2, and 42.8% with Bridge left out <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

The mix matters. Testing 89 <Term id="co-training">co-trained</Term> policies in 58,000 simulated and 2,835 real rollouts, Toyota Research Institute (February 2026) found that adding vision-language and cross-robot data substantially improved generalization, while discrete action tokens brought no significant benefit <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" />. Re-Mix learns how much of each OXE dataset to train on, and its mix beat an even one by 38% on average <Cite id="remix-2024" />. X-VLA gives each data source a few learned inputs, a "soft prompt", that tell one shared model which robot it is reading <Cite id="x-vla-2025" />. Moritz Reuss, in a personal 2025 overview, judged that "OXE is mostly low-quality data" <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />. Xiaomi-Robotics-1 (July 2026), pretrained on over 100,000 hours recorded by people with handheld UMI grippers, reports 57.4% on the RoboCasa365 simulation benchmark against a previous best of 46.6% <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />; its code is open <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-repo-2026" />.

## Octo and OpenVLA: the open models

RT-2 stayed closed; two 2024 releases gave researchers models they could download. Octo, trained on 800,000 OXE trajectories, takes instructions as words or goal images and fine-tunes within hours on a consumer GPU to robots with new sensors or controls. Out of the box on a WidowX arm, goal images gave 25% higher success than language <Cite id="octo-2024" />.

<Image id="octo-evaluation-setups-2024" />

OpenVLA, trained on 970,000 robot demonstrations, beat RT-2-X by 16.5 points of task success with seven times fewer parameters, over 29 tasks on two robots with five or ten trials each <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. SmolVLA later trained on one GPU <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />, and CogACT passes the language model's output to a separate diffusion model that produces the motion <Cite id="cogact-2024" />.

## Writing actions as words is slow

Each token waits for the one before it, which limits speed. OpenVLA writes its seven tokens one after another at every step <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />, about 6 moves per second on one gaming GPU <Cite id="openvla-2024" />, and the largest RT-2 managed 1 to 3 from cloud servers <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Physical Intelligence researchers likened this tokenization to "controlling your arm by verbally saying which muscles should contract" <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />. Their FAST tokenizer compresses a stretch of motion with the discrete cosine transform, the method behind JPEG images; a VLA using it matched diffusion-based VLAs on 10,000 hours of robot data while training up to five times faster <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. In theory, with stable dynamics and a smooth policy, the cost of rounding grows only polynomially with task length <Cite id="bc-action-quantization-2026" />.

The alternative is an <Term id="action-chunking">action chunk</Term>, many continuous moves output at once. Physical Intelligence's π0 (2024) adds an "action expert" that generates chunks by <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>, a relative of diffusion, at up to 50 moves per second <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. PD-VLA writes all of a chunk's tokens at once and then refines them, executing actions 2.52 times as often without retraining <Cite id="pd-vla-2025" />. OpenVLA-OFT (2025) retrained OpenVLA to output chunks: 109.7 actions per second against 4.2 on one data-center GPU, and, with a wrist camera and joint readings added, average LIBERO success <Cite id="libero-2023" /> up from 76.5% to 97.1% <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

## What is still hard

High scores hide brittleness. LIBERO-Plus found that models scoring 95% on LIBERO fell below 30% under modest changes such as a new camera angle, and tended to ignore the instruction completely <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />. Papers differ in actions, data and protocols, so their numbers rarely compare <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. RoboArena ranks policies in double-blind head-to-head trials on DROID robots at seven institutions <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />, and SIMPLER's simulated scores correlate with real-robot results <Cite id="simpler-2024" /> ([evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)).

Instructions are another gap. Stanford's Yiqing Xu and Jiajun Wu (October 2026) found that people's instructions grow only from 9.1 words for one-step tasks to 15.8 for tasks of three or more steps. On the 120 simulated tasks of NVIDIA's RoboLab benchmark, π0.5 fine-tuned on DROID succeeded 38.0% of the time with explicit commands and 15.2 points less when told only the intent <Cite id="commgap-2026" />.

## Where VLAs are heading

Some systems split the work: GR00T N1 pairs a vision-language "System 2" that interprets the scene with a fast diffusion "System 1" that moves <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. π0.7 adds strategy notes and subgoal images to the instruction, and Physical Intelligence reports it folding laundry on a robot type it had never been trained on <Cite id="pi07-2026" />; it offers its models through a hosted API, in beta since October 13, 2026 <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 controls full humanoids <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />, and Gemini Robotics 2.5 (November 10, 2026), for partners only, reports 30 to 100% higher success than earlier baselines across over 200 tasks <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" />.

Others predict video along with actions. NVIDIA's GR00T 2 succeeded 40% of the time on 50 tasks with no demonstrations on the target robot, and 95% after five demonstrations each, on tasks chosen because five sufficed <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />. Runway's open-weight Praxis-1 reports 46.2% on RoboLab-120 against 28.0% for π0.5 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" /> <Cite id="robolab-2026" />. Apple's AMP1 skips the VLA, letting a general-purpose AI model command the arm directly, and with no demonstrations reached 85.6% on RoboLab-120 against 49.2% for the leading VLA <Cite id="apple-amp1-2026" />.

## Choosing a model

Choosing a VLA means choosing a prior, what the model already knows, and a control interface: its action format, how far ahead it plans and how fast it runs. The two can change separately; OFT made OpenVLA faster and more successful without replacing it <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. The [foundation-model guide](/manipulation/foundation-models/) covers the prior, the [action-space guide](/manipulation/action-spaces/) the interface, and [generalist policies](/manipulation/generalist-policies/) and [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) what follows.
