A <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action (VLA)</Term> model is a robot policy built by adapting a vision-language model, pretrained on web-scale images and text, to output robot actions; earlier [behavior cloning](/manipulation/bc-foundations/) and [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) learned from robot data alone. The design question is how to get actions out of a model that outputs text <Cite id="rt2-2023" />.

## RT-1: actions become tokens

RT-1 (2022) conditions an ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet <Cite id="efficientnet-2019" /> on the instruction through FiLM <Cite id="film-2018" />, and TokenLearner <Cite id="tokenlearner-2021" /> cuts each of six images to eight tokens for a 19M-parameter decoder-only transformer; the full system has 35M parameters <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

Its 11 action coordinates are seven arm variables (translation, rotation and gripper opening), three base variables and a mode switch that can also end the episode. Each is discretized into 256 bins spaced uniformly within its bounds and trained with categorical cross-entropy <Cite id="rt1-2022" />. Drag “Moment in the movement” to watch each motion's value land in one of 256 bins.

The data covers about 130k episodes over more than 700 tasks, collected over 17 months with 13 robots, and the paper reports 3,000 real-world evaluation trials <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

RT-1 predicts one action at a time at 3 Hz, with a fixed 280 ms wait, the maximum observed latency of all components, to keep action timing consistent <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

## RT-2: actions become text

RT-2 (2023) writes each action dimension's 256 bin indices as strings in a <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language model</Term>'s token stream: PaLI-X already has a token for every integer up to 1000, while PaLM-E overwrites its 256 least frequently used tokens. The backbone, up to 55B parameters, is co-fine-tuned on robot trajectories and the original web vision-language data, which the authors report generalizes better than robot data alone <Cite id="rt2-2023" />.

That web knowledge shows up as emergent object choice, such as picking the smallest object, and multi-stage reasoning, such as using a rock as an improvised hammer <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Gemini Robotics 1.5 interleaves actions with natural-language reasoning <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />.

In a May 2025 research note, Danny Driess and colleagues at Physical Intelligence call this tokenization "not suitable for high-frequency, precise, or fluent motions," likening it to "controlling your arm by verbally saying which muscles should contract" <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />. The same lab's FAST paper locates the failure in simple per-dimension, per-timestep binning; its compressed tokens match diffusion VLAs on 10k hours of robot data while training up to 5 times faster <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />.

## Open X-Embodiment and RT-X: pooling the data

<Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> pools more than one million real robot trajectories spanning 22 embodiments, 527 skills and 160,266 tasks; its abstract names 21 institutions, and its dataset description covers 60 datasets from 34 labs. RT-1-X and RT-2-X trained on a nine-embodiment subset. RT-1-X beat the original methods on four of five small-data domains but underperformed RT-1 in the large-data setting, where the higher-capacity RT-2-X outperformed both. RT-2-X gained Google Robot skills from WidowX Bridge data, a <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> transfer, and performed roughly on par with RT-2 on unseen objects, backgrounds and environments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

In February 2026, Toyota Research Institute researchers compared 89 co-trained policies over 58,000 simulated and 2,835 real rollouts. Vision-language and cross-embodiment co-training substantially improved generalization, while discrete action-token variants brought no significant benefit <Cite id="lbm-cotraining-2026" />. Xiaomi-Robotics-1 (July 2026) pretrains on over 100,000 hours of UMI handheld-gripper and egocentric trajectories and reports 57.4% on RoboCasa365 against a previous best of 46.6%; its authors say code and checkpoints will be released <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />.

Moritz Reuss's October 2025 post, which he presents as his personal selection and opinion, says "OXE is mostly low-quality data" and "we still lack good methods to quantify data quality in imitation learning" <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />.

## Octo and OpenVLA: the open models

Two 2024 releases made the recipe reproducible outside Google. Octo ships 27M- and 93M-parameter checkpoints trained on 800k Open X-Embodiment trajectories; learned readout tokens in a block-wise-masked transformer feed a lightweight diffusion head that outputs action chunks, and fine-tuning can add a new sensor or output while retaining the transformer weights <Cite id="octo-2024" />. In zero-shot WidowX evaluation, Octo reports a "25% higher success rate" with goal images than with language, and the authors suggest goal images carry more task information; only 56% of its pretraining data has language annotations <Cite id="octo-2024" />.

OpenVLA builds on the Prismatic VLM <Cite id="prismatic-vlm-2024" />, with a Llama 2 7B backbone <Cite id="llama-2-2023" /> and fused DINOv2 <Cite id="dinov2-2023" /> and SigLIP <Cite id="siglip-2023" /> features. Trained on 970k robot demonstrations, it predicts seven coordinates for position, orientation and gripper, each split into 256 uniform bins between the training data's 1st and 99th quantiles instead of its minimum and maximum; bin indices replace the 256 least-used Llama tokens and are predicted autoregressively <Cite id="openvla-2024" /> <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

Its headline result is 16.5 points higher absolute task success than RT-2-X (55B) with "7x fewer parameters", over 29 tasks on two embodiments: 17 WidowX tasks with ten trials each and 12 Google-robot tasks with five, from matched initial robot and object states, with partial credit on some tasks <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Among later open models, SmolVLA trains on one GPU, deploys on consumer GPUs or CPUs and decouples action prediction from execution with asynchronous inference <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />.

## The discrete-token throughput problem

OpenVLA's original formulation decodes seven action tokens sequentially per timestep <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. RT-2 runs at 1 to 3 Hz for the 55B PaLI-X variant and about 5 Hz for the 5B variant, queried over the network from a multi-TPU cloud service <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. OpenVLA runs at about 6 Hz on one RTX 4090 in bfloat16, without compilation or speculative decoding, and its quantization analysis notes that slower inference changes the controller dynamics <Cite id="openvla-2024" />.

π0 instead adds a flow-matching expert that outputs action chunks at up to 50 Hz <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, and PD-VLA decodes in parallel by fixed-point iteration with no retraining, reaching 2.52 times the execution frequency <Cite id="pd-vla-2025" />. OpenVLA-OFT (2025) combines bidirectional parallel decoding, <Term id="action-chunking">action chunking</Term>, continuous actions and an L1-trained MLP head; its ALOHA variant, OFT+, adds FiLM for language grounding <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

On LIBERO <Cite id="libero-2023" />, OFT raises average success over four ten-task suites from 76.5% to 97.1% with an added wrist image and proprioceptive state, fine-tuned per suite and scored over 500 episodes each. On an A100, averaged over 100 one-image queries, L1 OFT generates 109.7 actions per second against OpenVLA's 4.2, and 71.4 with the added inputs <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. LIBERO-Plus finds that such models drop from 95% to below 30% under modest perturbations and tend to ignore language instructions completely <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />.

Real ALOHA runs use three images, robot state and absolute joint targets at 25 Hz, down from the platform's 50 Hz, and OFT+ executes all 25 actions before replanning, against eight on LIBERO. ALOHA tasks such as folding clothes are scored for partial completion, LIBERO for binary success <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

In the one-image LIBERO comparison with parallel decoding and eight-step chunks, L1 OFT averages 95.3% against 95.4% for a diffusion head using 50 denoising steps, and 90.7% against 91.1% on LIBERO-Long; the diffusion variants trained longer and converged and inferred more slowly. The demonstrations follow one consistent strategy per task, and the authors leave multimodal demonstrations and large-scale pretraining open <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Removing ACT's CVAE objective barely changes scripted-data results but cuts human-data success from 35.3% to 2%; its chunk-size ablation is in [action chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking/) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## State of the field

Action interfaces, data mixtures, inputs and execution protocols differ across these studies, so read token-generation and robot-control rates separately <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. With LIBERO near its ceiling, ranking needs harder tests ([evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)): RoboArena compares generalist policies in double-blind pairs on DROID robots at seven institutions <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />, and SIMPLER's simulated scores correlate with real performance <Cite id="simpler-2024" />.

## A 2026 selection rule

Choosing a VLA means choosing a prior (the visual, language and robot experience in the backbone) and a control interface (the action, horizon, frame and measured rate). OFT changed both throughput and success without replacing OpenVLA <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Write down the required semantic novelty, embodiment data, action contract and latency budget, pick the prior with the [foundation-model guide](/manipulation/foundation-models/) and the output with the [action-space guide](/manipulation/action-spaces/), then compare only models whose contracts match; [generalist policies](/manipulation/generalist-policies/) and [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) cover the rest.

GR00T N1 separates the two, with a vision-language System 2 over a diffusion-transformer System 1 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. π0.7 conditions on language plus strategy metadata and subgoal images, and Physical Intelligence reports zero-shot cross-embodiment laundry folding <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 controls full humanoids, including a 22-degree-of-freedom hand, and is available only to early-access partners <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.
