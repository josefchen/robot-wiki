A <Term id="vision-language-action-model">vision-language-action (VLA)</Term> model is a robot policy built by adapting a vision-language model to output robot actions. Earlier [behavior cloning](/manipulation/bc-foundations/) and [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) policies learned from robot data alone. It starts from a model pretrained on web-scale images and text, and the defining design question is how to get actions out of a model whose output space is text <Cite id="rt2-2023" />.

## RT-1: actions become tokens

RT-1 (2022) combines an ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet <Cite id="efficientnet-2019" />, a pretrained instruction embedding and FiLM language conditioning <Cite id="film-2018" />. TokenLearner <Cite id="tokenlearner-2021" /> cuts each of six images from 81 visual tokens to eight, giving 48 tokens for an eight-layer, 19M-parameter decoder-only transformer; the full system has 35M parameters <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

Its 11 action coordinates are seven arm variables (translation, rotation and gripper opening), three base variables (x, y and yaw) and a switch between arm control, base control and episode termination. Each is discretized into 256 bins spaced uniformly within its bounds and trained with categorical cross-entropy <Cite id="rt1-2022" />. Drag “Moment in the movement” to watch each motion's value land in one of 256 bins.

RT-1 reports about 130k episodes over more than 700 tasks, collected over 17 months with 13 robots, and 3,000 real-world evaluation trials. The authors argue that generalization needs both scale and breadth, with sufficiently connected tasks <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

RT-1 commands actions at 3 Hz and predicts the next action, with no chunk. A fixed wait of 280 ms, the maximum observed latency of all components, keeps action timing consistent <Cite id="rt1-2022" />.

## RT-2: actions become text

RT-2 (2023) brings web knowledge into control <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. Each action dimension is discretized into 256 bins, and the bin indices are serialized as strings into a <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language model</Term>'s token stream. PaLI-X already has a token for every integer up to 1000, so bins map onto number tokens, while PaLM-E overwrites its 256 least frequently used tokens. The backbone, up to 55B parameters, is co-fine-tuned on robot trajectories and the original web vision-language data, which the authors report generalizes better than robot data alone <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. PaLM-E had already shown positive transfer from joint web-scale language, vision and visual-language training <Cite id="palm-e-2023" />.

RT-2 also shows emergent object choice, such as picking the smallest object or the one closest to another, and multi-stage semantic reasoning, such as using a rock as an improvised hammer. A separate variant writes a natural-language plan before the action tokens: "Instruction: I’m hungry. Plan: pick rxbar chocolate." <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> Gemini Robotics 1.5 interleaves actions with natural-language reasoning <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />.

In a May 2025 research note, Danny Driess and colleagues at Physical Intelligence call this tokenization "not suitable for high-frequency, precise, or fluent motions," citing their π0 and π0-FAST experiments. They liken it to "controlling your arm by verbally saying which muscles should contract" <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />. The same lab's FAST paper locates the failure in simple per-dimension, per-timestep binning; its compressed tokens match diffusion VLAs on 10k hours of robot data while training up to 5 times faster <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. VLA-0 writes actions as plain text and, on LIBERO, outperforms π0.5-KI, OpenVLA-OFT and SmolVLA trained on the same robot data <Cite id="vla-0-2025" />.

## Open X-Embodiment and RT-X: pooling the data

<Term id="open-x-embodiment">Open X-Embodiment</Term> pools more than one million real robot trajectories spanning 22 embodiments, 527 skills and 160,266 tasks. Its abstract names 21 institutions; its dataset description covers 60 datasets from 34 labs. RT-1-X and RT-2-X trained on a nine-embodiment subset. RT-1-X beat the original methods on four of five small-data domains but underperformed RT-1 in the large-data setting, where the higher-capacity RT-2-X outperformed both. RT-2-X gained Google Robot skills from WidowX Bridge data, a <Term id="cross-embodiment">cross-embodiment</Term> transfer, and performed roughly on par with RT-2 on unseen objects, backgrounds and environments <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />.

Moritz Reuss's October 2025 post, which he presents as his personal selection and opinion, says "OXE is mostly low-quality data" and "we still lack good methods to quantify data quality in imitation learning" <Cite id="oxe-quality-critique-2026" />. Re-Mix's learned domain weights beat uniform weights by 38% and human-selected weights by 32% on the RT-X training data <Cite id="remix-2024" />.

## Octo and OpenVLA: the open models

Two 2024 releases made the recipe reproducible outside Google. Octo ships 27M- and 93M-parameter checkpoints trained on 800k Open X-Embodiment trajectories, with a separate pretrained T5-base (111M) language tokenizer. Observation and task tokens enter a block-wise-masked transformer; learned readout tokens attend to earlier inputs without being attended to, and a lightweight diffusion head maps them to action chunks. Fine-tuning for a new sensor or output can add positional embeddings, a lightweight encoder or a new head while retaining the transformer weights <Cite id="octo-2024" />.

In zero-shot WidowX evaluation, Octo reports a "25% higher success rate" with goal images than with language; the authors suggest goal images carry more task information. Only 56% of its pretraining data has language annotations, and better language conditioning is left as future work <Cite id="octo-2024" />.

OpenVLA builds on the Prismatic VLM <Cite id="prismatic-vlm-2024" />, with a Llama 2 7B backbone <Cite id="llama-2-2023" /> and fused DINOv2 <Cite id="dinov2-2023" /> and SigLIP <Cite id="siglip-2023" /> features. Trained on 970k robot demonstrations, it predicts seven coordinates for position, orientation and gripper. For each, 256 bins uniformly divide the interval between the training data's 1st and 99th quantiles, instead of the minimum and maximum; bin indices replace the 256 least-used Llama tokens and are predicted autoregressively <Cite id="openvla-2024" /> <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

OpenVLA's headline result is 16.5 points higher absolute task success than RT-2-X (55B) with "7x fewer parameters". It covers 29 tasks on two embodiments, 17 WidowX tasks with ten trials each and 12 Google-robot tasks with five, from matched initial robot and object states, with partial credit on some tasks <Cite id="openvla-2024" />.

Among later open models, SmolVLA trains on one GPU, deploys on consumer GPUs or CPUs and decouples action prediction from execution with asynchronous inference <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />. CogACT replaces quantization with a diffusion action transformer and beats OpenVLA by over 35% in simulation and 55% on real robots <Cite id="cogact-2024" />.

## The discrete-token throughput problem

OpenVLA's original formulation decodes seven action tokens sequentially per timestep <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. RT-2 runs at 1 to 3 Hz for the 55B PaLI-X variant and about 5 Hz for the 5B variant, queried over the network from a multi-TPU cloud service <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. OpenVLA runs at about 6 Hz on one RTX 4090 in bfloat16, without compilation or speculative decoding. Its Franka setups use 5 Hz and 15 Hz non-blocking controllers, and its quantization analysis notes that slower inference changes the controller dynamics <Cite id="openvla-2024" />.

π0 instead adds a flow-matching expert for action chunks at up to 50 Hz, which its authors say challenge autoregressive VLAs <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. PD-VLA decodes in parallel by fixed-point iteration with no retraining, reaching 2.52 times the execution frequency <Cite id="pd-vla-2025" />. OpenVLA-OFT (2025) combines bidirectional parallel decoding, <Term id="action-chunking">action chunking</Term>, continuous actions and an L1-trained MLP head; its ALOHA variant, OFT+, adds FiLM for language grounding <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

On LIBERO <Cite id="libero-2023" />, OFT raises average success over four ten-task suites from 76.5% to 97.1% with an added wrist image and proprioceptive state, fine-tuned per suite and scored over 500 episodes each. On an A100, averaged over 100 one-image queries, L1 OFT generates 109.7 actions per second against OpenVLA's 4.2, about 26 times as many, and 71.4 with the added inputs <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. LIBERO-Plus finds that such models drop from 95% to below 30% under modest perturbations and tend to ignore language instructions completely <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />.

Real ALOHA runs use three images, robot state and absolute joint targets at 25 Hz, down from the platform's 50 Hz. OFT+ predicts and executes all 25 actions before replanning; on LIBERO, eight. ALOHA tasks include folding clothes and language-directed scooping and placement, scored for partial completion, while LIBERO scores binary success <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.

In the one-image LIBERO comparison with parallel decoding and eight-step chunks, L1 OFT averages 95.3% against 95.4% for a diffusion head, and 90.7% against 91.1% on LIBERO-Long, where diffusion uses 50 denoising steps. The diffusion variants trained longer and converged and inferred more slowly. The demonstrations follow one consistent strategy per task, and the authors leave multimodal demonstrations and large-scale pretraining open <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Removing ACT's CVAE objective barely changes scripted-data results but cuts human-data success from 35.3% to 2%; its chunk-size ablation is in [action chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking/) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## State of the field

Action interfaces, data mixtures, inputs and execution protocols differ across these studies, so read token-generation and robot-control rates separately <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. With LIBERO near its ceiling, ranking needs harder tests ([evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/)): RoboArena compares generalist policies in double-blind pairs on DROID robots at seven institutions <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />, and SIMPLER's simulated scores correlate with real performance <Cite id="simpler-2024" />.

## A 2026 selection rule

A VLA is two decisions. The prior is the visual, language and robot experience in the backbone; the control interface is the action, horizon, frame and measured rate. A large prior can improve instruction following while a poor interface fails at contact: OFT changed both throughput and success without replacing OpenVLA <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. Before choosing a checkpoint, write down the required semantic novelty, available embodiment data, action contract and latency budget. Pick the prior with the [foundation-model guide](/manipulation/foundation-models/) and the output with the [action-space guide](/manipulation/action-spaces/), then compare only models whose contracts match; [generalist policies](/manipulation/generalist-policies/) and [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) cover the rest.

GR00T N1 separates the two, with a vision-language System 2 over a diffusion-transformer System 1 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. π0.7 conditions on language plus strategy metadata and subgoal images, and Physical Intelligence reports zero-shot cross-embodiment laundry folding <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 controls full humanoids, including a 22-degree-of-freedom hand, and is available only to early-access partners <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.
