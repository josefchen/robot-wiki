## Narrow the field before comparing scores

Filter on weights, licensing and training data to shortlist models (Gemini Robotics On-Device is only available to trusted testers, via an SDK <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />). Then shortlist further based on what actions the model can predict, its inference speed, what cameras it needs as input, and data requirements, depending on the robot.

<Image id="roboarena-evaluation-2025" />

Evaluations should be conducted under one protocol (same cameras, horizon, resets) and include a behavior-cloning policy as a baseline. TRI's study of Large Behavior Models used blind, randomized trials <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. RoboArena aggregated results from more than 600 double-blind, pairwise evaluation episodes and demonstrated improved accuracy in ranking seven generalist policies over a centralized evaluation <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />.

One training comparison used the same SO-101 pick-and-place task at five demonstration counts. With its action expert fine-tuned, π0.5 succeeded in 16 of 40 trials after 12 demonstrations, whereas ACT could only complete one. With 100 demonstrations, ACT completed 37 of 40 and π0.5 completed 30. The trials leave the size of that later advantage uncertain <Cite id="so101-demo-scaling-2026" />.

## Actions are numbers, tokens or refined noise

Actions are represented as plain numbers, word-like tokens, or noise refined into motion (via diffusion or <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>). In some cases, actions may be represented differently during training and inference: π0.5 is trained using compressed FAST action tokens <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" /> but decodes actions using flow matching <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

The <Term id="predicted-horizon">predicted horizon</Term> is how many actions a chunk holds; the <Term id="committed-horizon">committed horizon</Term> is how many run before replanning. [Real-time chunking](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) computes the next chunk during the current one <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## What each model builds on and controls

Backbone and conditioning are a model's pretrained parts and accepted inputs. Octo takes an instruction in words or a goal image <Cite id="octo-2024" />, and Skild says S1 performs a task shown in one video <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />.

Evidence for cross-embodiment learning can take one of three forms, of increasing strength: learning from data collected on many embodiments (as in Open X-Embodiment's 22 robot types <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />), fine-tuning on a new embodiment, or using a single set of model weights to control multiple embodiments. CrossFormer has the strongest: identical weights control very different robots after 900,000 trajectories from 20 embodiments <Cite id="crossformer-2024" />.

Hierarchy separates planning from motion. For example, π0.5 predicts a subtask and conditions its action prediction on it, inside one model <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, and π0 can also accept subtask prediction from another vision-language model <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. GR00T N1.7's SONIC workflow hands latent action tokens to a separate whole-body controller <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

Downloadable weights can still carry license limits: GR00T N1.7 has its code under Apache 2.0 but weights under the NVIDIA Open Model License <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />. openpi offers checkpoints for π0, π0-FAST and π0.5 <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />, and SmolVLA shares code, training data and pretrained models <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />.

## Seven rows carry caveats

| Row | Caveat |
| --- | --- |
| RT-1 <Cite id="rt1-2022" /> | "Limited" cross-embodiment: Kuka IIWA and Everyday Robots data, evaluated on Everyday Robots |

| RT-2 <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> | Excludes the chain-of-thought variant |
| OpenVLA <Cite id="openvla-2024" /> | 7B is the language model, beside a 600M visual encoder |

| GR00T N1.6 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" /> | Actions relative to the current state for most bodies |
| π*0.6 <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> | Recap training more than doubles throughput on some of the hardest tasks |

## Vendors leave out the details

| System | Gives | Leaves out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gemini Robotics 1.5 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />, 2 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />, 2.5 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" /> | 1.5: continuous actions, one checkpoint for ALOHA, two-arm Franka and Apollo | Action head, chunk length, command rate, parameter count |
| Helix 02 <Cite id="helix-02-2026" /> | 200 Hz joint targets, 1 kHz actuator commands; the first Helix ran a 7 to 9 Hz onboard VLM over an 80M-parameter, 200 Hz controller trained by regression <Cite id="helix-2025" /> | Training loss, chunk horizon, release terms |
| AgiBot GO-2 <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" /> | Slow planner, fast executor | Rates, backbone, output format, weight terms |
| Skild <Cite id="skild-series-c-2026" /> | A funding announcement | Reproducible evaluation, runtime details |

AgiBot's earlier GO-1 learns latent actions from more than 1 million trajectories in the AgiBot World dataset covering 217 tasks <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. Generalist's 99% average success for GEN-1, against 64% for earlier models with 1 hour of robot data per task, is self-reported <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.
