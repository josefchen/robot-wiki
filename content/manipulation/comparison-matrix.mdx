The comparison matrix is a table of major robot policies, from RT-1 (2022) to π0.7 (2026), compared on eight architectural axes <Cite id="rt1-2022" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. The axes are action representation, horizon, control frequency, backbone, conditioning, cross-embodiment, hierarchy and weights. Each cell records what its named source discloses. Set the weights filter to Downloadable.

## Use the matrix as a constraint solver

Remove models whose weights or training data cannot be used under the project's terms; Gemini Robotics On-Device, for example, is reached through a trusted-tester SDK <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />. Then remove models whose action representation, measured latency, observations or adaptation-data needs do not fit the robot. Benchmark performance comes last.

Compare the survivors under one trial protocol with the same cameras, action horizon and resets, and keep a behavior-cloning or action-chunking baseline. TRI's Large Behavior Model study used blind, randomized trials with statistical confidence <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />. RoboArena ranked seven generalist policies more accurately than centralized evaluation through over 600 double-blind pairwise real-robot episodes <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />. The [foundation-model guide](/manipulation/foundation-models/) explains what prior each family contributes, the [action-space guide](/manipulation/action-spaces/) whether its output fits, and the [evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/) why protocols resist a shared leaderboard.

## The eight axes

**Action representation** is the output mechanism: continuous regression, discrete action tokens, or a generative head such as diffusion or <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>. Training supervision and runtime generation can differ. The π0.5 paper trains with FAST tokens and generates continuous actions with flow matching, and calls autoregressive decoding expensive at inference <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

**Horizon** separates the planned chunk length $H$ from the steps executed $\hat{H}$ before re-planning: the <Term id="predicted-horizon">predicted horizon</Term> and the <Term id="committed-horizon">committed horizon</Term>. Diffusion Policy's simulated CNN Push-T reference uses observation, prediction and execution horizons of 2, 16 and 8 <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. π0.7 predicts 50 with five denoising steps and executes 15 or 25 <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. ACT's 100-step entry is its k=100 chunk-ablation reference <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. OFT's ALOHA OFT+ setting predicts and executes 25 actions at 25 Hz, and LIBERO eight <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. [Real-time chunking](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) generates the next chunk while the current one executes <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

**Control frequency** needs a named measurement setting. ACT's ALOHA setup runs at 50 Hz, with <Term id="temporal-ensembling">temporal ensembling</Term> querying the policy every timestep <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. RT-1 commands the Everyday Robots platform at 3 Hz <Cite id="rt1-2022" />. RT-2's multi-TPU cloud service runs PaLI-X-55B at 1 to 3 Hz and the 5B model at around 5 Hz <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. OpenVLA reaches about 6 Hz on one RTX 4090 in bfloat16 without speed-up tricks, and its Franka fine-tuning setups use 5 Hz and 15 Hz controllers <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Diffusion Policy's real UR5 Push-T predicts at 10 Hz and interpolates to 125 Hz <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

Gemini Robotics splits a cloud backbone from an on-robot decoder, and multi-action chunks turn about 250 ms of end-to-end latency into a 50 Hz effective control frequency <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />. GR00T N1 runs its VLM at 10 Hz on an L40 and samples a 16-action chunk in 63.9 ms <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />. The π0.6 card reports 63 ms per chunk with five denoising steps, three cameras and one H100 <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. π0.7 runs UR5e robots at 20 Hz and others at 50 Hz <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, and Generalist says GEN-1.5 produces 100 Hz action trajectories and keeps 30 seconds of video memory <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />.

**Backbone**, **conditioning** and **cross-embodiment** describe model components, inputs and the scope of robot-body transfer. Mixed-embodiment training, one checkpoint on several bodies and fine-tuning on a new body are different evidence. CrossFormer shows the strongest kind: the same weights control very different robots after training on 900K trajectories from 20 embodiments <Cite id="crossformer-2024" />. Skild says S1 executes a task shown in one video <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />, which adds demonstration video as a conditioning input.

**Hierarchy** distinguishes an internal high-level stage from a separately paired system. π0.5 predicts a semantic subtask before low-level actions <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. In the N1.7 SONIC workflow, a separate whole-body controller decodes the VLA's latent action tokens into joint commands <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />. RT-1 runs inside SayCan in its long-horizon kitchen experiments <Cite id="rt1-2022" />, and a separate high-level VLM gives π0 subtasks on several tasks <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

**Weights** records download availability, separately from license. The pinned openpi README lists π0, π0-FAST and π0.5 checkpoints <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. The N1.7 README lists downloadable weights, and its License section separates Apache 2.0 code from weights under the NVIDIA Open Model License <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />. SmolVLA releases all code, pretrained models and training data <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />.

## Row notes

RT-1's "limited" cross-embodiment label means Kuka IIWA data mixed with Everyday Robots data and evaluated on Everyday Robots; the paper announces code <Cite id="rt1-2022" />. ACT trains a roughly 80M-parameter model from scratch for each task and announces software and hardware <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. Diffusion Policy announces code, data and training details <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. RT-2 also studies PaLM-E-12B; its robot data is RT-1's 13-robot kitchen set, and a separate PaLI 3B model covers Language-Table. The action-only entry excludes its chain-of-thought variant <Cite id="rt2-2023" />.

OpenVLA predicts single-step actions, supports multi-robot use and fine-tuning, and releases downloadable checkpoints; its 7B label names the language model, beside a 600M visual encoder <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. OFT's "limited" label means adapting the pretrained model to separate tasks and robot setups <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. GR00T N1.6 predicts state-relative action chunks for most embodiments <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />. π*0.6 adds Recap post-training, which more than doubles throughput on some of the hardest tasks and roughly halves failure rates <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

Gemini Robotics 1.5's model card discloses continuous numerical action outputs but no action-head family, chunk length, command rate or parameter count. One checkpoint runs across ALOHA, bi-arm Franka and Apollo, its thinking is internal, and a separate GR-ER 1.5 orchestrator can pair with it; the long-horizon agent experiment adds post-training on bi-arm Franka <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />.

DeepMind's July 30, 2026 announcement pairs the Gemini Robotics 2 VLA with the separate ER 2 model. One checkpoint covers Apollo 2 with SharpaWave or Inspire hands and Franka Duo with Robotiq, while On-Device adaptations sit outside that result. The announcement gives no backbone, output parameterization, chunk length, control frequency or parameter count, and offers access through AI Studio, private preview and early-access partnerships <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" />.

Skild's row rests on its January 14, 2026 Series C announcement, which describes data sources and an omni-bodied claim but no reproducible cross-embodiment evaluation or runtime details <Cite id="skild-series-c-2026" />.

## What the matrix can establish

The matrix records concrete differences in output mechanism: ACT fixes its CVAE latent to the prior mean, zero, to decode deterministically <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />, while π0 generates continuous action chunks with a flow-matching action expert <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Generalist says GEN-1's base model trains on wearable human data without robot data and reaches 99% average success where earlier models reached 64%, with 1 hour of robot data per task <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />. On March 16, 2026, NVIDIA previewed GR00T N2, a robot foundation model based on DreamZero research <Cite id="nvidia-gtc-gr00t-n2-2026" />.

## Helix 02 and GO-2 disclosure limits

Figure's Helix 02 announcement gives S1 joint targets at 200 Hz and S0 actuator commands at 1 kHz, two stages of one stack. It names no output-head training loss, chunk horizon or weight-release terms <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Figure's February 2025 post on the first Helix disclosed a 7 to 9 Hz onboard VLM and an 80M-parameter S1 at 200 Hz over 35 degrees of freedom, trained end-to-end with a standard regression loss <Cite id="helix-2025" />. Figure reports that Index human-video pretraining, all else fixed, raised Helix 2.5's zero-shot success in 30 unseen homes from 9% to 56% <Cite id="helix-2-5-2026" />.

AgiBot describes lower-frequency System 2 semantic planning and higher-frequency System 1 action following, with no numerical rate, backbone, output encoding, chunk length, cross-body transfer or weight terms <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />. The predecessor GO-1 uses latent action representations trained on AgiBot World's more than 1 million trajectories across 217 tasks <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />.
