## Narrow the field before comparing scores

Benchmarks come last. First drop models whose weights, license or training data rule them out; Gemini Robotics On-Device, for example, goes only to trusted testers via an SDK <Cite id="gemini-robotics-on-device-2025" />. Then drop those whose actions, speed, cameras or data needs do not fit the robot.

<Image id="roboarena-evaluation-2025" />

Compare the rest under one protocol, with identical cameras, horizon and resets, and keep a behavior-cloning baseline. TRI's Large Behavior Model study used blind, randomized trials <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />, and RoboArena ranked seven generalist policies more accurately than centralized evaluation through over 600 double-blind pairwise episodes <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />. The [foundation-model guide](/manipulation/foundation-models/) covers priors.

## How actions are written and timed

Action representation is the form of the output: plain numbers, word-like tokens, or a generative head that refines noise into motion by diffusion or <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>. Training and running can differ: π0.5 learns from compressed FAST tokens <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" /> but generates actions by flow matching <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

The horizon has two parts: the <Term id="predicted-horizon">predicted horizon</Term>, how many actions a chunk holds, and the <Term id="committed-horizon">committed horizon</Term>, how many run before replanning. Diffusion Policy's simulated Push-T setup looks at 2 frames, predicts 16 actions and executes 8 <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. ACT's 100-step entry comes from its chunk-size tests <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />, OFT+ predicts and executes 25 actions at 25 Hz on ALOHA <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />, and π0.7 predicts 50 and executes 15 or 25 <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. [Real-time chunking](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) computes the next chunk during the current one <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

Control frequency depends on hardware. ACT runs at 50 Hz with <Term id="temporal-ensembling">temporal ensembling</Term> <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />, RT-1 at 3 Hz <Cite id="rt1-2022" />, the 55-billion-parameter RT-2 at 1 to 3 Hz from cloud servers <Cite id="rt2-2023" /> and OpenVLA at about 6 Hz on one RTX 4090 graphics card <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. Chunks hide latency: Gemini Robotics turns about 250 milliseconds of latency into effective 50 Hz control <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, GR00T N1 samples a 16-action chunk in 63.9 milliseconds <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />, π0.6 takes 63 <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />, and Generalist says GEN-1.5 produces 100 Hz trajectories <Cite id="gen-1-5-2026" />.

## What each model builds on and controls

Backbone and conditioning are a model's pretrained parts and accepted inputs. Octo takes an instruction in words or a goal image <Cite id="octo-2024" />, and Skild says S1 performs a task shown in one video <Cite id="skild-s1-2026" />.

Cross-embodiment, handling several kinds of robot, has three strengths of evidence: training on many robots' data, as with Open X-Embodiment's 22 robot types <Cite id="open-x-embodiment-2023" />; fine-tuning to a new body; and one checkpoint running different bodies. CrossFormer gives the strongest: identical weights control very different robots after 900,000 trajectories from 20 embodiments <Cite id="crossformer-2024" />.

Hierarchy is whether planning and motion are separate stages. π0.5 writes a subtask before acting, inside one model <Cite id="pi05-2025" />; π0 can take subtasks from a separate high-level vision-language model <Cite id="pi0-2024" />; GR00T N1.7's SONIC workflow hands latent action tokens to a separate whole-body controller <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />.

Weights separates downloading a model from being allowed to use it. openpi lists π0, π0-FAST and π0.5 checkpoints <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />, N1.7 pairs Apache 2.0 code with NVIDIA Open Model License weights <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />, and SmolVLA releases code, pretrained models and training data <Cite id="smolvla-2025" />.

## Notes on individual rows

RT-1's "limited" cross-embodiment training mixes Kuka IIWA and Everyday Robots data, evaluated on Everyday Robots <Cite id="rt1-2022" />. ACT trains a task-specific, roughly 80-million-parameter model from scratch <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />; RT-2's entry excludes its chain-of-thought variant <Cite id="rt2-2023" />. OpenVLA's 7B is its language model, beside a 600M visual encoder <Cite id="openvla-2024" />. OFT's "limited" means adapting a pretrained model to separate tasks and robot setups <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. GR00T N1.6 predicts actions relative to the current state for most bodies <Cite id="gr00t-n1-6-2025" />, and π*0.6's Recap training more than doubles throughput on some of the hardest tasks <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

## What vendors leave out

Gemini Robotics 1.5's card discloses continuous actions but no action head, chunk length, command rate or parameter count, though one checkpoint spans ALOHA, two-arm Franka and Apollo <Cite id="gemini-robotics-15-2025" />; Gemini Robotics 2 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-2026" /> and 2.5 <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2-5-2026" /> omit the same details. Figure gave Helix 02's rates, 200 Hz joint targets and 1 kHz actuator commands, but no training loss, chunk horizon or release terms <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />; the first Helix post gave a 7 to 9 Hz onboard VLM and an 80M-parameter controller at 200 Hz trained by standard regression <Cite id="helix-2025" />. AgiBot describes GO-2's slow planner and fast executor without rates, backbone, output format or weight terms <Cite id="agibot-go2-2026" />; GO-1 learned latent actions from AgiBot World's over 1 million trajectories across 217 tasks <Cite id="agibot-world-2025" />. Skild's row rests on a funding announcement without reproducible evaluation or runtime details <Cite id="skild-series-c-2026" />, and Generalist's 99% average success for GEN-1, against 64% for earlier models with 1 hour of robot data per task, is its own claim <Cite id="generalist-gen1-2026" />.

## The newest rows

The five newest rows are GR00T 2, Gemini Robotics 2.5, AgiBot GO-3 Preview, Praxis-1 and Xiaomi-Robotics-1. NVIDIA previewed GR00T N2, based on DreamZero, in March 2026 <Cite id="nvidia-gtc-gr00t-n2-2026" />. Its GR00T 2 (November 2026) denoises future video and an action chunk together; on a Unitree G1 its Turbo variant plans 1.6-second chunks about once a second, run at 50 Hz, with code due in December <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />. Runway's Praxis-1 is a 27-billion-parameter diffusion transformer for video and actions with Apache 2.0 weights <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" />. Xiaomi-Robotics-1 pairs a Qwen3-VL backbone with a flow-matching transformer in 2.6B to 10.5B sizes <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-2026" />, with Apache 2.0 checkpoints <Cite id="xiaomi-robotics-1-repo-2026" />.
