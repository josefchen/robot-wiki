<Term id="behavior-cloning">Behavior cloning</Term> trains a network to map what a robot senses to [what a demonstrator did](/how-robots-work/#puppeteer). It is the simplest <Term id="imitation-learning">imitation learning</Term>, and its small mistakes <Term id="compounding-error">compound</Term> <Cite id="ross-bagnell-2010" />.

## Driving found the flaw first

In 1988 ALVINN steered Carnegie Mellon's Navlab van from simulated road images <Cite id="alvinn-1988" />; by 1991 it learned to drive by watching a person for under five minutes <Cite id="pomerleau-1991" />. A good driver stays centered, so the network never saw a recovery. Pomerleau shifted each image into 14 off-center copies labeled with corrected steering <Cite id="pomerleau-1991" />.

Also: rotated views <Cite id="bojarski-e2e-driving-2016" />, synthetic perturbations <Cite id="chauffeurnet-2018" />.

Robot arms learn the same way, from ACT and Diffusion Policy <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" /> to large multitask models <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

## Small errors compound

Each slip moves the robot into states its training data never covered, where the next slip is likelier <Cite id="dagger-2011" />.

## Covariate shift costs up to T²ε

Training data come from the expert's states, but the robot acts in its own <Cite id="dagger-2011" />. Ross and Bagnell (2010) bounded the cost of this <Term id="covariate-shift">covariate shift</Term> <Cite id="ross-bagnell-2010" />.

With per-step cost in [0, 1] and error $\varepsilon$ on the expert's states, a cloned <Term id="policy">policy</Term>'s cost over $T$ steps can exceed the expert's by $T^2\varepsilon$, a tight bound <Cite id="dagger-2011" />:

$$
J(\pi) \leq J(\pi^*) + T^2\varepsilon
$$

Later theory cuts both ways.

| Result | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Data needed can be independent of task length <Cite id="foster-bc-horizon-2024" /> | Log-loss training, payoffs and model complexity controlled |
| A DAgger variant provably wins when recovery is cheap <Cite id="stagger-hybrid-il-2024" /> | Labels counted per state |
| A $T^2$ penalty survives even with expert queries <Cite id="rajaraman-il-limits-2020" /> | Worst-case discrete problems |
| Error can grow exponentially with task length <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" /> | Smooth deterministic imitators of continuous systems |

## Demonstrations hide several answers

Some tasks have several right answers: in Push-T the gripper can pass a block on either side, and Diffusion Policy learns both routes <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Energy-based implicit policies often beat mean-squared-error ones <Cite id="implicit-bc-2021" />.

Operators pause, so a policy that sees only the current frame can learn to stall <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />. Adding history can backfire, making a cloned policy worse under shift <Cite id="causal-confusion-2019" /> or copying the expert's previous action <Cite id="copycat-bc-2020" />.

Variety beats volume: past a threshold, more environments and objects help while more demonstrations per environment barely do <Cite id="lin-data-scaling-laws-2024" />. Behavior cloning at best matches its demonstrator <Cite id="robomimic-2021" />.

Also: generative gains traced to iterative computation <Cite id="much-ado-noising-2025" />, weighting failure-critical states <Cite id="scrim-state-criticality-2026" />, weight averaging against training noise <Cite id="butterfly-sgd-noise-2023" />, OCBench's open puzzles <Cite id="ocbench-2026" />.

## DAgger relabels the states the robot visits

<Term id="dagger">DAgger</Term> (Dataset Aggregation, 2011) has the expert label the states the learner reaches, turning imitation into online learning <Cite id="dagger-2011" />. Each round runs the policy, has the expert label every state it visited, and retrains on all the data.

Under idealized assumptions one of its policies meets the bound below, with $\varepsilon_N$ the best error achievable on visited states <Cite id="dagger-2011" />. The penalty is linear in $T$ only while $u$, the extra cost of one wrong action, stays bounded.

$$
J(\hat{\pi}) \leq J(\pi^*) + uT\varepsilon_N + O(1)
$$

Recovery data needs no expert: DART noises the demonstrator's commands and gained 62% on average over behavior cloning at grasping in clutter <Cite id="dart-2017" />. Others generate off-course images <Cite id="diffusion-meets-dagger-2024" /> or steer operators toward the learner's likely mistakes <Cite id="data-inoculation-2026" />.

## Human corrections beat more demonstrations

In HG-DAgger the person takes over to steer the robot back, and the recovery becomes training data <Cite id="hg-dagger-2019" />.

| Method | Result |
| --- | --- |
| BC-Z <Cite id="bc-z-2022" /> | Trained on interventions: 44% average success on 24 unseen tasks |
| Compliant Residual DAgger <Cite id="cr-dagger-2025" /> | Small corrections without pausing the robot: base success up 64% on four tasks |
| I3L, Oct 2026 <Cite id="i3l-2026" /> | 35.6 points over demonstration-only training on whole-body mobile manipulation |
| RaC <Cite id="rac-2025" /> | Imitation plateaued despite thousands of demonstrations; corrections beat the prior best with 10 times less collection |
| PHIL, Nov 2026 <Cite id="phil-proactive-correction-2026" /> | Shows operators the planned motion: 70.3% mean success from five correction episodes, against 44.0% for HG-DAgger |

Corrections now train vision-language-action models <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" /> <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> <Cite id="sop-2026" />. DAgger distills simulation experts into camera students too: in RebarSim, plain cloning in its place cut simulated success from 96.1% to 68.9% <Cite id="rebarsim-2026" />.

Also: NVIDIA's Recova correction pipeline <Cite id="recova-2026" />.

## Chunking, generative models and RL also curb drift

[Action chunking](/manipulation/action-chunking/) and exploratory data collection provably avoid exponential compounding in stable systems <Cite id="zhang-chunking-exploration-2025" />. A [diffusion policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) over a stabilizing controller can match the expert's per-step distribution <Cite id="block-generative-bc-2023" />.

GAIL <Cite id="gail-2016" /> and DARP's nearest-neighbor retrieval, 15 to 46% better than behavior cloning <Cite id="darp-2026" />, need no expert queries. HIL-SERL adds real-world RL and nears perfect success in 1 to 2.5 hours <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

Also: why chunking helps <Cite id="lazzati-why-chunking-2026" />.
