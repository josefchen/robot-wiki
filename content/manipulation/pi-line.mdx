The π line is Physical Intelligence's series of [vision-language-action models](/manipulation/vla-models/), from π0 (October 2024) to π0.7 (April 2026) <Cite id="pi0-blog-2024" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Each pairs a web-trained vision-language model with a smaller "action expert" that turns what it understands into smooth bursts of motion <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

<Image id="pi06-espresso-2025" />

## How a π model moves

The model reads two or three camera images, an instruction and the robot's joint readings. A pretrained <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language model</Term> processes images and words, and the action expert reads its internal state at every layer to generate the next 50 actions, one to two and a half seconds of motion. Starting from random noise, it follows a learned direction toward a plausible chunk in 10 steps; the robot carries out 16 or 25 of the actions, and the model looks again <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

<FlowMatchingTrajectory className="my-6" />

This is <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>, which replaced the <Term id="action-tokenization">discrete action tokens</Term> of earlier VLAs. Training mixes noise $\varepsilon$ with true actions along a straight line, $A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\varepsilon$, and learns the direction $u = A_t - \varepsilon$ <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Straight paths can be followed in coarse steps <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />, and Lipman et al. found them more efficient than diffusion paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />; π0.6 and π0.7 use five steps <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

## π0: one model, seven robots

π0 showed that one model could control very different machines. It was pretrained on about 10,000 hours of demonstrations from single-arm, two-arm and mobile robots in 7 configurations, plus Open X-Embodiment data, a pooled multi-lab robot dataset. The base model reached what the authors call "rudimentary proficiency"; harder tasks such as folding laundry needed fine-tuning on curated data <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

## π0-FAST: compressed action tokens

π0-FAST tried the alternative: actions as tokens, like words, but compressed. One token per joint per time step fails at high control rates, because neighboring tokens are nearly identical and predicting the next becomes trivial; such policies made no progress on table bussing at 20 Hz or T-shirt folding at 50 Hz <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. FAST describes a second of motion by its frequencies with the discrete cosine transform, as JPEG does for images, then compresses the rounded coefficients. Its universal FAST+ tokenizer learned from about one million one-second chunks <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />, "1M real robot action sequences" in PI's post <Cite id="pi-fast-blog-2025" />.

<Image id="pi0-fast-laundry-folding-2025" />

On 10,000 hours of data, π0-FAST matched π0 with up to five times fewer GPU hours of training. It runs slower, though: about 750 milliseconds per one-second chunk on an NVIDIA 4090 graphics card, against under 100 for π0, since it writes 30 to 60 tokens where π0 takes ten steps <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. It also took twice as long to finish table bussing <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

## π0.5: homes it has never seen

π0.5 aimed at open-world generalization: mobile robots working in homes absent from the training data. It trains on examples mixing images, commands, object detections, subtask labels and actions, and at run time first writes a subtask in words ("pick up the plate"), then generates its motion <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, a split PI had used in Hi Robot <Cite id="hi-robot-blog-2025" />. Removing either source of cross-robot data significantly hurt task scores, and removing web data mainly hurt language following <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

Knowledge Insulation trains both halves in one stage: the backbone on FAST tokens, vision-language and planning data, the expert by flow matching, with the expert's gradients kept out of the backbone. Freezing the backbone instead dropped shirt folding to 0%, and π0 needed 7.5 times the training steps to match on table bussing <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. openpi's π0.5 checkpoints were trained this way, but openpi supports only flow-matching heads <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />, so outside groups reimplement both <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" />.

## π0.6 and π*0.6: learning from practice

π0.6 is larger. Without task-specific fine-tuning it folds laundry reliably and assembles boxes 20% of the time, tasks that earlier needed fine-tuning for any success <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. In PI's Robot Olympics, fine-tuned π0.6 averaged 52% success, where a vision-language model fine-tuned without its robot pretraining had none <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

π*0.6 learns from its own attempts with Recap, combining demonstrations, teleoperators' corrections and autonomous experience. A value function, which predicts how well the task is going, marks each action as an improvement or not, human corrections always count as improvements, and the deployed policy favors improving actions <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. In PI's espresso example, a poor portafilter grasp dooms a later insertion <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />. The report places Recap closest to CFGRL <Cite id="cfgrl-2025" />; other groups find RL hard on flow-based π0 and π0.5 because flow matching makes action likelihoods intractable <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />.

On two-armed robots, practice more than doubled successful completions per hour for double-shot espresso and varied laundry (tested on a button-up shirt), against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning, and failures fell by "about a factor of two" <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. In February 2026, partners reported π0.6 folding laundry at a customer laundromat and packing live orders <Cite id="pi-partner-2026" />.

## Memory and practice on the robot

MEM, published March 3, 2026 <Cite id="mem-blog-2026" />, gives π0.6 memory: a video encoder plus a high-level policy that keeps a written summary of earlier events, for tasks needing up to fifteen minutes of memory, such as setting up a recipe or cleaning a kitchen <Cite id="mem-2026" />. RL tokens (March 19, 2026) train small actor and critic networks online on a compressed, frozen token from π0.6, speeding up the most precise stages of four tasks by up to 3 times; inserting an Ethernet cable took two hours of training and 15 minutes of robot data <Cite id="rlt-2026" />. [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) covers the wider family.

## π0.7: generalization through richer prompts

π0.7 treats vague prompts as the main barrier to [generalization](/frontier/generalization/). Built on π0.6 and MEM, it accepts prompts that hold the task and subtask in words, notes on a demonstration's quality, speed and mistakes, and subgoal images showing what the scene should look like next, from a world model built on BAGEL <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, pretrained on trillions of mixed tokens <Cite id="bagel-2025" />. Training drops random parts of this context and simulates <Term id="real-time-chunking">real-time chunking</Term> delays of up to 240 milliseconds <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, as in training-time RTC <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

π0.7 folded laundry on a two-armed UR5e robot with no laundry data for that robot, matching experienced teleoperators' task progress on their first try there <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. In an air-fryer example, plain-language coaching fine-tunes the high-level policy without new teleoperation, which the authors call "early signs of compositional task generalization" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. [Human video](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) entered the line through π0.5 co-fine-tuning, roughly doubling performance on scenarios seen only in human data <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

## Which checkpoints are open

PI released π0's code and weights in openpi on February 4, 2025, saying 1 to 20 hours of data sufficed to fine-tune varied tasks <Cite id="openpi-blog-2025" />. openpi's README (August 24, 2026 commit) lists base checkpoints for π0, π0-FAST and π0.5, plus versions fine-tuned for DROID, ALOHA and LIBERO <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. DROID holds 76,000 real-robot trajectories from 564 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and LIBERO 130 simulated tasks <Cite id="libero-2023" />. NVIDIA's Jetson AI Lab shows how to run π0.5 on its Jetson AGX Thor robot computer <Cite id="jetson-ai-lab-openpi-thor" />. No π0.6 or π0.7 weights are listed; since October 13, 2026, PI has offered its latest models for fine-tuning and hosted inference through the Pi API beta <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />. Bloomberg reported on October 27 that PI was in talks to raise at least \$1 billion at about a \$20 billion valuation <Cite id="bloomberg-pi-20b-2026" />.

## The baseline to beat

The open checkpoints became the common baseline for rival labs. Google DeepMind's re-implementation, trained on its own mixture, beat the public π0 checkpoint <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, and OpenVLA with the OFT recipe outperformed π0 fine-tuned with its default recipe <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />. In November 2026 reports, each run by a competitor, π0.5 trailed Meituan-Robotics-0 on three real tasks under matched corrections <Cite id="meituan-mr0-2026" />, scored 28.0% on RoboLab-120 against Praxis-1's 46.2% <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" />, and sorted 0% of conveyor items where GR00T 2 sorted 86% <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" />.
