The pi line is Physical Intelligence's series of [vision-language-action models](/manipulation/vla-models/), from π0 (October 2024) to π0.7 (April 2026) <Cite id="pi0-blog-2024" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

## π0: the action expert

π0 grafts a second transformer, the action expert of about 300M parameters, onto a pretrained <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language model</Term> initialized from PaliGemma; the backbone handles image and text tokens, and the expert's 50 action tokens attend into it at every layer through shared self-attention. Inputs are 2 or 3 RGB images, language and proprioceptive state, and the model predicts H = 50 actions, executing 16 per inference at 20 Hz on UR5e and Franka or 25 at 50 Hz on the other robots <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

Where [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) denoises with a DDPM, π0 trains the expert with conditional flow matching on a straight path. Training samples noise $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, forms $A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\varepsilon$, and regresses $v_\theta$ onto $u = A_t - \varepsilon$. Inference integrates from $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 1$ in 10 forward-Euler steps, with the image-text-state prefix KV-cached once so each step recomputes only the action suffix <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Lipman et al. found optimal-transport paths more efficient than diffusion paths for training and sampling <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />.

<Term id="cross-embodiment">Cross-embodiment</Term> pre-training combines single-arm, dual-arm and mobile manipulators across 7 robot configurations. The base model reaches what the authors call "rudimentary proficiency", and complex tasks such as laundry folding use curated post-training data <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. PI says π0, π0-FAST and π0.5 executed chunks synchronously, waiting for inference after each one <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />, which the RTC paper later compared with asynchronous execution on π0.5 <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## Why ten steps is enough

A DDPM path curves through noise space, while rectified flow straightens paths so that coarse time steps simulate them accurately <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />. Each Euler step costs a forward pass through the expert: π0 uses 10 steps <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, π0.6 uses five <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />, and π0.7 also uses five <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Consistency and one-step distillation, covered in [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/), pursue the same saving.

## π0-FAST: compressed action tokens

FAST traces the failure of per-dimension, per-timestep binning to highly correlated tokens, which make next-token prediction trivial; policies with this naive tokenization make no progress on table bussing at 20 Hz or T-shirt folding at 50 Hz <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. FAST instead applies a discrete cosine transform along time, rounds the coefficients and byte-pair-encodes the result, and its universal FAST+ tokenizer is trained on about one million one-second action chunks across embodiments <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />, or "1M real robot action sequences" in PI's post <Cite id="pi-fast-blog-2025" />.

<Image id="pi0-fast-laundry-folding-2025" />

Trained on a 10k-hour cross-embodied mixture, π0-FAST matches π0's generalist performance with up to five times fewer GPU hours. Inference costs more: about 750 ms per one-second chunk on an NVIDIA 4090, against within 100 ms for diffusion π0, since decoding needs 30 to 60 tokens instead of ten steps <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. The Knowledge Insulation note reports that π0-FAST took twice as long to finish single-embodiment table bussing <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

## π0.5 and Knowledge Insulation

π0.5 targets open-world generalization <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. It keeps the π0 skeleton, adds training examples that interleave images, language commands, object detections, subtask predictions and low-level actions, and at inference first predicts a language subtask ("pick up the plate"), then conditions the action expert on it. In long-horizon mobile manipulation in new homes, removing either cross-embodiment data source significantly hurts home-task scores, while removing web data leaves those scores nearly unchanged but strongly hurts language following and subtask inference <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

The Knowledge Insulation paper turns the two-stage π0.5 approach into one stage: the expert trains with flow matching for continuous inference, and the backbone trains on discrete FAST tokens, vision-language data and planning data, with expert gradients stopped at the backbone keys and values <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. With a frozen backbone, shirt folding drops to 0%; on generalist table bussing, π0 needs 7.5 times as many training steps to match; and vision-language co-training also gives the joint-training baseline good language following without stop-gradient <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. The openpi π0.5 checkpoints were trained with KI, but the repository supports only the flow-matching head <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />, so outside groups reimplement the FAST loss and KI <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" />.

## π0.6 and π*0.6: scaling and experience

The π0.6 model card, dated November 17, 2025, initializes the backbone from Gemma3 4B, gives the action expert about 860M parameters and keeps expert gradients out of the backbone. Without task-specific fine-tuning, π0.6 folds laundry reliably and fully assembles the box 20% of the time, tasks that previously needed fine-tuning to succeed at all <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. In PI's Robot Olympics, fine-tuned π0.6 averaged 52% success and 72% task progress, while a fine-tuned standard VLM without π0.6's robot pre-training had no successes and 9% progress <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

π*0.6 uses Recap to learn from demonstrations, expert teleoperation corrections and autonomous experience, with a language-conditioned value function whose advantage, against a task-dependent threshold, gives each action a binary improvement indicator; human corrections are forced positive, and evaluation samples with the positive indicator <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The report places this formulation closest to CFGRL <Cite id="cfgrl-2025" />, while outside groups find RL on flow-based π0 and π0.5 hard because flow matching makes action log-likelihoods intractable <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. The companion blog's example is a bad portafilter grasp that causes a later insertion failure <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

On a rig with two 6-DoF arms and 50 Hz joint-position control, on-robot experience more than doubles successful completions per hour for double-shot espresso and diverse laundry, tested on a button-up shirt, against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning. Failures fall by "about a factor of two" ("more than 2×" in the figure), the "90%+" summary excludes diverse laundry, and the box task reports stages separately, with remaining failures mostly in final placement <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

PI published MEM on March 3, 2026 <Cite id="mem-blog-2026" />. π0.6-MEM pairs a video encoder with a high-level policy that predicts updated natural-language summaries of earlier events, and handles tasks needing up to fifteen minutes of memory, such as recipe setup and kitchen cleanup. Its adaptation experiments train memory and memoryless policies on the same corrections, such as which way to open a refrigerator <Cite id="mem-2026" />.

## RL tokens

On March 19, 2026, PI described RL tokens: small actor and critic networks train online on a compressed, frozen token from π0.6, speeding up the most precise stages of four tasks by up to 3×; Ethernet insertion took two hours of training with 15 minutes of robot data <Cite id="rlt-2026" />. [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) covers the wider method family.

## π0.7: generalization as a prompting problem

π0.7 treats underspecified prompts as the [generalization](/frontier/generalization/) bottleneck. It is π0.6 plus MEM at about 5B parameters, and training simulates <Term id="real-time-chunking">real-time chunking</Term> delays of 0 to 12 timesteps, up to 240 ms on a 50 Hz robot <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, the training-time RTC method that on π0.6 matched inference-time RTC at lower compute <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

The context holds task and subtask language, with the subtask from a learned high-level policy or a human, and episode metadata on quality, speed and mistakes; training uses different subsets of this context but always keeps the joint or end-effector control-mode label. Multi-view subgoal images come from a BAGEL-initialized world model, and subgoal and subtask generation run in separate threads <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. BAGEL is a unified decoder-only model pretrained on trillions of interleaved tokens <Cite id="bagel-2025" />.

π0.7 folds laundry on a bimanual UR5e with no laundry data for that robot, matching the task progress of experienced teleoperators trying it there for the first time <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. In the air-fryer example, language coaching fine-tunes a high-level policy that generates the subtasks without new teleoperation, which the authors call "early signs of compositional task generalization" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. [Human video](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) joins the line through π0.5 co-fine-tuning, which roughly doubles performance on scenarios seen only in human data <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

## Which checkpoints are listed

PI released π0's code and weights in openpi on February 4, 2025, saying 1 to 20 hours of data sufficed to fine-tune a variety of tasks <Cite id="openpi-blog-2025" />. The openpi README at the pinned commit of August 24, 2026 lists base checkpoints for π0, π0-FAST and π0.5, plus π0-FAST, π0 and π0.5 models fine-tuned on DROID and a π0.5 LIBERO model <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. DROID holds 76k trajectories from 564 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and LIBERO has 130 tasks in four suites <Cite id="libero-2023" />.

In two outside comparisons, Google DeepMind's re-implementation trained on its own mixture beat the public π0 checkpoint <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />, and OpenVLA with the OFT recipe outperformed π0 fine-tuned with its default recipe <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" />.
