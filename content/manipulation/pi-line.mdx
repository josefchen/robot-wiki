The π line is Physical Intelligence's series of [vision-language-action models](/manipulation/vla-models/), from π0 (October 2024) to π0.7 (April 2026) <Cite id="pi0-blog-2024" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Each pairs a web-trained vision-language model with a smaller "action expert" that turns what it sees into bursts of motion <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

<Image id="pi06-espresso-2025" />

## A π model refines noise into motion

A pretrained <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language model</Term> reads camera images and the instruction. The action expert reads its state at every layer and outputs the next 50 actions, 1 to 2.5 seconds of motion. It starts from noise and takes 10 steps toward a plausible chunk; the robot runs 16 or 25, then the model replans <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

<FlowMatchingTrajectory className="my-6" />

This is <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term>, which replaced the <Term id="action-tokenization">discrete action tokens</Term> of earlier VLAs. Training mixes noise $\varepsilon$ with true actions along a straight line, $A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\varepsilon$, and learns the direction $u = A_t - \varepsilon$ <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Straight paths survive coarse steps <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" /> and train more efficiently than diffusion paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />.

## π0: one model, seven robots

π0 controlled 7 robot configurations with one model. Base π0 reached "rudimentary proficiency"; laundry folding needed fine-tuning on curated data <Cite id="pi0-2024" />.

## π0-FAST compresses actions into tokens

Per-step action tokens fail at high control rates because neighbors are nearly identical; such policies made no progress bussing tables at 20 Hz <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. FAST encodes a second of motion by its frequencies, as JPEG does images <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" /> <Cite id="pi-fast-blog-2025" />.

<Image id="pi0-fast-laundry-folding-2025" />

π0-FAST matched π0 with up to five times fewer GPU hours. It runs slower, about 750 ms per one-second chunk on an NVIDIA 4090 against under 100 ms for π0 <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />, and took twice as long to bus a table <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

## π0.5 works in homes it has never seen

π0.5 runs mobile robots in homes absent from its training data. It writes a subtask in words ("pick up the plate"), then the motion <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, as in PI's Hi Robot <Cite id="hi-robot-blog-2025" />. Removing cross-robot data cut task scores; removing web data mainly hurt language following <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

Knowledge Insulation trains both halves at once and blocks the expert's gradients from the backbone; π0 needed 7.5 times the training steps to match it on table bussing <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. openpi trains π0.5 this way but ships only flow-matching heads <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />, so others reimplement both <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" />.

## π*0.6 learns from its own practice

π0.6 folds laundry reliably and assembles boxes 20% of the time with no task fine-tuning <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. Fine-tuned on 11 Robot Olympics tasks, it averaged 52% success on them, where a VLM without robot pretraining scored zero <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

π*0.6 adds Recap. A value function labels each action, from demonstrations, corrections or autonomous runs, as an improvement or not, and the policy favors improvements <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. Against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning, practice more than doubled espresso and laundry throughput and roughly halved failures <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

Also: the espresso failure case <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />, Recap's nearest relative CFGRL <Cite id="cfgrl-2025" />, RL on flow-based π models <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />, partner deployments <Cite id="pi-partner-2026" />.

## Memory and practice run on the robot

MEM gives π0.6 up to fifteen minutes of memory from video and a written summary of events <Cite id="mem-2026" /> <Cite id="mem-blog-2026" />. RL tokens, a form of [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/), train small actor and critic networks on a frozen π0.6 token. They sped the most precise stages of four tasks up to 3 times <Cite id="rlt-2026" />.

## π0.7 generalizes through richer prompts

π0.7 treats vague prompts as the main barrier to [generalization](/frontier/generalization/). Prompts can carry the subtask, notes on a demonstration's quality and speed, and subgoal images from a BAGEL-based world model <Cite id="pi07-2026" /> <Cite id="bagel-2025" />. Training drops random parts of this context and simulates <Term id="real-time-chunking">real-time chunking</Term> delays up to 240 ms <Cite id="pi07-2026" /> <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

π0.7 folded laundry on a two-armed UR5e with no laundry data from it, matching experienced teleoperators' task progress on its first try <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Also: air-fryer coaching with no new teleoperation <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />; [human video](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) in π0.5, about twice as good on human-only scenarios <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

<span id="where-the-open-weights-stop" />

## Open weights stop at π0.5

openpi lists base checkpoints for π0, π0-FAST and π0.5, plus DROID, ALOHA and LIBERO fine-tunes <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. PI released it on February 4, 2025, saying 1 to 20 hours of data fine-tune varied tasks <Cite id="openpi-blog-2025" />. π0.6 and π0.7 weights are unlisted; PI's latest models have been on the Pi API beta since October 13, 2026 <Cite id="pi-api-beta-2026" />.

Also: DROID, 76,000 real trajectories <Cite id="droid-2024" />; LIBERO, 130 simulated tasks <Cite id="libero-2023" />; π0.5 on Jetson AGX Thor <Cite id="jetson-ai-lab-openpi-thor" />; PI's reported \$20 billion valuation talks <Cite id="bloomberg-pi-20b-2026" />.
