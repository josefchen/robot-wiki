The pi line is Physical Intelligence's series of [vision-language-action models](/manipulation/vla-models/), from π0 (October 2024) to π0.7 (April 2026) <Cite id="pi0-blog-2024" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

Successors changed tokenization, training data, memory and prompting. Drag the integration steps slider from 1 upward to watch the cloud reach the action modes.

## π0: the action expert

π0 grafts a second transformer, its roughly 300M-parameter action expert, onto a pretrained <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language model</Term> initialized from PaliGemma; the backbone handles image and text tokens; the expert's 50 action tokens attend into it every layer through shared self-attention. Inputs are 2 or 3 RGB images, language and proprioceptive state; it predicts H = 50 actions, executing 16 per inference at 20 Hz on UR5e and Franka or 25 at 50 Hz on the other robots <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. PaliGemma couples a SigLIP vision encoder and Gemma language model for transfer across vision-language tasks <Cite id="paligemma-2024" />.

Where [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) denoises with a DDPM, π0 trains the expert with conditional flow matching on a straight path. Training samples noise $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, forms $A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\varepsilon$, and regresses $v_\theta$ onto $u = A_t - \varepsilon$. Inference integrates from $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 1$ in 10 forward-Euler steps, KV-caching the image-text-state prefix once so steps recompute only the action suffix <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Lipman et al. found optimal-transport paths more efficient than diffusion paths for training and sampling <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />.

<Term id="cross-embodiment">Cross-embodiment</Term> pre-training combines single-arm, dual-arm and mobile manipulators across 7 robot configurations. The base model reaches the authors' "rudimentary proficiency"; complex tasks including laundry folding use curated post-training data <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. PI says π0, π0-FAST and π0.5 executed chunks synchronously, waiting after each for inference <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />, which the RTC paper later compared with asynchronous π0.5 execution <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## Why ten steps is enough

DDPM paths curve through noise space; rectified flow straightens paths so coarse time steps simulate them accurately <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />. Each Euler step costs one expert forward pass: π0 uses 10 steps <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, π0.6 uses five <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />, and π0.7 uses five <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Consistency and one-step distillation, covered in [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/), seek that saving.

## π0-FAST: compressed action tokens

FAST attributes per-dimension, per-timestep binning failures to highly correlated tokens making next-token prediction trivial; policies with this naive tokenization make no progress on table bussing at 20 Hz or T-shirt folding at 50 Hz <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. FAST instead applies a temporal discrete cosine transform, rounds the coefficients and byte-pair-encodes the result; its universal FAST+ tokenizer's training uses about one million one-second action chunks across embodiments <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />, or "1M real robot action sequences" in PI's post <Cite id="pi-fast-blog-2025" />.

<Image id="pi0-fast-laundry-folding-2025" />

Trained on a 10k-hour cross-embodied mixture, π0-FAST matches π0's generalist performance with up to five times fewer GPU hours. Inference costs more: about 750 ms per one-second chunk on an NVIDIA 4090, against within 100 ms for diffusion π0, since decoding needs 30 to 60 tokens versus ten steps <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. The Knowledge Insulation note reports π0-FAST took twice as long to finish single-embodiment table bussing <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

## π0.5 and Knowledge Insulation

π0.5 targets open-world generalization <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. It retains π0's skeleton, adds training examples interleaving images, language commands, object detections, subtask predictions and low-level actions, and at inference predicts a language subtask ("pick up the plate"), then conditions the action expert on it. In long-horizon mobile manipulation in new homes, removing either cross-embodiment data source significantly hurts home-task scores; removing web data leaves those scores nearly unchanged but strongly hurts language following and subtask inference <Cite id="pi05-2025" />.

The Knowledge Insulation paper reduces π0.5's two stages to one: the expert trains with flow matching for continuous inference; the backbone trains on discrete FAST tokens, vision-language data and planning data, stopping expert gradients at backbone keys and values <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. With a frozen backbone, shirt folding drops to 0%; on generalist table bussing, π0 needs 7.5 times the training steps to match; and vision-language co-training gives the joint-training baseline good language following without stop-gradient <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. openpi's π0.5 checkpoints were trained with KI, but the repository supports only flow-matching heads <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />, so outside groups reimplement FAST loss and KI <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" />.

## π0.6 and π*0.6: scaling and experience

π0.6's model card, dated November 17, 2025, initializes the backbone from Gemma3 4B, gives the action expert about 860M parameters and excludes expert gradients from the backbone. Without task-specific fine-tuning, π0.6 folds laundry reliably and completes box assembly 20% of the time, tasks previously requiring fine-tuning for any success <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. In PI's Robot Olympics, fine-tuned π0.6 averaged 52% success and 72% task progress; a fine-tuned standard VLM without π0.6's robot pre-training had no successes and 9% progress <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />.

π*0.6 uses Recap to learn from demonstrations, expert teleoperation corrections and autonomous experience, with a language-conditioned value function whose advantage, against a task-dependent threshold, gives each action a binary improvement indicator; human corrections are forced positive; evaluation samples with the positive indicator <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The report places this closest to CFGRL <Cite id="cfgrl-2025" />; outside groups find RL on flow-based π0 and π0.5 hard because flow matching makes action log-likelihoods intractable <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. The companion blog describes a bad portafilter grasp causing later insertion failure <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

With two 6-DoF arms and 50 Hz joint-position control, on-robot experience more than doubles successful completions per hour for double-shot espresso and diverse laundry, tested on a button-up shirt, against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning. Failures fall by "about a factor of two" ("more than 2×" in the figure), the "90%+" summary excludes diverse laundry, and the box task reports stages separately, with remaining failures mostly in final placement <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

PI's February 2026 partner report: Weave describes π0.6 folding laundry at a customer laundromat; Ultra describes live order packing with a human intervention system collecting training data <Cite id="pi-partner-2026" />.

PI published MEM March 3, 2026 <Cite id="mem-blog-2026" />. π0.6-MEM pairs a video encoder with a high-level policy that predicts updated natural-language summaries of earlier events, and handles tasks needing up to fifteen minutes of memory, including recipe setup and kitchen cleanup. Adaptation experiments train memory and memoryless policies on the same corrections, including which way to open a refrigerator <Cite id="mem-2026" />.

## RL tokens

On March 19, 2026, PI described RL tokens: small actor and critic networks train online on π0.6's compressed, frozen token, speeding four tasks' most precise stages by up to 3×; Ethernet insertion took two training hours with 15 minutes of robot data <Cite id="rlt-2026" />. [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) covers the wider method family.

## π0.7: generalization as a prompting problem

π0.7 treats underspecified prompts as the [generalization](/frontier/generalization/) bottleneck. It is π0.6 plus MEM at about 5B parameters; training simulates <Term id="real-time-chunking">real-time chunking</Term> delays of 0 to 12 timesteps, up to 240 ms on a 50 Hz robot <Cite id="pi07-2026" />, the training-time RTC method matching inference-time RTC on π0.6 at lower compute <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

Context holds task and subtask language, the latter from a learned high-level policy or human, and episode metadata on quality, speed and mistakes; training uses different context subsets but always keeps the joint or end-effector control-mode label. Multi-view subgoal images come from a BAGEL-initialized world model; subgoal and subtask generation run in separate threads <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. BAGEL is a unified decoder-only model pretrained on trillions of interleaved tokens <Cite id="bagel-2025" />.

π0.7 folds laundry on a bimanual UR5e without laundry data for that robot, matching experienced teleoperators' task progress on their first try there <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. In the air-fryer example, language coaching fine-tunes a high-level policy generating subtasks without new teleoperation, which the authors call "early signs of compositional task generalization" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. [Human video](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) joins the line through π0.5 co-fine-tuning, roughly doubling performance on scenarios seen only in human data <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

## Which checkpoints are listed

PI released π0's code and weights in openpi on February 4, 2025, saying 1 to 20 hours' data sufficed to fine-tune varied tasks <Cite id="openpi-blog-2025" />. The openpi README's pinned August 24, 2026 commit lists base checkpoints for π0, π0-FAST and π0.5, plus π0-FAST, π0 and π0.5 models fine-tuned on DROID and a π0.5 LIBERO model <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. DROID holds 76k trajectories from 564 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />; LIBERO has 130 tasks in four suites <Cite id="libero-2023" />.
