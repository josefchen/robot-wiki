The pi line is Physical Intelligence's series of [vision-language-action models](/manipulation/vla-models/), from π0 (October 2024) to π0.7 (April 2026) <Cite id="pi0-blog-2024" /> <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.

π0 kept a pretrained vision-language backbone and replaced <Term id="action-tokenization">discrete action tokens</Term> with a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> action expert <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Later models changed tokenization, training data, memory and prompting. Drag the integration steps slider from 1 upward to watch the cloud reach the action modes.

## π0: the action expert

π0 grafts a second transformer onto a pretrained <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language model</Term> initialized from PaliGemma. The paper calls the result "a single transformer with two sets of weights (also known as experts)." The backbone handles image and text tokens, and the action expert, about 300M parameters, handles robotics tokens. Its 50 action tokens attend into the backbone at every layer through shared self-attention, with a bidirectional mask among themselves. Inputs are 2 or 3 RGB images, language and proprioceptive state, and the model predicts H = 50 actions. UR5e and Franka setups run at 20 Hz and execute 16 actions per inference; the other robots run at 50 Hz and execute 25 <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. PaliGemma combines the SigLIP-So400m encoder with Gemma-2B <Cite id="paligemma-2024" />, and PI's launch post calls it a 3 billion parameter starting point <Cite id="pi0-blog-2024" />.

Where [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) denoises with a DDPM, π0 trains the expert with conditional flow matching on a straight path. Sample noise $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, form $A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\varepsilon$, and regress $v_\theta$ onto $u = A_t - \varepsilon$, drawing $\tau$ from a beta distribution weighted toward the noisy end. Inference integrates from $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 1$ in 10 forward-Euler steps; the image-text-state prefix is KV-cached once, so each step recomputes only the action suffix <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. Lipman et al. found optimal-transport paths more efficient than diffusion paths for training and sampling <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />.

<Term id="cross-embodiment">Cross-embodiment</Term> pre-training combines single-arm, dual-arm and mobile manipulators with different configuration and action spaces. The base model reaches what the authors call "rudimentary proficiency", and complex tasks such as laundry folding use curated post-training data <Cite id="pi0-2024" />. The paper counts 7 robot configurations, the launch post "8 distinct robots" <Cite id="pi0-blog-2024" />, and the openpi post "OXE and 7 of our robot platforms" <Cite id="openpi-blog-2025" />. PI says π0, π0-FAST and π0.5 executed chunks synchronously, finishing one chunk and waiting for inference before the next <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />; the RTC paper later compares asynchronous execution with stopping between chunks on π0.5 <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />.

## Why ten steps is enough

A DDPM path curves through noise space, while rectified flow straightens paths so that coarse time steps simulate them accurately <Cite id="rectified-flow-2022" />. Each Euler step costs a forward pass through the expert: π0 uses 10 steps <Cite id="pi0-2024" />, π0.6 uses five and reports 63 ms per chunk on one H100 with three cameras <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />, and π0.7 also uses five <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. NVIDIA's Jetson AI Lab measured π0.5 (LIBERO checkpoint, action horizon 10) on Jetson AGX Thor at about 132 ms in PyTorch BF16 and about 49 ms with TensorRT FP8 and NVFP4 <Cite id="jetson-ai-lab-openpi-thor" />. Consistency and one-step distillation, covered in [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/), pursue the same saving.

## π0-FAST: tokens done right

FAST traces the failure of per-dimension, per-timestep binning to highly correlated tokens, which make next-token prediction trivial, for example by copying the previous token. Policies with this naive tokenization make no progress on table bussing at 20 Hz or T-shirt folding at 50 Hz <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />.

FAST normalizes each action dimension, applies a discrete cosine transform along time, scales and rounds the coefficients, and byte-pair-encodes the flattened sequence. Rounding trades fidelity for compression, and BPE compresses the integers losslessly. The universal FAST+ tokenizer is trained on about one million one-second action chunks across embodiments, action spaces and control frequencies; the abstract calls them trajectories <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. PI's post calls them "1M real robot action sequences" <Cite id="pi-fast-blog-2025" />.

Trained on a 10k-hour cross-embodied mixture, π0-FAST matches π0's generalist performance with five times fewer GPU hours, which the abstract calls "up to 5x" <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. Inference costs more: about 750 ms per one-second chunk on an NVIDIA 4090, against within 100 ms for diffusion π0, since decoding needs 30 to 60 tokens instead of ten steps <Cite id="pi0-fast-2025" />. The Knowledge Insulation note reports that π0-FAST took twice as long to finish single-embodiment table bussing <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-2025" />.

## π0.5 and Knowledge Insulation

π0.5 targets open-world generalization <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. It keeps the π0 skeleton and adds hybrid examples that interleave images, language commands, object detections, semantic subtask predictions and low-level actions. At inference it first predicts a language subtask ("pick up the plate") at low frequency, then conditions the action expert on it at high frequency, with no separate planner. In long-horizon mobile manipulation in new homes, removing either cross-embodiment data source significantly hurts home-task scores; removing web data changes them insignificantly and strongly hurts language following and subtask inference <Cite id="pi05-2025" />. Hi Robot had already placed π0 as "System 1" under a high-level VLM with the same backbone <Cite id="hi-robot-blog-2025" />.

The Knowledge Insulation paper studies how a newly initialized continuous expert interferes with pretrained VLM representations; in one example π0, told to bus a spoon, grasps trash instead. It turns the two-stage π0.5 approach into one stage. The expert trains with flow matching for continuous inference, and the backbone trains on discrete FAST tokens, vision-language data and planning data. Expert gradients stop at the backbone keys and values, the expert attends only to the image, language and state prefix, and the backbone does not attend to the expert <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />.

With a frozen backbone, shirt folding drops to 0%, and FAST tokens adapt the backbone without action-expert gradients. On generalist table bussing, π0 needs 7.5 times as many training steps to match. Vision-language co-training also gives the joint-training baseline good language following without stop-gradient, and KI's language following improves but stays imperfect <Cite id="knowledge-insulation-paper-2025" />. The openpi π0.5 checkpoints were trained with KI, but the repository supports only the flow-matching head <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />, so outside groups reimplement the FAST loss and KI <Cite id="garg-self-demonstrated-control-2026" />.

## π0.6 and π*0.6: scaling and experience

The π0.6 model card, dated November 17, 2025, initializes the backbone from Gemma3 4B and labels a SigLIP 400M encoder; the action expert has about 860M parameters and the backbone's layer count. Pre-training accepts up to four 448×448 images, and Knowledge Insulation supervises the backbone with FAST tokens while keeping expert gradients out of it. Without task-specific fine-tuning, π0.6 folds laundry reliably and fully assembles the box 20% of the time, tasks that previously needed fine-tuning to succeed at all <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. Gemma 3 spans 1 to 27 billion parameters <Cite id="gemma3-2025" />. In PI's Robot Olympics, fine-tuned π0.6 averaged 52% success and 72% task progress. A fine-tuned standard VLM without π0.6's robot pre-training had no successes and 9% progress <Cite id="pi-olympics-2025" />. PI's partner Ultra reports π0.6 packing orders for a full shift at 96.4% autonomy <Cite id="pi-partner-2026" />.

π*0.6 uses Recap to learn from demonstrations, expert teleoperation corrections and autonomous experience: offline-RL pre-training, task-specific demonstration fine-tuning, then rounds of collecting experience and retraining. A language-conditioned distributional value function predicts negative remaining steps for successes and a large negative value for failures, normalized per task. Each action gets a binary improvement indicator from its advantage and a task-dependent threshold, the policy learns from good and bad data, human corrections are forced positive, and evaluation samples with the positive indicator <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The report places this formulation closest to CFGRL, which links policy improvement to diffusion guidance <Cite id="cfgrl-2025" />, while outside groups find RL on flow-based π0 and π0.5 hard because flow matching makes action log-likelihoods intractable <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. The companion blog's example is a bad portafilter grasp that causes a later insertion failure <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

The test rig has two 6-DoF arms, parallel-jaw grippers, three cameras and 50 Hz joint-position control. Against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning, on-robot experience more than doubles successful completions per hour for double-shot espresso (all steps within 200 seconds) and diverse laundry (11 item types, tested on a button-up shirt within 500 seconds). The results text calls the failure reduction "about a factor of two" and the discussion "roughly 2×", where the figure says "more than 2×"; the "90%+" summary excludes diverse laundry. The easier T-shirt-or-shorts laundry task has a 200-second limit and the box task 600 seconds. The box task reports stages separately, with about 90% for folding and labeling and remaining failures mostly in final placement <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

PI published MEM on March 3, 2026 <Cite id="mem-blog-2026" />. π0.6-MEM pairs a video encoder that interleaves bidirectional spatial attention with causal-temporal attention and a high-level policy that predicts updated natural-language summaries of earlier events, trained on labels from an off-the-shelf language model. It handles tasks needing up to fifteen minutes of memory, such as recipe setup and kitchen cleanup. Its adaptation experiments train memory and memoryless policies on the same corrections: chopstick grasp height after misses, and which way to open a refrigerator. Chopsticks are tested at the lowest table height after data from the upper half, and fridge success requires opening within four grasps <Cite id="mem-2026" />.

## RL tokens

On March 19, 2026, PI described RL tokens: the π0.6 VLA learns a compressed, frozen RL token that feeds small actor and critic networks trained online. Across four tasks it speeds up the most precise stages by up to 3×; Ethernet insertion took two hours of training with 15 minutes of robot data <Cite id="rlt-2026" />. [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/) covers the wider method family.

## π0.7: generalization as a prompting problem

π0.7 treats underspecified prompts as the [generalization](/frontier/generalization/) bottleneck <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. It is π0.6 plus MEM at about 5B parameters, with the flow timestep injected through adaptive RMSNorm and proprioceptive state entering through a linear projection instead of text tokens. Training simulates <Term id="real-time-chunking">real-time chunking</Term> delays of 0 to 12 timesteps, up to 240 ms on a 50 Hz robot; UR5e experiments run at 20 Hz and the others at 50 Hz <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. Simulating delay in training is the training-time RTC method, which on π0.6 matched inference-time RTC's task performance and speed at lower compute <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />.

The context holds task and subtask language, with the subtask from a learned high-level policy or a human. Episode metadata gives quality, speed and mistakes, set at runtime to quality 5, no mistakes and a speed from a task-specific episode-length percentile. Training uses different subsets of this context but always keeps the joint or end-effector control-mode label. Multi-view subgoal images come from a BAGEL-initialized world model. Subgoal and subtask generation run in separate threads, and the VLA uses the latest context <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. BAGEL is a unified decoder-only model pretrained on trillions of interleaved tokens, with future-frame prediction among its abilities <Cite id="bagel-2025" />.

π0.7 folds laundry on a bimanual UR5e with no laundry data for that robot, matching the task progress of experienced teleoperators trying it there for the first time, and does better with visual subgoals <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. In the air-fryer example a zero-shot prompt gives a partial attempt; language coaching then fine-tunes a high-level policy that generates the subtasks without new teleoperation, which the authors call "early signs of compositional task generalization" <Cite id="pi07-blog-2026" />. [Human video](/manipulation/cross-embodiment/) joins the line through π0.5 co-fine-tuning, which roughly doubles performance on scenarios seen only in human data once robot pre-training is diverse enough <Cite id="pi-human-to-robot-2025" />.

## Which checkpoints are listed

PI released π0's code and weights in openpi on February 4, 2025, saying 1 to 20 hours of data sufficed to fine-tune a variety of tasks <Cite id="openpi-blog-2025" />. The openpi README at the pinned commit of August 24, 2026 lists base checkpoints for π0, π0-FAST and π0.5. It adds π0-FAST, π0 and π0.5 models fine-tuned on DROID, the π0.5 one with knowledge insulation, and a π0.5 LIBERO model <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />. DROID holds 76k trajectories, or 350 hours, from 564 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and LIBERO has 130 tasks in four suites <Cite id="libero-2023" />.
