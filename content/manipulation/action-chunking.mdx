With <Term id="action-chunking">action chunking</Term>, a robot policy predicts a short burst of future actions, a chunk, instead of one move at a time <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. Fewer decisions mean fewer slips.

<Image id="aloha-unleashed-tasks-2024" />

## Fewer decisions, fewer slips

A policy that picks one move at a time makes thousands of decisions per task, and each slip can carry it where no demonstration went. In [behavior cloning](/manipulation/bc-foundations/) that cost can grow with the square of task length; <Term id="dagger">DAgger</Term> makes it linear <Cite id="dagger-2011" />. Chunks of k actions cut the decisions k-fold <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

With continuous actions, smooth deterministic imitators can let errors grow exponentially <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" />, and chunking prevents that in stable systems <Cite id="zhang-chunking-exploration-2025" />. Three 2026 studies found this account incomplete.

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Lazzati, Levine et al., Aug 2026 <Cite id="lazzati-why-chunking-2026" /> | Chunks gain by drawing on earlier observations and acting like an ensemble, not from shorter horizons or smoother motion |
| Zeng, Tedrake et al., Aug 2026 <Cite id="open-loop-revisited-2026" /> | Open-loop stretches mainly help short-memory policies; with enough memory, replanning every step did best |
| Goyal, Oct 2026 <Cite id="chunking-stability-measure-2026" /> | Across twelve tasks, few states reliably shrink injected errors |

Also: the term's origin in psychology <Cite id="lai-action-chunking-2022" />.

## ACT made chunking standard

Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT, 2023) came with ALOHA, a low-cost two-arm [teleoperation rig](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/), and learned to open a translucent condiment cup from 10 minutes of demonstrations <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

People repeat a motion slightly differently, so ACT is a <Term id="cvae">conditional variational autoencoder</Term>: it compresses each demonstrated chunk into a style variable, set to zero at test time. Without it, success on human demonstrations fell from 35.3% to 2% <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## Chunk size trades error against reaction time

<ChunkSizeCurve className="my-6" />

Small chunks bring back <Term id="compounding-error">compounding error</Term>; large ones react slowly to surprises <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. ACT's success peaked at chunks of 100 and dipped at 200 and 400 <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />, and flow-based VLAs rise and fall the same way <Cite id="autohorizon-2026" />.

Choosing the length on the fly lifted real ALOHA and Franka success from 50.7% to 70.4% with PACE <Cite id="pace-2026" />. Mixture of Horizons blends lengths through a learned gate <Cite id="moh-2025" />.

## Overlapping chunks are averaged into one action

Switching chunks abruptly jerks the arm. ACT's code uses <Term id="temporal-ensembling">temporal ensembling</Term>: it queries the policy every step and averages the overlapping predictions <Cite id="act-reference-2023" />. The weights $w_i = \exp(-m \cdot i)$, $m = 0.01$, favor the oldest ($i = 0$); the best decay varies by task <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />.

<Image id="temporal-ensembling" />

## Real-Time Chunking keeps the committed moves

Averaging fails when consecutive chunks go opposite ways around an obstacle <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />, or when the model is slow. <Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> (RTC) treats [the hand-off](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) as inpainting: it freezes the actions that run during computation and fills in the rest. With π0.5 under 100 or 200 ms of added delay, ensembling triggered protective stops while RTC held throughput <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" /> <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />.

<LatencyComparison className="my-6" />

With moving objects, background replanning helped only while planned actions never ran out <Cite id="libero-dynamic-2026" />. LeRobot ships RTC <Cite id="lerobot-rtc-docs" />.

| Method | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Training-time RTC <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" /> | Matches RTC with less computation |
| REMAC <Cite id="remac-2026" /> | Corrects chunks to match what the cameras now see |
| A2C2 <Cite id="a2c2-2025" /> | Per-step corrections; beats RTC by 23 points in the Kinetix simulator |
| PTS <Cite id="path-preserving-time-scaling-2026" /> | Replays each planned path faster: π0.5 throughput up 33% on LIBERO under added latency, no retraining |
| ODEAction, Nov 2026 <Cite id="odeaction-2026" /> | Drops chunk boundaries; a learned differential equation generates each action from the last |

## Every major policy now chunks

RT-1 predicted one action per step <Cite id="rt1-2022" />; [diffusion](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/), flow and vision-language-action policies now all chunk <Cite id="dehp-2026" />. [π0](/manipulation/pi-line/) generates chunks by <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term> <Cite id="pi0-2024" />; π0.5 adds FAST tokens <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, but openpi ships only the flow head <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />.

## Chunking moved to mobile robots and RL

Mobile ALOHA put the recipe on a wheeled two-arm robot, and co-training with static ALOHA data raised its success <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />. ALOHA Unleashed scaled it to over 26,000 real demonstrations for 5 tasks <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />. In [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/), Q-chunking learns values over whole chunks for better exploration <Cite id="q-chunking-2025" />.
