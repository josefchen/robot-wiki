Action chunking is a policy design in which each inference predicts a sequence of future actions instead of one <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. The term comes from psychology, where actions grouped into one unit are cheaper to store and execute <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> <Cite id="lai-action-chunking-2022" />.

## Why chunking works

<Term id="behavior-cloning">Behavior cloning</Term> with per-step error ε on the expert's states can accumulate cost quadratic in episode length T, because each mistake carries the robot into states the expert never visited <Cite id="dagger-2011" />. <Term id="dagger">DAgger</Term> relabels the states the policy visits; its excess cost is linear in T only if u, the rise in expert cost-to-go after one action, does not grow with T <Cite id="dagger-2011" />:

$$
\underset{\text{behavior cloning}}{O(\varepsilon T^2)} \qquad \underset{\text{DAgger}}{O(u\,\varepsilon T)}
$$

<Term id="action-chunking">Action chunking</Term> shortens the horizon instead: k actions per inference cut it k-fold <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. With continuous actions, smooth deterministic imitators can suffer execution error exponentially larger in the horizon than their training error, part of why chunking and diffusion policies work <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" />, and chunking avoids that compounding through control-theoretic stability <Cite id="zhang-chunking-exploration-2025" />.

## ACT: chunking with a CVAE

Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), introduced with the ALOHA bimanual [teleoperation hardware](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/), wraps chunking in a <Term id="cvae">conditional variational autoencoder</Term>: training encodes each demonstrated action sequence as a latent style variable, and at test time the policy decodes a chunk from camera images and joint positions with that latent at zero. Dropping the CVAE objective barely changes scripted-data results and cuts human-data success from 35.3% to 2% <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

The 14 action coordinates are six joints and one gripper coordinate for each of two six-DoF ViperX arms; the paper's "7+7=14 DoF" counts the grippers. ACT's implementation description specifies L1 reconstruction, while the printed algorithm uses MSE; both add a weighted KL term <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

### The chunk-size dial

In ACT's ablation, averaged over two simulated tasks with temporal ensembling off, success rises from 1% at k=1 to 44% at k=100 and dips at k=200 and k=400, near open-loop control; chunked BC-ConvMLP and VINN follow the same trend <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. A small k brings back <Term id="compounding-error">compounding error</Term>, and a large one reacts slowly to unexpected states <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. The best execution horizon is task-dependent: in flow-based VLAs success rises and then falls as it lengthens <Cite id="autohorizon-2026" />, and PACE, which picks it online, lifted real ALOHA and Franka success from 50.7% to 70.4% <Cite id="pace-2026" />.

## Temporal ensembling and its limits

The pinned ACT reference implementation uses <Term id="temporal-ensembling">temporal ensembling</Term>: it queries the policy every timestep and averages, oldest to newest, the retained predictions for the current action. Weights are $w_i = \exp(-m \cdot i)$, where $i = 0$ is the earliest prediction and $m = 0.01$, so the oldest prediction weighs most <Cite id="act-reference-2023" />. The best decay rate varies by task <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />.

<Image id="temporal-ensembling" />

```python title="temporal_ensembling.py"
# ACT reference convention: preds contains current-step predictions already
# selected and ordered oldest-to-newest. Index 0 is the oldest retained
# prediction, not "zero chunks ago". Reference m=0.01. This helper does not
# reproduce ACT's raw-buffer nonzero occupancy filter.
def temporal_ensemble(preds: list[np.ndarray], m: float = 0.01) -> np.ndarray:
    weights = np.exp(-m * np.arange(len(preds)))   # w_i = exp(-m * i)
    weights /= weights.sum()
    return np.tensordot(weights, np.stack(preds), axes=1)
```

For oldest-to-newest predictions `[0, 10, 20]` after selection, $m = 0.01$ gives normalized weights of about `[0.33667217, 0.33332222, 0.33000561]` and an average of `9.93333444`; the reference occupancy filter would discard a literal zero <Cite id="act-reference-2023" />.

When consecutive chunks diverge in strategy, say left versus right around an obstacle, averaging them can backfire <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. Demonstration pauses, unpredictable from a single Markovian state, freeze single-step policies, as both ACT and [Diffusion Policy](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/) document <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" /> <Cite id="diffusion-policy-2023" />.

<Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> (RTC) treats [the hand-off between chunks](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) as inpainting: it freezes actions that will already have run and generates the rest. With π0.5 at five denoising steps, model latency was 76 ms for the baselines and 97 ms for RTC, plus 10 to 20 ms over LAN. At 100 or 200 ms of injected delay, temporal-ensembling variants triggered protective stops, while RTC held average task throughput through +200 ms without retraining <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" />, over six tasks, ten episodes each <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />.

A training-time variant matched inference-time RTC at lower compute <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" />, LeRobot ships RTC for Pi0, Pi0.5 and SmolVLA <Cite id="lerobot-rtc-docs" />, and A2C2's per-step correction head beats RTC by 23 points on Kinetix <Cite id="a2c2-2025" />. Octo's ALOHA fine-tuning example predicts 64 actions and executes 12 before replanning <Cite id="octo-2024" />, and RT-1 predicts one action per timestep; its paper announces open code <Cite id="rt1-2022" />. The π0.6 model card, dated November 17, 2025, reports 63 ms per action chunk with five denoising steps and three cameras on one H100 <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />. π0.7 predicts 50-step chunks and executes 15 or 25, at 20 Hz on UR5e and 50 Hz elsewhere; it distills experience from RL-trained π*0.6 <Cite id="pi07-2026" />. NVIDIA's GR00T N1.7 README lists a predicted horizon of 40 and downloadable weights, under Apache 2.0 in its overview and the NVIDIA Open Model License in its License section <Cite id="isaac-gr00t-repo-2026" />; GR00T N1 processed actions "in chunks as in Zhao et al. (2023)" with H=16 <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" />.

Figure's January 27, 2026 announcement has Helix 02's S1 sending full-body joint targets at 200 Hz, tracked by S0 actuator commands at 1 kHz <Cite id="helix-02-2026" />. Gemini Robotics turns about 250 ms of end-to-end latency into an effective 50 Hz control frequency by packing several actions into each chunk <Cite id="gemini-robotics-2025" />.

## Chunking on mobile robots and in RL

Mobile ALOHA moves the recipe to a wheeled whole-body platform, where co-training on static ALOHA data raises success substantially <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />, and ALOHA Unleashed scaled it to over 26,000 real demonstrations for 5 tasks with a diffusion policy <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />. In [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/), Q-chunking runs RL in a chunked action space for better exploration and unbiased n-step backups <Cite id="q-chunking-2025" />.
