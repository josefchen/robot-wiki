With <Term id="action-chunking">action chunking</Term>, a robot policy predicts a short burst of future actions, a chunk, instead of one move at a time <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

<Image id="aloha-unleashed-tasks-2024" />

## Fewer decisions, fewer slips

Policies that output one action at a time must make thousands of decisions per task (with a cost that can scale quadratically with task length in [behavior cloning](/manipulation/bc-foundations/), and linearly in <Term id="dagger">DAgger</Term> <Cite id="dagger-2011" />), and can wander into behavior not represented in demonstrations. Chunks of k actions cut the decisions k-fold <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

For imitation with smooth, deterministic policies and continuous actions, compounding error can grow exponentially <Cite id="simchowitz-continuous-il-2025" />, and action chunking prevents this in stable systems <Cite id="zhang-chunking-exploration-2025" />. Three studies from 2026 found that this picture is incomplete.

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Lazzati, Levine et al., Aug 2026 <Cite id="lazzati-why-chunking-2026" /> | Chunks gain by drawing on earlier observations and acting like an ensemble, not from shorter horizons or smoother motion |
| Zeng, Tedrake et al., Aug 2026 <Cite id="open-loop-revisited-2026" /> | Open-loop stretches mainly help short-memory policies; with enough memory, replanning every step did best |
| Goyal, Oct 2026 <Cite id="chunking-stability-measure-2026" /> | Across twelve tasks, few states reliably shrink injected errors |

Also: the term's origin in psychology <Cite id="lai-action-chunking-2022" />.

## ACT made chunking standard

Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT, 2023) came with ALOHA, a low-cost two-arm [teleoperation rig](/data-hardware/teleop-rigs/), and learned to open a translucent condiment cup from 10 minutes of demonstrations <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

Action chunks are compressed into a style variable in order to deal with variations between demonstrations, as humans are not able to repeat actions exactly the same way. Thus, ACT is a <Term id="cvae">conditional variational autoencoder</Term>. At test time this variable is set to zero. Without this component, ACT performs worse (2% instead of 35.3% success on human demonstrations) <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />.

## Chunk size trades error against reaction time

<ChunkSizeCurve className="my-6" />

Smaller chunks lead to <Term id="compounding-error">compounding error</Term>, while larger chunks cannot respond quickly to unexpected situations <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />. For example, ACT achieved its best performance with action chunks of length 100, and performance dipped when the chunk length was increased to 200 or 400 <Cite id="act-aloha-2023" />. VLAs based on flow matching also exhibit similar performance trends <Cite id="autohorizon-2026" />.

Dynamically choosing the length increased the success rate on real ALOHA and Franka tasks from 50.7% to 70.4% when using PACE <Cite id="pace-2026" />. Mixture of Horizons instead blends lengths through a learned gate <Cite id="moh-2025" />.

A π0.5 study compared two simulation benchmarks and three tasks on real robots. Predicting about one second ahead worked best, but the optimal number of timesteps to play back depended on the checkpoint. Keeping successive chunks continuous helped more than choosing the lowest-latency method <Cite id="vla-reactive-execution-2026" />.

## Overlapping chunks are averaged into one action

Switching abruptly between chunks will cause jerky arm movements. In the original implementation, ACT uses <Term id="temporal-ensembling">temporal ensembling</Term>. That is, it makes a prediction every time step and then averages the predictions that overlap in time (with exponentially decaying weights $w_i = \exp(-m \cdot i)$, favoring older predictions, where $m = 0.01$ and $i = 0$ is the oldest prediction) <Cite id="act-reference-2023" />. The optimal decay hyperparameter varies by task <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />.

## Real-Time Chunking keeps the committed moves

Averaging fails when consecutive chunks go opposite ways around an obstacle <Cite id="bidirectional-decoding-2024" />, or when the model is slow. In <Term id="real-time-chunking">Real-Time Chunking</Term> (RTC), [the hand-off](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) is instead framed as an inpainting problem, where actions executed during inference are frozen and the model infers the remaining actions. Using π0.5 with either 100 or 200 ms of additional latency, ensembling caused protective stops while RTC maintained throughput <Cite id="real-time-chunking-2025" /> <Cite id="pi-real-time-chunking-blog-2025" />.

<LatencyComparison className="my-6" />

With moving objects, background replanning helped only while planned actions never ran out <Cite id="libero-dynamic-2026" />. RTC is supported in LeRobot <Cite id="lerobot-rtc-docs" />.

| Method | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Training-time RTC <Cite id="training-time-rtc-2025" /> | Matches RTC with less computation |
| REMAC <Cite id="remac-2026" /> | Corrects chunks to match what the cameras now see |
| A2C2 <Cite id="a2c2-2025" /> | Per-step corrections; beats RTC by 23 points in the Kinetix simulator |
| PTS <Cite id="path-preserving-time-scaling-2026" /> | Replays each planned path faster: π0.5 throughput up 33% on LIBERO under added latency, no retraining |
| ODEAction, Nov 2026 <Cite id="odeaction-2026" /> | Drops chunk boundaries; a learned differential equation generates each action from the last |

## Every major policy now chunks

Instead of predicting one action per step as RT-1 did <Cite id="rt1-2022" />, modern [diffusion](/manipulation/diffusion-policy/), flow and vision-language-action policies all predict action chunks <Cite id="dehp-2026" />. [π0](/manipulation/pi-line/) predicts action chunks using <Term id="flow-matching">flow matching</Term> <Cite id="pi0-2024" /> and π0.5 additionally uses FAST tokens <Cite id="pi05-2025" />, although openpi only implements the flow prediction head <Cite id="openpi-repo-2024" />.

## Chunking moved to mobile robots and RL

Mobile ALOHA put the recipe on a wheeled two-arm robot, and co-training with static ALOHA data raised its success <Cite id="mobile-aloha-2024" />. Scaling data further, ALOHA Unleashed collected more than 26,000 real robot demonstrations to train policies for 5 tasks <Cite id="aloha-unleashed-2024" />. In [RL fine-tuning](/manipulation/rl-finetuning/), Q-chunking learns values over whole chunks for better exploration <Cite id="q-chunking-2025" />.
