RL fine-tuning is the use of reinforcement learning to improve a pretrained robot policy from its own successes and failures. <Term id="imitation-learning">Imitation learning</Term> is brittle off the demonstration distribution because errors compound <Cite id="dagger-2011" />, and Physical Intelligence finds reliable success much harder than occasional success <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

## The flow-matching log-likelihood problem

<Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> runs several epochs of minibatch updates on a clipped surrogate objective <Cite id="ppo-2017" />, which needs the likelihood of each action taken. <Term id="flow-matching">Flow matching</Term> trains by regressing vector fields along fixed conditional probability paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, so the executed action's log-likelihood is intractable <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. Online RL applied directly to large VLAs is also unstable and needs compute beyond most local machines <Cite id="ire-vla-2025" />.

DPPO (Ren et al., 2024) follows DDPO, which posed diffusion denoising as a multi-step decision problem <Cite id="ddpo-2023" />. DPPO embeds denoising inside the environment MDP: each denoising transition has an analytic Gaussian likelihood, and the environment advances and pays reward only after the final step. Only the last selected denoising steps are fine-tuned, with a frozen copy of the network for earlier ones <Cite id="dppo-2024" />.

DPPO is the strongest of the compared diffusion-based RL methods on state-input Robomimic tasks, though off-policy methods are more sample-efficient on HalfCheetah and a Gaussian policy slightly beats it on the low-randomness Lamp task. A simulation-trained One-leg assembly policy transferred zero-shot to a Franka Emika Panda and succeeded in 16 of 20 trials at 10 Hz <Cite id="dppo-2024" />.

ReinFlow injects learnable noise into a flow policy's path for exact per-step likelihoods, saving 82.63% of wall time against DPPO on legged locomotion <Cite id="reinflow-2025" />, and FPO instead builds a PPO-clip ratio from the conditional flow-matching loss <Cite id="fpo-2025" />. QF3 (UC Berkeley and Amazon FAR, October 2026), an off-policy method that adds the critic's action gradient to the flow-matching loss, is reported as the first off-policy flow RL method to train humanoid locomotion from scratch and transfer it zero-shot to hardware, with a 10× wall-clock speedup over the on-policy FPO++ <Cite id="qf3-2026" />.

ConRFT (Chen et al., 2025) fine-tunes a consistency-policy action head, a sampler distilled from a diffusion policy <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" />, while freezing the visual encoders and transformer backbone of Octo-small on a 7-DoF Franka. Offline Cal-ConRFT pairs a consistency-based <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> loss with a Cal-QL critic, from 20 to 30 demonstrations per task, and online HIL-ConRFT adds human takeovers to the demonstration buffer <Cite id="conrft-2025" />.

ConRFT reports 96.3% mean <Term id="success-rate">success rate</Term> across eight tasks, 20 trials each, after 15 to 90 minutes of listed online training. ConRFT's headline 144% is a rounded relative gain over offline Cal-ConRFT's 39.4% mean, which the SFT baseline also scores. Online learning curves can include successes produced by human takeovers, and the paper warns of reward-classifier hacking and limited generalization <Cite id="conrft-2025" />. ConRFT says every compared method used interventions, but PA-RL reports fine-tuning OpenVLA autonomously with Cal-QL, from 40% to 70% in 40 real-world minutes <Cite id="pa-rl-2024" />.

pi_RL (Chen et al., 2026) fine-tunes flow-based policies with online PPO. Flow-Noise learns Gaussian noise for denoising transitions jointly with the velocity network and uses the joint likelihood of the denoising path in place of the executed action's marginal likelihood. Flow-SDE uses Gaussian transition likelihoods in a two-layer denoising and environment MDP, with one randomly chosen denoising step stochastic and the rest deterministic <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />.

Main experiments fine-tune π0 and π0.5 in simulation on LIBERO, ManiSkill, MetaWorld and CALVIN, and gains under distribution shift do not consistently reach the five unseen MetaWorld ML45 tasks. A Real2Sim2Real case transfers zero-shot to a Franka Panda, reporting 40% success without a trial count <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />.

## Recap: advantage-conditioned policy learning

Recap, the method behind π*0.6, combines demonstrations, expert teleoperation corrections during rollouts and autonomous experience, in rounds of collection and retraining. A language-conditioned distributional value function predicts negative remaining steps for successes and a large negative value for failures. In post-training the advantage uses rewards over the next N = 50 steps plus the later value minus the current value, and a task-dependent threshold turns it into a binary indicator, with human corrections assigned the positive one <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

The text "Advantage: positive" or "Advantage: negative" precedes the actions, so good and bad data both train the policy, and evaluation uses the positive-conditioned policy; indicator dropout permits classifier-free guidance <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />, which combines jointly trained conditional and unconditional estimates <Cite id="cfg-2022" />. The KL-regularized objective has the exponential solution:

$$
\hat{\pi}(a \mid o) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o)\,\exp\!\left(A^{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(o,a)/\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}\right).
$$

Advantage-weighted regression fits that target with two supervised steps <Cite id="awr-2019" />. Recap's binary conditioning instead writes the improved policy as

$$
\hat{\pi}(a \mid o,\ell) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o,\ell)
\left(\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid I,o,\ell)}
{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o,\ell)}\right)^{\beta_{\mathrm{CFG}}}.
$$

At $\beta_{\mathrm{CFG}}=1$ this reduces to the indicator-conditioned policy. Recap thereby avoids a PPO-style objective: training combines next-token prediction with the flow-matching loss, and Knowledge Insulation stops the action expert's gradient at the backbone interface <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />.

On-robot experience more than doubles successful completions per hour for double-shot espresso and diverse laundry, against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning, on a static bimanual setup with a button-up-shirt test in place of the 11-item laundry set. Failures fall by "about a factor of two" ("more than 2×" elsewhere in the report); the "90%+" summary excludes diverse laundry, and the box chart reports stages separately <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The [pi line](/manipulation/pi-line/) lists the hardware and time limits.

The companion blog's video caption gives 5:30am to 11:30pm of espresso making, while the report says "13 hours straight" <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" /> <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The results are Physical Intelligence's own, with human reward labeling, interventions, resets and batch offline updates <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. In March 2026 Physical Intelligence added RL tokens: small actor and critic networks train online on a compact token from π0.6, speeding the most precise stage of four tasks by up to 3× <Cite id="rlt-2026" />.

## HIL-SERL: a human in the loop, RL on the robot

HIL-SERL (Luo et al., Science Robotics 2025) follows SERL, which learned PCB assembly, cable routing and object relocation in 25 to 50 minutes per policy <Cite id="serl-2024" />, and uses RLPD, off-policy RL with offline data mixed in <Cite id="rlpd-2023" />. Intervention transitions enter both the demonstration and RL buffers, autonomous ones only the RL buffer, and training typically starts from 20 to 30 demonstrations; Jenga whipping starts from 30 with no real-time takeovers <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

HIL-SERL completes 100% of observed evaluation trials, 100 per task, except IKEA whole assembly with 10 trials and at most two attempts per sub-policy. Training takes 1 to 2.5 hours for nearly all tasks and 6 for timing-belt assembly, including scripted motion, rollouts, intended stops and computation. Against HG-DAgger with matched episodes and interventions, average success is 49.7% versus 100% and cycle time 9.6 versus 5.4 seconds <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

Online learning curves can include human-assisted successes, the paper reports DP's dashboard-assembly success as both 18% and 28%, and Luo and colleagues did not test unstructured environments or extensive randomization <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />. The [RL for Robotics module](/rl-sim2real/rl-for-robotics/) covers the off-policy context, and [parallel simulation RL](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl/) the simulation route.

Mulligan (Stanford, October 2026) starts each round of supervised collection at the initial states where the policy failed or has not been tried; with value-based action selection, it raised final-round success on three real tasks by 10 to 34 percentage points over HG-DAgger, scored on 2,550 held-out, blinded episodes <Cite id="mulligan-2026" />.

## Residual RL and distillation back into the generalist

Residual RL adds a learned correction to a fixed controller <Cite id="residual-rl-2018" />. PLD (Probe, Learn, Distill; Xiao et al., 2025) freezes a VLA, trains task-specific residual actors with off-policy RL, lets them recover from the states the base policy reaches, and distills the trajectories back into the generalist through SFT. Residual exploration carries no safety guarantee <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

On LIBERO, averages are 99.2% for the model labelled OpenVLA against 91.8% for its baseline, and 97.2% against 93.4% for π0. On a four-task SimplerEnv suite, Octo-SFT rises from 71.8% to 96.6% and the WidowX carrot task from 43.3% to 93.9%; the printed average delta is +24.9, though the means differ by 24.8 <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

On a Franka, PLD records 30/30 randomized trials for cube pick-up and peg insertion, but 28/30 for a blue cube and 20/30 for a red one under changed clutter. On YAM, four GPU insertion and removal stages distill into one BC policy that runs at least an hour continuously with recovery, though per-stage one-shot success stays below 100% <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

RLDG (Xu et al., 2024) uses HIL-SERL specialists to generate data for fine-tuning OpenVLA and Octo, so the generalist never receives RL updates. In FMB single-object assembly, RL supplies insertion data while humans supply grasping and transport, and OpenVLA succeeds in 20/20 trials against 12/20 with human demonstrations <Cite id="rldg-2024" />. Relabeling suggests action quality, more than state distribution, drives the benefit; the method needs task rewards, speed-optimized RL data can cause premature object drops, and OpenVLA commands actions at 4 Hz while RL runs at 10 Hz <Cite id="rldg-2024" />.

## EXPO-FT: value-guided edits on a frontier VLA

EXPO (Perry Dong, Qiyang Li, Dorsa Sadigh and Chelsea Finn; ICLR 2026) trains a large flow or diffusion base policy with a stable imitation objective plus a lightweight Gaussian edit policy that nudges sampled actions toward higher value, avoiding gradients through the denoising chain, and reports up to 2 to 3× better sample efficiency on average <Cite id="expo-2025" />.

EXPO-FT, from Dong, Kuo-Han Hung, Tian Gao, Sadigh and Finn, extends EXPO to VLA action chunks and human teleoperation corrections on π0.5. Tasks the base cannot do zero-shot get imitation fine-tuning to around 40% success or above before online RL, and sparse binary rewards come from rule-based detectors over 95% accurate. It reports 30/30 on every evaluated task within an average of 19.1 minutes of online robot data across eight task variants <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

<ExpoFtResults className="my-6" />

The paper calls HIL-SERL highly reliable in its original evaluations; this suite randomizes a much larger initial-state space, and the standard budget was too small for learning to start on Cube Pick and Pool Shot. With extra samples, HIL-SERL reaches 27/30 and 13/30 there <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" /> <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

DSRL keeps the base policy frozen and runs RL over its latent-noise space <Cite id="dsrl-2025" />. Dong and Finn argue it has a ceiling: "If the skill a robot needs isn't already somewhere in what the base model learned during pretraining, no amount of noise-searching will produce the action ...". DSRL averages 19/30 in their comparison <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" /> <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

Dong and Finn's September 2026 blog post says: "RL shows incredible promise in training frontier robotics models, but it is not yet a recipe. It's a craft." EXPO-FT still needs a human to define success, reset the scene and intervene, longer horizons strain the value function, and "Nineteen minutes of robot interaction is not nineteen minutes of post-training," because gradient updates dominate wall-clock time <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" />. Real-Time EXPO-FT lets a lightweight edit policy react to the latest observation while a slow VLA proposes chunks, and on four dynamic real tasks average performance rises from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />; [real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) covers the latency budgets.

## Real-world and fleet-scale results

RL-100, published in Science Robotics in July 2026, applies one clipped-PPO objective within the denoising process across offline and online stages and reports 1000 of 1000 successful episodes on eight real tasks <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />. LWD runs offline-to-online RL on a fleet of 16 dual-arm robots, where one generalist policy reaches 95% average success across eight real tasks <Cite id="lwd-2026" />.

## What RL buys, with caveats

Liu et al.'s NeurIPS 2025 study finds PPO beats the tested DPO/TPO and GRPO implementations for OpenVLA pick-and-place with sparse task rewards, with stronger semantic and execution generalization than supervised fine-tuning and comparable visual robustness. A September 2025 revision adds appendix tests, and SFT wins the articulated task's visual average and rephrased instructions, and the action-chunking multi-receptacle test; the main-text limitations still say all experiments are simulated pick-and-place <Cite id="rl-vla-generalization-2025" />.

PAIR-VLA adds paired-view invariance and sensitivity objectives to PPO in simulated ManiSkill3 pick-and-place and reports better OOD success than PPO under the tested shifts. The sensitivity-only ablation loses to PPO on target-pose success, the camera-view variant trails PPO slightly in-distribution, the task-preserving views rely on simulator object masks, and real-world transfer is untested <Cite id="pair-vla-2026" />.

SimpleVLA-RL lifts OpenVLA-OFT's LIBERO average from 91% to 99% <Cite id="simplevla-rl-2025" />. Reward specification remains open: a benchmark of open and proprietary VLMs as robot reward models finds that no model excels across tasks <Cite id="roboreward-2026" />. Physical Intelligence says π0.7 runs an espresso machine out of the box at a level matching much more specialized RL-finetuned models <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.
