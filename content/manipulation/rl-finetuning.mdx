RL fine-tuning is the use of reinforcement learning to improve a pretrained robot policy from its own successes and failures. <Term id="imitation-learning">Imitation learning</Term> is brittle off the demonstration distribution because errors compound <Cite id="dagger-2011" />, and Physical Intelligence finds reliable success much harder than occasional success <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

Dong and Finn call RL for frontier robot models "not yet a recipe" <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" />. Switch to “What it learns”.

## The flow-matching log-likelihood problem

<Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> runs several epochs of minibatch updates on a clipped surrogate objective <Cite id="ppo-2017" />, which needs the likelihood of each action taken. <Term id="flow-matching">Flow matching</Term> trains by regressing vector fields along fixed conditional probability paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, so the executed action's log-likelihood is intractable <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. Applying online RL directly to large VLAs also brings training instability and compute beyond most local machines <Cite id="ire-vla-2025" />.

DPPO (Ren et al., 2024) follows DDPO, which posed diffusion denoising as a multi-step decision problem <Cite id="ddpo-2023" />. DPPO embeds denoising inside the environment MDP: each denoising transition has an analytic Gaussian likelihood, and the environment advances and pays reward only after the final step. Environment-discounted advantages are multiplied by a denoising discount that downweights earlier, noisier steps, and the value function depends on the environment state. Only the last selected denoising steps are fine-tuned, with a frozen copy of the network for earlier ones <Cite id="dppo-2024" />.

DPPO is the strongest of the compared diffusion-based RL methods on state-input Robomimic tasks, and its PPO comparisons also cover Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture policies. Some Gym baselines are competitive, off-policy methods are more sample-efficient on HalfCheetah, its iterations run slower than several baselines, and a Gaussian policy slightly beats it on the low-randomness Lamp task. A simulation-trained One-leg assembly policy transferred zero-shot to a Franka Emika Panda, with no on-robot RL, and succeeded in 16 of 20 trials at 10 Hz <Cite id="dppo-2024" />.

ReinFlow injects learnable noise into a flow policy's path for exact per-step likelihoods, and on legged locomotion reports 135.36% average net growth while saving 82.63% of wall time against DPPO <Cite id="reinflow-2025" />. FPO instead builds a PPO-clip ratio from the conditional flow-matching loss and sidesteps exact likelihoods <Cite id="fpo-2025" />.

ConRFT (Chen et al., 2025) fine-tunes a consistency-policy action head, a sampler distilled from a diffusion policy <Cite id="consistency-policy-2024" />, while freezing the visual encoders and transformer backbone. Its eight-task study uses Octo-small on a 7-DoF Franka at 10 Hz. Offline Cal-ConRFT pairs a consistency-based <Term id="behavior-cloning">behavior cloning</Term> loss and a negative-Q actor loss with a Cal-QL critic, from 20 to 30 demonstrations per task. Online HIL-ConRFT reweights both actor losses and uses a standard temporal-difference critic. Batches sample equally from demonstration and online buffers, and human takeovers enter the demonstration buffer. The training-noise grid has 40 sub-intervals; the five-step setting belongs to the diffusion-policy baseline <Cite id="conrft-2025" />.

ConRFT reports 96.3% mean <Term id="success-rate">success rate</Term> across eight tasks, 20 trials each. Listed online training times run 15 to 90 minutes, mean 48.8, while the abstract and results prose say 45 to 90; they include scripted motions, rollouts and onboard computation on an RTX A6000. The 144% is a rounded relative gain over offline Cal-ConRFT's 39.4% mean, which the SFT baseline happens to match. Online learning curves can include successes produced by human takeovers, and the paper warns of reward-classifier hacking and limited generalization <Cite id="conrft-2025" />. ConRFT says every compared method used interventions, but PA-RL's own abstract reports fine-tuning OpenVLA autonomously with Cal-QL, from 40% to 70% in 40 real-world minutes <Cite id="pa-rl-2024" />.

pi_RL (Chen et al., 2026) fine-tunes flow-based policies with online PPO. Flow-Noise learns Gaussian noise for denoising transitions jointly with the velocity network and uses the joint likelihood of the denoising path in place of the executed action's marginal likelihood; the noise network is dropped for deterministic inference. Flow-SDE uses Gaussian transition likelihoods in a two-layer denoising and environment MDP, making one randomly chosen denoising step stochastic and the rest deterministic ODE updates <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />.

Main experiments use π0 and π0.5. One configuration fine-tunes the full model with supervision, then freezes the VLM and trains a 300M-parameter action expert on eight H100 80GB GPUs. Other studies compare PPO with GRPO, add VLM LoRA, and run PPO with Flow-SDE on GR00T N1.5 <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. GRPO is a memory-saving PPO variant from mathematical-reasoning research <Cite id="grpo-2024" />.

Evaluations cover LIBERO with 500 initial states per suite, ManiSkill under visual, semantic and execution shifts, MetaWorld, and CALVIN Scene D with 1,000 five-subtask episodes. Gains under distribution shift do not consistently reach the five unseen MetaWorld ML45 tasks. The paper conflicts on whether π0.5's proprioceptive state enters the VLM and reports D-to-D CALVIN results beside ABC-to-D training. RL training is simulated; a Real2Sim2Real case uses 20 motion-planner trajectories for SFT and 100 RL iterations, then transfers zero-shot to a Franka Panda with a RealSense D435, reporting 40% success without a trial count <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />.

## Recap: advantage-conditioned policy learning

Recap, the method behind π*0.6, combines demonstrations, expert teleoperation corrections during rollouts and autonomous experience. Training runs offline-RL pre-training, task demonstration fine-tuning, then batch rounds of collection and retraining <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

A language-conditioned distributional value function predicts negative remaining steps for successes and a large negative value for failures, normalized per task. In post-training the advantage uses rewards over the next N = 50 steps plus the later value minus the current value; pre-training uses an episode-return estimator. A task-dependent threshold turns the estimate into a binary indicator, and human corrections are assigned the positive one <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

The text "Advantage: positive" or "Advantage: negative" follows the predicted subtask and precedes the actions, so good and bad data both train the policy. Evaluation uses the positive-conditioned policy, and indicator dropout permits classifier-free guidance <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />, which combines jointly trained conditional and unconditional estimates <Cite id="cfg-2022" />. The KL-regularized objective has the exponential solution:

$$
\hat{\pi}(a \mid o) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o)\,\exp\!\left(A^{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(o,a)/\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}\right).
$$

Advantage-weighted regression fits that target with two supervised steps, one for the value function and one for weighted actions <Cite id="awr-2019" />. Recap's binary conditioning follows a related improvement-indicator construction and writes the improved policy as

$$
\hat{\pi}(a \mid o,\ell) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o,\ell)
\left(\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid I,o,\ell)}
{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o,\ell)}\right)^{\beta_{\mathrm{CFG}}}.
$$

At $\beta_{\mathrm{CFG}}=1$ this reduces to the indicator-conditioned policy. Recap thereby avoids a PPO-style objective: training combines next-token prediction with the flow-matching loss, and Knowledge Insulation stops the action expert's gradient at the backbone interface <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />.

On-robot experience more than doubles successful completions per hour for double-shot espresso and diverse laundry, against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning, on a static bimanual setup with a button-up-shirt test in place of the 11-item laundry set. One passage calls the failure reduction "about a factor of two" and another "more than 2×"; the "90%+" summary excludes diverse laundry, and the box chart reports stages separately <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The [pi line](/manipulation/pi-line/) gives the hardware and time limits.

The companion blog says espresso throughput and success rate more than doubled, and its video caption gives 5:30am to 11:30pm of espresso making, while the report says "13 hours straight" <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" /> <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. The results are Physical Intelligence's own, with human reward labeling, interventions, resets and batch offline updates; the T-shirt and shorts experiment uses autonomous data without corrections <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. SOP, from another group, instantiates its fleet post-training system with RECAP as well as HG-DAgger <Cite id="sop-2026" />.

In March 2026 Physical Intelligence added RL tokens: small actor and critic networks train online on a compact token from π0.6, speeding the most precise stage of four tasks by up to 3×. Ethernet insertion took two hours of training with 15 minutes of robot data <Cite id="rlt-2026" />.

## HIL-SERL: a human in the loop, RL on the robot

HIL-SERL (Luo et al., Science Robotics 2025) follows SERL, an earlier suite from Luo and colleagues that learned PCB assembly, cable routing and object relocation in 25 to 50 minutes per policy <Cite id="serl-2024" />. Its learner is RLPD, which applies off-policy RL with offline data mixed in <Cite id="rlpd-2023" />. Learning samples equally from demonstration and online buffers; intervention transitions enter both, autonomous ones only the RL buffer. Training typically starts from 20 to 30 demonstrations, and Jenga whipping starts from 30 with no real-time takeovers <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

HIL-SERL completes 100% of observed evaluation trials, 100 per task, except IKEA whole assembly with 10 trials and at most two attempts per sub-policy. Training takes 1 to 2.5 hours for nearly all tasks and 6 for timing-belt assembly, including scripted motion, rollouts, intended stops and computation on one RTX 4090. Against HG-DAgger with matched episodes and interventions, average success is 49.7% versus 100% and cycle time 9.6 versus 5.4 seconds, 1.8× faster. Jenga whipping and object flipping compare against flat BC on 50 and 200 demonstrations, and a separate 200-demonstration comparison covers DP, BC, IBRL, Residual RL and DAPG <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

Online learning curves can include human-assisted successes, and the paper gives DP 18% on dashboard assembly in one place and 28% in another. Luo and colleagues did not test unstructured environments or extensive randomization, leave much longer horizons uncertain and report code availability <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />. The [RL for Robotics module](/rl-sim2real/rl-for-robotics/) covers the off-policy context, and [parallel simulation RL](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl/) the simulation route.

## Residual RL and distillation back into the generalist

Residual RL adds a learned correction to a fixed controller, so the final policy superposes both signals <Cite id="residual-rl-2018" />. PLD (Probe, Learn, Distill; Xiao et al., 2025) freezes a VLA while training task-specific residual actors with off-policy RL. Hybrid collection rolls out the base policy, then lets a residual specialist recover from the states it reaches, and the trajectories fine-tune the generalist through SFT. Residual exploration carries no safety guarantee <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

Averages are 99.2% for the model labelled OpenVLA against 91.8% for its baseline, and 97.2% against 93.4% for π0, on LIBERO-Spatial, Object and Goal with ten tasks per suite and 50 episodes per task. On a four-task SimplerEnv suite, Octo-SFT rises from 71.8% to 96.6% and the WidowX carrot task from 43.3% to 93.9%; the printed average delta is +24.9, though the means differ by 24.8 <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

On a Franka, from 200 teleoperated trajectories, PLD records 30/30 randomized trials for cube pick-up and peg insertion, as do the comparison data sources on peg insertion. Under changed clutter it records 28/30 for a blue cube and 20/30 for a red one. Peg insertion uses human hole repositioning. On YAM, four GPU insertion and removal stages train for at most eight hours per subtask and distill into one BC policy that runs at least an hour continuously with recovery; per-stage one-shot success stays below 100% <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

RLDG (Xu et al., 2024) uses HIL-SERL specialists to generate data for fine-tuning OpenVLA and Octo, so the generalist never receives RL updates. Comparisons hold the task setup, training configuration, observation and action spaces and successful-episode count fixed and change only the data source. In FMB single-object assembly, RL supplies insertion data while humans supply grasping and transport, and OpenVLA succeeds in 20/20 trials against 12/20 with human demonstrations <Cite id="rldg-2024" />.

Relabeling human-data actions with an RL policy suggests action quality, more than state distribution, drives the benefit. The method needs task rewards, and speed-optimized RL data can cause premature object drops after distillation. OpenVLA commands actions at 4 Hz, while data collection, RL and Octo run at 10 Hz over a 1 kHz impedance controller <Cite id="rldg-2024" />.

## EXPO-FT: value-guided edits on a frontier VLA

EXPO (Perry Dong, Qiyang Li, Dorsa Sadigh and Chelsea Finn; ICLR 2026) trains a large flow or diffusion base policy with a stable imitation objective plus a lightweight Gaussian edit policy that nudges sampled actions toward higher value. An on-the-fly policy executes and backs up the best of the base and edited actions, avoiding gradients through the denoising chain. EXPO reports up to 2 to 3× better sample efficiency on average, both when fine-tuning a pretrained policy from offline data and when using offline data to train online <Cite id="expo-2025" />.

EXPO-FT, from Dong, Kuo-Han Hung, Tian Gao, Sadigh and Finn, extends EXPO to VLA action chunks, adds human teleoperation corrections and runs on π0.5 initialized from a task-specific SFT checkpoint. Tasks the base cannot do zero-shot get imitation fine-tuning to around 40% success or above before online RL. Rewards are sparse and binary, from rule-based detectors over 95% accurate, and the robot takes end-effector velocity commands at 10 Hz. It reports 30/30 on every evaluated task within an average of 19.1 minutes of online robot data across eight task variants, and compares four tasks against SFT, HG-DAgger, DSRL and HIL-SERL <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

<ExpoFtResults className="my-6" />

The paper calls HIL-SERL highly reliable in its original evaluations; this suite randomizes a much larger initial-state space, and the standard budget was too small for learning to start on Cube Pick and Pool Shot. With extra samples, HIL-SERL reaches 27/30 and 13/30 there, against a 5.5/30 average under the standard protocol <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" /> <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

DSRL keeps the base policy frozen and runs RL over its latent-noise space with black-box access, and its authors show real-world autonomous improvement of diffusion policies and pretrained generalists <Cite id="dsrl-2025" />. Dong and Finn argue it has a ceiling: "If the skill a robot needs isn't already somewhere in what the base model learned during pretraining, no amount of noise-searching will produce the action ...". DSRL averages 19/30 in their comparison <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" /> <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

A September 2026 paper from Seungeun Rho, Wontaek Kim, Danfei Xu and Sehoon Ha studies the same limit using preferences alone. Their PrefPI steers pretrained diffusion policies and π0.5 using relative preferences over the policy's own trajectories, aiming at behaviors the initial policy rarely or never produces; on real hardware, 150 preference-labeled trajectories raised object transport height from 10.7 cm to 19.8 cm <Cite id="prefpi-2026" />.

SeeQ, from Saksham Singh, Aviral Kumar and colleagues at Carnegie Mellon (September 2026), steers at test time with a value function. It learns Q-values for the currently active subtask, which shortens the horizon enough for temporal-difference learning on broad, suboptimal data. Picking the best of eight π0.5 action chunks raised shirt hanging from 10/24 to 22/24 and lid sealing from 10/24 to 15/24 <Cite id="seeq-2026" />.

Dong and Finn's September 2026 blog post says pretrained VLAs give capable demos with no shared post-training recipe behind them: "RL shows incredible promise in training frontier robotics models, but it is not yet a recipe. It's a craft." They call for a stable algorithm plus standard protocols for reward specification, resets, human input, hyperparameters and initialization. EXPO-FT still needs a human to define success, reset the scene and intervene, longer horizons strain the value function, and "Nineteen minutes of robot interaction is not nineteen minutes of post-training," because gradient updates dominate wall-clock time <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" />.

The September 2026 follow-up, Real-Time EXPO-FT, lets a lightweight edit policy react to the latest observation while a slow VLA proposes action chunks. On Kinetix the delayed policy beats every delayed and non-delayed competitor in 10 of 10 environments. On four dynamic real tasks with at most 10 minutes of online data, average performance rises from 42% to 97% with no human intervention <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />. [Real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/) covers the latency budgets.

## Real-world and fleet-scale results

RL-100, published in Science Robotics in July 2026, applies one clipped-PPO objective within the denoising process across offline and online stages. It reports 1000 of 1000 successful episodes on eight real tasks, including 250 consecutive successes on one <Cite id="rl-100-2025" />. LWD runs offline-to-online RL on a fleet of 16 dual-arm robots, where one generalist policy reaches 95% average success across eight real tasks <Cite id="lwd-2026" />. SOP's performance scales near-linearly with the number of robots in its fleet <Cite id="sop-2026" />. ENPIRE gives coding agents the whole real-robot improvement loop, from scene reset and verification to training-code changes. Its authors report 99% success on dexterous tasks such as fastening a zip tie, and eight agents cut the time to near-perfect pin insertion from over 1.5 hours to about 40 minutes <Cite id="enpire-2026" />.

## What RL actually buys, and the caveats

Liu et al.'s NeurIPS 2025 study finds PPO beats the tested DPO/TPO and GRPO implementations for OpenVLA pick-and-place with sparse task rewards. Against supervised fine-tuning it reports stronger semantic and execution generalization and comparable visual robustness. A September 2025 revision adds appendix tests: a preliminary sim-to-real trial, an action-chunking comparison and an articulated-object task. SFT wins the articulated task's visual average and rephrased instructions, and the action-chunking multi-receptacle test. The main-text limitations still say all experiments are simulated pick-and-place <Cite id="rl-vla-generalization-2025" />.

PAIR-VLA adds paired-view invariance and sensitivity objectives to PPO in simulated ManiSkill3 pick-and-place with OpenVLA and π0.5. It preserves action distributions under task-preserving changes and separates them under target-pose changes, arguing that task rewards alone give limited guidance here, and reports better OOD success than PPO under the tested shifts. The sensitivity-only ablation loses to PPO on target-pose success, the camera-view variant trails PPO slightly in-distribution, the task-preserving views rely on simulator object masks, and real-world transfer is untested <Cite id="pair-vla-2026" />.

SimpleVLA-RL lifts OpenVLA-OFT's LIBERO average from 91% to 99% and reports "pushcut", in which the policy discovers patterns unseen in earlier training <Cite id="simplevla-rl-2025" />. Reward specification remains open, and a benchmark of open and proprietary VLMs as robot reward models finds that no model excels across tasks <Cite id="roboreward-2026" />. Physical Intelligence says π0.7 runs an espresso machine out of the box at a level matching much more specialized RL-finetuned models <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.
