RL fine-tuning improves a pretrained robot policy with <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term>: the robot attempts a task, a reward scores each attempt, and the policy shifts toward what worked. Imitation alone is brittle once errors carry the robot off its demonstrations <Cite id="dagger-2011" />, and reliable success is much harder than occasional success <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

## Practice turns attempts into training data

RL scores each attempt with a reward, often a success detector or a person's verdict. A <Term id="value-function">value function</Term> estimates how well things are going; a step that leaves the robot better off than expected has positive advantage, and training pushes the policy toward it. On a real robot, a person also resets the scene and takes over before a mistake does damage <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

## Diffusion and flow policies hide their probabilities

Standard RL such as <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> raises the probability of actions that did well, so it needs each action's probability <Cite id="ppo-2017" />. <Term id="flow-matching">Flow-matching</Term> policies train by regressing vector fields along fixed probability paths <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, so the executed action's likelihood cannot be computed <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. Online RL on large VLAs is also unstable and needs more compute than most local machines have <Cite id="ire-vla-2025" />.

## Each denoising step can become a decision

One fix makes the denoising steps part of the task, so each step has a simple Gaussian probability and reward arrives after the last.

| Method | Trick | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DPPO <Cite id="dppo-2024" />, after DDPO <Cite id="ddpo-2023" /> | Denoising steps inside the decision process | Beat other diffusion RL on Robomimic <Cite id="robomimic-2021" />; 16 of 20 real table-leg assemblies |
| ReinFlow | Learnable noise gives exact step probabilities | 82.63% less wall time than DPPO on legged locomotion <Cite id="reinflow-2025" /> |
| FPO | PPO-style ratio from the flow-matching loss | <Cite id="fpo-2025" /> |
| QF3 | Adds the critic's action gradient to that loss | First <Term id="off-policy">off-policy</Term> flow RL for humanoid walking from scratch, by its authors' account; 10 times faster than FPO++ <Cite id="qf3-2026" /> |
| pi_RL | Learned step noise for π0 <Cite id="pi0-2024" /> and π0.5 <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> | Gains on four simulation benchmarks, not on five unseen MetaWorld tasks <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" /> |

## Recap conditions the policy instead of computing gradients

Recap, the method behind π*0.6, sidesteps the probability problem. A value function predicts steps remaining to success, and each action is labeled "Advantage: positive" or "Advantage: negative" in the prompt. Good and bad data both train the policy, and at deployment it is asked for the positive version <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

The target is the improved policy of KL-regularized RL, the reference policy reweighted toward high-advantage actions:

$$
\hat{\pi}(a \mid o) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o)\,\exp\!\left(A^{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(o,a)/\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}\right).
$$

Advantage-weighted regression fits it with two supervised steps <Cite id="awr-2019" />; Recap reaches it by conditioning. Dropping the label at random enables classifier-free guidance <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> <Cite id="cfg-2022" />, and a guidance weight above one leans further toward the positive label. Knowledge insulation stops the action expert's gradient at the backbone <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />.

Against offline RL plus demonstration fine-tuning, on-robot practice more than doubled successful espresso and laundry completions per hour and roughly halved failures <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. One run made espresso for "13 hours straight", with human reward labels, interventions and resets <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

Also: RL tokens, up to 3 times faster on the most precise stage of four tasks <Cite id="rlt-2026" />; the [π line](/manipulation/pi-line/) covers the models.

## A person on hand makes real-robot RL work

HIL-SERL runs RL on the physical robot from 20 to 30 demonstrations, with a person who can take over, building on SERL <Cite id="serl-2024" /> and RLPD <Cite id="rlpd-2023" />. It succeeded in every evaluation trial after 1 to 2.5 hours of training on nearly all tasks. HG-DAgger <Cite id="hg-dagger-2019" />, which imitates the person's corrections, averaged 49.7% <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

## Small RL helpers can teach the generalist

Another route leaves the large model alone and trains something small, like residual RL's correction to a fixed controller <Cite id="residual-rl-2018" />. PLD freezes a VLA, trains residual actors to recover from states the base reaches, and distills their trajectories back into the generalist <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

## EXPO-FT edits actions toward higher value

EXPO pairs a large flow or diffusion policy, trained by stable imitation, with a small edit policy that nudges sampled actions toward higher value. No gradient passes through the denoising chain, and sample efficiency improves 2 to 3 times on average <Cite id="expo-2025" />. EXPO-FT extends it to π0.5's action chunks and human corrections, reaching 30 of 30 on every task after an average 19.1 minutes of online robot data <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

<ExpoFtResults className="my-6" />

These tests vary starting states far more than HIL-SERL's; with its standard budget, HIL-SERL never started learning Cube Pick or Pool Shot <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" /> <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />. DSRL instead runs RL over a frozen policy's starting noise <Cite id="dsrl-2025" />. Noise search cannot produce a skill the base never learned, Dong and Finn argue, and DSRL averages 19 of 30 in their comparison <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" /> <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

<NoiseOrEdit className="my-6" />

Real-Time EXPO-FT lets the edit policy react to the latest view ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/)), lifting four dynamic real tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />. Dong and Finn still call RL "not yet a recipe. It's a craft." A person defines success, resets and intervenes, and "Nineteen minutes of robot interaction is not nineteen minutes of post-training" <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" />.

Also: SeeQ, values for the current subtask only <Cite id="seeq-2026" />; PrefPI, preference-conditioned policies <Cite id="prefpi-2026" />.

## RL's generalization gains are mostly simulated

Evidence that RL generalizes better than supervised fine-tuning comes mostly from simulation. Liu et al. found PPO beat three other objectives, GRPO <Cite id="grpo-2024" /> among them, and generalized better than supervised fine-tuning, all on simulated pick-and-place <Cite id="rl-vla-generalization-2025" />. With enough training tasks, value-based recipes raised held-out success by up to 58 points in simulation; with few, they forgot <Cite id="generalist-rl-improvement-2026" />.

Also: SimpleVLA-RL lifts OpenVLA-OFT <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" /> on LIBERO from 91% to 99% <Cite id="simplevla-rl-2025" />.

Much of the cost lies outside the algorithm. No vision-language model judges robot reward well across tasks <Cite id="roboreward-2026" />, and naive VLA fine-tuning fails without scene resets <Cite id="cronos-2026" />. Coding agents now automate resets, rollouts and checks: ENPIRE's agents trained policies to 99% on tasks such as fastening a zip tie <Cite id="enpire-2026" />.

Better pretraining shrinks the need. Physical Intelligence says π0.7 runs an espresso machine out of the box, matching specialized RL-finetuned models <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.
