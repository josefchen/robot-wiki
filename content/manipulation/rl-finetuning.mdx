RL fine-tuning improves a pretrained robot policy with <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term>: the robot performs a task, a reward is computed for that trial, and the policy is adapted to encourage behavior that did well. Imitation learning alone is brittle to compounding errors that carry the robot off its demonstrations <Cite id="dagger-2011" />. Reliable success is much harder than occasional success <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

## Practice turns attempts into training data

RL rewards each episode with some metric of performance, often a success detector or human judgment. A <Term id="value-function">value function</Term> estimates how well things are going; a step that leaves the robot better off than expected has positive advantage, and training pushes the policy toward it. On a real robot, a person also resets the scene and takes over before a mistake does damage <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

## Diffusion and flow policies hide their probabilities

Standard RL such as <Term id="ppo">PPO</Term> raises the probability of actions that did well, so it needs each action's probability <Cite id="ppo-2017" />. <Term id="flow-matching">Flow-matching</Term> policies optimize by fitting a velocity field along predetermined probability trajectories <Cite id="lipman-flow-matching-2022" />, making it infeasible to determine the probability of the taken action <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" />. Online RL on large VLAs is also unstable and needs more compute than most local machines have <Cite id="ire-vla-2025" />.

## Each denoising step can become a decision

One fix makes the denoising process part of the task, with each denoising step a decision. The reward only comes at the end, but the probability of the denoising chain is easy to compute (a product of Gaussians).

| Method | Trick | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DPPO <Cite id="dppo-2024" />, after DDPO <Cite id="ddpo-2023" /> | Denoising steps inside the decision process | Beat other diffusion RL on Robomimic <Cite id="robomimic-2021" />; 16 of 20 real table-leg assemblies |
| ReinFlow | Learnable noise gives exact step probabilities | 82.63% less wall time than DPPO on legged locomotion <Cite id="reinflow-2025" /> |
| FPO | PPO-style ratio from the flow-matching loss | <Cite id="fpo-2025" /> |
| QF3 | Adds the critic's action gradient to that loss | First <Term id="off-policy">off-policy</Term> flow RL for humanoid walking from scratch, by its authors' account; 10 times faster than FPO++ <Cite id="qf3-2026" /> |
| pi_RL | Learned step noise for π0 <Cite id="pi0-2024" /> and π0.5 <Cite id="pi05-2025" /> | Gains on four simulation benchmarks, not on five unseen MetaWorld tasks <Cite id="pi-rl-2026" /> |

## Recap conditions the policy instead of computing gradients

Recap, the method behind π*0.6, sidesteps the probability problem. A value function predicts steps remaining to success, and each action is labeled "Advantage: positive" or "Advantage: negative" in the prompt. Good and bad data both train the policy, and at deployment it is asked for the positive version <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />.

The target is given by a KL-regularized RL policy improvement step, resulting in an improved policy that upweights actions with high advantages under the reference policy:

$$
\hat{\pi}(a \mid o) \propto \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a \mid o)\,\exp\!\left(A^{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(o,a)/\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}\right).
$$

Advantage-weighted regression fits it in two supervised steps <Cite id="awr-2019" />. Recap instead reaches it by conditioning. Randomly dropping the conditioning label during training enables inference with classifier-free guidance <Cite id="pistar06-2025" /> <Cite id="cfg-2022" />, and using a guidance weight larger than one leans further toward the positive label. Knowledge insulation stops the gradient of the action expert's loss from backpropagating into the backbone <Cite id="pi06-model-card-2025" />.

Against offline RL followed by fine-tuning on demonstrations, on-robot learning more than doubled the number of successful espresso and laundry tasks completed per hour and approximately halved the number of failures <Cite id="pistar06-2025" />. In one case the system made espresso for "13 hours straight" (with human-provided reward labels, interventions and resets) <Cite id="pistar06-blog-2025" />.

Also: RL tokens, up to 3 times faster on the most precise stage of four tasks <Cite id="rlt-2026" />; the [π line](/manipulation/pi-line/) covers the models.

## A person on hand makes real-robot RL work

HIL-SERL runs RL on the physical robot from 20 to 30 demonstrations, with a person who can take over, building on SERL <Cite id="serl-2024" /> and RLPD <Cite id="rlpd-2023" />. After 1 to 2.5 hours of training, HIL-SERL succeeded in all evaluation trials for almost all tasks. HG-DAgger <Cite id="hg-dagger-2019" />, which learns by imitation from the human corrections, achieves an average performance of 49.7% <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />.

## Small RL helpers can teach the generalist

Another route leaves the large model alone and trains something small, like residual RL's correction to a fixed controller <Cite id="residual-rl-2018" />. PLD keeps the VLA frozen while learning residual RL policies on top of it to recover from states encountered with the frozen VLA, and then distills data collected with the residual RL policies back into the VLA <Cite id="pld-2026" />.

## EXPO-FT edits actions toward higher value

EXPO pairs a large flow or diffusion policy, trained by stable imitation, with a small edit policy that nudges sampled actions toward higher value. No gradient passes through the denoising chain, and sample efficiency improves 2 to 3 times on average <Cite id="expo-2025" />. EXPO-FT extends EXPO to fine-tune π0.5 with action chunks and human corrections, achieving 30 of 30 successes on all tasks with an average of 19.1 minutes of online robot data <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

<ExpoFtResults className="my-6" />

These tasks have much greater variation in initial conditions than HIL-SERL's; with its standard budget, HIL-SERL never started learning Cube Pick or Pool Shot <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" /> <Cite id="hil-serl-2024" />. DSRL instead runs RL over a frozen policy's starting noise <Cite id="dsrl-2025" />. As argued by Dong and Finn, noise-based exploration methods such as DSRL are unable to discover skills that are absent from the initial policy, and DSRL averages 19 out of 30 in their comparison <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" /> <Cite id="expo-ft-2026" />.

<NoiseOrEdit className="my-6" />

Real-Time EXPO-FT conditions the edit policy on the latest view ([real-time execution](/manipulation/realtime-execution/)), improving performance on four dynamic real tasks from 42% to 97% <Cite id="realtime-expo-ft-2026" />. Dong and Finn still call RL "not yet a recipe. It's a craft." There is a human in the loop deciding what success looks like, and intervening or resetting as needed: "Nineteen minutes of robot interaction is not nineteen minutes of post-training" <Cite id="perry-dong-post-training-2026" />.

Also: SeeQ, values for the current subtask only <Cite id="seeq-2026" />; PrefPI, preference-conditioned policies <Cite id="prefpi-2026" />.

## RL's generalization gains are mostly simulated

Evidence that RL generalizes better than supervised fine-tuning comes mostly from simulation. For instance, in simulated pick-and-place, Liu et al. found that PPO outperformed three other training objectives (including GRPO <Cite id="grpo-2024" />) and generalized better than supervised fine-tuning <Cite id="rl-vla-generalization-2025" />. Value-based recipes increased success rates on held-out tasks by up to 58 points in simulation, given a large enough number of training tasks; otherwise, the models suffered from forgetting <Cite id="generalist-rl-improvement-2026" />.

Also: SimpleVLA-RL lifts OpenVLA-OFT <Cite id="openvla-oft-2025" /> on LIBERO from 91% to 99% <Cite id="simplevla-rl-2025" />.

Much of the cost lies outside the algorithm. There is not a vision-language model that can provide good robot rewards across tasks <Cite id="roboreward-2026" />, and naive fine-tuning of VLAs does not work without resetting the scene <Cite id="cronos-2026" />. Coding agents now automate resets, rollouts and checks: ENPIRE's agents trained policies to perform tasks such as fastening a zip tie with a 99% success rate <Cite id="enpire-2026" />.

Physical Intelligence says π0.7 runs an espresso machine with no additional tuning, performing on par with models that were specifically RL-finetuned on this task <Cite id="pi07-2026" />.
