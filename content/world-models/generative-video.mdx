A generative video [world model](/world-models/taxonomy/) is a video generator that also takes the robot's action, so it shows what the camera would see after that move. It can stand in for the real world to train and test policies, and some now act as the policy <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

<Image id="gr1-video-prediction-2023" />

Policies trained only inside UniSim ran in the real world zero-shot <Cite id="unisim-2023" />. The catch is a model that renders realistic frames while ignoring the action: "visual realism does not imply physical understanding" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.

<span id="cosmos-3-the-omni-model" />
## Cosmos 3 replaces a stack of separate models with one omni-model

NVIDIA calls a home robot built from separate planner, policy and action-prediction models "fragmented" and "computationally wasteful" <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />. Cosmos 3, launched on May 31, 2026, does those jobs in one model <Cite id="nvidia-cosmos-3-launch-2026" />.

It pairs a <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language</Term> reasoner with a generator of video, audio and actions, so it can predict video from actions, actions from video, or both as a policy <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />. Open checkpoints from November 2026 simulate YAM and AgiBot two-arm robots <Cite id="cosmos3-interactive-sim-2026" />.

Also: its predecessor Predict2.5 <Cite id="cosmos-predict2-5-2025" />.

<span id="genie-2-and-genie-3-interactive-worlds" />
## Genie 3 renders worlds at 24 fps that hold for a few minutes

| Model | Release | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genie | 2024 | Learned its own actions from unlabeled internet video; 2D worlds at about 1 fps <Cite id="genie-1-2024" /> |
| Genie 2 | December 2024 | 3D worlds consistent for up to a minute <Cite id="genie-2-2024" /> |
| Genie 3 | August 2025, research preview | Text-prompted worlds at 24 fps and 720p, largely consistent for a few minutes <Cite id="genie-3-2025" /> |
| Project Genie | January 2026, AI Ultra subscribers | Sessions capped at 60 seconds per world <Cite id="project-genie-2026" /><Cite id="google-project-genie-2026" /> |
| Genie 4 | October 2026, trusted testers | Adds audio and text steering by Gemini; simulated Apptronik's Apollo humanoid playing checkers <Cite id="genie-4-2026" /> |

In Genie 3 the <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> simulated a SIMA agent's navigation actions without knowing its goal; no robot manipulation was involved <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />. Genie 4's object permanence drifts over longer sessions <Cite id="genie-4-2026" />.

<span id="gr-1-to-gr-3-video-pre-training-for-manipulation" />
## GR-1 and GR-2 built on video pre-training; GR-3 switched to a VLA

ByteDance's GR-1 predicted future images and actions together, raising CALVIN success from 88.9% to 94.9%, and from 53.3% to 85.4% in unseen scenes <Cite id="gr-1-2023" />. GR-2 pretrained on 38 million internet clips and reported 97.7% average success over 100+ tasks <Cite id="gr-2-2024" />; GR-3 switched to a vision-language-action design <Cite id="gr-3-2025" />.

<span id="world-action-models-as-policies" />
## World-action models now run as closed-loop robot policies

A world-action model (WAM) predicts future video and the robot's next actions together. NVIDIA's DreamZero, a 14-billion-parameter video model, controls a real robot seven times a second and more than doubles the generalization of leading [VLAs](/manipulation/vla-models/) <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. Fast-WAM traces the gain to video training: skipping video generation at test time stays competitive, while dropping video co-training costs far more <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" />.

These results are self-reported, mostly in simulation.

| System | What it showed | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmos Policy (2026) | A video model becomes a policy without architectural changes <Cite id="cosmos-policy-2026" /> | 98.5% on LIBERO, 67.1% on RoboCasa; 82.2% on perturbed LIBERO-Plus <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> |
| LingBot-VA (2026) | Predicts frames and acts together, feeding real observations back in <Cite id="lingbot-va-2026" /> | 74.2% on perturbed RoboTwin 2.0-Plus <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> |
| OpenWAM (October 2026) | Pretrains on over 10,000 hours of robot video <Cite id="openwam-2026" /> | LIBERO-Long success from 68.4% to 97.8% |
| Long-WAM (2026) | Longer memory helps only if the video model was pretrained frame by frame <Cite id="long-wam-2026" /> | RoboCasa from 63.3% to 78.7%; real moving-cup stacking 95%, against none of 20 for π0.5 and Fast-WAM |
| DreamCan (2026) | Plans with a few generated subgoal images <Cite id="dreamcan-2026" /> | 91.2% on LIBERO-Long, against 82.4% for evenly spaced subgoals and 92.4% for π0.5 |
| GR00T 2 (November 2026) | NVIDIA's open WAM, built on Cosmos-Predict2.5 <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" /> | 95% on 50 new tasks from five demonstrations each, tasks chosen partly because five sufficed <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| Praxis-1 (2026) | Runway's 27-billion-parameter WAM <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-2026" /> | Open weights, Apache 2.0 license <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" /> |

<span id="1x-and-odyssey-the-commercial-edges" />
## 1X still fails dexterous tasks, while Odyssey streams worlds live

1X's NEO policy builds on a 14-billion-parameter video model further trained on 900 hours of first-person human video and 70 hours of robot data. Over 30 runs per task it scored 0% on drawing a smiley and on pouring cereal, and it takes 11 seconds to generate 5 seconds of video <Cite id="1x-world-model-2026" />.

Odyssey-2 streams a new frame every 50 milliseconds, generated from earlier frames and user input <Cite id="odyssey-2-2025" />. A driving policy trained on 20 simulated hours in a frozen Odyssey-3 then drove real roads in India. By Odyssey's measure, it went about 77% as far between safety-driver interventions as a policy trained on real footage <Cite id="odyssey-3-2026" />.

Also: 1X's World Model Lab <Cite id="1x-world-model-lab-2026" />, Odyssey's Starchild-1 with audio <Cite id="odyssey-starchild-1-2026" />, its multiplayer Agora-1 <Cite id="odyssey-agora-1-2026" />.

<span id="the-conditioning-problem" />
## Weak action conditioning is the field's main bottleneck

A 2026 survey names weak action conditioning the bottleneck: futures come out "semantically plausible or intention-consistent, but not necessarily faithful to the physical consequences of the candidate action" <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. In closed-loop tests, controllability mattered more than visual quality, and action-observation post-training beat upgrading the base generator <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />.

Expert demonstrations vary their actions so little that a model can ignore them, and WorldEcho found such models break on unfamiliar actions <Cite id="worldecho-2026" />. A Princeton and Columbia group proved that how much actions influence next states caps what any model can learn. In simulated pushing, 500 high-influence episodes plus 3,000 demonstrations matched 300,000 expert episodes at following gripper commands <Cite id="aim-2026" />.

Also: DIAMOND, an Atari agent trained inside a diffusion world model <Cite id="diamond-world-model-2024" />.

<span id="does-it-work-as-a-simulator-the-evidence-both-ways" />
## In IWS tests, generated demonstrations rivalled real ones for two policies

The Interactive World Simulator (IWS), a <Term id="latent-dynamics">latent dynamics</Term> model, runs stable 128-by-128-pixel interaction for 10+ minutes at up to 15 frames per second on one RTX 4090. Policies trained only on its demonstrations matched real-data policies: Diffusion Policy succeeded 87.9% of the time against 90.3%, and ACT 76.2% against 73.6% <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.

Latents trained to be easy to predict gave up to five times cheaper inference and 15% to 24% lower errors out of distribution and over long horizons <Cite id="predictable-latents-2026" />.

## Some video simulators rank policies in real-world order, others do not

A simulator that orders policies as real trials do picks the best one before any robot runs. Pearson r = 1 is a perfect match.

| Simulator | Checked against | Agreement with reality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RoboWorld (2026), scored by GPT-4o | 8 RoboArena policies, 4,186 rollouts | Pearson r = 0.989 <Cite id="roboworld-2026" /> |
| Runway GWM-Robotics (February 2026), human graders | 8 RoboArena policies, 1,450 rollouts | Pearson r = 0.95 on progress scores <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" /> |
| DeepMind's Veo-based simulator (2025) | Over 1,600 real evaluations | Predicted relative policy performance <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" /> |
| Ctrl-World, tested by PolaRiS (2025) | Diverse scenes | Hallucinated in object interaction; mis-ranked policies at rank violation 0.22 <Cite id="polaris-2025" /> |
| PhysCanvas (October 2026), vision and touch | Four real tasks | Correlation from 0.602 to 0.885 after reward-model post-training <Cite id="physcanvas-2026" /> |

RoboWorld's correlation rests on eight data points <Cite id="roboworld-2026" />, and Runway's study tests ranking, not absolute success <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />.

## Generated experience trains real policies, but contact-rich tasks stay open

| System | Generated experience | Real result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RISE | <Term id="reinforcement-learning">Reinforcement learning</Term> inside a world model that predicts and scores progress | 35 to 45 points better on three tasks, including dynamic brick sorting <Cite id="rise-2026" /> |
| GRAIL | Over 20,000 humanoid sequences from 3D assets and video models | 84% at picking up objects, 90% at climbing stairs on a Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" /> |
| CASTER | A video of a person, then a robot motion optimized in a digital twin | 76.6% zero-shot over 12 Franka tasks, against 42.5% for copying the object path <Cite id="caster-2026" /> |

The survey calls visual plausibility "only a weak proxy for control utility" and touch indispensable, since cameras miss friction, stiffness and contact stability. World-model policies also cost far more compute than <Term id="vision-language-action-model">VLAs</Term>, and step-by-step video generation adds delay <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

<span id="an-evaluation-ladder-for-video-models" />
## Test action response before trusting a video model's rankings

Start with paired interventions that change only the action from identical frames. Check direction of motion, moment of contact and object identity before image quality, then lengthen the horizon, then rank policies against blinded real trials. By 1X's estimate, a 70%-accurate world model picks the better of two policies 15% apart 90% of the time <Cite id="1x-world-model-2025" />.

The [evaluation guide](/world-models/evaluation/) gives the full protocol; see also [the evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/) and the non-generative [JEPA](/world-models/jepa/).
