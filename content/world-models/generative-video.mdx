A generative video [world model](/world-models/taxonomy/) is a video generator that also takes the robot's action as input, so it shows what the camera would see if the robot made that move. It can stand in for the real world to train and test robot policies, and some now act as the policy <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

<Image id="gr1-video-prediction-2023" />

UniSim (2023) showed the idea works: policies trained only inside its simulator ran in the real world zero-shot <Cite id="unisim-2023" />. The catch is that a video model can render realistic frames while ignoring the action, so two different moves from the same start frame produce the same future. Video benchmarks find exactly this: "visual realism does not imply physical understanding" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.

<span id="cosmos-3-the-omni-model" />
## Cosmos 3 replaces a stack of separate models with one omni-model

NVIDIA's Cosmos 3 report calls a home robot built from a separate planner, policy and action-prediction model "fragmented" and "computationally wasteful" <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />. Cosmos 3, launched at GTC Taipei on May 31, 2026, does those jobs in one model <Cite id="nvidia-cosmos-3-launch-2026" />; its predecessor Predict2.5 had combined text-, image- and video-prompted generation <Cite id="cosmos-predict2-5-2025" />.

It pairs a <Term id="vision-language-model">vision-language</Term> reasoner with a generator of video, audio and actions, and can predict video from actions, actions from video, or both as a policy. Each robot use still needs its own post-training <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />. In November 2026 NVIDIA released open checkpoints that simulate YAM and AgiBot two-arm robots driven by a policy or a joystick <Cite id="cosmos3-interactive-sim-2026" />.

<span id="genie-2-and-genie-3-interactive-worlds" />
## Genie 3 renders worlds at 24 fps that hold for a few minutes

The original Genie learned its own set of actions from unlabeled internet video and generated 2D worlds at about one frame per second <Cite id="genie-1-2024" />. Genie 2 (December 2024) made 3D worlds that stayed consistent for up to a minute <Cite id="genie-2-2024" />.

Genie 3, announced on August 5, 2025 as a limited research preview, renders text-prompted worlds navigable in real time at 24 frames per second and 720p, largely consistent for a few minutes. A SIMA game-playing agent sent it navigation actions, which the <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> simulated without knowing the agent's goal; no robot manipulation was involved, and DeepMind lists a constrained action space among its limits <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />. Project Genie, opened to AI Ultra subscribers in January 2026, caps sessions at 60 seconds per world <Cite id="project-genie-2026" /><Cite id="google-project-genie-2026" />.

Genie 4, given to trusted testers on October 27, 2026, adds audio and lets Gemini steer the scene by text. DeepMind showed it simulating Apptronik's Apollo humanoid playing checkers, but object permanence drifts over longer sessions and complex physical interactions remain open <Cite id="genie-4-2026" />.

<span id="gr-1-to-gr-3-video-pre-training-for-manipulation" />
## GR-1 and GR-2 built on video pre-training; GR-3 switched to a VLA

ByteDance's GR series tested whether predicting video helps a robot act. GR-1, pretrained on video, predicted future images and actions together; on the CALVIN simulation benchmark it raised the best success rate from 88.9% to 94.9%, and in unseen scenes from 53.3% to 85.4% <Cite id="gr-1-2023" />. GR-2 pretrained on 38 million internet clips and reported 97.7% average success across more than 100 tasks <Cite id="gr-2-2024" />. GR-3 then switched to a vision-language-action design <Cite id="gr-3-2025" />.

<span id="world-action-models-as-policies" />
## World-action models now run as closed-loop robot policies

A world-action model (WAM) predicts future video and the robot's next actions together, so what a video model knows about motion feeds into control. NVIDIA's DreamZero, a 14-billion-parameter video model, controls a real robot seven times a second and more than doubles the generalization of leading [VLAs](/manipulation/vla-models/) <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. Under visual and language perturbations, VLAs such as π0.5 matched WAMs on some tasks only with extensive, diverse training <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" />. Fast-WAM suggests the benefit comes mainly from training on video: skipping video generation at test time stays competitive, while removing video co-training costs far more <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" />.

Later WAM results are self-reported, mostly on the LIBERO, RoboCasa and RoboTwin simulation benchmarks.

| System | What it showed | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmos Policy (2026) | Turns a video model into a policy without architectural changes <Cite id="cosmos-policy-2026" /> | 98.5% on LIBERO, 67.1% on RoboCasa; 82.2% on perturbed LIBERO-Plus <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> |
| LingBot-VA (2026) | Predicts frames and acts together, feeding real observations back in <Cite id="lingbot-va-2026" /> | 74.2% on perturbed RoboTwin 2.0-Plus <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> |
| OpenWAM (October 2026) | Pretrains on over 10,000 hours of robot video <Cite id="openwam-2026" /> | LIBERO-Long success from 68.4% to 97.8% |
| Long-WAM (2026) | Longer memory helps only if the video model was pretrained frame by frame <Cite id="long-wam-2026" /> | RoboCasa from 63.3% to 78.7% with 19.2 s of history; real moving-cup stacking 95%, against none of 20 for π0.5 and Fast-WAM |
| DreamCan (2026) | Plans with a few generated subgoal images <Cite id="dreamcan-2026" /> | 91.2% on LIBERO-Long, against 82.4% for evenly spaced subgoals and 92.4% reported for π0.5 |
| GR00T 2 (November 2026) | NVIDIA's open WAM, built on Cosmos-Predict2.5 <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" /> | 95% on 50 new tasks after five demonstrations each, tasks chosen partly because five sufficed <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| Praxis-1 (2026) | Runway's 27-billion-parameter WAM, announced in September <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-2026" /> | Open weights, Apache 2.0 license <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" /> |

<span id="1x-and-odyssey-the-commercial-edges" />
## 1X still fails dexterous tasks, while Odyssey streams worlds live

1X's NEO humanoid policy builds on a 14-billion-parameter video model trained further on 900 hours of first-person human video and 70 hours of robot data; a separate model reads actions off the generated video. Over 30 runs per task it scored 0% on drawing a smiley and on pouring cereal, and the model takes 11 seconds to generate 5 seconds of video <Cite id="1x-world-model-2026" />. In June 2026 1X opened a World Model Lab, led by Sam Sinha from Luma AI <Cite id="1x-world-model-lab-2026" />.

Odyssey-2 (October 2025) streams a new frame every 50 milliseconds, about 20 per second, each generated from earlier frames and user input <Cite id="odyssey-2-2025" />. Starchild-1 adds synchronized audio <Cite id="odyssey-starchild-1-2026" />, and Agora-1 keeps a shared game state so several players see one consistent world <Cite id="odyssey-agora-1-2026" />. On top of a frozen Odyssey-3 (September 2026), a driving policy trained on 20 hours of simulated driving ran on real roads in India, traveling about 77% as far between safety-driver interventions as a policy trained on real footage, by Odyssey's own measure <Cite id="odyssey-3-2026" />.

<span id="the-conditioning-problem" />
## Weak action conditioning is the field's main bottleneck

Hou and colleagues' 2026 survey puts causal conditioning gaps first among open challenges, calling weak action conditioning the technical bottleneck: models trained mostly on observation history and task intent produce futures "semantically plausible or intention-consistent, but not necessarily faithful to the physical consequences of the candidate action" <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. In closed-loop tests, controllability mattered more than visual quality, and post-training on action-observation data beat upgrading the base video generator <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />. DIAMOND, an agent trained entirely inside a diffusion world model, showed on Atari games that keeping visual detail helps the agent <Cite id="diamond-world-model-2024" />.

The weakness starts in the data. Expert demonstrations vary their actions so little that a model can predict the next frame while ignoring them. A Princeton and Columbia group proved that how much a dataset's actions influence its next states caps what any model trained on it can learn; in a simulated pushing task, 500 episodes collected to maximize that influence, added to 3,000 demonstrations, matched a model trained on 300,000 expert episodes at following gripper commands <Cite id="aim-2026" />. The WorldEcho benchmark (August 2026) found expert-trained models drift or break on actions outside the demonstrations <Cite id="worldecho-2026" />.

<span id="does-it-work-as-a-simulator-the-evidence-both-ways" />
## In IWS tests, generated demonstrations rivalled real ones for two policies

The Interactive World Simulator (IWS), a <Term id="latent-dynamics">latent dynamics</Term> model, reports more than 10 minutes of stable interaction at up to 15 frames per second on one RTX 4090 graphics card, at 128 by 128 pixels. Policies trained only on its generated demonstrations performed comparably to those trained on as much real data: Diffusion Policy succeeded 87.9% of the time against 90.3%, and ACT 76.2% against 73.6%. Each data point used 100 simulated or 10 physical evaluations, the simulator itself was trained on real play data, and its own scores ran optimistic on three of four tasks tested <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.

How the latent space is built matters: training it to be easy to predict gave up to five times cheaper inference and 15% to 24% lower errors out of distribution and over long horizons <Cite id="predictable-latents-2026" />.

## Some video simulators rank policies in real-world order, others do not

If a simulator orders policies the way real trials do, a team can pick the best before touching a robot. A Pearson correlation of 1 means simulated and real scores line up perfectly.

| Simulator | Checked against | Agreement with reality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RoboWorld (2026), scored by GPT-4o | February 2026 RoboArena leaderboard: 8 policies, 4,186 rollouts | Pearson r = 0.989 <Cite id="roboworld-2026" /> |
| Runway GWM-Robotics (February 2026), scored by human graders | 8 RoboArena policies, 1,450 rollouts | Pearson r = 0.95 on progress scores <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" /> |
| DeepMind's Veo-based simulator (2025) | Over 1,600 real evaluations, including unfamiliar conditions | Predicted relative policy performance <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" /> |
| Ctrl-World, tested by PolaRiS (2025) | Diverse scenes | Hallucinated during object interaction; mis-ranked policies (rank-violation score 0.22) <Cite id="polaris-2025" /> |
| PhysCanvas (October 2026), vision and touch | Four real tasks | Correlation with real success from 0.602 to 0.885 after reward-model post-training <Cite id="physcanvas-2026" /> |

RoboWorld's correlation rests on eight data points, and the paper reports object artifacts at contact and model scores about one point above human ones <Cite id="roboworld-2026" />. Veo episodes last 8 seconds, and contact-rich, small-object interaction remains an open challenge <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. Runway says its study tests ranking only and does not predict absolute success rates <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />.

## Generated experience trains real policies, but contact-rich tasks stay open

RISE let a policy practice by <Term id="reinforcement-learning">reinforcement learning</Term> inside a world model that predicts the future and scores progress, gaining 35 to 45 absolute points on three real tasks, including dynamic brick sorting <Cite id="rise-2026" />. GRAIL generated over 20,000 humanoid sequences from 3D assets and video models; visual policies trained only on them succeeded 84% of the time at picking up objects and 90% at climbing stairs on a real Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" />. CASTER, from Virginia Tech and Johns Hopkins, generates a video of a person doing the task, then optimizes a feasible robot motion in a digital twin; over 12 real tasks on a Franka arm it succeeded zero-shot 76.6% of the time, against 42.5% for copying each video's object path exactly; its failures at pouring and covering came from physics the twin did not model <Cite id="caster-2026" />.

Contact is where these models fall short. The survey calls visual plausibility "only a weak proxy for control utility" and touch sensing indispensable, since cameras and joint readings miss friction, stiffness and contact stability. World-model policies also cost far more compute than <Term id="vision-language-action-model">VLAs</Term>, and generating video step by step adds delay <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

<span id="an-evaluation-ladder-for-video-models" />
## Test action response before trusting a video model's rankings

Start with paired interventions that change only the action from identical frames. Check direction of motion, moment of contact and object identity before image quality, then lengthen the horizon on held-out transitions. Only then rank policies against blinded real trials. By 1X's estimate, with a 15% real gap between two policies, a world model with 70% accuracy picks the better one 90% of the time <Cite id="1x-world-model-2025" />. The [evaluation guide](/world-models/evaluation/) gives the full protocol; see also [the evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/), [world models versus simulators](/world-models/world-models-vs-simulators/) and the non-generative [JEPA](/world-models/jepa/).
