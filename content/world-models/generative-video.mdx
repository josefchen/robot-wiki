Generative video [world models](/world-models/taxonomy/) are video predictors that generate future pixels from the current frame and an action or text prompt, standing in for the environment. They supply training data, policy evaluation, RL post-training and interactive worlds. UniSim trained policies purely inside such a simulator and deployed them zero-shot in the real world <Cite id="unisim-2023" />.

Switch "Video model" from "Listens to the action" to "Ignores the action": the two futures from one start frame collapse onto the same picture, the action-sensitivity score falls to near zero, and the visual-realism score stays put. Video generators show this split: "visual realism does not imply physical understanding" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.

<span id="cosmos-3-the-omni-model" />
## Cosmos 3 replaces a stack of separate models with one omni-model

In the Cosmos 3 report, NVIDIA calls a home robot built from a separate VLM planner, VLA or WAM, and forward-dynamics model "fragmented" and "computationally wasteful" <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />. Cosmos 3, launched at GTC Taipei on 31 May 2026, unifies those capabilities in one omni-model <Cite id="nvidia-cosmos-3-launch-2026" />.

Its Mixture-of-Transformers backbone routes language and ViT-encoded vision to a causal autoregressive reasoner and VAE-encoded vision, audio and actions to a diffusion generator, each with its own parameters. Diffusion tokens attend to both streams, while reasoner tokens never read diffusion tokens <span className="block" data-cosmos-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />.</span> It supports <Term id="vision-language-model">VLM</Term> understanding, video generation and three action modes: forward dynamics (video from clean actions), inverse dynamics (actions from clean video) and policy (both). Specialized results come from separate post-training, such as a DROID policy started from mid-trained Cosmos3-Nano with fresh action modules. Nano has 16B parameters on a dense 8B transformer and Super 64B on a dense 32B one <span className="block" data-cosmos-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />.</span>

<span id="genie-2-and-genie-3-interactive-worlds" />
## Genie 3 renders worlds at 24 fps that hold for a few minutes

The original Genie learned latent actions and generated 2D worlds at about one frame per second <Cite id="genie-1-2024" />. Genie 2 (December 2024) moved to 3D, with consistent worlds for up to a minute and most examples lasting 10 to 20 seconds <Cite id="genie-2-2024" />.

In their August 5, 2025 announcement, Jack Parker-Holder and Shlomi Fruchter describe Genie 3 as a limited research preview: text-prompted worlds navigable in real time at 24 fps and 720p, largely consistent for a few minutes. A SIMA agent sent navigation actions to Genie 3, and the <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> simulated the future without knowing the agent's goal; the test involved no robot manipulation. DeepMind lists five limits: a constrained direct action space, multi-agent interaction, geographic accuracy, legible text only when supplied, and a few minutes of continuous interaction <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />.

Project Genie, released in January 2026 for AI Ultra subscribers, caps sessions at 60 seconds per world <Cite id="project-genie-2026" /><Cite id="google-project-genie-2026" />. Waymo reports that its World Model, "built upon Genie 3", generates camera and lidar data for driving simulation <Cite id="waymo-world-model-2026" />.

<span id="gr-1-to-gr-3-video-pre-training-for-manipulation" />
## GR-1 and GR-2 built on video pre-training; GR-3 switched to a VLA

GR-1 applied large-scale video generative pre-training to multi-task manipulation, predicting future images and actions end to end. On CALVIN it moved success from 88.9% to 94.9%, and in zero-shot unseen scenes from 53.3% to 85.4% <Cite id="gr-1-2023" />. GR-2 pre-trained on 38 million internet clips and over 50 billion tokens, then fine-tuned jointly for video generation and action prediction, reporting 97.7% average success across more than 100 tasks <Cite id="gr-2-2024" />. The lineage then pivoted: GR-3 is a 4B-parameter VLA trained by vision-language co-training, VR human trajectories and robot imitation <Cite id="gr-3-2025" />.

<span id="world-action-models-as-policies" />
## World-action models now run as closed-loop robot policies

A world-action model (WAM) predicts future video and actions together. NVIDIA reports that DreamZero runs a 14B autoregressive video-diffusion model as a 7 Hz closed-loop policy and more than doubles the generalization of state-of-the-art [VLAs](/manipulation/vla-models/) on real robots <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />. Cosmos Policy post-trains Cosmos-Predict2 with no architectural modifications and reaches 98.5% on LIBERO and 67.1% on RoboCasa <Cite id="cosmos-policy-2026" />. LingBot-VA overlaps action prediction with motor execution and calls video world modeling "a fresh and independent foundation for robot learning" <Cite id="lingbot-va-2026" />.

Under perturbation, Cosmos-Policy reached 82.2% on LIBERO-Plus and LingBot-VA 74.2% on RoboTwin 2.0-Plus, while VLAs such as π0.5 matched them on some tasks only with extensive, diverse training <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" />. Fast-WAM stays competitive without test-time video generation, while removing video co-training costs far more <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" />.

Two October 2026 papers measured WAM design choices directly. OpenWAM, from Heng Yu, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu, Ehsan Adeli and colleagues at Stanford, pretrains the Wan2.2-5B video model causally on more than 10,000 hours of robot video and attaches an action expert that can generate jointly, video first, action first or decoupled. Against the original Wan2.2-5B starting point, that training raised video-then-action success on LIBERO-Long from 68.4% to 97.8% <Cite id="openwam-2026" />. Long-WAM, from Wei Huang, Linxi Fan, Song Han, Yukang Chen and colleagues at NVIDIA, MIT and HKU, found that longer visual history pays off when the video model was pretrained autoregressively. On RoboCasa GR-1, extending context from 0 to 19.2 seconds raised success from 63.3% to 78.7%, while a bidirectionally pretrained start showed no net gain. Each action chunk took 107.4 ms on an RTX 5090, and on real robots it stacked moving cups in 95% of trials, a task where π0.5 and Fast-WAM succeeded in none of 20 <Cite id="long-wam-2026" />.

<span id="1x-and-odyssey-the-commercial-edges" />
## 1X still fails dexterous tasks, while Odyssey streams worlds live

1X reports that its NEO policy builds on a 14B video model, mid-trained on 900 hours of egocentric human video and fine-tuned on 70 hours of robot data, with an inverse-dynamics model (IDM) extracting actions. Over 30 runs per task it scored 0% on drawing a smiley and pouring cereal. The backbone takes 11 seconds per 5 seconds of video, plus 1 second for the IDM <Cite id="1x-world-model-2026" />. In June 2026, 1X launched a World Model Lab led by Sam Sinha, previously a founding research scientist at Luma AI, to pre-train on web video, egocentric human data, simulation, teleoperation and on-policy NEO data <Cite id="1x-world-model-lab-2026" />.

Odyssey-2 (October 2025) generates each frame causally from prior frames and user input, streaming a new frame every 50 ms (about 20 fps), steerable by text <Cite id="odyssey-2-2025" />. Starchild-1 adds audio <Cite id="odyssey-starchild-1-2026" />, and Agora-1 lets up to four participants share one simulation <Cite id="odyssey-agora-1-2026" />.

<span id="the-conditioning-problem" />
## Weak action conditioning is the field's main bottleneck

The 2026 survey lists causal conditioning gaps first among its open challenges and calls weak action conditioning the technical bottleneck. Models trained mostly from observation history and task intent produce futures "semantically plausible or intention-consistent, but not necessarily faithful to the physical consequences of the candidate action" <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. A weakly conditioned model still renders sharp video, so the failure stays invisible in a demo reel. In closed-loop tests, controllability mattered more than visual quality, and post-training on action-observation data beat upgrading the base generator <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />.

<span id="does-it-work-as-a-simulator-the-evidence-both-ways" />
## In IWS tests, generated demonstrations rivalled real ones for two policies

The Interactive World Simulator pairs a CNN image encoder and a consistency-model decoder with an action-conditioned <Term id="latent-dynamics">latent dynamics</Term> model. It reports more than 10 minutes of stable generated-video interaction at up to 15 FPS on a single RTX 4090, at a default 128×128 resolution; quantitative video comparisons use 192 steps (19.2 seconds) <span className="block" data-generative-video-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.</span>

In 100-episode mixtures, generated-only versus real-only policy data scored 87.9% versus 90.3% for DP and 76.2% versus 73.6% for ACT, and π0.5 rose from 73.1% to 88.8% with more real data. Points used 100 MuJoCo or 10 physical evaluations, and the simulator was trained on interaction data including real play. A four-task test from 20 initial configurations gave positively biased simulator scores except for T pushing <span className="block" data-generative-video-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.</span>

## Some video simulators rank policies in real-world order, others do not

RoboWorld pairs an autoregressive video world model, trained by Step Forcing and run with four-step denoising, with a task-progress VLM scorer. In its fourth revision, 4,186 generated 30-second rollouts gave eight policy-level scores matching the 26 February 2026 RoboArena leaderboard at Pearson r = 0.989 and Spearman rho = 0.970 (p < 0.001); the eight aggregates are the correlation points. GPT-4o scores progress from 0 to 5 from fixed external views. The paper reports contact-induced object artifacts and VLM scores about one point above human scores over roughly 100 rollouts <Cite id="roboworld-2026" />.

DeepMind reports its Veo-based simulator predicted relative policy performance in nominal and out-of-distribution conditions, checked against more than 1,600 real evaluations. Its episodes last 8 seconds, and its authors call contact-rich, small-object interaction an open challenge <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. NVIDIA reports that DreamDojo, pretrained on 44k hours of egocentric human video, runs at 10.81 FPS and targets "open-world, contact-rich tasks" <Cite id="dreamdojo-2026" />. PolaRiS found that the open Ctrl-World hallucinated during object interaction in diverse scenes and mis-ranked policies at MMRV 0.22 <Cite id="polaris-2025" />.

In February 2026 Runway reported a similar ranking test for its GWM-Robotics world model: across eight vision-language-action policies from RoboArena, with 1,450 simulated rollouts scored by human graders, the policies' simulated and real progress scores had a Pearson correlation of 0.95 <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. Runway says the study tests policy ranking only and does not predict absolute success rates <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. In September 2026 it announced Praxis-1, a world action model built on the video pretraining behind its general world models, which it is testing with partners before an open-weight release <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-2026" />.

## Generated experience trains real policies, but contact-rich tasks stay open

Generated experience also trains policies. RISE ran RL inside a compositional world model and gained 35 to 45 absolute points on three real tasks, including dynamic brick sorting <Cite id="rise-2026" />. GRAIL used 3D assets and video-model priors to generate over 20,000 humanoid sequences, and visual policies trained only on them reached 84% success on object pick-up and 90% on stair-climbing on a real Unitree G1 <Cite id="grail-2026" />.

The survey calls visual plausibility "only a weak proxy for control utility" and says vision and proprioception miss friction, stiffness and contact stability, making tactile integration indispensable. World-model policies are also far more computationally intensive than <Term id="vision-language-action-model">VLAs</Term> in training and inference, because they jointly predict video and actions or need fine-tuning, and iterative diffusion denoising adds latency <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

By late 2026, generative video models serve policy ranking, synthetic demonstrations, RL post-training and closed-loop control in narrow task families. Contact-rich interaction, especially with small objects, remains open <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />, and in 1X's own tests dexterous tasks such as pouring still fail <Cite id="1x-world-model-2026" />. The gating property, as in the figure, is whether the predicted future listens to the action.

<span id="an-evaluation-ladder-for-video-models" />
## Test action response before trusting a video model's rankings

Start with paired interventions: identical initial frames, only the action changed, checking direction, contact onset and object identity before image quality. Then lengthen the horizon on held-out transitions until geometry, action sensitivity or consistency fails. Only then rank policies against blinded real trials, or test generated training episodes on untouched real scenes. By 1X's estimate, with a 15% real gap between two policies, a world model with 70% accuracy picks the better one 90% of the time <Cite id="1x-world-model-2025" />. The [evaluation guide](/world-models/evaluation/) gives the full protocol, [the evaluation crisis](/data-hardware/evaluation-crisis/) explains why real trials are scarce, [world models versus simulators](/world-models/world-models-vs-simulators/) covers engines and hybrids, and [JEPA](/world-models/jepa/) the non-generative alternative.
