Generative simulation uses AI models to write a simulator's content, its tasks, scenes, objects and rewards, while a physics engine, itself a hand-built <Term id="world-model">world model</Term>, still computes how things move <Cite id="robogen-2024" />. A 2026 survey finds the field shifting "from visual realism toward interaction readiness" <Cite id="3d-gen-embodied-survey-2026" />.

<span id="generate-content-inside-a-physics-engine" />
## A physics engine keeps generated content physically consistent

[Generative video](/world-models/generative-video/) learns physics from pixels, and its plausible futures can ignore the pending action; the 2026 world-model survey calls visual plausibility a weak proxy for usefulness in control <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. Generative simulation avoids that path. A solver executes the generated task, layout, object or reward, so a mug with the wrong friction may slide too far but cannot fall upward. ManiSkill3 runs physics and rendering in parallel on the GPU, even for differing environments <Cite id="maniskill3-2024" />, and Isaac Lab 3.0 (October 2026) runs one task interface across several physics and rendering back ends <Cite id="isaac-lab-3-0-2026" />.

<span id="robogen-propose-generate-learn" />
## RoboGen automates task proposal, but humans still inspect the results

RoboGen runs a propose, generate and learn cycle. Given a robot type and a sampled object, GPT-4 proposes a task, writes the scene configuration, splits the task into sub-tasks, picks a learning method for each and supplies rewards or target shapes. People still design the prompts and inspect scenes, rewards and skills, and the authors name large-scale skill verification and sim-to-real transfer as open problems <Cite id="robogen-2024" />.

GenSim used GPT-4 to grow a benchmark tenfold to over 100 tasks, and policies pretrained on them beat baselines by 25% on unseen real long-horizon tasks <Cite id="gensim-2023" />. RoboTwin 2.0 repairs generated task code with the simulator in the loop; a VLA trained on its data plus 10 real demonstrations improved 367% over using the 10 alone <Cite id="robotwin-2-2025" />. Code2Worlds-2 (October 2026) adds a simulator critic that checks penetration, stability, contact forces and joint limits before exporting a scene to MuJoCo <Cite id="code2worlds-2-2026" />.

<span id="holodeck-language-guided-environment-generation" />
## Holodeck builds rooms from prompts but does not test manipulation

Holodeck uses GPT-4 to turn a text prompt into an AI2-THOR environment. It picks Objaverse objects and proposes loose spatial relations, which a search-based solver meets as far as collisions and room boundaries allow. Its evaluation fine-tuned a navigation model in generated scenes and tested it in artist-designed rooms, so it says nothing about physically valid manipulation, and the authors report trouble with complex layouts and missing assets <Cite id="holodeck-2024" />. ProcTHOR procedurally generates interactive houses for navigation, rearrangement and arm manipulation <Cite id="procthor-2022" />.

Objects are the weak link. Objaverse supplies over 800,000 3D models without physical properties <Cite id="objaverse-2022" />. SceneSmith adds text-to-3D assets, retrieval of objects with moving parts and estimates of physical properties, placing 3 to 6 times more objects than prior methods with under 2% collisions and 96% of objects stable <Cite id="scenesmith-2026" />.

<span id="robocasa" />
## More generated demonstrations raised RoboCasa success from 26.3% to 47.6%

RoboCasa, a simulated kitchen benchmark, was built partly by generative models. Its 120 kitchens combine 10 floor plans with 12 styles and hold 2,509 objects in 153 categories, from Objaverse and Luma.ai text-to-3D. Of its 100 tasks, 75 composite ones were drafted by GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5, then filtered, edited and coded by people. MimicGen multiplies human demonstrations by adapting them to new scenes and keeping successes <Cite id="robocasa-2024" />; on its own it turned about 200 human demonstrations into over 50,000 across 18 tasks <Cite id="mimicgen-2023" />.

<Image id="robocasa-kitchens-2024" />

In a scaling study, a language-conditioned imitation policy for a Franka Panda arm on a mobile base learned 24 basic tasks from 100, 300 or 3,000 generated demonstrations each. Tested on unseen objects in five kitchens, two in unseen styles, with 50 trials per task, it succeeded 26.3%, 35.0% and 47.6% of the time, though individual tasks did not improve steadily <Cite id="robocasa-2024" />. RoboCasa365 extends the platform to 365 tasks in 2,500 kitchens, with over 600 hours of human and 1,600 hours of synthetic demonstrations <Cite id="robocasa365-2026" />.

## Generators expand a few demonstrations or one image into training data

| System | Starts from | Produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isaac Lab Mimic | As little as one demonstration | Subtask segments recombined for new object poses <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| SkillGen | 60 human demonstrations | Over 24,000, with agents 24% more successful on average <Cite id="skillmimicgen-2024" /> |
| NVIDIA, for GR00T N1 | Human demonstrations | 780,000 simulated trajectories, equal to 6,500 hours of human demonstration, in 11 hours <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" /> |

GRS turns one real color-and-depth image into a scene of matched simulation assets, then revises generated task code until a scripted policy completes the task; its reported reward average excludes runtime-error cases, and sim-to-real transfer is future work <Cite id="grs-2024" />. AutoRSR (October 2026) scans a real workspace into metric-scale simulation assets. Policies trained on 1,000 generated episodes per task reached 73.3% to 86.7% real success on three tasks, comparable to π0.5 trained on 150 teleoperated demonstrations <Cite id="autorsr-2026" />.

<span id="the-push-test" />
## Generative simulation borrows its physics from the engine

A simulated push has three layers. Appearance renders the mug, as a reconstruction, texture or video frame might. A physics proxy adds collision shape, mass and friction. The simulator then integrates the push, so the same push slides the mug the same distance every time, $d = v^2/(2\mu g)$. Cosmos-Transfer1 generates only the first layer, from segmentation, depth and edge maps <Cite id="cosmos-transfer1-2025" />, and so does a Gaussian Splatting twin, as the [taxonomy](/world-models/taxonomy/) explains <Cite id="3dgs-2023" />. Generative video tries to learn the third layer; generative simulation borrows the second and third from an engine. RoboRender changes the look of simulated video while preserving geometry, robot motion and action labels <Cite id="roborender-2026" />, and NVIDIA's November 2026 Cosmos framework can render observations from an external simulator's physical state, so the engine keeps the dynamics <Cite id="cosmos-interactive-sim-2026" />.

<span id="limitations" />
## Generated content still needs physical and human checks

A scene is only as physical as its objects. A text-to-3D mug's mass, friction and center of mass are estimates, and generated assets "often overlook rich and important physical properties" <Cite id="physx-3d-2025" />. World Labs warns that generated geometry can look correct while intersecting itself or sitting at the wrong scale <Cite id="worldlabs-taxonomy-2026" />. Calibration is partly automated: Scalable Real2Sim identifies collision geometry and inertial parameters from joint-torque sensors and a camera <Cite id="scalable-real2sim-2025" />, DrEureka builds the ranges for <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term> <Cite id="dreureka-2024" />, and Articulate-Anything raised the success of modeling objects with moving parts to 75%, from 8.7% to 12.2% for prior work <Cite id="articulate-anything-2024" />. The 3D-generation survey names "the gap between geometric quality and physical validity" a main bottleneck <Cite id="3d-gen-embodied-survey-2026" />.

Generated goals can miss what a person wants. Eureka notes that its task metric may not match the intended behavior; its authors call one generated reward "amenable to post-hoc human inspection and editing" but do not require such inspection <Cite id="eureka-2024" />. Text2Reward's generated rewards matched or beat expert rewards on 13 of 17 manipulation tasks <Cite id="text2reward-2023" />. See [reward design](/rl-sim2real/reward-design-mpc/). Diversity has sources too: Holodeck's authors document cultural biases from the language model and asset retrieval, with only qualitative evidence that new prompts help <Cite id="holodeck-2024" />, and RoboCasa's floor plans and styles came from home-design and architecture magazines <Cite id="robocasa-2024" />.

<span id="where-this-leaves-the-field" />
## Policies trained on generated variations now transfer to real robots

Learned world models hold narrow roles in [evaluation](/world-models/evaluation/) and data generation; generative models more often author tasks, scenes, assets and supervision for physics engines <Cite id="robogen-2024" /> <Cite id="holodeck-2024" /> <Cite id="robocasa-2024" />. Variety beats exactness. Policies trained on digital cousins, similar but not identical simulated scenes, reached 90% zero-shot sim-to-real success against 25% for exact digital twins <Cite id="digital-cousins-2024" />, and in 2026 SimFoundry's object, scene and task cousins raised zero-shot real success by 17%, 21% and 40% <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />.

Choosing what to simulate matters. MetaGenSim, from Georgia Tech (October 2026), learns which simulator settings produce useful co-training data for a real task. With 10 real demonstrations per task on a Franka Panda, its data gave 58 full-task successes in 80 real trials over four tasks, against 23 for uniform simulation data, 26 for real data alone and 57 for hand-tuned settings <Cite id="metagensim-2026" />.

<span id="the-hybrid-boundary" />
## Generators propose content and the engine integrates contact

A generator proposes layouts, tasks, textures and starting states. An asset pipeline checks for interpenetrating parts and attaches physical properties, as Infinigen-Articulated does for 18 categories of objects with moving parts <Cite id="infinigen-articulated-2025" />. The engine integrates contacts, a supervisor labels outcomes, and the dataset records each field's source. 3D world generators follow the same split: World Labs' Marble exports worlds as Gaussian splats and as low-fidelity collision meshes for coarse physics <Cite id="marble-2025" />. Engines also model more contact: Newton 1.0 adds signed-distance-field collision and hydroelastic contact for tight-tolerance assembly such as connector insertion <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" />.

World Labs' Atlas takes images, camera poses and depth and outputs point clouds or Gaussian splats, filling in unseen regions <Cite id="worldlabs-atlas-2026" />. Its public release on November 12, 2026 converts splats to meshes and targets robotics simulation among other uses <Cite id="worldlabs-atlas-release-2026" />.

Check asset scale and collision geometry before dynamics, static stability before rollouts, and contact parameters against real measurements before claiming transfer. Steerable scene generation enforces feasibility "via projection and simulation" and releases over 44 million scenes <Cite id="steerable-scene-generation-2025" />. Keep failed scenes as adversarial tests. The [simulator comparison](/world-models/world-models-vs-simulators/) gives the selection rule.
