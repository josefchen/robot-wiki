Generative simulation refers to using AI models to generate components of a simulator: scenes, tasks, objects and rewards. Meanwhile, a physics engine (which is also a hand-built <Term id="world-model">world model</Term>) is still used to simulate the dynamics <Cite id="robogen-2024" />. According to a 2026 survey, generative simulation is on its way "from visual realism toward interaction readiness" <Cite id="3d-gen-embodied-survey-2026" />.

<Image id="robocasa-object-library-2024" />

Realism is not a good proxy for physical accuracy: on Physics-IQ, physical understanding in video generators was "unrelated to visual realism" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.

<span id="generate-content-inside-a-physics-engine" />
## A physics engine keeps generated content physically consistent

The generated tasks, layouts, objects and rewards are executed by a physics engine: a generated mug with incorrect friction might slide more than it should, but it cannot fall up. [Generative video](/world-models/generative-video/) models learn physical dynamics from visual observations instead, and a 2026 survey argues that visual plausibility is a weak proxy for control <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. ManiSkill3 runs physics and rendering in parallel on the GPU <Cite id="maniskill3-2024" />, and Isaac Lab 3.0 runs one task interface across several physics back ends <Cite id="isaac-lab-3-0-2026" />.

<span id="robogen-propose-generate-learn" />
## RoboGen automates task proposal, but humans still inspect the results

RoboGen leverages GPT-4 to propose a task given a robot and a sampled object, generate a scene description, decompose the task into sub-tasks, and devise reward functions. Human-written prompts are still required in RoboGen, and humans are needed to evaluate the generated tasks <Cite id="robogen-2024" />.

| System | Method | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GenSim | GPT-4 grows a benchmark tenfold, to 100+ tasks | Beat baselines by 25% on unseen real long-horizon tasks <Cite id="gensim-2023" /> |
| RoboTwin 2.0 | Repairs task code with the simulator in the loop | 367% better than 10 real demonstrations alone <Cite id="robotwin-2-2025" /> |
| Code2Worlds-2 (Oct 2026) | A critic checks penetration, stability, forces and joint limits | Scenes exported to MuJoCo <Cite id="code2worlds-2-2026" /> |

<span id="holodeck-language-guided-environment-generation" />
## Holodeck builds rooms from prompts but does not test manipulation

Holodeck is a system to automatically generate AI2-THOR scenes from natural language descriptions using objects from Objaverse and a constraint solver to place objects in the scene while accounting for collisions and the boundaries of the room. Its tests used navigation only, so it says nothing about valid manipulation <Cite id="holodeck-2024" />. ProcTHOR is a data generator that procedurally generates interactive houses for navigation, rearrangement and arm manipulation tasks <Cite id="procthor-2022" />.

Objects are the weak link. Objaverse consists of more than 800,000 3D models that do not have physical properties <Cite id="objaverse-2022" />. SceneSmith estimates physical properties and enables placing 3 to 6 times more objects than previous approaches while having less than 2% of the objects in collision and 96% of objects being stable <Cite id="scenesmith-2026" />.

<span id="robocasa" />
## More generated demonstrations raised RoboCasa success from 26.3% to 47.6%

In RoboCasa, GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5 generated 75 out of 100 tasks (before human refinement) in its benchmark with 2,509 objects in 120 simulated kitchens. MimicGen amplifies demonstrations by transforming them to fit different scenes and retaining successful examples <Cite id="robocasa-2024" />; MimicGen alone expanded a dataset from about 200 human demos to over 50,000 <Cite id="mimicgen-2023" />.

<Image id="robocasa-kitchens-2024" />

Scaling the number of generated demonstrations per task for a policy on the Franka Panda robot to 100, 300 or 3,000 leads to success rates on unseen objects of 26.3%, 35.0% and 47.6% respectively on 24 basic tasks <Cite id="robocasa-2024" />. RoboCasa365 contains more than 600 hours of human demonstration data and 1,600 hours of synthetic demonstration data across 365 tasks in 2,500 kitchen environments <Cite id="robocasa365-2026" />.

## Generators expand a few demonstrations or one image into training data

| System | Starts from | Produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isaac Lab Mimic | As little as one demonstration | Subtask segments recombined for new object poses <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| SkillGen | 60 human demonstrations | Over 24,000, with agents 24% more successful on average <Cite id="skillmimicgen-2024" /> |
| NVIDIA, for GR00T N1 | Human demonstrations | 780,000 simulated trajectories, equal to 6,500 hours of human demonstration, in 11 hours <Cite id="gr00t-n1-2025" /> |
| GRS | One real color-and-depth image | A matched simulation scene, with task code revised until a scripted policy succeeds <Cite id="grs-2024" /> |
| AutoRSR (Oct 2026) | A scanned real workspace | 1,000 episodes per task; 73.3% to 86.7% real success on three tasks, comparable to π0.5 on 150 teleoperated demonstrations <Cite id="autorsr-2026" /> |

<span id="the-push-test" />
## Generative simulation borrows its physics from the engine

A simulated push has three layers: a visual appearance, a simplified model used to compute physics (shape, mass and friction), and an integrator to update the state (sliding a mug will always result in a distance of $d = v^2/(2\mu g)$). Cosmos-Transfer1 only generates the first layer given segmentation, depth and edge maps <Cite id="cosmos-transfer1-2025" />, as does a [Gaussian Splatting twin](/world-models/taxonomy/) <Cite id="3dgs-2023" />. Generative video tries to learn the third layer; generative simulation borrows the second and third from an engine.

RoboRender re-renders videos from simulation while preserving geometry, robot motion as well as action labels <Cite id="roborender-2026" />. NVIDIA's November 2026 Cosmos framework renders from an external simulator's physical state, so the engine keeps the dynamics <Cite id="cosmos-interactive-sim-2026" />.

<span id="limitations" />
## Generated content still needs physical and human checks

A scene is only as physical as its objects. Generated assets "often overlook rich and important physical properties" <Cite id="physx-3d-2025" />. Even if the generated geometry looks right, it might intersect itself or have an inappropriate scale <Cite id="worldlabs-taxonomy-2026" />.

| System | Automates | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scalable Real2Sim | Collision geometry and inertia | Identified from joint-torque sensors and a camera <Cite id="scalable-real2sim-2025" /> |
| DrEureka | Ranges for <Term id="domain-randomization">domain randomization</Term> | Built automatically <Cite id="dreureka-2024" /> |
| Articulate-Anything | Objects with moving parts | 75% success, against 8.7% to 12.2% for prior work <Cite id="articulate-anything-2024" /> |

Generated goals can miss what a person wants. Eureka's task metric may not match the intended behavior, and its authors do not require validating the generated reward function with human inspection <Cite id="eureka-2024" />. The reward functions generated in Text2Reward perform as well as or better than rewards designed by human experts in 13 of 17 manipulation tasks <Cite id="text2reward-2023" />; see [reward design](/rl-sim2real/reward-design-mpc/).

<span id="where-this-leaves-the-field" />
## Policies trained on generated variations now transfer to real robots

Variety beats exactness: zero-shot sim-to-real success rates were 90% for policies learned on environments that are cousins (similar, but not exact replicas) of their real-world counterpart, as opposed to 25% for exact digital twins <Cite id="digital-cousins-2024" />. In SimFoundry, learning on cousin objects, scenes and tasks leads to 17%, 21% and 40% improvement in zero-shot real-world success rate, respectively <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />.

MetaGenSim learns a set of simulator parameters that generate useful co-training data. With 10 real demonstrations per task, its data gave 58 successes in 80 real trials <Cite id="metagensim-2026" />. Real data alone gave 26, uniform simulation 23 and hand-tuned settings 57.

<span id="the-hybrid-boundary" />
## Generators propose content and the engine integrates contact

A generator proposes object layouts, tasks, and initial states; an asset pipeline annotates objects with physical properties; and the simulation engine handles contact between objects. Infinigen-Articulated checks interpenetration for articulated objects spanning 18 categories <Cite id="infinigen-articulated-2025" />. Newton 1.0 introduces hydroelastic contact, which is important for tightly fitting assemblies such as plugging in a connector <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" />.

World Labs follows the same pattern, with its Marble product generating worlds that it exports as Gaussian splats complemented by low-fidelity meshes for collision detection <Cite id="marble-2025" />. Atlas can generate splats or point clouds from images (with camera poses) and depth maps <Cite id="worldlabs-atlas-2026" />, and a new feature (as of November 12, 2026) can convert splats to meshes to enable simulation <Cite id="worldlabs-atlas-release-2026" />.

Before doing dynamics, check the scale and collision geometry. Before rollouts, check stability. And before claiming transfer, check contact against real measurements. Steerable scene generation enforces feasibility of the generated scenes "via projection and simulation", and 44 million of these scenes have been released <Cite id="steerable-scene-generation-2025" />. The [simulator comparison](/world-models/world-models-vs-simulators/) gives the selection rule.
