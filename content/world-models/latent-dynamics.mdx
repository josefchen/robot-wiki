A <Term id="latent-dynamics">latent-dynamics</Term> world model compresses what a robot senses into a short list of numbers, its latent state, and learns how that state and the reward change under each action. The agent then practices or plans inside that imagination <Cite id="dreamer-2019" />.

<Image id="tdmpc2-task-grid-2023" />

It is one of six groups in the [taxonomy](/world-models/taxonomy/). Imagining is cheap, but each step drifts further from reality, and the system's own dynamics set how fast that error compounds <Cite id="compounding-error-2022" />.

<span id="the-rssm-a-state-you-can-dream-in" />
## Agents trained inside a recurrent latent model reached human-level Atari

DreamerV3's Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) pairs a memory of the past with a random state for what cannot be predicted. It learns to reconstruct its inputs and to predict the reward and whether the episode continues <Cite id="dreamerv3-2023" />.

Dreamer's actor and critic train purely on imagined rollouts started from real states <Cite id="dreamer-2019" />. DreamerV2 was the first agent to reach human level on the 55-task Atari benchmark by learning inside a world model <Cite id="dreamerv2-2020" />.

Also: training inside a dream <Cite id="ha-world-models-2018" />, PlaNet <Cite id="planet-2018" />, Embed to Control <Cite id="e2c-2015" />, <Term id="transformer">transformer</Term> cores in IRIS <Cite id="iris-world-model-2022" /> and TransDreamer <Cite id="transdreamer-2022" />.

## DreamerV3 ran 150 tasks on one configuration

DreamerV3 ran more than 150 tasks across eight domains with fixed hyperparameters <Cite id="dreamerv3-2023" />. Its 2025 Nature paper reports the first Minecraft diamonds collected from scratch without human data or curricula <Cite id="dreamerv3-nature-2025" />. The game was simplified, with faster block breaking and 12 milestone rewards <Cite id="dreamerv3-2023" />. Dreamer 4 then got diamonds purely from offline data, with a world model that runs in real time <Cite id="dreamer4-2025" />.

<span id="td-mpc-and-td-mpc2-drop-the-decoder-plan-at-every-step" />
## TD-MPC2 drops the decoder and plans at every step

Reconstructing pixels spends effort on details the task does not need. MuZero planned with a model that predicts only reward, policy and value <Cite id="muzero-2019" />, and value-equivalence theory backs that choice <Cite id="value-equivalence-2020" />. TD-MPC predicts reward, value and the next latent state, and at every step scores short sampled action sequences with the model plus a learned value <Cite id="tdmpc-2022" />.

TD-MPC2 adds candidates from a learned policy and starts each search from the last solution. One 317-million-parameter agent learned 80 tasks offline from the data of 240 single-task agents, and scores rose across five model sizes <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />.

| Model | Instead of a pixel decoder |
| --- | --- |
| MuDreamer | Predicts value and action <Cite id="mudreamer-2024" /> |
| Masked world models | Learns from a separately trained masked autoencoder <Cite id="masked-world-models-2022" /> |
| DreamerPro | Learns prototypes that ignore distracting backgrounds <Cite id="dreamerpro-2021" /> |
| world2vec (Oct 2026) | Long-horizon dynamics with at least 10x fewer parameters than DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 or LeWM <Cite id="world2vec-2026" /> |

<span id="daydreamer-imagination-on-physical-hardware" />
## DayDreamer taught a real quadruped to walk in about an hour

DayDreamer, built on DreamerV2, learned online on four real robots with no simulator. In about an hour a Unitree A1 learned to roll off its back, stand and walk, while model-free SAC only learned to roll over <Cite id="daydreamer-2022" />. Dreamer's lead author, Danijar Hafner, has left Google DeepMind for a stealth humanoid startup built on model-based RL <Cite id="mittr-hafner-2026" />.

<span id="the-horizon-problem" />
## Latent models imagine only 3 to 16 steps ahead

Errors compound, so latent models look briefly ahead. DreamerV3 and DayDreamer imagine 15 or 16 steps <Cite id="dreamerv3-2023" /> <Cite id="dreamerv3-nature-2025" /> <Cite id="daydreamer-2022" />, and TD-MPC2 plans 3 and lets its value estimate cover the rest <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />.

Hierarchy stretches the reach:

| Method | Structure | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HWM | World models at several time scales | Real Franka pick-and-place from 0% to 70% <Cite id="hwm-2026" /> |
| Puppeteer | A world-model agent commands a low-level controller | Simulated humanoid <Cite id="puppeteer-2024" /> |
| VQ-WM | Plans over discrete states a feedback controller reaches | Physical Tower of Hanoi solved in every trial <Cite id="vq-wm-2026" /> |

Scale does not remove the errors: a 350-million-parameter model trained on 427 hours across 210 tasks still hallucinated <Cite id="wm-hallucination-2026" />.

Also: short branched rollouts in MBPO <Cite id="mbpo-2019" />, per-example horizons in STEVE <Cite id="steve-2018" />.

<span id="successors-and-open-limits" />
## Simulation-trained world models now transfer to real legged robots

A world model can learn from a physics simulator and then replace it. Policies trained inside Robotic World Model (RWM), which feeds its own predictions back in, transferred zero-shot to ANYmal D and Unitree G1 robots, even under pushes <Cite id="robotic-world-model-2025" />.

| Method | How it learns | Real result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RWM-U | Offline datasets only, with uncertainty estimates | A quadruped and a humanoid <Cite id="rwm-u-2025" /> |
| SimDist | Pretrains in simulation, updates only the latent dynamics on hardware | Kept improving where prior methods stalled <Cite id="simdist-2026" /> |
| SuperMimic (Oct 2026) | One differentiable model fit to logged humanoid simulator data | In 3 hours on 1 GPU beat a 7-day, 128-GPU tracking baseline; ran on a Unitree G1 <Cite id="supermimic-2026" /> |
| DiffDreamer | A learned model of the surroundings plus a differentiable drone simulator | Proof-of-concept real flights <Cite id="diffdreamer-2026" /> |

## Refinements to latent planning bring uneven gains

Dream-MPC refines proposed action sequences along the gradient of predicted return and penalizes uncertainty. Swapped into pretrained BMPC agents, it raised the interquartile mean over 24 simulated tasks, including HumanoidBench <Cite id="humanoidbench-2024" />, by 26.7%. In TD-MPC2 it did not consistently match the original planner <Cite id="dream-mpc-2026" />.

| Method | Change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BMPC | Learns its policy by imitating the planner | Base for Dream-MPC's gain <Cite id="bmpc-2025" /> |
| TD-M(PC)² | Policy constraint against "persistent value overestimation" | Largest gains on humanoids with 61 <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term> <Cite id="td-m-pc2-2025" /> |
| Σ-LeWM (Nov 2026) | Decoder-free, two extra prediction losses | Faster planning on two simulated manipulation tasks <Cite id="sigma-lewm-2026" /> |
| Fast-WAM | Video world-action model that skips imagination at run time | Real towel-folding latency from 810 to 190 ms <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" /> |

## TD-MPC2's authors expect broad generalization to need far more tasks

TD-MPC2's authors expect broad generalization "will likely require several orders of magnitude more tasks" <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />. The same group's Newt trained one language-conditioned world model across 200 tasks <Cite id="newt-2025" />. In simulation, one model trained on up to 90 insertion tasks reached 56% zero-shot success on unseen parts, against 7% model-free <Cite id="insertion-world-models-2026" />.

<span id="pick-the-models-role-before-its-architecture" />
## Imagination tolerates slow models; online planning needs predictable latency

Training in imagination, as in Dreamer, tolerates a slow model but can bias the policy without warning. Online planning, as in TD-MPC2, needs predictable latency and a fallback. A latent model can also just supply a representation: one simulation-trained model predicted real legged trajectories well enough to inform a separate controller <Cite id="wmp-2024" />.
