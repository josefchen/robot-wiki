World-model evaluation is the testing of a robotics <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> against the decisions a robot must make, from one-step action responses to agreement with real policy rankings. Image metrics cannot establish that: on Physics-IQ, physical understanding in video generators was "unrelated to visual realism" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.

<Image id="simpler-real-vs-sim-2024" />

The central failure is action indifference: smooth, photorealistic futures that ignore the robot's action. A 2026 survey of robot world models rates controllability above visual plausibility <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />, and the closed-loop World-in-World benchmark found it mattered more than visual quality for task success <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />.

<span id="start-with-the-intended-use" />
## Perception scores miss function, so each use needs its own test

WorldArena scores 14 models separately as data engines, policy evaluators and action planners, and reports a "significant perception-functionality gap" <Cite id="worldarena-2026" />. NVIDIA reports its DreamGen Bench video scores tracking downstream policy success <Cite id="dreamgen-2025" />. 1X saw unchanged policy weights degrade within days after subtle lighting or background changes <Cite id="1x-world-model-2024" />.

<span id="level-1-one-step-action-sensitivity" />
## Level 1: open models often fail to follow a changed action

Build matched counterfactuals: fix the observation, noise, prompt and history, and change one action dimension. WorldEval found raw action inputs often yield videos that ignore the action <Cite id="worldeval-2025" />, and IRASim and Ctrl-World add frame-level action conditioning <Cite id="irasim-2024" /> <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />. Across six open action-conditioned world models and more than 18,000 test instances, WorldSimProbe found systematic degradation in how actions are realized <Cite id="worldsimprobe-2026" />.

<span id="level-2-temporal-and-physical-consistency" />
## Level 2: pooled video metrics hide physical errors

Roll out autoregressively, score task events such as grasp, slip and release, and report error per horizon. Fréchet Video Distance "increases only slightly with large temporal corruption" <Cite id="fvd-content-bias-2024" />. WorldModelBench uses 67K human labels to catch violations such as objects changing size <Cite id="worldmodelbench-2025" />, and video models trained on simple physics failed out of distribution, copying the closest training example <Cite id="video-physical-law-2024" />.

<Image id="roboworld-bair-forcing-2026" />

<span id="level-3-task-progress-scoring" />
## Level 3: the progress scorer needs its own test

Many evaluators pair a video generator with a vision-language scorer, as WorldGym does, so each half needs its own test <Cite id="worldgym-2025" />. GVL fixes poor naive VLM value prediction by ordering shuffled frames, scoring more than 300 real tasks <Cite id="gvl-2024" />. Runway and DreamDojo used human graders instead, Runway collecting over 16,000 ratings <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" /> <Cite id="dreamdojo-2026" />.

<span id="level-4-policy-ranking" />
## Level 4: the best simulators order policies as real trials do

A policy evaluator must reproduce reality's ordering, measured as Pearson correlation and Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. Real comparisons often rest on 10 to 50 trials, and adding trials after peeking risks p-hacking; the STEP sequential test cuts trials by up to 32% <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />.

<Image id="roboarena-evaluation-2025" />

Google DeepMind reports its Veo simulator ranked eight Gemini Robotics checkpoints against more than 1,600 real evaluations at MMRV 0.03, though its absolute success predictions ran low <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. WorldGym reached r = 0.78 <Cite id="worldgym-2025" />, and NVIDIA reports r = 0.995 for DreamDojo <Cite id="dreamdojo-2026" />. Runway reports Pearson 0.95 and MMRV 0.033 over eight RoboArena policies, testing ranking only <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. PolaRiS found that Ctrl-World "produces heavy hallucinations during object interaction", mis-ranking policies at MMRV 0.22 <Cite id="polaris-2025" />.

Physics simulation sets the bar. In June 2026, Yuke Zhu and colleagues reported that SimFoundry, which rebuilds a real scene in simulation from video, predicted real results across 7 tasks and 5 policy architectures at mean Pearson 0.911 and MMRV 0.018 <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />. PolaRiS reports r = 0.9 against real trials <Cite id="polaris-2025" />, and X2Real 0.84 <Cite id="x2real-2026" />.

<span id="level-5-closed-loop-usefulness" />
## Level 5: planning and fine-tuning in a world model improved outcomes

IRASim planning raised Push-T IoU from 0.637 to 0.961 <Cite id="irasim-2024" />, and fine-tuning on Ctrl-World's imagined successes raised policy success by 44.7% <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />. Post-training on action-observation data helped more than a stronger pretrained video generator <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />.

SIMPLER's test, whether simulated and real policy performance correlate under matched visual and physical conditions, applies to learned simulators too <Cite id="simpler-2024" />. RoboArena widens real evaluation beyond one laboratory <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />.

<span id="control-the-evaluation-shifts" />
## Saturated benchmarks hide robustness gaps

Use a factorial matrix to expose shifts one "unseen" split could conceal. RoboLab-120 sorts 120 tasks into visual, procedural and relational axes and measures sensitivity to controlled perturbations <Cite id="robolab-2026" />:

LIBERO-Plus used such perturbations to uncover gaps a saturated benchmark hid <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />, and PolaRiS found nearly all tested policies scoring 90 to 95% on LIBERO, poorly correlated with real performance <Cite id="polaris-2025" />.

<span id="leakage-and-circularity" />
## Disjoint data and blind trials guard against leakage

Foundation models can see the benchmark in pretraining video, and scorers can share a backbone with the policy. Ctrl-World trains on DROID <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />, which spans 564 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and Runway's RoboArena ranking put pi05_droid first <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. X2Real enforces "strictly disjoint training-evaluation pipelines" <Cite id="x2real-2026" />, and TRI compared policies in blind, randomized trials <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

<span id="the-minimum-credible-report" />
## A credible report pins the benchmark version and the real anchor

A credible report states the use case, action representation, horizon and real anchor, and separates generator from scorer error. It names the benchmark version: Physics-IQ Verified refined 57.6% of samples and shifted rankings at Kendall's τ = 0.46 <Cite id="physics-iq-verified-2026" />. By 1X's arithmetic, with a 15% real gap between two policies, a 70%-accurate world model picks the better one 90% of the time <Cite id="1x-world-model-2025" />.
