World-model evaluation is the testing of a robotics <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> against the decisions a robot must make, from one-step action responses to agreement with real policy rankings. Image metrics diagnose rendering and cannot establish control utility. On Physics-IQ, physical understanding in video generators was "unrelated to visual realism" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.

The central failure is action indifference: smooth, photorealistic futures that ignore the robot's action. The 2026 survey of robot world models rates controllability and action-grounded consistency above visual plausibility <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. The closed-loop World-in-World benchmark found controllability mattered more than visual quality for task success <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />. Try the self-check on ranking robot policies.

<span id="start-with-the-intended-use" />
## Perception scores miss function, so each use needs its own test

Planning, policy training, policy evaluation and data generation each need their own endpoint. WorldArena scores 14 models separately as data engines, policy evaluators and action planners and reports a "significant perception-functionality gap" <Cite id="worldarena-2026" />. NVIDIA reports its DreamGen Bench video scores tracking downstream policy success <Cite id="dreamgen-2025" />. 1X reports unchanged policy weights degrading within days after subtle lighting or background changes <Cite id="1x-world-model-2024" />.

<span id="level-1-one-step-action-sensitivity" />
## Level 1: open models often fail to follow a changed action

Build matched counterfactuals: fix the observation, noise, prompt and history, and change one action dimension. From one initial state, 1X's model predicts four distinct NEO futures for four action sequences <Cite id="1x-world-model-2025" />. WorldEval found raw action inputs often yield videos that ignore the action <Cite id="worldeval-2025" />, and IRASim and Ctrl-World add frame-level action conditioning <Cite id="irasim-2024" /> <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />. WorldSimProbe turns this into a test suite: supplied actions must produce matching agent motion and grounded environment responses. Across six open action-conditioned world models and more than 18,000 instances from RoboTwin, ManiSkill and LIBERO, it found systematic degradation in how actions are realized <Cite id="worldsimprobe-2026" />.

<span id="level-2-temporal-and-physical-consistency" />
## Level 2: pooled video metrics hide physical errors

Roll out autoregressively, score task events such as grasp, slip and release, and report error per horizon. Pooled metrics hide failures: Fréchet Video Distance "increases only slightly with large temporal corruption" <Cite id="fvd-content-bias-2024" />. WorldModelBench uses 67K human labels to catch violations such as objects changing size <Cite id="worldmodelbench-2025" />. Video models trained on simple physics failed out of distribution, copying the closest training example <Cite id="video-physical-law-2024" />.

DeepMind reports real-time Genie 3 generation at 720p and 24 frames per second, with a constrained direct action space and consistency lasting minutes <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />.

<span id="level-3-task-progress-scoring" />
## Level 3: the progress scorer needs its own test

Many evaluators pair a video generator with a vision-language scorer, as WorldGym does, so each half needs its own test <Cite id="worldgym-2025" />. GVL fixes poor naive VLM value prediction by ordering shuffled frames, scoring more than 300 real tasks <Cite id="gvl-2024" />. Runway and DreamDojo used human graders instead, Runway collecting over 16,000 ratings <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" /> <Cite id="dreamdojo-2026" />. Interactive World Simulator is built as an action-controllable environment for policy training and evaluation <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.

<span id="level-4-policy-ranking" />
## Level 4: the best simulators order policies as real trials do

A policy evaluator must reproduce reality's ordering, measured as Pearson correlation and Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. Real comparisons often rest on 10 to 50 trials, and adding trials after peeking risks p-hacking; the STEP sequential test cuts trials by up to 32% <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />.

Google DeepMind reports its Veo simulator ranked eight Gemini Robotics checkpoints against more than 1,600 real evaluations at MMRV 0.03, though its absolute success predictions ran low <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. The same system red-teams policies, generating scenes that expose behavior violating physical or semantic safety constraints <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. WorldGym reached r = 0.78 <Cite id="worldgym-2025" />, WorldEval a mean Pearson of 0.942 against 0.411 for a real-to-sim baseline <Cite id="worldeval-2025" />, and NVIDIA reports r = 0.995 for DreamDojo <Cite id="dreamdojo-2026" />. Runway reports Pearson 0.95 and MMRV 0.033 over eight RoboArena policies, testing ranking only <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />.

RoboWorld, a fast autoregressive neural simulator with task-progress-aware scoring, compares its rankings with real policy evaluation <Cite id="roboworld-2026" />. PolaRiS found that Ctrl-World "produces heavy hallucinations during object interaction", mis-ranking policies at MMRV 0.22 <Cite id="polaris-2025" />.

Physics simulation sets the bar. In June 2026, Yuke Zhu and colleagues reported that SimFoundry, which rebuilds a real scene in simulation from video, predicted real results across 7 tasks and 5 policy architectures at mean Pearson 0.911 and MMRV 0.018 <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" />. PolaRiS reports r = 0.9 against real trials <Cite id="polaris-2025" />, and X2Real 0.84 <Cite id="x2real-2026" />.

<span id="level-5-closed-loop-usefulness" />
## Level 5: planning and fine-tuning in a world model improved outcomes

The final test is whether the model changes a real outcome. IRASim planning raised Push-T IoU from 0.637 to 0.961 <Cite id="irasim-2024" />, and fine-tuning on Ctrl-World's imagined successes raised policy success by 44.7% <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />. Post-training on action-observation data helped more than a stronger pretrained video generator <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />.

SIMPLER tests whether simulated policy performance correlates with real performance under matched visual and physical conditions, a standard for learned simulators too <Cite id="simpler-2024" />. RoboArena gathers real evaluations across sites, widening the evidence beyond one laboratory <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />.

LIBERO-Plus showed that controlled perturbations uncover robustness gaps hidden by a saturated benchmark <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />. PolaRiS found nearly all tested policies scoring 90 to 95% on LIBERO, poorly correlated with real performance <Cite id="polaris-2025" />.

<span id="leakage-and-circularity" />
## Disjoint data and blind trials guard against leakage

Foundation models can see the benchmark in pretraining video, and scorers can share a backbone with the policy. Ctrl-World trains on DROID <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" />, a dataset of 564 scenes <Cite id="droid-2024" />, and Runway's RoboArena ranking put pi05_droid first <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. X2Real enforces "strictly disjoint training-evaluation pipelines" <Cite id="x2real-2026" />, and TRI compared policies in blind, randomized trials <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />.

<span id="the-minimum-credible-report" />
## A credible report pins the benchmark version and the real anchor

A credible report states the use case, action representation, horizon, sampling, real anchor and failure categories, and separates generator from scorer error. It names the benchmark version: Physics-IQ Verified refined 57.6% of samples and shifted rankings at Kendall's τ = 0.46 <Cite id="physics-iq-verified-2026" />. By 1X's arithmetic, with a 15% real gap between two policies, a 70%-accurate world model picks the better one 90% of the time <Cite id="1x-world-model-2025" />.
