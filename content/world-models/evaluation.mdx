<span id="start-with-the-intended-use" />
## Perception scores miss function, so each use needs its own test

The central pitfall is action indifference: predicted futures look realistic while disregarding the influence of the action. In closed-loop task completion, controllability was more important than visual quality <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" />, and it was rated more important than plausibility in a 2026 survey <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

WorldArena evaluated 14 world models as data engines, evaluators and planners, and found a "significant perception-functionality gap" <Cite id="worldarena-2026" />. 1X saw their policy, with the weights unchanged, regress within days after subtle changes in lighting <Cite id="1x-world-model-2024" />.

Also: DreamGen Bench, whose scores correlate with policy success <Cite id="dreamgen-2025" />.

<span id="level-1-one-step-action-sensitivity" />
## Level 1: open models often fail to follow a changed action

A sanity check: vary one action dimension while holding the others constant and see if predictions change accordingly. Across 18,000+ test cases, WorldSimProbe found actions systematically under-realized in predictions of six open world models <Cite id="worldsimprobe-2026" />.

Also: WorldEval <Cite id="worldeval-2025" />, and WorldEcho for actions not covered by demonstrations <Cite id="worldecho-2026" />.

<span id="level-2-temporal-and-physical-consistency" />
## Level 2: pooled video metrics hide physical errors

Score grasp and slip events within each future horizon, rolling the model out with its own predictions. Fréchet Video Distance "increases only slightly with large temporal corruption" <Cite id="fvd-content-bias-2024" />.

| Test | Method | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WorldModelBench | 67,000 human labels | Catches objects resizing <Cite id="worldmodelbench-2025" /> |
| Physical-law study | Models trained on simple physics | Copy the closest training example out of distribution <Cite id="video-physical-law-2024" /> |
| WMEval (Oct 2026) | Same actions in Unity and the model | Error against true state <Cite id="wmeval-2026" /> |

<span id="level-3-task-progress-scoring" />
## Level 3: the progress scorer needs its own test

WorldGym couples a video generator with a progress assessor based on a vision-language model; test each half separately <Cite id="worldgym-2025" />. GVL sharpens such scores by ordering shuffled frames, over 300+ real tasks <Cite id="gvl-2024" />. Both DreamDojo <Cite id="dreamdojo-2026" /> and Runway <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" /> opted for human evaluation.

<span id="level-4-policy-ranking" />
## Level 4: the best simulators order policies as real trials do

Agreement is evaluated using Pearson r (1 implies perfect agreement) and Mean Maximum Rank Violation, MMRV (lower values are better) <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" />. The STEP sequential test cuts the 10 to 50 real trials a comparison needs by up to 32% <Cite id="optimal-stopping-2025" />.

<Image id="roboarena-evaluation-2025" />

| Evaluator | Kind | Agreement with real trials |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Veo, 8 Gemini Robotics checkpoints | World model | MMRV 0.03 on 80 pairs of scenes and instructions in nominal conditions. Veo underpredicted absolute success <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" /> |
| WorldGym | World model | r = 0.78 <Cite id="worldgym-2025" /> |
| DreamDojo | World model | r = 0.995, NVIDIA's figure <Cite id="dreamdojo-2026" /> |
| Ctrl-World, tested by PolaRiS | World model | MMRV 0.22, "heavy hallucinations" in contact <Cite id="polaris-2025" /> |
| PhysCanvas, with touch | World model | r from 0.602 to 0.885 on 4 tasks after reward post-training <Cite id="physcanvas-2026" /> |
| SimFoundry, scenes rebuilt from video | Physics simulator | r = 0.911, MMRV 0.018, 7 tasks, 5 policy types <Cite id="simfoundry-2026" /> |
| PolaRiS | Physics simulator | r = 0.9 <Cite id="polaris-2025" /> |
| X2Real | Physics simulator | r = 0.84 <Cite id="x2real-2026" /> |

Even when they can correctly rank policies, simulators may fail to accurately predict absolute returns. For example, on LIBERO-Long, all three video models have an absolute relative error of over 50% when estimating the return, while RoboFQE, which predicts returns based on previously recorded trajectories, has an error less than 6% <Cite id="robofqe-2026" />.

Physics simulation sets the bar but has artifacts. For example, objects ejected in the RoboTwin simulation with one choice of collision constant, and tuning this constant improved policy success rate on tasks involving containers from 89% to 98% <Cite id="simuguard-2026" />. WorldFailBench shows that seven world models are largely unable to replicate when and why policies fail <Cite id="worldfailbench-2026" />.

<span id="level-5-closed-loop-usefulness" />
## Level 5: planning and fine-tuning in a world model improved outcomes

The last test puts the model in the loop and measures the real robot.

| Use | Model | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planning | IRASim | Push-T goal overlap from 0.637 to 0.961 <Cite id="irasim-2024" /> |
| Fine-tuning on imagined successes | Ctrl-World | Policy success up 44.7% <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" /> |
| Learning in video rollouts | WEVO | On three physical G1 tasks, success averaged 23.3% before reinforcement learning, 75.0% after training in the video model and 48.3% after training on the robot. Each policy ran 20 trials per task <Cite id="wevo-2026" /> |
| Action-observation post-training | World-in-World | Beat a stronger pretrained generator <Cite id="world-in-world-2025" /> |

Picking the best action after seeing how every candidate turned out credits the policy for lucky outcomes. On LIBERO-Plus, researchers reran all actions proposed by Cosmos Policy 17 times and estimated that the selection explained only 24% to 52% of the apparent improvement <Cite id="wam-best-of-n-2026" />.

Also: SIMPLER's sim-real correlation test <Cite id="simpler-2024" />, RoboArena's multi-lab real evaluation <Cite id="roboarena-2025" />.

<span id="control-the-evaluation-shifts" />
## Saturated benchmarks hide robustness gaps

Vary one factor at a time; one "unseen" split hides what breaks a policy. Nearly all policies evaluated by PolaRiS exhibit performance ranging from 90% to 95% on LIBERO, yet there is little correlation between this metric and real performance <Cite id="polaris-2025" />. Perturbations introduced in LIBERO-Plus exposed the gaps <Cite id="libero-plus-2025" />.

<EvalShiftTable className="my-6" />

RoboLab-120 introduces visual, procedural and relational perturbations on 120 tasks <Cite id="robolab-2026" />, and the best-performing world-action model achieves success in 46.2% of the 1,200 episodes, per Runway <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" />.

<span id="leakage-and-circularity" />
## Disjoint data and blind trials guard against leakage

World models can be pretrained on video from benchmark environments. Ctrl-World is trained on DROID data (564 scenes) <Cite id="ctrl-world-2025" /> <Cite id="droid-2024" />, the same dataset that the #1 policy in Runway's RoboArena ranking <Cite id="runway-gwm-robotics-eval-2026" /> was trained on. TRI ran blind, randomized trials <Cite id="tri-lbm-2025" />, and X2Real keeps "strictly disjoint training-evaluation pipelines" <Cite id="x2real-2026" />.

<span id="the-minimum-credible-report" />
## A credible report pins the benchmark version and the real anchor

Report the use case, and specify the action format, horizon, real anchor and benchmark version. Physics-IQ Verified refined 57.6% of samples, and its rankings correlate only 0.46 with the original <Cite id="physics-iq-verified-2026" />. 1X calculates that if a world model is 70% accurate, it can choose the better of two policies (15% different) 90% of the time <Cite id="1x-world-model-2025" />.
