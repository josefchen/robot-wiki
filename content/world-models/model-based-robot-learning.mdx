Model-based robot learning teaches a robot how the world responds to its actions, then uses that model to plan, to practice in imagination, or both <Cite id="mbrl-survey-2020" />. It saves real robot time, which is slow, costly and sometimes unsafe.

<Image id="daydreamer-a1-rollover-2022" />

The loop predicts what candidate actions would do, scores the outcomes, acts, observes and updates the model, as Richard Sutton proposed in 1990 <Cite id="dyna-1990" />.

<span id="the-model-based-decomposition" />
## Model-based means predicting the effect of an action

A 2026 survey defines a world model as "an action-conditioned predictive system" <Cite id="manip-wm-survey-2026" />. Classical model-predictive control (MPC) takes it from explicit physics such as MuJoCo <Cite id="mujoco-2012" />; model-based learning fits some or all of it to data.

A <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> need not predict pixels. NTT's November 2026 model adds a physics module to a video generator, and screening actions with it raised a small robot's shelf-loading success from 73.1% to 92.3% <Cite id="ntt-physics-wm-2026" />.

Also: value-equivalent models <Cite id="value-equivalence-2020" />, PILCO's probabilistic dynamics <Cite id="pilco-2015" />.

<span id="two-ways-to-use-imagined-experience" />
## A model can plan each step or train a policy in imagination

A planner rolls sampled action sequences through the model, executes the best first action and replans. New goals need no retraining, at the price of many predictions per real step ([reward design and MPC](/rl-sim2real/reward-design-mpc/)). A policy can instead practice in imagination or learn from gradients through the model.

| Route | Example | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | Visual foresight, 2016 | A real robot pushed objects it had never seen <Cite id="deep-visual-foresight-2016" /> |
| Plan | TD-MPC2 | One agent, 80 tasks <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" /> |
| Plan | PointWorld | 0.1 s per step on a real Franka, no demonstrations <Cite id="pointworld-2026" /> |
| Imagine | Dreamer | Practices in a learned [latent](/world-models/latent-dynamics/) space <Cite id="dreamer-2019" /> |
| Imagine | DreamerV3 | 150+ tasks, one configuration <Cite id="dreamerv3-2023" /> |
| Imagine | Dreamer 4 | Purely offline, since robot interaction "can be unsafe and slow" <Cite id="dreamer4-2025" /> |
| Differentiate | Stochastic Value Gradients, 2015 | Real observations limit compounding error <Cite id="stochastic-value-gradients-2015" /> |
| Differentiate | SuperMimic (Oct 2026) | In 3 GPU-hours beat a tracker trained 7 days on 128 GPUs <Cite id="supermimic-2026" /> |

<span id="uncertainty-aware-models" />
## Uncertainty estimates keep policies from exploiting model errors

A policy trained inside a model "tends to exploit regions where insufficient data is available"; ME-TRPO counters this with an ensemble of models <Cite id="me-trpo-2018" />. PETS, a probabilistic ensemble, matched model-free final performance on a simulated half-cheetah with 8 and 125 times fewer samples than SAC and PPO <Cite id="pets-2018" />.

Also: short branched rollouts in MBPO <Cite id="mbpo-2019" />; for [offline RL](/rl-sim2real/offline-rl/), MOPO's uncertainty penalty <Cite id="mopo-2020" /> and MOReL's pessimistic model <Cite id="morel-2020" />.

<span id="why-physical-robots-make-the-model-harder" />
## Robots see only part of the state

Cameras miss forces, friction and compliance; adding depth to color images improved fabric-folding success by 80% <Cite id="visuospatial-foresight-2020" />. Hansen and Wang call world-model hallucination "inherently a data coverage issue" and adapt a model to unseen simulated environments with 50 trajectories <Cite id="wm-hallucination-2026" />.

Test-time adaptation recovers hidden factors. One frozen October 2026 video world model infers a 32-number description of friction or mass from one or two interactions. Over four real-robot tasks, its chosen actions succeeded 70.80% of the time against 26.47% without it <Cite id="t2z-2026" />.

Also: OpTTA, 13.5 points more planning success under distribution shift <Cite id="optta-2026" />.

## Real robots learn useful models in minutes to hours

| Robot | Method | Real data | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hand with 24 <Term id="degrees-of-freedom">degrees of freedom</Term> | PDDM | 4 h | Contact-rich manipulation <Cite id="pddm-2019" /> |
| Quadruped | DayDreamer | 1 h | Stood up and walked <Cite id="daydreamer-2022" /> |
| Unitree Go1 | SSRL: learned forces on known equations of motion | A few minutes | Walking gaits <Cite id="ssrl-2024" /> |
| 11.5-ton excavator | Dynamics ensemble and sampling-based MPC | 20 min | Tracked as well as controllers trained on 100 to 150 min; sub-centimeter mean error at speed after 40 min <Cite id="excavator-mbrl-2026" /> |
| Soft robot | Policy trained on a 6-dimensional model fit to logs | 3 h of training | Replaced an estimated 3 years on hardware <Cite id="soft-robot-assm-rl-2026" /> |

In simulation, one TD-MPC2 model trained on up to 90 insertion tasks reached 56% zero-shot success on unseen objects, against 7% model-free <Cite id="insertion-world-models-2026" />.

## Model error is task-weighted

A two-millimeter error does not matter in free space and decides the outcome at a tight connector. One-step likelihood "is not always correlated with control performance" <Cite id="objective-mismatch-2020" />, and in one 2026 latent model it was uncorrelated with planning success across 15 checkpoints <Cite id="one-step-fidelity-2026" />. Test action consistency and task usefulness instead <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />, using the [evaluation guide](/world-models/evaluation/).

<span id="neural-simulators-and-hybrid-models" />
## Neural and hybrid simulators now train real-robot policies

| System | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Robotic World Model | A neural-network simulator for policy optimization <Cite id="robotic-world-model-2025" /> |
| RWM-U | Real quadruped and humanoid policies trained entirely offline, with uncertainty estimates <Cite id="rwm-u-2025" /> |
| RISE | A VLA improved inside a [video world model](/world-models/generative-video/) gained 35 to 45 points on three real tasks <Cite id="rise-2026" /> |
| PhysCanvas | π0.5's mean real success from 59.1% to 70.8% <Cite id="physcanvas-2026" /> |
| GR00T 2 | NVIDIA post-trains it on real robots and in a world model <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |

<Image id="roboworld-synthetic-scenes-2026" />

Hybrids keep known physics and learn what it misses. TossingBot learns corrections to a physics-based throw and reached more than 500 mean picks per hour <Cite id="tossingbot-2019" />; ASAP fits an action correction on a real Unitree G1 humanoid <Cite id="asap-2025" />. [World models versus physics simulators](/world-models/world-models-vs-simulators/) compares the options.

<span id="when-to-choose-model-based-learning" />
## Model-based learning pays off when real interaction is expensive

Choose it when goals change faster than dynamics and planning fits the time per step. Plan with a model only once its action rankings track real ones. In a 2026 agile-driving study SAC won in simulation, yet "only TD-MPC2 consistently outperforms the MPPI baseline on the physical platform" <Cite id="reset-free-driving-2026" />.

Toyota Research Institute and UC San Diego (October 2026) pretrained a racing world model in simulation and corrected it on the real car. With 44 minutes of real data, all 8 runs on one track succeeded, with a best sector of 49.55 s against 52.47 s for MPC. A run on real data alone was stopped for safety <Cite id="pia-2026" />.
