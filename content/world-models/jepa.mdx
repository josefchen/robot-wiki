The Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) is a [world-model design](/world-models/taxonomy/) that predicts the representation of the future instead of its pixels: an encoder and predictor learn to match the embedding a target encoder gives the true future <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. It is the main non-generative alternative to generative <Term id="world-model">world models</Term>.

Drag "Options tried each step" in the figure and the fan of tried moves follows it. "Plan next move" takes one more step. Planning in a frozen feature space works without reconstruction: DINO-WM solved six environments zero-shot without demonstrations, reward models or inverse models <Cite id="dino-wm-2024" />.

<span id="the-argument-for-representation-prediction" />
## Predicting embeddings skips detail that control does not need

A generative objective spends capacity on leaf texture and sensor noise that control does not need. In deep linear models, JEPA objectives are "biased to learn high-influence features" <Cite id="jepa-implicit-bias-2024" />. In violation-of-expectation tests, predictors trained in a learned representation space showed object permanence and shape consistency, while pixel-space predictors and multimodal LLMs scored near chance <Cite id="intuitive-physics-2025" />.

I-JEPA introduced the architecture as "a non-generative approach" that predicts the representations of target image blocks from a context block <Cite id="ijepa-2023" />. V-JEPA extended it to masked video, with no pixel reconstruction, negative examples, text or pretrained image encoder <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />.

<span id="v-jepa-2-and-its-successors" />
## V-JEPA 2 adds robot control with under 62 hours of robot video

V-JEPA 2 first learns action-free representations by masked prediction on over 1 million hours of internet video plus images, with a 1B-parameter ViT-g encoder and a 22M pretraining predictor. A frozen ViT-g384 with a four-layer attentive probe reaches 77.3% top-1 accuracy on Something-Something-v2, using 64 frames, two temporal and three spatial crops and the best of 20 heads <span className="block" data-jepa-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.</span>

V-JEPA 2-AC then freezes the encoder and trains a roughly 300M-parameter action-conditioned predictor on less than 62 hours of Droid robot video. "Unlabeled" means no reward, task or success labels; actions are changes in end-effector position, orientation and gripper state. It ran zero-shot on Franka Emika Panda arms in two labs absent from Droid, though Droid itself is Franka teleoperation and the authors hand-picked camera positions. Its four tabletop tasks used image goals, with two subgoal images for pick-and-place, at ten trials per setting <span className="block" data-jepa-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.</span>

## Latent planning works on tabletop picks, and successors improve it

Control is energy minimization in latent space. The Cross-Entropy Method searches 800 candidates over ten iterations at horizon one for actions whose predicted feature map is closest in L1 to the goal's, executes the first action and replans. This goal distance has no demonstrated calibration to a value function, and the pixel decoder trained for visualization plays no part in control <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. Meta reports a 1.2B-parameter model with 65% to 80% pick-and-place success on new objects in unseen environments, given visual subgoals <Cite id="meta-vjepa2-blog-2025" />.

V-JEPA 2.1 adds dense predictive losses and reports "a 20-point improvement in real-robot grasping success rate over V-JEPA-2 AC" <Cite id="vjepa-2-1-2026" />. A Meta FAIR design study produced a JEPA world model that beats DINO-WM and V-JEPA-2-AC in navigation and manipulation <Cite id="jepa-wm-2025" />.

<span id="the-institutional-bet-ami-labs" />
## LeCun's AMI Labs raised over a billion dollars for world models

LeCun left Meta and co-founded AMI Labs (Advanced Machine Intelligence), which raised \$1.03 billion at a \$3.5 billion pre-money valuation in March 2026 to build world models <Cite id="ami-labs-2026" />. LeCun is executive chairman and Alex LeBrun is CEO <Cite id="lecun-ami-techcrunch-2025" />. LeBrun told TechCrunch that "'world models' will be the next buzzword": "In six months, every company will call itself a world model to raise funding." <Cite id="ami-labs-2026" />.

<span id="the-counterargument-stated-fairly" />
## JEPA has no steerable simulator, and its latents are hard to validate

1. Every currently usable interactive simulator is generative. Genie 3, Cosmos 3, Odyssey-2 and the Interactive World Simulator render pixels a user can inspect and steer <Cite id="genie-3-2025" /><span className="block" data-cosmos-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" /></span><Cite id="odyssey-2-2025" /><Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />. No interactive, action-controllable JEPA environment has shipped.
2. Latent-only objectives are hard to validate: without a decoder, a reader cannot see what the model believes. V-JEPA prevents collapse with an exponential-moving-average target encoder and stop-gradient, a design inherited from BYOL <Cite id="byol-2020" />, and its authors state their adaptation of BYOL's justification as a hypothesis <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />. LeJEPA removes those heuristics with "no stop-gradient, no teacher-student" <Cite id="lejepa-2025" />, and Klindt and colleagues prove that it linearly recovers a world's latent variables under stated assumptions <Cite id="lejepa-world-model-2026" />. In complex scenes, most models still score at chance on IntPhys 2 while humans are near perfect <Cite id="intphys2-2025" />.

## Robot results do not yet separate the two camps

3. V-JEPA 2-AC's reported robot tests are bounded tabletop skills. Reaching, grasping, reach-with-object and pick-and-place with supplied goals and subgoals do not establish contact-rich assembly, deformable manipulation or whole-body control <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. SkyJEPA, a June 2026 preprint from Pratyaksh Rao, Yann LeCun, Giuseppe Loianno and colleagues, runs a JEPA-style latent dynamics model with sampling-based optimal control on a quadrotor's embedded hardware. It reports outdoor closed-loop flight with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer <Cite id="skyjepa-2026" />.
4. The camps post similar numbers. On LIBERO, latent world-model VLAs (VLA-JEPA 97.2, JEPA-VLA 96.4) sit beside video-based ones (Cosmos Policy and LingBot-VA 98.5, Say-Dream-ACT 98.1) <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. VLA-JEPA argues that pixel-anchored latent actions learn nuisance motion <Cite id="vla-jepa-2026" />, and JEPA-VLA that V-JEPA 2 embeddings discard unpredictable factors <Cite id="jepa-vla-2026" />. On the harder LIBERO-Plus, Cosmos-Policy reaches 82.2% <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> and JEPA-WAM 79.2% without large-scale robot pretraining, or 86.3% as a π0.5 instantiation <Cite id="jepa-wam-2026" />.
5. Fast-WAM separates video co-training from test-time generation. It keeps a future-video training objective but at inference only encodes the current frame and runs action denoising on a chunk. On LIBERO and RoboTwin 2.0, removing video co-training hurt success more than removing explicit future generation. That leaves a general JEPA advantage unproven. <span className="block" data-jepa-source-placement="line-start"><Cite id="fast-wam-2026" /></span>

<span id="what-would-settle-it" />
## An interactive JEPA world or a contact-rich win would settle it

Either of two results would break the stalemate: a JEPA environment a user can steer the way Genie 3 is steered today <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />, or a JEPA advantage on contact-rich tasks, where both camps' evidence runs out. Until then the JEPA line has the cheapest demonstrated path from observation to zero-shot planning <Cite id="leworldmodel-2026" />, and the [generative video](/world-models/generative-video/) line has the only usable simulators.

In September 2026, Haoran Zhu, Wancong Zhang, Yann LeCun and Anna Choromanska tested JEPA world models as driving planners with no driving policy trained, using ground-truth future observations as goals. They report that earlier JEPA models were either accurate but slow or fast but too weak to plan. Their AD-E2E-JEPA projector gives a 100-fold inference speedup while keeping planning performance, rolling out 256 candidate trajectories over 8 frames in 0.8 seconds <Cite id="ad-e2e-jepa-2026" />.

H-JEPA, from Yann LeCun (AMI Labs and NYU) and colleagues (October 2026), stacks action-conditioned JEPAs that each predict farther ahead and hand subgoals to the level below. On Visual AntMaze a three-level hierarchy raised success from 18% to 73% with less planner compute <Cite id="h-jepa-2026" />.

Leonardo Toso, Yann LeCun, James Anderson and Oumayma Bounou of Columbia, NYU and AMI Labs showed in October 2026 that next-step prediction with anti-collapse regularization can reach its minimum loss while discarding a system's controllable unstable modes. The learned state is then impossible to stabilize. They add an inverse-dynamics loss that reconstructs actions and prove, for linear systems, that exact reconstruction keeps every state direction reachable within the horizon. Experiments on CartPole, Walker2D and PointMaze from pixels extend the finding to nonlinear tasks <Cite id="jepa-unstable-modes-2026" />.

<span id="a-deployment-rule-for-representation-prediction" />
## Use JEPA when reconstructing pixels adds no evidence

Use a JEPA-style model when the decision depends on task-relevant change and reconstructing pixels adds no evidence, as in goal-conditioned planning. Decoder-free latent planning already scales, with one 317M-parameter TD-MPC2 agent performing 80 tasks <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />. Synthetic video, visual debugging and simulator replacement fit poorly, because the decoder-free state hides what it omitted. Test whether nearby embeddings mean equivalent outcomes and whether action rankings match hardware, and add out-of-distribution detectors or planner fallbacks, since embedding distance is uncalibrated confidence (see the [model-based](/world-models/model-based-robot-learning/) and [evaluation](/world-models/evaluation/) guides).
