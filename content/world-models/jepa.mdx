A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) is a [world-model design](/world-models/taxonomy/) that predicts an abstract description of the future, an embedding, instead of the future image itself: an encoder and predictor learn to match a target encoder's embedding of what actually happens <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. It is the main non-generative alternative to generative <Term id="world-model">world models</Term>.

To act, a JEPA planner encodes the current view and a goal image, tries candidate actions in embedding space and keeps the one whose predicted embedding lands closest to the goal, without drawing an image. Planning this way, DINO-WM solved six environments zero-shot without demonstrations, reward models or inverse models <Cite id="dino-wm-2024" />.

<span id="the-argument-for-representation-prediction" />
## Predicting embeddings skips detail that control does not need

A model that predicts pixels spends effort on leaf texture and sensor noise, which do not help a robot decide; predicting embeddings lets it ignore them. In deep linear models, JEPA objectives are "biased to learn high-influence features" <Cite id="jepa-implicit-bias-2024" />. In tests built on physically impossible events, predictors trained in a learned representation space showed object permanence and shape consistency, while pixel-space predictors and multimodal language models scored near chance <Cite id="intuitive-physics-2025" />.

I-JEPA introduced the architecture as "a non-generative approach" that predicts the representations of hidden blocks of an image <Cite id="ijepa-2023" />. V-JEPA extended it to video with parts masked out, using no pixel reconstruction, negative examples, text or pretrained image encoder <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />.

<span id="v-jepa-2-and-its-successors" />
## V-JEPA 2 adds robot control with under 62 hours of robot video

V-JEPA 2 first learns from over a million hours of internet video plus images, with no actions, by predicting embeddings of masked parts. It then recognizes 77.3% of actions in Something-Something-v2, a benchmark of motion-heavy clips <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

V-JEPA 2-AC then trains an action-conditioned predictor on the frozen encoder with under 62 hours of Droid robot video. The video carries no reward, task or success labels, but does record each change in the gripper's position, orientation and opening. The model ran zero-shot on Franka Emika Panda arms in two labs absent from Droid, though Droid itself is Franka teleoperation and the authors hand-picked camera positions. Its four tabletop tasks used image goals, with two subgoal images for pick-and-place, at ten trials per setting <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

## Latent planning works on tabletop picks, and successors improve it

At each step the planner samples 800 candidate actions over ten rounds, scores each one step ahead by the distance between its predicted embedding and the goal's, executes the best and replans. Nothing shows that this distance tracks real progress, and the pixel decoder trained for visualization plays no part in control <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

One study trains embedding distances to approximate goal-conditioned value <Cite id="jepa-value-planning-2026" />, and Time-to-Goal (November 2026) learns a distance that ranks states by how soon they reach the goal, raising the average planning success of a frozen LeWorldModel from 32.7% to 63.0% on four benchmarks <Cite id="t2g-jepa-2026" />. Meta reports a 1.2-billion-parameter model with 65% to 80% pick-and-place success on new objects in unseen environments, given visual subgoals <Cite id="meta-vjepa2-blog-2025" />. V-JEPA 2.1 adds dense prediction losses and reports "a 20-point improvement in real-robot grasping success rate over V-JEPA-2 AC" <Cite id="vjepa-2-1-2026" />, and a Meta FAIR design study built a JEPA world model that beat DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC in navigation and manipulation <Cite id="jepa-wm-2025" />.

## Robot results do not yet separate the two camps

JEPA-based and video-based policies score alike on the LIBERO simulation benchmark and its perturbed version, LIBERO-Plus.

| Model (approach) | LIBERO | LIBERO-Plus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VLA-JEPA, JEPA-VLA (JEPA) | 97.2, 96.4 <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" /> | |
| Cosmos Policy, LingBot-VA, Say-Dream-ACT (video) | 98.5, 98.5, 98.1 <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" /> | Cosmos Policy 82.2 <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> |
| JEPA-WAM (JEPA) | | 79.2 without large-scale robot pretraining, 86.3 built on π0.5 <Cite id="jepa-wam-2026" /> |

VLA-JEPA argues that latent actions anchored to pixels learn irrelevant motion <Cite id="vla-jepa-2026" />, and JEPA-VLA that V-JEPA 2 embeddings discard unpredictable factors <Cite id="jepa-vla-2026" />. La-JEPA (November 2026) trains a compact policy on V-JEPA 2.1 features; it leads policies without robot pretraining on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0, matches π0.5 on four real tasks, and its features beat video-autoencoder ones most clearly on unfamiliar inputs <Cite id="la-jepa-2026" />.

Fast-WAM trains on future video but at test time only encodes the current frame and outputs actions; removing video co-training hurt more than removing future generation, which leaves a general JEPA advantage unproven <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" />. V-JEPA 2-AC's robot tests are bounded tabletop skills, which do not establish contact-rich assembly, deformable objects or whole-body control <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. SkyJEPA, a June 2026 preprint co-authored by LeCun, runs a JEPA-style model and sampling-based control on a quadrotor's onboard computer, reporting outdoor closed-loop flight transferred zero-shot from simulation <Cite id="skyjepa-2026" />.

<span id="the-counterargument-stated-fairly" />
## JEPA has no steerable simulator, and its latents are hard to validate

Every interactive simulator in use is generative. Genie 3 and 4, Cosmos 3, Odyssey-2 and 3 and the Interactive World Simulator render pixels a user can inspect and steer <Cite id="genie-3-2025" /> <Cite id="genie-4-2026" /> <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" /> <Cite id="odyssey-2-2025" /> <Cite id="odyssey-3-2026" /> <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />; no interactive, action-controllable JEPA environment has shipped.

Without a decoder no one can see what the model believes, and an October 2026 perspective finds causal checks of JEPA latents largely unexplored <Cite id="wm-interpretability-2026" />. Training loss is a poor guide: in one JEPA world model, one-step prediction error and planning success were uncorrelated across 15 checkpoints (r = -0.010) <Cite id="one-step-fidelity-2026" />. Then there is collapse, where every input maps to one embedding. V-JEPA prevents it with a slowly updated target encoder and a stop-gradient inherited from BYOL <Cite id="byol-2020" />, a design its authors call a hypothesis <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />. LeJEPA removes those heuristics, with "no stop-gradient, no teacher-student" <Cite id="lejepa-2025" />; LeVJEPA applies it to video <Cite id="levjepa-2026" />, and Klindt and colleagues prove that it linearly recovers a world's hidden variables under stated assumptions <Cite id="lejepa-world-model-2026" />. On the IntPhys 2 physics test most models still score at chance and humans near perfect <Cite id="intphys2-2025" />.

<span id="what-would-settle-it" />
## An interactive JEPA world or a contact-rich win would settle it

Two results would settle it: a JEPA environment that a user can steer as Genie is steered today <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />, or a JEPA advantage on contact-rich tasks, where both camps' evidence runs out. Until then JEPA has the cheapest demonstrated path from watching video to zero-shot planning <Cite id="leworldmodel-2026" />, and [generative video](/world-models/generative-video/) the only usable simulators.

LeCun's group targets speed, horizon and stability. As a driving planner (September 2026), its AD-E2E-JEPA projector ran 100 times faster at equal planning performance, rolling out 256 candidate trajectories over 8 frames in 0.8 seconds <Cite id="ad-e2e-jepa-2026" />. H-JEPA (October 2026) stacks action-conditioned JEPAs, each predicting farther ahead and handing subgoals to the level below; on Visual AntMaze three levels raised success from 18% to 73% with less planner compute <Cite id="h-jepa-2026" />.

Toso, LeCun and colleagues showed in October 2026 that next-step prediction with anti-collapse regularization can reach its lowest loss while discarding a system's controllable unstable modes, leaving a state that cannot be stabilized. An added loss that reconstructs the actions provably keeps every state direction reachable in linear systems, and the result extends to CartPole, Walker2D and PointMaze from pixels <Cite id="jepa-unstable-modes-2026" />. Σ-LeWM (November 2026) adds a similar action-reconstruction loss and a backward-prediction loss to LeWorldModel and plans faster <Cite id="sigma-lewm-2026" />. LeHami replaces the predictor with an action-conditioned Hamiltonian flow that preserves latent volume at every step. On a simulated pendulum seen only in pixels its angular-velocity error was 2.8 times lower than LeWorldModel's over the first ten steps, yet on a goal-reaching pendulum task the best LeWorldModel variant still succeeded more often, 0.636 against 0.304 over three seeds <Cite id="lehami-2026" />.

## LeCun's AMI Labs raised over a billion dollars for world models

Yann LeCun left Meta and co-founded AMI Labs (Advanced Machine Intelligence), which raised \$1.03 billion <Cite id="ami-labs-launch-2026" /> at a \$3.5 billion pre-money valuation in March 2026 <Cite id="ami-labs-2026" />. LeCun is executive chairman and Alex LeBrun is CEO <Cite id="lecun-ami-techcrunch-2025" />. LeBrun told TechCrunch that "'world models' will be the next buzzword": "In six months, every company will call itself a world model to raise funding." <Cite id="ami-labs-2026" /> In July 2026 it was still pre-product, courting robotics and manufacturing partners <Cite id="techcrunch-lebrun-2026" />, and in October LeCun said AMI would release its world model as open source for researchers and with open weights for others <Cite id="chosun-lecun-2026" />.

<span id="a-deployment-rule-for-representation-prediction" />
## Use JEPA when reconstructing pixels adds no evidence

A JEPA-style model fits when pixels add nothing to the decision, as in goal-conditioned planning. Decoder-free latent planning already scales, with one 317-million-parameter TD-MPC2 agent performing 80 tasks <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />. Synthetic video, visual debugging and simulator replacement fit poorly, since a decoder-free state hides what it omits. Test whether nearby embeddings mean equivalent outcomes and whether action rankings match hardware, and add out-of-distribution detectors or planner fallbacks, since embedding distance is not a calibrated confidence (see the [model-based](/world-models/model-based-robot-learning/) and [evaluation](/world-models/evaluation/) guides).
