A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) is a [world-model design](/world-models/taxonomy/) that predicts an embedding, an abstract description of the future, instead of the future image <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. It is the main non-generative alternative to generative <Term id="world-model">world models</Term>.

A JEPA planner encodes the current view and a goal image, tries candidate actions in embedding space and keeps the one landing closest to the goal. Planning this way, DINO-WM solved six environments zero-shot without demonstrations, reward models or inverse models <Cite id="dino-wm-2024" />.

<span id="the-argument-for-representation-prediction" />
## Predicting embeddings skips detail that control does not need

A pixel predictor spends effort on leaf texture and sensor noise that do not help a robot decide. In deep linear models, JEPA objectives are "biased to learn high-influence features" <Cite id="jepa-implicit-bias-2024" />. On physically impossible events, predictors in a learned representation space showed object permanence, while pixel predictors scored near chance <Cite id="intuitive-physics-2025" />.

Also: I-JEPA, "a non-generative approach" for images <Cite id="ijepa-2023" />, and its video successor V-JEPA <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />.

<span id="v-jepa-2-and-its-successors" />
## V-JEPA 2 adds robot control with under 62 hours of robot video

V-JEPA 2 learns from over a million hours of internet video and images, with no actions, and recognizes 77.3% of actions in the motion-heavy Something-Something-v2 <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

V-JEPA 2-AC then trains an action-conditioned predictor on the frozen encoder with under 62 hours of Droid robot video, labeled only with gripper motion. It ran zero-shot on Franka arms in two labs absent from Droid, on four tabletop tasks with image goals <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

<Image id="vjepa2-overview-2025" />

## Latent planning works on tabletop picks, and successors improve it

Each step, the planner samples 800 candidate actions over ten rounds, scores each by embedding distance to the goal, executes the best and replans <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. Nothing shows that this distance tracks real progress.

Also: embedding distance trained as goal-conditioned value <Cite id="jepa-value-planning-2026" />.

| Successor | Change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time-to-Goal (November 2026) | Ranks states by how soon they reach the goal | Frozen LeWorldModel from 32.7% to 63.0% on four benchmarks <Cite id="t2g-jepa-2026" /> |
| Meta, 1.2 billion parameters | Visual subgoals | 65% to 80% pick-and-place on new objects in unseen environments <Cite id="meta-vjepa2-blog-2025" /> |
| V-JEPA 2.1 | Dense prediction losses | "a 20-point improvement in real-robot grasping success rate" <Cite id="vjepa-2-1-2026" /> |
| Meta FAIR design study | A tuned JEPA world model | Beat DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC in navigation and manipulation <Cite id="jepa-wm-2025" /> |

## Robot results do not yet separate the two camps

JEPA and video policies score alike on the LIBERO simulation benchmark and its perturbed version, LIBERO-Plus.

| Model (approach) | LIBERO | LIBERO-Plus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VLA-JEPA, JEPA-VLA (JEPA) | 97.2, 96.4 <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" /> | |
| Cosmos Policy, LingBot-VA, Say-Dream-ACT (video) | 98.5, 98.5, 98.1 <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" /> | Cosmos Policy 82.2 <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> |
| JEPA-WAM (JEPA) | | 79.2 without large-scale robot pretraining, 86.3 built on π0.5 <Cite id="jepa-wam-2026" /> |

La-JEPA trains a compact policy on V-JEPA 2.1 features and matches π0.5 on four real tasks <Cite id="la-jepa-2026" />. Fast-WAM's gains came from video co-training more than future generation, so a general JEPA advantage is unproven <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" />. SkyJEPA flew a quadrotor outdoors with an onboard JEPA-style model, transferred zero-shot from simulation <Cite id="skyjepa-2026" />.

Also: VLA-JEPA on pixel-anchored latent actions <Cite id="vla-jepa-2026" />, JEPA-VLA on what V-JEPA 2 embeddings discard <Cite id="jepa-vla-2026" />.

<span id="the-counterargument-stated-fairly" />
## JEPA has no steerable simulator, and its latents are hard to validate

Every interactive simulator in use is generative, rendering pixels a user can steer; no action-controllable JEPA environment has shipped.

Also: Genie 3 <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />, Genie 4 <Cite id="genie-4-2026" />, Cosmos 3 <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />, Odyssey-2 <Cite id="odyssey-2-2025" />, Odyssey-3 <Cite id="odyssey-3-2026" />, the Interactive World Simulator <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.

Without a decoder no one can see what the model believes, and causal checks of JEPA latents are largely unexplored <Cite id="wm-interpretability-2026" />. Training loss is a poor guide: across 15 checkpoints of one JEPA world model, one-step error and planning success were uncorrelated (r = -0.010) <Cite id="one-step-fidelity-2026" />.

Collapse maps every input to one embedding. V-JEPA prevents it with a slow target encoder and a stop-gradient from BYOL <Cite id="byol-2020" />, a design its authors call a hypothesis <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />. LeJEPA drops those heuristics, with "no stop-gradient, no teacher-student" <Cite id="lejepa-2025" />.

Also: LeVJEPA for video <Cite id="levjepa-2026" />, a proof that LeJEPA recovers hidden variables <Cite id="lejepa-world-model-2026" />, IntPhys 2, where most models still score at chance <Cite id="intphys2-2025" />.

<span id="what-would-settle-it" />
## An interactive JEPA world or a contact-rich win would settle it

Two results would settle it: a JEPA world a user can steer as Genie is steered today <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />, or a JEPA win on contact-rich tasks. Until then JEPA has the cheapest proven path from watching video to zero-shot planning <Cite id="leworldmodel-2026" />, and [generative video](/world-models/generative-video/) the only usable simulators.

LeCun's group showed that next-step prediction with anti-collapse regularization can reach its lowest loss while discarding a system's controllable unstable modes, so the state cannot be stabilized <Cite id="jepa-unstable-modes-2026" />.

| Work | Change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AD-E2E-JEPA (September 2026) | Driving planner | 100 times faster at equal planning performance; 256 trajectories over 8 frames in 0.8 s <Cite id="ad-e2e-jepa-2026" /> |
| H-JEPA (October 2026) | Stacked JEPAs hand subgoals down | Visual AntMaze success from 18% to 73% with less planner compute <Cite id="h-jepa-2026" /> |
| Toso, LeCun and colleagues (October 2026) | Loss that reconstructs actions | Every state direction stays reachable in linear systems; holds on CartPole, Walker2D and PointMaze <Cite id="jepa-unstable-modes-2026" /> |
| Σ-LeWM (November 2026) | Action-reconstruction and backward-prediction losses | Plans faster than LeWorldModel <Cite id="sigma-lewm-2026" /> |
| LeHami | Volume-preserving Hamiltonian predictor | 2.8 times lower pendulum velocity error, yet 0.304 goal-reaching success against 0.636 for LeWorldModel <Cite id="lehami-2026" /> |

## LeCun's AMI Labs raised over a billion dollars for world models

Yann LeCun left Meta and co-founded AMI Labs, which raised \$1.03 billion <Cite id="ami-labs-launch-2026" /> at a \$3.5 billion pre-money valuation in March 2026 <Cite id="ami-labs-2026" />. LeCun is executive chairman and Alex LeBrun is CEO <Cite id="lecun-ami-techcrunch-2025" />. LeBrun warned: "In six months, every company will call itself a world model to raise funding." <Cite id="ami-labs-2026" />

In July 2026 AMI was still pre-product <Cite id="techcrunch-lebrun-2026" />, and in October LeCun said it would release its world model openly <Cite id="chosun-lecun-2026" />.

<span id="a-deployment-rule-for-representation-prediction" />
## Use JEPA when reconstructing pixels adds no evidence

A JEPA-style model fits when pixels add nothing to the decision, as in goal-conditioned planning; one decoder-free TD-MPC2 agent of 317 million parameters performs 80 tasks <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />. It fits synthetic video, visual debugging and simulator replacement poorly, since a decoder-free state hides what it omits. Test whether nearby embeddings mean equivalent outcomes and whether action rankings match hardware, because embedding distance is not a calibrated confidence.
