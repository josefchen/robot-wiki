A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) is a [world-model design](/world-models/taxonomy/) that predicts an embedding, an abstract description of the future, instead of the future image <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. It is the main non-generative alternative to generative <Term id="world-model">world models</Term>.

A JEPA planner encodes the current view and goal image and plans in the embedding space. It tries actions and selects the one that leads to an embedding closest to the goal embedding. Using this planner, DINO-WM is able to solve six environments zero-shot, without demonstrations, reward models or inverse models <Cite id="dino-wm-2024" />.

<span id="the-argument-for-representation-prediction" />
## Predicting embeddings skips detail that control does not need

Pixel predictors waste their capacity modeling details irrelevant for robotic decision making, such as sensor noise and leaf textures. In deep linear models, JEPA objectives are "biased to learn high-influence features" <Cite id="jepa-implicit-bias-2024" />. Prediction in a learned representation space exhibited object permanence on physically impossible events, whereas performance of pixel predictors was close to chance level <Cite id="intuitive-physics-2025" />.

Also: I-JEPA, "a non-generative approach" for images <Cite id="ijepa-2023" />, and its video successor V-JEPA <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />.

<span id="v-jepa-2-and-its-successors" />
## V-JEPA 2 adds robot control with under 62 hours of robot video

V-JEPA 2 is trained on over a million hours of images and videos from the internet (with no actions) and gets 77.3% on the motion-heavy Something-Something-v2 <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

V-JEPA 2-AC then trains an action-conditioned predictor on top of the frozen video encoder using less than 62 hours of videos from Droid robots, where the only action labels available are gripper actions. It was deployed zero-shot on Franka arms in two labs not present in Droid, on four tabletop manipulation tasks with image goals <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

<Image id="vjepa2-overview-2025" />

## Latent planning works on tabletop picks, and successors improve it

In the reported one-step planning setup, the planner samples 800 candidate actions in each of ten rounds. It scores them by embedding distance to the goal, updates the sampling distribution from the strongest candidates, then executes the final mean action and replans <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. There is no evidence that the embedding distance is correlated with actual task progress.

Also: embedding distance trained as goal-conditioned value <Cite id="jepa-value-planning-2026" />.

| Successor | Change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time-to-Goal (November 2026) | Ranks states by how soon they reach the goal | Frozen LeWorldModel from 32.7% to 63.0% on four benchmarks <Cite id="t2g-jepa-2026" /> |
| Meta, 1.2 billion parameters | Visual subgoals | 65% to 80% pick-and-place on new objects in unseen environments <Cite id="meta-vjepa2-blog-2025" /> |
| V-JEPA 2.1 | Dense prediction losses | "a 20-point improvement in real-robot grasping success rate" <Cite id="vjepa-2-1-2026" /> |
| Meta FAIR design study | A tuned JEPA world model | Beat DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC in navigation and manipulation <Cite id="jepa-wm-2025" /> |

## Robot results do not yet separate the two camps

On the LIBERO simulation benchmark, policies trained with JEPA achieve comparable performance to video policies. The same trend can be observed on its perturbed version, LIBERO-Plus.

| Model (approach) | LIBERO | LIBERO-Plus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VLA-JEPA, JEPA-VLA (JEPA) | 97.2, 96.4 <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" /> | |
| Cosmos Policy, LingBot-VA, Say-Dream-ACT (video) | 98.5, 98.5, 98.1 <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" /> | Cosmos Policy 82.2 <Cite id="wam-robustness-2026" /> |
| JEPA-WAM (JEPA) | | 79.2 without large-scale robot pretraining, 86.3 built on π0.5 <Cite id="jepa-wam-2026" /> |

La-JEPA learns compact policies using features from V-JEPA 2.1 and achieves performance on par with π0.5 on four real-robot tasks <Cite id="la-jepa-2026" />. Fast-WAM finds improvements stem from video co-training more than future generation, leaving it unclear whether JEPA offers advantages in general <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" />. SkyJEPA flew a quadrotor outdoors with an onboard JEPA-style model, transferred zero-shot from simulation <Cite id="skyjepa-2026" />.

Also: VLA-JEPA on pixel-anchored latent actions <Cite id="vla-jepa-2026" />, JEPA-VLA on what V-JEPA 2 embeddings discard <Cite id="jepa-vla-2026" />.

<span id="the-counterargument-stated-fairly" />
## JEPA has no steerable simulator, and its latents are hard to validate

Every interactive simulator that has been deployed in practice to date has been a generative model, rendering pixels a user can steer; no action-controllable JEPA environment has shipped.

Also: Genie 3 <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />, Genie 4 <Cite id="genie-4-2026" />, Cosmos 3 <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />, Odyssey-2 <Cite id="odyssey-2-2025" />, Odyssey-3 <Cite id="odyssey-3-2026" />, the Interactive World Simulator <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.

Without a decoder, no one can see what the model believes, and causal probes of JEPA latents remain largely unexplored <Cite id="wm-interpretability-2026" />. Training loss also offers little insight: in one JEPA world model, across 15 training checkpoints, there was no correlation (r = -0.010) between one-step prediction error and planning success rate <Cite id="one-step-fidelity-2026" />.

Collapse refers to a degenerate solution in which a model learns to map all inputs to one embedding. V-JEPA avoids collapse by employing a slow target encoder and a stop-gradient from BYOL <Cite id="byol-2020" />, a design its authors term a hypothesis <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />. LeJEPA does not require such heuristics, with "no stop-gradient, no teacher-student" <Cite id="lejepa-2025" />.

Also: LeVJEPA for video <Cite id="levjepa-2026" />, a proof that LeJEPA recovers hidden variables <Cite id="lejepa-world-model-2026" />, IntPhys 2, where most models still score at chance <Cite id="intphys2-2025" />.

<span id="what-would-settle-it" />
## An interactive JEPA world or a contact-rich win would settle it

Two results would settle it: a JEPA world a user can steer as Genie is steered today <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />, or a JEPA win on contact-rich tasks. Until then, JEPA models offer the cheapest demonstrated route from video to zero-shot planning <Cite id="leworldmodel-2026" />, while [generative video](/world-models/generative-video/) models offer the only simulators people can use today.

LeCun's group found that next-step prediction trained with anti-collapse regularization can minimize its loss function while ignoring a system's controllable unstable modes, precluding stabilization <Cite id="jepa-unstable-modes-2026" />.

| Work | Change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AD-E2E-JEPA (September 2026) | Driving planner | 100 times faster at equal planning performance; 256 trajectories over 8 frames in 0.8 s <Cite id="ad-e2e-jepa-2026" /> |
| H-JEPA (October 2026) | Stacked JEPAs hand subgoals down | Visual AntMaze success from 18% to 73% with less planner compute <Cite id="h-jepa-2026" /> |
| Toso, LeCun and colleagues (October 2026) | Loss that reconstructs actions | Every state direction stays reachable in linear systems; holds on CartPole, Walker2D and PointMaze <Cite id="jepa-unstable-modes-2026" /> |
| Strohm and colleagues (October 2026) | Inverse-dynamics loss makes latents action-sensitive | SLIM multi-object pushing, goal frames: four-tier mean success 0.003 to 0.35, hard tier 0.003 to 0.16, for a PushT-solving LeWorldModel <Cite id="jepa-plannable-2026" /> |
| Σ-LeWM (November 2026) | Action-reconstruction and backward-prediction losses | Plans faster than LeWorldModel <Cite id="sigma-lewm-2026" /> |
| LeHami | Volume-preserving Hamiltonian predictor | 2.8 times lower pendulum velocity error, yet 0.304 goal-reaching success against 0.636 for LeWorldModel <Cite id="lehami-2026" /> |

<span id="a-deployment-rule-for-representation-prediction" />
## Use JEPA when reconstructing pixels adds no evidence

A JEPA-style model fits when pixels add nothing to the decision, as in goal-conditioned planning; one decoder-free TD-MPC2 agent with 317 million parameters performs 80 tasks <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />. However, without a decoder it is hard to tell what information the state representation is missing, making it poorly suited to synthetic video, visual debugging or simulator replacement. Test whether nearby embeddings lead to equivalent outcomes, and validate that the relative ordering of actions is consistent with ground truth on real hardware, because embedding distance is not a calibrated confidence.

For more details, see the [model-based](/world-models/model-based-robot-learning/) and [evaluation](/world-models/evaluation/) guides.
