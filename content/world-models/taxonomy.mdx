A <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> predicts what happens next when a robot acts: if the gripper pushes this cup, where does the cup go? With that answer a robot can plan, practice in imagination and be tested without risking hardware <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

<Image id="dreamerv3-open-loop-quadruped-2023" />

## A world model imagines one step at a time

It takes the present, often camera images, and a candidate action, and predicts the next moment. Chained, they form a sequence to judge before moving. Ha and Schmidhuber's 2018 agent trained entirely inside its "hallucinated dream" and worked in the real game <Cite id="ha-world-models-2018" />.

Also: World Labs' split into renderers, simulators and planners <Cite id="worldlabs-taxonomy-2026" />.

<span id="the-functional-cut" />
## A model counts only if it predicts the effect of actions

The prediction must depend on the action: a clip of a sliding cup is useless unless it changes when the push changes. Hou and colleagues' 2026 survey agrees: "a model does not qualify as a world model in our sense simply because it generates plausible future images or videos" <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. 
Also: a second 2026 survey, "an action-conditioned predictive system" <Cite id="manip-wm-survey-2026" />.

## A physics engine counts; a photo twin does not

The MuJoCo physics engine is written by hand, yet it predicts the next state from a state and an action <Cite id="mujoco-2012" />. TD-MPC2 and classical <Term id="mpc">model-predictive control (MPC)</Term> share one pattern: try actions in a model, then act on the best.

<Term id="gaussian-splatting">3D Gaussian Splatting</Term> (3DGS) turns photographs into a photorealistic 3D scene viewable from any camera <Cite id="3dgs-2023" />. It predicts appearance; motion comes from an attached physics engine, as in PhysGaussian <Cite id="physgaussian-2023" /> or World Labs' Marble, whose collision meshes are "intended for coarse physics simulation" <Cite id="marble-2025" />. See [generative simulation](/world-models/generative-sim/).

## Six families differ in what they predict

<WmTaxonomyTable className="my-6 wide-measure" />

### Latent dynamics: predict a compact state, dream in it

Dreamer-style models squeeze each image into a short <Term id="latent-dynamics">latent</Term> state, learn how it changes, and train the controller mostly on imagined trajectories. DreamerV3 handled over 150 tasks with one configuration <Cite id="dreamerv3-2023" />.

Also: first Minecraft diamonds from scratch <Cite id="dreamerv3-nature-2025" />, DayDreamer's hour-long quadruped training <Cite id="daydreamer-2022" />, Dreamer 4's unlabeled video <Cite id="dreamer4-2025" />, [latent dynamics](/world-models/latent-dynamics/).

### Decoder-free latent: no image, just value

TD-MPC2 predicts the next latent state, the reward and the <Term id="value-function">value</Term>, and plans by rolling sampled action sequences through the model. One agent learned 80 tasks across several robot bodies <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />.

### Generative video: predict the pixels

These video generators also take actions or text prompts ([generative video](/world-models/generative-video/)). Genie 3 renders text-prompted worlds explorable in real time at 24 frames per second, largely consistent for a few minutes <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />.

Also: Genie 4, with audio and Gemini steering <Cite id="genie-4-2026" />; Cosmos 3, which NVIDIA pitches as video generator, scene reader and policy in one <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />.

### JEPA: predict the representation, never the pixels

A joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) predicts an abstract description of the future, keeping a moving object's path and ignoring each leaf on a tree <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. After over a million hours of action-free video and under 62 hours of robot video, V-JEPA 2's action-conditioned version picked and placed objects zero-shot in two new labs <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

Also: V-JEPA's feature-only objective <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />, LeWorldModel trained end to end from pixels <Cite id="leworldmodel-2026" />, Σ-LeWM <Cite id="sigma-lewm-2026" />, the [JEPA module](/world-models/jepa/).

### World-action models: imagine and act in one network

A world-action model (WAM) trains a pretrained video model to predict robot actions and future frames <Cite id="nvidia-wam-blog-2026" />. Unlike a [VLA](/manipulation/vla-models/), a vision-language-action policy, it learns physical dynamics <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />.

| Model | Result |
| --- | --- |
| DreamZero, 14B parameters | Controls real robots 7 times a second; more than doubles leading VLAs' generalization <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" /> |
| WorldVLA | Predicting actions and frames together improves both <Cite id="worldvla-2025" /> |
| Fast-WAM | Competitive when it skips imagination at test time; drops sharply without video in training <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" /> |
| GR00T 2 (Nov 2026) | 71% on 50 new tasks after one demonstration, 95% after five <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" />, on tasks chosen because five sufficed <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| Praxis-1 (Runway, open weights) | 46.2% on RoboLab-120 against 28.0% for π0.5 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" /> |

### Structured models: predict objects, points and facts

These predict objects, 3D points or facts such as "the cup is on the table".

| Model | Predicts | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interaction Networks, 2016 | Object graphs | Orbits and collisions <Cite id="interaction-networks-2016" /> |
| OccWorld | Occupied space around a car | <Cite id="occworld-2023" /> |
| PointWorld | 3D point motion | 0.1 s per step, fast enough to steer a real arm without demonstrations <Cite id="pointworld-2026" /> |
| GPWM (Nov 2026) | Particles | 31% lower error than the best prior learned model in six domains <Cite id="gpwm-2026" /> |
| Learned symbolic operators | Facts | "A lossy abstraction of the transition model" <Cite id="symbolic-operators-2021" /> |
| VisualPredicator | Facts it invents | Generalizes better to unfamiliar tasks <Cite id="visualpredicator-2024" /> |

<span id="what-each-paradigm-is-actually-for" />
## Video models serve evaluation and data; latent models plan without images

Video models stand in for the world when a policy needs camera images. A Veo-based simulator predicted the ranking of Gemini Robotics policies, checked against more than 1,600 real evaluations <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. Planning only compares outcomes, so decoder-free latent and JEPA models never draw an image.

Also: Interactive World Simulator data, as good for training as equal real data <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.

## Three tests before believing a world-model claim

Fluent output can hide failure.

| Test | Asks | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intervention | Change the action, keep history fixed | "Action-marginalized" hallucination, a future that ignores the action, is a measured failure <Cite id="wm-hallucination-2026" /> |
| Ranking | Does the model order policies as real trials do? | |
| Transfer | Repeat both on new objects, contacts, viewpoints and horizons | Video models trained on simple physics failed outside their training range <Cite id="video-physical-law-2024" /> |

Visual quality counts last: in six video generators, physical understanding was "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.
