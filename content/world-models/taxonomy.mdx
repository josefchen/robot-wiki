Given the current state of the world, say images from a robot's cameras, and an action, say the gripper pushing a cup, a <Term id="world-model">world model</Term> makes a prediction about the next state: where the cup goes. With that prediction a robot can plan, practice in imagination and have its performance tested without risking hardware <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

<Image id="dreamerv3-open-loop-quadruped-2023" />

## A world model imagines one step at a time

It takes a state, often camera images, and a candidate action, and predicts the next moment. These predictions can be chained into longer rollouts, which agents can evaluate before committing to actions. In 2018, Ha and Schmidhuber's agent trained entirely inside its "hallucinated dream" and transferred the learned policy to the real game <Cite id="ha-world-models-2018" />.

Also: World Labs' split into renderers, simulators and planners <Cite id="worldlabs-taxonomy-2026" />.

<span id="the-functional-cut" />
## A model counts only if it predicts the effect of actions

For a predictive model to be considered a world model, its predictions must depend on actions: it is not enough if it predicts a cup will slide but the trajectory of that slide does not depend on how the cup is pushed. As emphasized in a 2026 survey by Hou and colleagues, "a model does not qualify as a world model in our sense simply because it generates plausible future images or videos" <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />. Also: a second 2026 survey, "an action-conditioned predictive system" <Cite id="manip-wm-survey-2026" />.

## A physics engine counts; a photo twin does not

The MuJoCo physics engine is hand-written, but given a state and an action it predicts the next state <Cite id="mujoco-2012" />. Both TD-MPC2 and classical <Term id="mpc">model-predictive control (MPC)</Term> methods use models to simulate the consequences of candidate actions, and then perform the action that looks best.

<Term id="gaussian-splatting">3D Gaussian Splatting</Term> (3DGS) learns a 3D representation from photos that can be rendered photorealistically from any viewpoint <Cite id="3dgs-2023" />. It predicts only appearance. To simulate dynamics, PhysGaussian <Cite id="physgaussian-2023" /> and World Labs' Marble rely on an attached physics engine; Marble describes its collision meshes as "intended for coarse physics simulation" <Cite id="marble-2025" />. See also [generative simulation](/world-models/generative-sim/).

## Six families differ in what they predict

<WmTaxonomyTable className="my-6 wide-measure" />

### Latent dynamics: predict a compact state, dream in it

Dreamer-style methods learn a compact <Term id="latent-dynamics">latent</Term> representation of images, model its temporal dynamics, and learn policies mostly using imagined rollouts. Using one set of hyperparameters, DreamerV3 handled more than 150 tasks <Cite id="dreamerv3-2023" />.

Also: the first Minecraft diamonds from scratch <Cite id="dreamerv3-nature-2025" />, an hour of quadruped training in DayDreamer <Cite id="daydreamer-2022" />, Dreamer 4 learning from unlabeled video <Cite id="dreamer4-2025" />, and [latent dynamics](/world-models/latent-dynamics/).

### Decoder-free latent: no image, just value

TD-MPC2 predicts next latent states along with rewards and <Term id="value-function">value</Term> estimates. During planning, TD-MPC2 samples action sequences and rolls them out in the learned model. One agent learned 80 tasks across different robot embodiments <Cite id="tdmpc2-2023" />.

### Generative video: predict the pixels

These video generators also take actions or text prompts ([generative video](/world-models/generative-video/)). Genie 3 generates interactive worlds from text prompts at 24 frames per second, explorable in real time and largely consistent for a few minutes <Cite id="genie-3-2025" />.

Also: Genie 4, which adds audio and can be controlled with Gemini <Cite id="genie-4-2026" />; Cosmos 3, which NVIDIA bills as video generator, scene reader and policy in one <Cite id="cosmos-3-2026" />.

### JEPA: predict the representation, never the pixels

A joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) predicts an abstract description of the future, keeping a moving object's path and ignoring each leaf on a tree <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. After training on more than a million hours of video without action labels and less than 62 hours of robot video, an action-conditioned version of V-JEPA 2 picked and placed objects zero-shot in two unseen labs <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />.

Also: V-JEPA's feature-only objective <Cite id="vjepa-2024" />, LeWorldModel, trained end to end from pixels <Cite id="leworldmodel-2026" />, Σ-LeWM <Cite id="sigma-lewm-2026" />, and the [JEPA module](/world-models/jepa/).

### World-action models: imagine and act in one network

A world-action model (WAM) fine-tunes a pretrained video model to predict robot actions and future frames <Cite id="nvidia-wam-blog-2026" />. In contrast to a [VLA](/manipulation/vla-models/), a vision-language-action policy, it learns the dynamics of the physical world <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" />.

| Model | Result |
| --- | --- |
| DreamZero, 14B parameters | Controls real robots 7 times a second; more than doubles leading VLAs' generalization <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" /> |
| WorldVLA | Predicting actions and frames together improves both <Cite id="worldvla-2025" /> |
| Fast-WAM | Competitive when it skips imagination at test time; drops sharply without video in training <Cite id="fast-wam-2026" /> |
| GR00T 2 (Nov 2026) | 71% on 50 new tasks after one demonstration, 95% after five <Cite id="nvidia-corl-gr00t-2-2026" />, on tasks chosen because five sufficed <Cite id="gr00t-2-2026" /> |
| Praxis-1 (Runway, open weights) | 46.2% on RoboLab-120 against 28.0% for π0.5 <Cite id="runway-praxis-1-open-2026" /> |

### Structured models: predict objects, points and facts

Structured world models predict objects, 3D points or facts such as "the cup is on the table".

| Model | Predicts | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interaction Networks, 2016 | Object graphs | Orbits and collisions <Cite id="interaction-networks-2016" /> |
| OccWorld | Occupied space around a car | <Cite id="occworld-2023" /> |
| PointWorld | 3D point motion | 0.1 s per step, fast enough to steer a real arm without demonstrations <Cite id="pointworld-2026" /> |
| GPWM (Nov 2026) | Particles | 31% lower error than the best prior learned model in six domains <Cite id="gpwm-2026" /> |
| Learned symbolic operators | Facts | "A lossy abstraction of the transition model" <Cite id="symbolic-operators-2021" /> |
| VisualPredicator | Facts it invents | Generalizes better to unfamiliar tasks <Cite id="visualpredicator-2024" /> |

<span id="what-each-paradigm-is-actually-for" />
## Video models serve evaluation and data; latent models plan without images

Video generation models stand in for the world when a policy needs camera images. A world model based on Veo predicted the ranking of Gemini Robotics policies, checked against more than 1,600 real-world evaluation runs <Cite id="veo-world-simulator-2025" />. Planning only needs to compare outcomes and does not require generating an image, so decoder-free latent and JEPA models never draw one.

Also: data from Interactive World Simulator, as useful for policy training as an equal amount of real data <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" />.

## Three tests before believing a world-model claim

Fluent output can mask failure.

| Test | Asks | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intervention | Change the action, keep history fixed | "Action-marginalized" hallucination, a future that ignores the action, is a measured failure <Cite id="wm-hallucination-2026" /> |
| Ranking | Does the model order policies as real trials do? | |
| Transfer | Repeat both on new objects, contacts, viewpoints and horizons | Video models trained on simple physics failed outside their training range <Cite id="video-physical-law-2024" /> |

Visual quality is the least important criterion: in six video generation models, physical understanding was "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.
