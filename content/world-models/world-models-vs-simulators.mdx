Learned <Term id="world-model">world models</Term> and physics simulators answer one question: what will this action do? MuJoCo computes the answer from explicit geometry, dynamics and contact rules <Cite id="mujoco-2012" />; a learned model fits it to data, and many systems combine both.

Hwangbo and colleagues kept ANYmal's rigid-body physics and learned an "actuator net" for its 12 joints; the result ran about a thousand times faster than real time <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />.

## A simulator is written down; a world model is learned

A simulator outputs state that programs can check, such as joint angles; a renderer outputs only what a camera would see <Cite id="worldlabs-taxonomy-2026" />. MuJoCo writes bodies, constraints and contact as explicit equations and parameters <Cite id="mujoco-2012" />.

| Engine | What it brings |
| --- | --- |
| MuJoCo 4.0 (Nov 2026) | Speculative contacts run humanoid stair climbing and thin-object grasping 6 to 9 times faster <Cite id="mujoco-4-2026" /> |
| Isaac Lab | GPU simulation, sensors, tasks and batched environments for robot learning <Cite id="isaac-lab-2025" /> |
| Newton | Open GPU engine at the Linux Foundation since 2025 <Cite id="newton-lf-2025" />; experimental Skild AI solver in Nov 2026 <Cite id="newton-v1-8-0-2026" /> |
| MuJoCo Warp | 252 times faster than MJX on locomotion, by NVIDIA's count <Cite id="newton-manipulation-blog-2026" /> |

A learned world model estimates transitions from recorded data, often in a latent or video space, and trades parameter errors for data gaps. Hansen and Wang call hallucination "inherently a data coverage issue" <Cite id="wm-hallucination-2026" />. Graph Network-based Simulators keep particles but learn their motion <Cite id="graph-network-simulator-2020" />, and GPWM (November 2026) extends this to cloth and granular materials with 31% lower error <Cite id="gpwm-2026" />.

Also: the roles a 2026 survey lists <Cite id="world-model-survey-2026" />.

## A latent planner needs seconds per action, a video planner minutes

Physics is the cheap baseline: Isaac Gym steps thousands of environments in parallel on one GPU and trained robot tasks 100 to 1,000 times faster than CPU pipelines <Cite id="isaac-gym-2021" />.

<WorldModelCostTable className="my-6" />

V-JEPA 2-AC, a latent planner, takes about 16 seconds per action; a Cosmos video planner took about 4 minutes, roughly 15 times slower, both looking one step ahead <Cite id="vjepa2-2025" />. LeWorldModel plans up to 48 times faster than foundation-model world models <Cite id="leworldmodel-2026" />.

Also: Llama 3 8B as the text baseline <Cite id="llama-3-2024" /> <Cite id="llama-3-herd-2024" />.

## Clip generators run far slower than interactive world models

NVIDIA measured Cosmos Predict2's 14-billion-parameter Video2World at 56 GB of graphics memory and about 85 seconds per 480p clip on a GB200 <Cite id="cosmos-predict2-perf-2025" />. A policy that calls such a generator at every step inherits that delay.

| System | Speed |
| --- | --- |
| Genie 3 | 24 frames per second at 720p <Cite id="genie-3-2025" /> |
| Interactive World Simulator | 15 frames per second for over 10 minutes on one RTX 4090 <Cite id="interactive-world-simulator-2026" /> |
| DreamZero | 7 robot commands a second from a 14-billion-parameter video model <Cite id="dreamzero-2026" /> |
| 3D Gaussian Splatting | About 7 ms per frame for 1 to 5 million Gaussians, with no dynamics <Cite id="3dgs-2023" /> |

Also: Cosmos Predict1, 4 to 14 billion parameters <Cite id="cosmos-predict-2025" />.

## Physics stays ahead for untried actions and checkable safety limits

A planner, as in [model-based RL](/world-models/model-based-robot-learning/), tries actions outside the data by design. A physics engine integrates them while its modeled regimes hold; a learned model extrapolates, and the planner exploits its errors. Across six video generators, physical understanding was "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" <Cite id="physics-iq-2025" />.

Explicit state can be checked: joint limits, collision geometry and force bounds. Predicted video gives a safety monitor no such numbers.

<span id="where-learning-has-the-advantage" />
## Learning captures appearance and contact that are costly to model

Learned models pick up textures, reflections, cables and clutter that are expensive to render, and deformable contact and wear that resist hand-written parameters. Robotic World Model aims a neural simulator at policy optimization <Cite id="robotic-world-model-2025" />; RoboWorld aims one at policy evaluation, with rankings checked against real robots <Cite id="roboworld-2026" />.

<span id="three-hybrid-patterns" />
## Hybrids keep the engine and learn what it misses

Residual models are the most conservative hybrid because they fit named errors, such as actuator delay <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />. [Generative simulation](/world-models/generative-sim/) uses models to author scenes while the engine keeps contact causal.

| Pattern | Example | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn appearance | RoboGSim | Gaussian-splat scenes on a physics engine <Cite id="robogsim-2024" /> |
| Learn appearance | SplatSim | 86.25% zero-shot from splat-rendered images alone, against 97.5% with real data <Cite id="splatsim-2024" /> |
| Learn appearance | AutoRSR (Oct 2026) | Automated scan to simulation; 73.3% to 86.7% real success on three tasks <Cite id="autorsr-2026" /> |
| Learn appearance | PhysTwin | Spring-mass twins of ropes, plush toys, cloth and packages from video <Cite id="phystwin-2025" /> |
| Learn appearance | Soft-body evaluator | Scores packing and rope-routing policies in such twins <Cite id="softbody-splat-eval-2025" /> |
| Learn appearance | Cosmos (Nov 2026) | Renders video from an external simulator's state <Cite id="cosmos-interactive-sim-2026" /> |
| Generate content | RoboCasa, RoboCasa365 | Large sets of everyday manipulation environments <Cite id="robocasa-2024" /> <Cite id="robocasa365-2026" /> |
| Learn residuals | ASAP | Beat system identification and domain randomization on Unitree G1 tracking error <Cite id="asap-2025" /> |
| Adapt on hardware | SimDist | Sim-trained, only latent dynamics updated on real data; kept improving on manipulation and quadruped walking <Cite id="simdist-2026" /> |

## Evaluation must use the same real anchor

Both kinds have errors that policies learn to exploit, such as soft contacts in physics or visual shortcuts in learned models. One October 2026 audit traced objects ejected in a RoboTwin benchmark to a single collision constant <Cite id="simuguard-2026" />. SIMPLER builds matched environments and correlates simulated with real policy performance <Cite id="simpler-2024" />.

Score both on the same real outcome, with several policy families and held-out environments ([world-model evaluation](/world-models/evaluation/)).

## Choose by what can be specified and what the data cover

Use explicit physics when mechanics can be specified, untried actions are common, constraints need inspection or parallel stepping matters ([parallel simulation for RL](/rl-sim2real/parallel-sim-rl/)). Use learned models when observations hold structure costly to author and the data cover the decisions. Use hybrids for localized mismatches, as ANYmal's simulator did <Cite id="hwangbo-2019" />.

[Classical control](/classical/control/) and [latent dynamics](/world-models/latent-dynamics/) cover the two ends.
